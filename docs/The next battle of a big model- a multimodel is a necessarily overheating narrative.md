# The next battle of the big model: are multimodels inevitable or overheated narratives?

A deep argument about the future direction of AI.

---

# Introduction: An inescapable battle for direction

In May 2024, OpenAI released a GPS-4o. At the launch, the viewers were surprised when models analysed human faces in real time and responded with emotional voices. This image created a near-inherited consensus in the global scientific community: the future of large models is in a multi-model setting.

But is that the right thing to do?

In discussing the "future direction", we have mixed two different questions: whether multimodules are valuable (almost all agree); and whether multimodules should now be the preferred main direction, or even be used to dilute the depth of the pure text language model (a question that is truly divisive).

The article attempts to dissect these two issues and to give a more honest judgement, drawing on technological advances, product cases and market data in recent years.

---

# First dimension: the real value of perception extension

The most intuitive argument in favour of multi-module priority is that human perception is itself multi-module.

In the field of health, Google's Med-PaLM has reached the level of specialist in chest films, eye images, pathogenic scissors, and Stanford's research shows that the accuracy of joint visual-text diagnosis is 23 percentage points higher than that of pure text-based medical analysis. In the field of education, the student's photo-resolution function has increased participation by 40%. In the creative industry, Midjourney, Suno, Adobe Firefly has enabled tens of millions of ordinary people to have creative capacity at the professional level for the first time.

These are not demonstration cases, but commercial sites with a real user base.

From an informational point of view, this trend is also inherent in the logic. The amount of video information produced on Earth every day is far greater than the amount of textual information. A frame of 1080P images contains thousands of words. An AI that can only handle text, and it's a collection of polar kids in the world of human information.

---

# Second dimension: whether the "maturity" of the LLM is overestimated

However, "LLM is mature enough to turn to multi-model" theory, which is not easy to look at.

What is the evidence of "manifest maturity"? ChatGPT lives on over 200 million moons, and business clients cover more than 90% of the wealth of 500. This is impressive in terms of the size of the user.

But the picture is much more complicated in terms of reliability. Stanford’s 2024 evaluation shows that the LLM hallucination rate in medical literature summing up missions is still as high as 20-30%. The problem in the legal field is more specific: New York lawyers cite incidents that have been sanctioned by the courts for false cases that they have submitted to AI. 200 million users use a tool that is so high every day, suggesting that tools are popular, not mature.

More importantly, OpenAI’s o1/o3 series shows the potential of another path. Abandoning the fast-changing RLHF route, returning to the depth reasoning training, and increasing the ARC-AGI benchmark is several times the sum of all the multi-module advances over the past two years. This suggests that the LLM’s reasoning depth is far from reaching the ceiling.

---

# Third dimension: the multi-modular technology depth contest

Multimodel advocates often cite an exciting scene: GPT-4o looks at a photo and understands emotions, analyses the images, and provides a deep response in the context. Isn't that "manifest maturity"?

Here is a key technical detail that deserves serious consideration.

Almost all the current multi-model large model structures are ductular: the visual encoder converts images into token, and the language model processes them. There is no real two-way interaction between visual reasoning and linguistic reasoning — visual input is ultimately translated into language, and the core treatment remains linguistic reasoning.

One experiment illustrates the problem: if you tell the GPT-4o image in words ("a sad girl with a background of war ruins"), the quality of the analysis and direct viewing of the image is almost non-different. The visual input is "received end," the linguistic reasoning is "process end" - multi-model "synchronous" is mostly hallucination in the current structure, and more accurately described as: LLM is the core engine and multi-module is external input.

The 2024 study by the Massachusetts Institute of Technology also found that most model models have systemic failures in the visual reasoning of the real world – especially in the understanding of spatial relationships, counting, and 3D structures, which is significantly weaker than humans. Sora produced videos that were physically wrong in terms of liquid reversals, body-piercing models, and so on: the models mimic visual models at the statistical level, but do not really understand physical realities.

# Fourth dimension: the layer of commercial value

Multimodel commercial values require an important distinction.

What are enterprises doing with multi-module AI? Salesforce’s visual analysis core is to identify the product categories in the picture, HubSpot’s image is understood to be testing the frequency of brand logos, and plant safety tests to identify whether workers wear safety hats. These tasks really require machine vision (computer visual), not modeled large languages – enough in 2019 for ResNet. Most commercial security plants are also light-quantified, specialized visual models because the generic model is too costly and too slow to reason.

The report ofressen Horowitz 2024 confirms this: most corporate AI projects have reduced multimodule functions because of extra-predictable costs. Token costs of multimodule reasoning are several times higher than the text reasoning, which is a real constraint in price-sensitive markets.

There are high-value scenarios that really require the depth of multi-module models — complex medical image analysis, cross-module understanding of scientific data — but these scenarios require more area-specific models than generic multi-model models. In medical image diagnosis, the accuracy rate of GPT-4V (general multi-model) is 63%, and the specialized medical visual model (med-Gemini) is 84%, and the gap has not narrowed significantly with scale.

---

# Fifth dimension: the duality of data bottlenecks

Multimodel advocates have an important argument: text data is running out. Meta Research team estimates that high-quality Internet text data will reach the peak in 2026-2028. Images and video data are far more numerous than text, and are the way out of the "data wall".

This argument is true, but the logical chain is incomplete.

The video on YouTube does not have a high-quality synonym, and the image's alt text is extremely simple – multi-module data are large, but large amounts of noise. By contrast, synthetic data are another way of expanding text data – AlphaProof of DeepMind, using purely synthetic mathematics to prove data, has made a historic breakthrough in the international mathematical Olympics. In areas where objective validation mechanisms (procedures can function and prove their validity), synthetic data can effectively break through the data wall.

Both paths have their value and limitations, but the direct equivalent of "text data reaching the top" is "priority for multimodels" and is a simplification that skips many of the middle steps.

---

# Sixth dimension: asymmetrical risks of security ethics

The security risks of multi-module technologies are more difficult to handle than pure text and are often underestimated.

Deep forgery is the most typical security risk for multi-module technology. In 2024, a Hong Kong company was defrauded for a multi-modeled videoconference of $25 million. The harmfulness of multi-module content is more subtle – visual dependence, cross-language visual infarction, and dissemination of prohibited information under the guise of "normal" images — and more difficult to verify than text is exponentially.

C2PA digital watermarks, etc., are effective in laboratories, but in real-life transmission (transcoding, screenshot, compression) and in the application of visual emotional recognition, MIT Media Labs also found that facial muscle movements are much less relevant to emotional state than usually assumed, and that there are inherent accuracy defects and ethical risks to the application of emotional computations based on this scientific hypothesis.

This is not to deny the technology itself, but to say that the rapid expansion of multi-model capabilities in the period of undeveloped security alignment requires greater caution rather than rapid progress on the grounds that "governance will keep up with it".

---

# Seventh dimension: time series of resource allocation

In sum, the core of the debate is not "the value of multimodules", but "the timing and proportion of them" .

An interesting observation is that there is a clear gap between the concerns of the top global A.I. Labs in research publishing and the concerns in product publishing. At the product level, multimodels are the focus of marketing and the main players in the launch; at the research level, the most profound advances (o1/o3 reasoning breakthroughs, systematization of the thinking chain, self-conformity methods, validation mechanisms for synthetic data) are mostly in the area of purely linguistic models.

This fork illustrates something: the product goes in more exemplary shapes because users can see, feel and experience more intuitively; but the real problem of intelligence is that researchers still dig in the soil of linguistic reasoning.

Historically, there is a "situation ahead of technology" phase in every generation of technological developments. The current discussion of the model is partly in this category -- it may be in the right direction in the long run, but the proportion of short-term resource investment may be overestimated.

---

# Conclusion: The foundations and roofs, either one or the other

The most honest answer is not "More Models are the Future" or "LLM is the Focus," but: both are important but in order of priority.

At this stage, the reliability, depth of reasoning and security alignment of language models remain the most deserving priority directions for pooling resources. These capabilities are the foundations. Multimodular perception expansion is a real value added, but the resource is directed to the top when the foundations are not stable, with high structural risks.

This is not technical conservatism, but respect for engineering realities.

The release of the GPT-4o is truly impressive, and the business of a multi-model is really moving forward. But the real change in the world is not the function of "seeing the map," but the whole that has not yet been achieved when AI can actually integrate perception, reasoning, and reliability.
