## Start with the Numbers: Is $550 Billion Expensive?

In less than 3 years, ByteDance's secondary-market valuation rose from $220 billion to $600 billion, nearly tripling. This happened at a private company with no public financial statements and no daily mark-to-market price. It was driven by fragmented employee share transfers and institutional transactions.

Whether a price that rose this quickly is credible is the core question this article aims to answer.

### Valuation Timeline

| Time | Valuation | Event |
|------|------|------|
| December 2021 | ~$400 billion | Peak valuation |
| June 2023 | ~$220 billion | Trough for Chinese concept stocks, secondary-market panic |
| August 2025 | **$330 billion** | Internal employee share buyback price |
| November 2025 | **$480 billion** | Secondary-market transaction |
| February 2026 | **$550 billion** | General Atlantic sold equity |
| April 2026 | **~$600 billion** | Latest secondary-market transaction round |
| May 6, 2026 | **$313.46** per share | Nasdaq Private Market valuation |

(Sources: Yahoo Finance, TSG Invest, Nasdaq Private Market)

### Valuation Benchmarking: Is ByteDance Expensive?

| Company | Market cap/valuation | 2025 revenue | PS (price-to-sales) | 2025 net profit | PE |
|------|---------|---------|------------|-----------|-----|
| **ByteDance** | **$550 billion** | **$186 billion** | **3.0x** | **Down >70%, adjusted ~$15-20 billion** | **28-37x (adjusted)** |
| Meta | ~$1.7 trillion | ~$170 billion | 10x | ~$50 billion | 34x |
| Tencent | ~$550 billion | ~$90 billion | 6.1x | ~$50 billion | 11x |
| Alibaba | ~$330 billion | ~$130 billion | 2.5x | ~$20 billion | 16x |

(Note: ByteDance's 2025 reported net profit was materially reduced by IFRS accounting treatment and AI investment. The table above uses an "adjusted" basis. Sources: company filings and media reports)

Several key findings:

**1. On price-to-sales, ByteDance is not expensive**

PS is 3.0x, below Meta's 10x and also below Tencent's 6.1x. Given ByteDance's 20%+ revenue growth, a 3x PS multiple does not look overvalued.

**2. On price-to-earnings, it depends which profit number you use**

- Using 2024 net profit of $33 billion: PE is about **17x**, very cheap
- Using 2025 adjusted profit of ~$15-20 billion after the AI-driven collapse: PE is about **28-37x**, reasonable but not cheap
- Using expected 2024 profit of $50 billion, reflecting pre-AI investment earnings power: PE is about **11x**, extremely cheap

**The key valuation debate is whether the 2025 profit collapse is a "one-off strategic investment" or the "new normal."**

If AI investment is cyclical, similar to Meta's heavy Reality Labs investment in 2022-2023, profits will recover in 2027-2028 and the current valuation is low. If AI investment continues for many years, similar to Amazon's long-term profit sacrifice, a high PE will become normal.

Counterargument: a private-company valuation is "seller-priced." General Atlantic says it is worth $550 billion because it wants to sell at that price. The true market price can be verified only through public trading. Secondary-market liquidity is extremely low, and price discovery is far less reliable than for listed companies.

## Why ByteDance Has Not Gone Public

This is one of the questions investors ask most often.

Since 2018, ByteDance has repeatedly been rumored to be "about to list," but no substantive progress has followed. In April 2026, Li Liang, vice president of Douyin Group, publicly denied rumors that "ByteDance will list in Hong Kong," saying he could not rule out that someone was hyping "ByteDance concept stocks" (source: Douyin Bulletin).

### Reasons for Not Listing

| Reason | Explanation |
|------|------|
| **Zhang Yiming does not need public-market financing** | The company has ample cash flow and does not need money. The only benefit of listing would be to provide exit channels for early investors and employees, but ByteDance has already solved that through regular share buybacks and secondary-market transactions |
| **Listing means transparency** | ByteDance would need to disclose detailed financial data, undergo regulatory review, and face questions from analysts and media. For a company "accustomed to opacity," this is a huge cost |
| **Dual regulatory risk from China and the U.S.** | China's regulatory stance toward internet companies remains uncertain; U.S. audit requirements for Chinese concept stocks are intertwined with the TikTok issue. Any listing venue carries political risk |
| **The valuation is already high** | A $550 billion valuation has already been achieved through the secondary market, and listing may not produce a higher premium |
| **Control** | After listing, ByteDance would have to face demands from public shareholders, including dividends, buybacks, and disclosure. This would weaken Zhang Yiming's absolute control |

### Possible Listing Paths

| Path | Probability (est.) | Timeline |
|------|-----------|--------|
| **Subsidiary lists first (Dongchedi Hong Kong IPO)** | Relatively high | **Possibly in 2026** |
| Overall ByteDance Hong Kong listing | Low | After 2028 |
| ByteDance U.S. listing | Extremely low | Almost impossible before the TikTok issue is resolved |
| ByteDance A-share/STAR Market listing | Extremely low | VIE structure restrictions |

**Latest development: ByteDance's auto information platform "Dongchedi" is planning a 2026 Hong Kong IPO, aiming to raise $1.0-1.5 billion at a valuation of about $3.0 billion.** If successful, it would be ByteDance's **first independently listed subsidiary** (sources: Sina Finance, 36Kr).

The significance of a Dongchedi listing is not its size, because $3.0 billion is immaterial for ByteDance. Rather, it may **test the capital market's acceptance of "ByteDance system" companies**. If the response is good, it could pave the way for larger listings later.

Counterargument: not listing is unfavorable to early investors and employees because exit channels are limited and valuation is opaque. But for Zhang Yiming and management, it is beneficial: they are not constrained by public shareholders and can freely make long-term investments, such as AI. From management's perspective, the incentive not to list is far stronger than the incentive to list.

## Can Ordinary Investors Participate?

Direct ways to participate in ByteDance are extremely limited:

| Method | Threshold | Feasibility |
|------|------|--------|
| **Buying Pre-IPO shares in the secondary market** | Accredited investor certification, minimum investment usually $100,000+ | Limited to high-net-worth investors/institutions |
| **Through platforms such as UpMarket/Nasdaq Private Market** | Accredited investors | Possible, but liquidity is poor and discounts/premiums are uncertain |
| **Investing in ByteDance shareholders** | Indirect participation | SoftBank Vision Fund holds ByteDance shares, but ByteDance is only a limited share of SoftBank's total assets |
| **Waiting for an IPO** | Uncertain | Timing uncertain |
| **Investing in ByteDance competitors/beneficiaries** | Public markets | Tencent, Kuaishou, Meta, etc. |

**For the vast majority of ordinary investors, ByteDance is currently not investable.** This is both a regret and a form of protection. You cannot make a wise investment decision in a market with extreme information asymmetry.

## Three Scenarios: What Is ByteDance Worth in 5 Years?

### Scenario 1: Base Case: $800-1000 Billion (Probability Around 55%)

**Assumptions:**
- Douyin's domestic business maintains 10-15% growth and its moat remains solid
- TikTok USDS operates stably, and ByteDance retains a limited but valuable economic interest
- AI investment begins to generate returns in 2027-2028, and Volcano Engine revenue exceeds RMB 50 billion
- ByteDance does not list, and valuation gradually rises through the secondary market
- Total revenue reaches $300-350 billion, and profit recovers to $40-60 billion

**Conclusion:** From the current $550 billion to $800-1000 billion, the 5-year return would be about **45-80%**, or **8-13%** annualized.

### Scenario 2: Bull Case: $1200-1500 Billion (Probability Around 20%)

**Assumptions:**
- Douyin + TikTok e-commerce fully breaks out, with combined GMV exceeding RMB 10 trillion
- Doubao/Volcano Engine becomes the leader in China's AI infrastructure
- TikTok globalization reaccelerates, with the USDS model removing regulatory concerns
- Some ByteDance businesses or the overall company list, and the market awards a premium
- Total revenue reaches $400 billion+, and profit reaches $80 billion+

**Conclusion:** From $550 billion to $1200-1500 billion, the 5-year return would be **120-170%**, or **17-22%** annualized.

### Scenario 3: Bear Case: $200-300 Billion (Probability Around 25%)

**Assumptions:**
- AI investment returns fall short of expectations, keeping profits depressed for the long term
- More countries copy the U.S. and force TikTok divestitures, dismembering the overseas business
- Douyin's domestic business hits a ceiling, with growth falling to single digits
- U.S.-China relations deteriorate further, affecting ByteDance's global business
- Zhang Yiming exits management or loses strategic direction

**Conclusion:** A fall from $550 billion to $200-300 billion would mean a 5-year loss of **45-64%**.

**The probability-weighted expected return is for reference only. Scenario probabilities themselves are subjective judgments.** What matters is not calculating a precise number, but understanding the key variables that drive valuation change.

If I had to list the 5 most important variables for ByteDance over the next 5 years, I would rank them as follows:

| Rank | Variable | Why it matters |

## Final Judgment

Returning to the opening question: **Is ByteDance worth $550 billion?**

If you value "certainty," ByteDance is not for you. Its financial data are opaque, its core assets face geopolitical risk, the payback cycle for AI investment is unknown, and ordinary investors cannot participate.

If you value "growth," ByteDance may be one of the most attractive technology companies in the world. Revenue of $186 billion is still growing at 20%, its AI layout is at the front of China, and the user scale of Douyin + TikTok is comparable to Meta.

**But "attractiveness" and "investment value" are two different things.** A company whose financial statements you cannot see, whose shares you cannot buy, and whose listing timeline you do not know is, however attractive, still just a spectator sport for ordinary investors.

The conclusion of this series is not "buy" or "do not buy." It is:

> **See clearly first, then decide. If you cannot see clearly, do not decide.**

For ByteDance, "not being able to see clearly" is itself the most important conclusion.
