# AI Valuation Pockets: Two AI Monetization Leaders Held Back by "Old Labels"

> Research date: June 5, 2026 | Data through: June 4, 2026

Markets have a habit: once they put a label on a company, it is hard to peel it off. Salesforce has been called a "CRM company" for 20 years, and Adobe has been called a "design software company" for 30 years. Yet as their AI businesses grow at 200%, the market is still valuing them with "old software company" multiples.

This report focuses on two companies whose AI monetization is being severely underestimated: Salesforce (CRM) and Adobe (ADBE). It breaks down the substance of their AI businesses, the degree of valuation mismatch, and the investment opportunity.

## 1. Salesforce (CRM): Buying the Enterprise AI Agent Leader at 22x PE

**Current share price: $190.61 | Market cap: ~$185 billion | PE (TTM): 22.7x | Forward PE: 14.2x**
(Data sources: [MacroTrends](https://www.macrotrends.net/stocks/charts/CRM/salesforce/pe-ratio), [GuruFocus](https://www.gurufocus.com/term/pettm/CRM))

### 1. Core Thesis: The Market Sees 10% Revenue Growth, Not 200% AI Growth

Salesforce generated $41.5 billion of revenue in FY2026 (ended January 2026), up 10% year over year. In the latest Q1 FY2027 quarter (ended April 2026), revenue was $11.1 billion, up 13% year over year. On the surface, it looks like a "stable but boring" SaaS company.

But underneath: **Agentforce and Data 360 ARR has reached $3.4 billion, up more than 200% year over year.** Agentforce alone contributed $1.2 billion of ARR, up 205% year over year. In other words, the AI business is growing 20 times faster than the company overall.

The market assigns a 22x PE because it only sees the 10% overall growth rate. But if AI rises from roughly 8% of revenue today to 20%, the company's growth profile will look entirely different.

(Data sources: [Salesforce Q4 FY2026 earnings](https://www.salesforce.com/news/press-releases/2026/02/25/fy26-q4-earnings/), [Q1 FY2027 earnings](https://investor.salesforce.com/news/news-details/2026/Salesforce-Reports-Record-First-Quarter-Fiscal-2026-Results/default.aspx))

### 2. Deep Dive on Agentforce: What Is an AI Agent, and Why Salesforce?

**An AI Agent is not a chatbot.** A chatbot answers questions according to a script. An AI Agent can reason independently, call data, and execute actions. For example, if a customer calls and says, "I want to return the shoes I bought last week," a traditional customer service system requires a human to look up the order, verify the information, and initiate the refund process. An Agentforce AI Agent can complete the entire chain automatically: understanding the customer's intent, retrieving order data from the CRM, verifying return eligibility, and initiating the refund in the ERP system, all without human intervention.

**Salesforce has natural advantages in AI Agents for three reasons:**

- **Data moat**: The world's largest enterprise customer relationship database. AI Agents need customer history, purchase records, and service records to make decisions. Those data already sit inside Salesforce. An AI Agent without data is an engine spinning in place.
- **Workflow moat**: Salesforce's Sales Cloud, Service Cloud, and Commerce Cloud cover the full chain from sales to post-sale service. AI Agents do not just need to "think"; they need to "act." Salesforce's workflow platform lets them execute actions directly.
- **Trust moat**: Enterprises will not hand sensitive customer data to startups. Salesforce serves 150,000+ enterprise customers, and 90% of the Fortune 500 are customers.

**What do 29,000 Agentforce deals tell us?** In Q4 FY2026, Agentforce added 50% more deals sequentially, reaching a cumulative 29,000. But the more important data point is this: only roughly 8% of Salesforce customers have adopted Agentforce so far. That means **92% of the installed base has not yet converted**, leaving substantial internal penetration runway.

Agentforce 360 became generally available in February 2026. Powered by the Atlas reasoning engine, it can automatically resolve 85% of customer inquiries and reduce human handoff rates to as low as 5%.

### 4. Scenario Analysis: If AI Rises to 15-20% of Revenue

|------|-----------|--------|------------|---------|--------|
| Current state | 8% | 200% | 5% | ~10% | 22x |

**The current forward PE of just 14.2x implies the market is not even paying a fair price for the traditional business, let alone the AI upside.**

### 7. Is the FY2030 Revenue Target of $63 Billion Credible?

In its FY2026 Q4 earnings report, Salesforce raised its FY2030 revenue target to $63 billion (including Informatica), with an organic growth target of $60 billion+ (implying roughly 10%+ CAGR from FY2026 to FY2030). It also introduced a "50 by FY30" framework, defined as subscription growth plus non-GAAP operating margin reaching 50.

**Credibility assessment**:

- Moving from $41.5 billion to $63 billion requires roughly 11% CAGR. Given the current 13% growth rate and the acceleration in AI, this is **entirely achievable**.
- Agentforce has penetrated only 8% of customers today. If penetration rises to 30-40%, and ARPU increases, the AI business alone could contribute $6-8 billion of incremental revenue.
- The company also announced a $50 billion buyback plan, signaling management confidence in cash flow and earnings power.

**But watch the details**: the $63 billion target includes Informatica revenue, while the organic growth portion is $60 billion+. The actual organic CAGR is roughly 10%, which is not aggressive, but also not especially conservative.

(Data sources: [Seeking Alpha](https://seekingalpha.com/news/4557433-salesforce-targets-63b-revenue-by-fy30-while-expanding-agentforce-and-50b-buyback), [Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/salesforce-targets-60b-revenue-fy30-unveils-50-fy30-growth-framework))

### 9. Risks

- **Traditional CRM growth slows**: If AI penetration falls short of expectations, overall growth could slip back to 8-9%, which would not support a valuation rerating.
- **AI monetization is slower than expected**: Agentforce's pricing model is still evolving (from $2/conversation to Flex Credits to AELA), suggesting the monetization path has not been fully validated. High implementation costs ($150,000-$600,000 per year) may limit SMB adoption.
- **Microsoft competition**: If Microsoft deeply integrates Copilot with Dynamics 365, it could become a real threat in CRM AI.
- **Macro risk**: Lower enterprise IT spending could slow the pace of AI purchases.

### 10. Conclusion: Buy

**Salesforce is an AI winner whose valuation is being severely constrained by an "old label."** A 22x PE only reflects a traditional CRM company growing 10%, while completely ignoring Agentforce's 200%+ growth rate and $3.4 billion of AI ARR. A 14x forward PE is even more absurd: at this price, investors are not even paying up for a mature SaaS company without an AI business.

**Buy thesis**:
1. As AI rises from 8% to 20% of revenue, overall growth should accelerate from 10% to 14-16%
2. A fair PE should be 25-30x, implying 30-50% upside
3. A 34% margin + $14.4 billion of free cash flow + a $50 billion buyback provide downside support
4. 92% of the installed base has not yet adopted Agentforce, making internal penetration the most visible source of growth

**Expected return**: If the AI business develops as expected, FY2028 EPS could reach $12-14. At 25x PE, that implies a $300-350 share price, or 60-85% upside from the current $190.

**Positioning suggestion**: Investors can establish a core position at the current price (3-5%) and track quarterly Agentforce ARR growth and customer penetration as add signals.

## 2. Adobe (ADBE): Buying the AI Creative Tools Monopolist at 15x PE

**Current share price: $254.93 | Market cap: ~$105 billion | PE (TTM): 14.9x | Forward PE: 10.5x**
(Data sources: [GuruFocus](https://www.gurufocus.com/term/pe-ratio/ADBE), [FinanceCharts](https://www.financecharts.com/stocks/ADBE/value/pe-ratio))

### 1. Core Thesis: A Creative Software Monopolist Trades at Its Lowest Historical PE Because the Market Mistakes "Incremental" for "Substitutive"

Adobe's PE has fallen from a 10-year average of 44.6x to 14.9x. This is not because the company has suffered a major business problem: Q1 FY2026 revenue was $6.4 billion, up 12% year over year; non-GAAP EPS was $6.06, up 19%; margin was 47.4%; and the free cash flow margin was 45.7%.

The market's fear comes down to one question: **Will AI disrupt Adobe?** Tools such as Midjourney, Canva, and Sora make it feel as if "everyone can design." Does that mean Adobe's professional tools will be displaced?

The data tell the opposite story: **Firefly shows that AI is incremental to Adobe, not a replacement.**

(Data sources: [Adobe Q1 FY2026 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/796343/000079634326000056/adbe-20260227.htm), [StockOpine](https://www.stockopine.com/p/adobes-q1-fy26-strong-execution-but))

### 2. Deep Dive on Firefly Product and Commercialization

**What is Firefly?** Adobe Firefly is Adobe's in-house generative AI engine, deeply integrated across Photoshop, Illustrator, Premiere Pro, and the rest of its product suite. What can it do? In Photoshop, a user can select an area, describe the desired content in natural language, and let AI generate and blend it seamlessly. In Premiere Pro, AI can automatically extend video frames. This is not a standalone AI toy; it is an AI accelerator embedded inside professional workflows.

**Key data**:
- More than 22 billion AI assets generated cumulatively (as of April 2025)
- 75% of Fortune 500 companies use Firefly
- More than 6 million monthly active users (mid-2026, up 65% year over year)
- 45% of Creative Cloud subscribers adopted Firefly within 12 months
- Generative credit consumption grew 45%+ sequentially

**But direct revenue is only around $400 million. Why?**

Because Firefly's business model is not "selling AI"; it is "using AI to sell more subscriptions." Firefly has three main monetization paths:
1. **Improving Creative Cloud stickiness and retention**: Net revenue retention exceeds 130%, and Firefly is a key driver of renewals and upgrades
2. **Lifting ARPU**: Users upgrade to higher-priced plans to obtain more generative credits
3. **Firefly Services API**: A standalone enterprise API service starting at $1,000/month, aimed at companies that need large-scale generation of brand-safe content

$400 million of direct revenue may look small, but Firefly-driven indirect revenue (better retention, higher ARPU, new user acquisition) could be 5-10 times the direct revenue. It is like asking, "How much direct revenue does Face ID generate for the iPhone?" The question itself is wrong.

(Data sources: [Fueler](https://fueler.io/blog/adobe-firefly-usage-revenue-valuation-growth-statistics), [CompaniesHistory](https://www.companieshistory.com/adobe-firefly-statistics/))

### 3. Is AI a Threat or an Opportunity?

**Bear case (AI is a threat)**:
- Midjourney/Stable Diffusion allow non-professionals to generate high-quality images, lowering the barrier to creative tools
- Canva uses AI to make "everyone a designer," eroding Adobe Express and the entry-level market
- AI may reduce demand for professional designers, reducing the number of paid Creative Cloud users
- Figma has roughly 80% share in UI/UX design; after Adobe abandoned its $20 billion acquisition, Figma remains an independent threat

**Bull case (AI is an opportunity)**:
- **Copyright safety is the key differentiator**: Firefly is trained only on Adobe Stock licensed materials, openly licensed content, and public-domain materials. This matters enormously. Midjourney faces copyright lawsuits from Disney, Universal, and Warner Bros. (2025-2026), and enterprise legal teams are reluctant to use tools with legal risk. **Adobe is the only major AI image generation tool offering IP indemnification.**
- **Embedded advantage**: Firefly is not a standalone app. It is embedded in the tools that 32.5 million Creative Cloud subscribers open every day. Midjourney requires users to switch tools; Adobe delivers AI directly within users' existing workflows.
- **AI expands the market rather than shrinking it**: 72% of designers already use generative AI in their workflows, and 91% say AI improves output quality. AI makes designers more efficient; it does not eliminate them. Higher efficiency means more design demand can be fulfilled.
- **AI design tools market share**: Firefly leads with 29% market share, ahead of Midjourney, Canva AI, and DALL-E.

**Conclusion: AI is both a risk and an opportunity for Adobe, but the opportunity far outweighs the risk.** Copyright safety alone is enough to determine the enterprise choice between Firefly and Midjourney. Enterprises will not take legal risk just to save a little money.

(Data sources: [Neuronad](https://neuronad.com/adobe-firefly-vs-midjourney/), [FluxNote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/adobe-firefly-vs-midjourney-for-commercial-use), [Figma](https://www.figma.com/resource-library/ai-design-tools/))

### 4. Valuation Deep Dive

**Where does a 15x PE sit in Adobe's history?**

| Time frame | Average PE |
|---------|--------|
| 10-year average | 44.6x |
| 5-year average | 39.2x |
| 3-year average | 36.2x |
| 12-month average | 19.2x |
| **Current** | **14.9x** |

**A 14.9x PE is Adobe's absolute historical low since its SaaS transition.** It is 67% below the 10-year average and 59% below the 3-year average. This valuation level usually appears only when a company faces an existential crisis, yet Adobe revenue is growing 12%, margins are near 50%, and free cash flow is abundant.

**DCF valuation**:
Using a two-stage free cash flow discount model:
- Estimated 2026 FCF of roughly $9.65 billion, gradually rising to roughly $12.65 billion by 2030
- Discount rate of 10%
- Model-implied intrinsic value of roughly $525/share
- Current price of $255, implying a discount of roughly 53%

Even if we apply a 30% haircut to the DCF valuation to account for AI uncertainty, intrinsic value is still around $370, or 45% upside from the current price.

**If Firefly reaches $2-3 billion of annual revenue, what PE should Adobe command?**
Assume FY2029 Firefly direct + indirect revenue contribution reaches $2-3 billion (7-10% of total revenue) and grows at 30%+:
- Total revenue would reach $30-32 billion, lifting overall growth to 13-15%
- The corresponding PE should be 25-30x
- On expected FY2029 EPS of $22-24, the share price should be in the $550-720 range

(Data sources: [Simply Wall St](https://simplywall.st/stocks/us/software/nasdaq-adbe/adobe), [MacroTrends](https://www.macrotrends.net/stocks/charts/ADBE/adobe/pe-ratio))

### 5. Creative Cloud Subscription Stickiness

| Metric | Data |
|------|------|
| Creative Cloud paid subscribers | ~41 million (as of year-end 2025) |
| Net additions | 1 million+ per quarter (about 11,600 per day) |
| Net revenue retention | 130%+ |

| Total ARR | $26.06 billion (Q1 FY2026) |

**What does 130%+ net revenue retention mean?** Even without acquiring any new customers, Adobe can grow revenue 30% annually through expansion within the existing customer base. That is top-tier in SaaS and says two things: (1) customer churn is minimal; (2) customers spend more every year. Since Firefly launched, this trend has accelerated.

**The 41 million Creative Cloud subscribers are Adobe's deepest moat.** Designers' entire workflow, from assets to design to output, lives inside the Adobe ecosystem. Switching costs are extremely high. A designer who has used Photoshop for 10 years will not migrate to Canva simply because Canva added AI features, because their skills, templates, and asset library are all tied to the Adobe ecosystem.

### 6. Buybacks and Shareholder Returns

Adobe is executing a $25 billion share repurchase program (through March 2028), and repurchased $2.48 billion in Q1 FY2026 alone. At the current market cap of $105 billion, a $25 billion buyback equals 24% of market capitalization, which is a very aggressive repurchase pace.

Large-scale buybacks at historical-low PE levels are the most direct way for management to express confidence with real capital.

(Data source: [TradingKey](https://www.tradingkey.com/analysis/stocks/us-stocks/261808995-adobe-stock-buyback-ai-firefly-valuation-cashflow-ceo-ai-tradingkey))

### 7. Risks

- **AI reduces demand for creative tools**: If AI truly makes "everyone a designer," the TAM for professional creative tools could shrink. But current data suggest AI is expanding creative demand rather than shrinking it, and user growth is accelerating.
- **Canva/Figma competition**: Canva is growing quickly in non-professional design, while Figma dominates UI/UX design. Adobe's core market (professional creative production) remains safe for now, but edge markets are being eroded.
- **Growth of only 10-12%**: At the current PE, 10-12% growth is actually enough to support valuation repair. But if growth falls below 8%, the rerating catalyst weakens.
- **CEO transition risk**: Adobe is going through a CEO transition, and the new management team's strategic execution remains to be proven.
- **Firefly monetization falls short**: If Firefly never moves from "improving stickiness" to "direct revenue generation," the market may continue discounting the AI story.

### 8. Conclusion: Strong Buy

**Adobe is one of the most undervalued high-quality assets in the entire technology sector.** At 14.9x PE, investors are buying a company with:
- A monopoly-like creative software position (29% AI design market share, 41 million paid users)
- 12% revenue growth, 47% margins, and a 46% free cash flow margin
- 130%+ net revenue retention
- A copyright-safe AI moat (the only major AI image tool offering IP indemnification)
- A $25 billion buyback program being executed near historical valuation lows

**This valuation level would only make sense if the company faced an existential threat, but Adobe's fundamentals do not support that conclusion.**

**Buy thesis**:
1. Valuation mean reversion: a move from 14.9x back to the 3-year average of 36x would more than double the share price
2. AI monetization acceleration: Firefly's direct + indirect contribution should lift total growth from 12% to 14-16%
3. Buybacks accelerate EPS growth: the $25 billion buyback is being executed at depressed valuations, so EPS growth should exceed revenue growth
4. DCF intrinsic value is $525, implying a 53% discount today

**Expected return**:
- Conservative scenario (PE recovers to 25x): FY2027 EPS ~$22, share price $550, upside 115%
- Base scenario (PE recovers to 30x): FY2027 EPS ~$22, share price $660, upside 160%
- Bearish scenario (PE stays at 20x): FY2027 EPS ~$22, share price $440, upside 73%

**Positioning suggestion**: Investors can establish a larger position at the current price (5-8%). Adobe offers a "low valuation + high certainty" combination, with strong downside protection (15x PE + $25 billion buyback) and substantial upside optionality (valuation mean reversion + accelerating AI monetization). It is one of the few AI investment opportunities in the 2026 U.S. equity market with a truly ample margin of safety.
