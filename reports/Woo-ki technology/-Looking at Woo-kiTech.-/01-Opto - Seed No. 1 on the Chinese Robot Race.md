## A post-90s generation and his robot empire

A valuation of 42 billion corresponds to revenue of 1.7 billion. PS 25 times, revenue increased 13 times in three years, and gross profit margin is higher than Apple - Yushu Technology is currently the most watched and misunderstood company on the Chinese robotics track.

At the 2025 Spring Festival Gala, 16 quadruped robots lined up on the stage, performed somersaults, and danced hip-hop. For the first time, audiences across the country knew the name "Ushu Technology". But in the capital circle, this company is no longer a new face - it has embarked on a growth curve that makes all robot peers jealous:

| Year | Revenue | Growth rate | Excluding non-net profit | Gross profit margin |
|------|------|------|-----------|--------|
| 2023 | **159 million yuan** | — | Loss | — |
| 2024 | **392 million yuan** | +147% | Small profit | — |
| 2025 | **1.708 billion yuan** | **+335%** | **600 million yuan (+674%)** | **60.27%** |

(Data source: Yushu Technology prospectus)

Revenue has increased **13 times** in three years, and gross profit margins have been **60%**—a number that exceeds that of Apple and is almost unheard of among hardware companies.

On March 20, 2026, Yushu Technology officially submitted an IPO application for the Science and Technology Innovation Board, intending to raise **4.2 billion**, corresponding to a valuation of approximately **42 billion**. If the listing is successful, it will become the "first A-share humanoid robot stock"**.

The founder, Wang Xingxing, was born in 1990 in Ningbo, Zhejiang. I received a bachelor's degree from Zhejiang University of Science and Technology and a master's degree in mechanical engineering from Shanghai University. After graduation, I stayed at DJI for less than half a year before quitting my job to start my own business. He was 26 years old when he founded Yushu Technology in 2016 and is now 36 years old - holding **26.97%** of the shares and controlling nearly **70% of the voting rights**.

One sentence summarizes the current situation of Yushu Technology: **The world's largest humanoid robot shipments, the global share of quadruped robots is about 70%, the gross profit margin is 60%, and it has just submitted an IPO - but a valuation of 42 billion corresponds to a revenue of 1.7 billion, a PS of about 25 times. **

This is an exciting company. But after getting excited, you need to calmly ask a few questions.

## Three common misunderstandings about Yushu Technology

### Misunderstanding 1: Yushu Technology "makes robot dogs"

This is the most widespread perception and is outdated.

In 2025, Yushu Technology’s revenue structure will undergo a key turning point:

| Business | Revenue proportion | Change |
|------|---------|------|
| Quadruped robots (robot dogs) | About 48% | Still the basic market |
| **Humanoid robots** | **About 52%** | **For the first time more than four-legged robots** |

(Data source: Yushu Technology prospectus)

Humanoid robot revenue will surpass four-legged robots for the first time in 2025 and become the number one business. Yushu is no longer a "robot dog company", it is transforming from four-legged to humanoid - this is the core support point of its 42 billion valuation.

**But the opposite view is**: **73.6%** of humanoid robot revenue comes from scientific research and education customers (data for the first three quarters of 2025, source: prospectus), and the real industrial and consumer scenarios have not yet opened. The reason why quadruped robots are looked down upon is precisely because they have completed the path from scientific research to commercial use - humanoid robots are still in the stage of "selling to laboratories".

### Misunderstanding 2: Yushu = "Chinese version of Boston Dynamics"

This analogy seems reasonable, but in fact it is very wrong.

| Dimension | Boston Dynamics | Yushu Technology |
|------|-----------|---------|
| Established | 1992, incubated by MIT | 2016, Hangzhou |
| Technical roadmap | Hydraulics → Switch to electric drive in 2024 | **Choose electric drive from the first day** |
| Product positioning | High-end, one million US dollars per unit | Mid- to low-end, humanoid starting from **29,900 yuan** |
| Cumulative sales | About 2,000 robot dog Spots | Four-legged **30,000+ units**, humanoid **5,500 units** |
| Business model | R&D-driven, long-term losses | Cost-driven, **profitable by 2025** |
| Parent company | Hyundai Motor Group | Independent, about to IPO |

(Data sources: prospectus, TrendForce, public reports)

Boston Dynamics "does the most difficult technology first, and then considers the cost" - a single Atlas unit sells for over one million US dollars. Yushu, in turn, "maximizes costs first and wins by volume" - the price of the R1 humanoid robot starts at **29,900 yuan**, which is less than **1/200** of Atlas.

Boston Dynamics founder Raibert publicly stated that he had purchased Yushu's robots for testing and said "they did a great job" (Source: Observer.com, 2025.2).

A more accurate analogy is: ** Yushu is to Boston Dynamics, similar to Xiaomi is to Apple** - entering the market with the ultimate cost-effectiveness and establishing barriers based on scale effects.

**But the opposing view is**: Xiaomi can succeed because smartphones already have a mature application ecosystem. There is no "killer application" for humanoid robots - a price war in a market with no clear demand may just be "cheap but no one will buy it".

### Misunderstanding 3: Number one in shipments = win

In 2025, Yushu’s humanoid robots shipped **5,500 units**, ranking first in the world. But this "first" needs a few comments:

| Company | 2025 Shipments | Core Strengths |
|------|-----------|---------|
| **Yushu Technology** | **5,500 units (No. 1)** | Cost-effective + full-stack self-developed hardware |
| Zhiyuan Robot | 5,168 units (2nd) | Strong AI capabilities + supply chain integration |
| Tesla Optimus | <5,000 units | **FSD end-to-end large model**, mass production in 2027 |
| Figure AI | Small amount | OpenAI cooperation, BMW factory pilot |
| UBTECH | About 600 units | Already on the market, 3,000+ patents |

(Data source: TrendForce, public reports)

Yushu and Zhiyuan together account for about **80%** of global humanoid robot shipments - but the total market size is too small. Global shipments of humanoid robots in 2025 will be less than **15,000 units**, which is less than the sales volume of a medium-sized mobile phone brand in a day.

**The more critical issue is**: shipments first depend on "hardware cost-effectiveness" - but the final competitiveness of humanoid robots is likely not to be in the hardware, but in the **AI brain**. Tesla has FSD end-to-end large models that can be directly migrated to Optimus, and Figure AI has the support of OpenAI - Yushu's R&D investment only accounts for **7.73%** of revenue (approximately **90.2 million yuan**), and there is an order of magnitude gap between Tesla's AI team of 10,000 people.

**The number one position in shipments is a fact today, but it is not a moat for tomorrow. **
