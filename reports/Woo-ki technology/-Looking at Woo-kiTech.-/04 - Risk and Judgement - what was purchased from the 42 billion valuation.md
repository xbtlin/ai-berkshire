** Core issue**: The 42 billion valuation assumes "all good" - industry eruptions, commercialization advances, AI patches. If no one assumes anything, valuation risks a significant return.

# What should investors think of this company?

This series does not give advice on business. But it helps you figure out the framework for thinking.

If you look good, you need to believe:

1. The human robot industry really broke out in 3-5 years (from 15,000 to hundreds of thousands)
2. The cost advantage of Woo-sook can be translated into lasting barriers to scale
3. AI Shortboards can be supplemented by IPO + external collaboration, or the hardware platform route is valuable enough
Tesla Optimus will not pose a fatal threat in 2-3 years.
5. 42 billion valuations are absorbed by growth after 2-3 years

If you look at the sky tree, you need to believe:

1. Human robots are false demands and specialized robots (machine arms, etc.) are sufficient to meet the industrial landscape

# A key thinking framework

** Don't ask "How's Woo-Tech?" ** Ask "How's the 42 billion worth of Woo-Tech?"

Woo-gi is a good hardware company, and that's not controversial. The question is: has the price been fully priced for "excellent" even overdrafted?

To compare, Tesla was also a good company in 2021, but the market value of $1.2 trillion means that the market has already pre-priced growth expectations for 2025 — buying investors at that point, with limited returns even if the company continues to grow.

** 42 billion valued tree technology may face the same situation: companies continue to grow, but valuations do not necessarily continue to rise.**

If you're still feeling "unsure" after this series -- congratulations, that means you understand.
