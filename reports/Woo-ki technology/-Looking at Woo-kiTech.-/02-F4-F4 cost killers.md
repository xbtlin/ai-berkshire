# Products and technology - first-class hardware "cost killer"

## What exactly does Yushu sell?

A humanoid robot sells for 29,900 yuan - a price that is more than 200 times cheaper than the Boston Dynamics Atlas. The gross profit margin is as high as 60%, surpassing Apple. The fact that these two things are true at the same time shows that Yushu has done something really different in terms of cost structure.

Yushu Technology has three product lines, and the logic of the three lines is completely different.

### Line One: Quadruped Robots – Proven Cash Cows

Quadruped robots are Yushu's starting business, with cumulative sales exceeding **30,000 units** and a global market share of approximately **70%** (source: TrendForce).

| Products | Positioning | Price | Target Customers |
|------|------|------|---------|
| Go2 Air | Introduction to consumption | About **$1,600** (about 11,000 yuan) | Geeks, education |
| Go2 Pro | Advanced consumption | About **$2,800** (about 20,000 yuan) | Developer, scientific research |
| B2 | Industrial grade | **tens of thousands of yuan** grade | Power inspection, fire protection, logistics |
| As2 | New generation quadruped | — | Embodied intelligence platform |

(Data source: Yushu Technology official website, prospectus)

The four-legged line has completed the entire cycle from scientific research to commercial use:

- 2022: Research and education income accounts for **68.61%** of Sizu
- The first three quarters of 2025: Commercial consumption becomes the largest source, accounting for **42.30%**; industry applications account for **26.12%**

(Data source: prospectus)

Central enterprises such as State Grid and PetroChina have deployed Yushu four-legged robots on a large scale for inspections. This means that the four-legged business no longer relies on "selling to the laboratory", but has real repurchase scenarios.

**But the opposing view is**: The market ceiling for quadruped robots is limited. The global annual shipment of quadruped robots is about **30,000-40,000 units**, and Yushu already accounts for 70% - there is not much room for continued growth. This is also the fundamental reason why Yushu must bet on humanoid robots.

### Second Line: Humanoid Robots—The Core Bet with a 42 Billion Valuation

In 2025, the revenue of humanoid robots will exceed that of four-legged robots for the first time, accounting for approximately **52%** of total revenue, with **5,500 units shipped** ranking first in the world.

| Product | Height | Price | Positioning | Key Competencies |
|------|------|------|------|---------|
| H1 | 180cm | About **$90,000** | Full-size flagship | Strong athletic ability |
| G1 | 127cm | **$13,500-16,000** | Compact | Main shipping force |
| **R1** | — | **29,900-39,900 yuan** | **The lowest-priced humanoid robot** | Consumer-level attempt |
| H2 | 180cm | — | 2026 new flagship | Kick, somersault |

(Data source: prospectus, public press conference)

The pricing of R1 is particularly noteworthy - a humanoid robot starting from **29,900 yuan**, the lowest price in the world. For reference, the Boston Dynamics Atlas is over one million US dollars per unit, and the Tesla Optimus is expected to be **$20,000-25,000** (target price after mass production).

**But the key question is**: Who were these 5,500 units sold to?

| Customer Type | Share of Humanoid Robot Revenue |
|---------|-----------------|
| Scientific research and education | **73.60%** |
| Business consumption | 17.39% |
| Industry Application | 9.01% |

(Data source: prospectus, first three quarters of 2025)

**Three-quarters of humanoid robots are sold to universities and research institutions. ** This isn’t a bad thing – scientific customers are early adopters of new categories, and Four Legs has done so too. But this means that humanoid robots are still far away from real "factory work" and "home service".

**Opposite view**: The scientific research and education market itself has a ceiling. How many universities and laboratories around the world need humanoid robots? If commercial consumption and industry applications cannot be opened, the growth of humanoid robots will soon hit a wall.

**Positive opinion**: Quadruped robots accounted for 69% of scientific research in 2022, which has dropped to 32% by 2025 - the same evolutionary path is being repeated for humanoid robots, it is only a matter of time.

### The third line: core components - the real hidden barrier

The most easily overlooked competitiveness of Yushu is not in the complete machine, but in its components.

The localization rate of core components exceeds **90%**, and the self-developed list includes:

| Parts | Yushu's self-development | Industry comparison |
|--------|------------|---------|
| Joint motor | Self-developed, torque density **45Nm/kg** | Boston Dynamics about 40Nm/kg |
| Reducer | Self-developed | Most competitive products outsourced |
| Controller | Self-developed | Most competitive products outsourced |
| LiDAR | Self-developed, cost **<1,000 yuan** | Market average price is several thousand yuan |

(Data source: prospectus)

**The torque density of the joint motor is 45Nm/kg, which exceeds Boston Dynamics' 40Nm/kg** - this is a sign of Yushu's hard power at the hardware level.

The strategic significance of self-developed components lies in two points:

1. **Cost control**: The cost of self-developed motor is about **1/10** of Boston Power hydraulic system (source: public industry analysis). This is the fundamental reason why R1 can be sold for 29,900 yuan.
2. **Supply chain independence**: not blocked by external suppliers, especially important in the geopolitical environment

**Contrary view**: Self-development of parts and components is an advantage in the early stages, but when the industry scales up, a standardized supply chain may be more efficient than self-development. DJI’s success relies on self-research, but the mobile phone industry has proven that the standardization path of Qualcomm + TSMC can also win.

## Where does the 60% gross profit margin come from?

A hardware company has a gross profit margin of **60.27%**, a number that requires explanation.

### Reason 1: Full-stack self-research reduces costs

The most expensive parts of the machine (motor, reducer, controller) are all self-developed, avoiding outsourced premiums and middleman profits. According to industry analysis, Yushu’s overall machine cost is **30-50%** lower than similar competing products.

### Reason 2: Premium for scientific research and education customers

Scientific research and education customers are less sensitive to price and more sensitive to technical parameters. The G1 robot sold to universities costs **$13,500-16,000**, but the cost may be only **30-40%** of the selling price.

**There is a hidden worry here**: The 60% gross profit margin is supported to a certain extent by the high premiums from scientific research and education customers. If the sales structure shifts to industrial customers and consumers in the future, gross profit margin will most likely decline. Industrial customers have strong bargaining power, and consumers are more price-sensitive—the R1 starts at 29,900 yuan, and the gross profit margin is likely to be well below 60%.

### Reason 3: The scale effect is beginning to appear

The cumulative number of 30,000 quadruped robots has diluted the fixed costs of the production line. If humanoid robot shipments reach **10,000-20,000 units** in 2026 (Wang Xingxing’s public target, source: public report), the scale effect will be further unleashed.

**Contrary view**: 60% gross profit margin may be an "early dividend" rather than a "long-term norm". Compared with DJI - the early gross profit margin was also very high. As the price war and the market matured, the gross profit margin gradually fell back to the 40-50% range. Yushu's gross profit margin will most likely go through a similar process.

## "First-rate hardware, second-rate AI" - Yushu's biggest structural contradiction

There is no doubt about Yushu's hardware capabilities - the motor torque density exceeds Boston Dynamics, the localization rate is 90%+, and the cost control is world-leading.

**But the ultimate value of humanoid robots is not in "how fast they run or how high they jump", but in "understanding instructions, making decisions independently, and completing complex tasks." ** This is an AI capability, not a hardware capability.

A set of comparisons makes this gap intuitive:

| Dimension | Yushu Technology | Tesla Optimus |
|------|---------|---------------|
| R&D investment | **90.2 million yuan** (accounting for 7.73% of revenue) | Billions of dollars (AI team exceeds 10,000 people) |
| AI path | Reinforcement learning (BeamDojo framework) | **FSD end-to-end large model migration** |
| Training data | Mainly simulation environment | Millions of Tesla real driving data |
| Embodied intelligence model | Open source VLA/WMA, insufficient maturity | Closed source, rapid iteration |
| AI autonomous decision-making ability | Medium | **Industry-leading** |

(Data source: prospectus, Tesla public information)

Yushu's AI strategy is "open source + external cooperation" - self-developed BeamDojo reinforcement learning framework for motion control, while open source VLA (Visual-Language-Action) and WMA (World Model-Action) models to attract the community. But compared with Tesla's path of "directly migrating large self-driving models to robots", there is a generational gap.

Of the **4.2 billion** raised in the IPO, **2.022 billion** (48%) was used for "intelligent robot model research and development" - this shows that Yushu himself knows that the AI brain is the biggest shortcoming.

**The core question here is**: Is 4.2 billion yuan enough?

- Tesla AI team’s R&D budget could exceed **$10 billion** a year
- OpenAI 2025 funding **$6.6 billion**
- Yushu raised 4.2 billion yuan in IPO, but only 2 billion yuan was allocated to AI research and development - it will be spent over many years

**2 billion yuan vs tens of billions of dollars** - this is not a competition of the same magnitude.

**But the positive point of view is**: AI capabilities do not necessarily have to be built from scratch. There are a large number of large AI model companies in China (Baidu, Alibaba, Zhipu, etc.), and Yushu can do "hardware platform + AI interface" - similar to Android phones that do not require their own operating system. The value of Yushu may not lie in "creating the smartest brain", but in "creating the best body".

## One sentence summary

** Yushu Technology is one of the world's best robotic hardware companies - 60% gross profit margin, full-stack self-research, and the lowest cost in the world. But the final outcome of humanoid robots is not "whose body is good", but "whose brain is smart". The 42 billion valuation is not just a bet on hardware barriers, but also whether Yushu can narrow the gap with Tesla in terms of AI brain. **
