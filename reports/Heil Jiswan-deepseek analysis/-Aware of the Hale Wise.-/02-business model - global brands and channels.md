# Two, how deep is the moat: one by one

# Afloat I: Absolute leadership of ice-washing

** Intensity: extremely strong**

Hal's share of the refrigerator market ** ~ 47.7%**, washing machines ~ 47.4%** -- nearly half of the market. Globally, refrigerators continue to be the first in the world ** 17 years**, washing machines continue to be the first in the world ** 16 years**.

This leadership is not "favourable" but rather a decades-long **brand awareness + channel network + technology patents**. Consumers buy refrigerators with the first thought of Hal, and once this mind has been created, it is difficult for competitors to shake in the short term.

** The counter-view**: The share of the United States in refrigerators and washing machines is slowly rising, sunk by a value-for-money strategy and more efficient channels. The market share gap is narrowing, albeit slowly.

The pricing power of this high-end brand is real - the average price of Casatine products is **3 times the industry average, **35%. And more importantly, it takes a long time for competitors to catch up once a high-end brand is established.

** The counter-view**: The US COLMO has grown faster than Cassatius in recent years, and the brand gap is closing. More importantly, Cassatius is only ** ~ 13% of Heir's total revenue ** -- a moat that is deep but protects only a small fraction of the company.

# Three Protector of the City: Globalizing Home-Based Operating Networks

** Intensity: medium equivocal**

Heir has **35 industrial parks, 163 manufacturing centres, and 13 lighthouse plants (the largest in the world). GE Applides produce **80% of the products in the United States, which makes Hal more resilient than a pure export-oriented enterprise under tariff shocks.

Heir’s global model is "**buy local brands + home-grown operations **" instead of simply "China-made exports." This means that Heir has a GE brand-news and channel network in North America, a high-end location in New Zealand, a local AQUA experience in Japan.

** The counter-view**: Localization operations imply higher costs and more complex management. The seven brands operate independently, with limited synergies. The GE Applides and Hel China systems are not yet fully connected. This "dispersal globalization" is one of the central reasons for the high Hel cost rate.

** The counter-view**: The ecological compatibility of the Hong Men and the Mimmy family is stronger and the user migration costs are lower. Consumers prefer to pay for one-sing function rather than "scenario." Three-wing birds may be in the right direction, but not the moat of Haile — because the moat needs to be built, not "building".

# V. General review of business models

Summarize the analysis above, and the Heir Jiji business model can be summarized as follows:

**Enaps**:
1. Absolute global leadership of ice-washing species, deep in the moat
2. The high-end brand of Casati has established real pricing rights and consumer mind
3. Globalization is the most complete and multi-branded localization experience is beyond reach
High quality cash flow - running cash flow/net profit 5 averaged annually **146%**

** Disadvantage**:
1. Net interest rate **6.67%** far below beauty **9.75%** and Grea **~ 15%**
Cost rate **19% ** higher than beauty 3.5 percentage points and difficult to fast-press
3. Over half of overseas income but low profit margins, "globalization" is more a story of scale than a story of profit
4. Air conditioning operations are vulnerable in the largest single-goods market
5. Three-wing birds are ecologically heavily invested but not validated in business models.

** Qualitative**: The Hale thinker is a global white power giant with an uneven distribution of the moat** — almost invincible in areas of good practice (ice shampoo, high end), but far less efficient dimensions (cost control, profit transformation) than competitors.
