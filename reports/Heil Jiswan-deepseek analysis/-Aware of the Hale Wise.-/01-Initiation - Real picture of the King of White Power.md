## For the same price of 100 yuan, Haier only earns 6.7 yuan and Midea earns 9.8 yuan.

It has ranked first in retail sales of global major appliances for 17 consecutive years. A P/E ratio of 10x and a dividend yield of 5.6%. On paper, it looks like a bargain.

But looking at the financial report, another side emerged: the net profit margin is only two-thirds of Midea, the gross profit margin has dropped by nearly 5 percentage points in two years, and both revenue and net profit will decline in the first quarter of 2026. Cheap, but cheap for a reason. What this series wants to do is to clarify the "reason".

Let’s first look at a set of numbers in 2025 to re-understand Haier Smart Home:

But don't jump to conclusions. Look at it together with the beautiful ones:

**Haier’s revenue is 66% of Midea’s, but its net profit is only 44% of Midea’s**. For the same product sold for 100 yuan, Haier can only earn **6.7 yuan**, Midea earns **9.8 yuan**, and Gree earns **15 yuan**.

This is the core contradiction of Haier Smart Home: the largest scale in the world and the lowest profit efficiency in the industry. How to understand this contradiction directly determines your judgment of this stock.

## What exactly is Haier Smart Home?

Define Haier Smart Home in one sentence. The most accurate version is:

> **Haier Smart Home = a global white goods empire (seven major brands + 35 industrial parks + 163 manufacturing centers) + a smart home ecological story that is still being verified (Three-Winged Bird) + a stock priced by the market with an "efficiency discount". **

Let’s break it down and look at three things.

### First thing: global brand matrix

Haier is more than just "Haier". It has seven major brands, covering different markets and price segments:

| Brand | Positioning | Market Coverage |
|------|------|---------|
| **Haier** | Volkswagen main brand | China + Global |
| **Casarte** | High-end | China |
| **Leader** | Young, cost-effective | China |
| **GE Appliances** | Main player in North America | North America |
| **Fisher & Paykel** | Super high-end | Australia and New Zealand |
| **AQUA** | Japan + Southeast Asia | Asia Pacific |
| **Candy** | European Market | Europe |

This global model of "multi-brand localization operations" is **unique** among A-share home appliance companies. Midea mainly relies on its own brands to go overseas and OEM, while Gree’s overseas revenue only accounts for 18%. Haier is the only Chinese home appliance company whose overseas revenue exceeds its domestic revenue.

**Opposite view**: The multi-brand matrix also means **high management complexity and difficulty in lowering expense ratios**. Each of the seven brands has its own R&D team, marketing expenses, and channel system. This is one of the structural reasons why Haier’s expense ratio is **19%** (much higher than Midea’s **15.5%**).

### The second thing: Casarte—Haier’s most valuable asset

If you can only choose the most valuable thing about Haier Smart Home, the answer is not the "world's largest" scale, but **Casarte**.

Some key data:
- Casarte's revenue in 2016 was **2.2 billion**, and in 2025 it will be approximately **38 billion** (source: brokerage research report estimate), an increase of approximately **17 times** in 10 years
- Offline share of refrigerators above RMB 10,000: **44%** (Source: 2025 Annual Report)
- Offline share of washing machines above RMB 10,000: **75%** (Source: 2025 Annual Report)
- Share of air conditioners above RMB 15,000: **53%** (Source: 2025 Annual Report)
- Brand value: **92.8 billion** (Source: Euromonitor International)

Casarte's position in China's high-end home appliance market is similar to Moutai's position in the high-end liquor market - the minds of consumers have been formed. If you go offline to look at refrigerators worth more than 10,000 yuan, the first one the shopping guide will most likely recommend is Casarte.

**But there is a key fact**: Casarte accounts for about **~13%** of Haier Smart Home’s total revenue (estimated). In other words, Haier's business with the most pricing power only accounts for one-eighth of its revenue. The remaining 87% of revenue comes from mass brands and overseas operations with limited pricing power.

**Contrary view**: The growth rate of Midea’s high-end brand COLMO has exceeded that of Casarte in recent years, and the gap is narrowing. The ceiling of the high-end market is limited, and Casarte's penetration rate in first- and second-tier cities is already very high, so there is doubt about the room for continued growth.

### The third thing: Three-winged Bird - a story whose value needs to be verified

Haier began to promote "Three Winged Bird" - a "scenario brand" in 2020, trying to shift from selling single products to selling whole-house smart home solutions.

**Management's narrative**: From selling refrigerators to selling "Internet of Food", from selling washing machines to selling "Internet of Clothing", what users buy is not products but "lifestyle".

**The reality of the financial report**: Three-winged Bird contributes **less than 5%** to revenue (estimated), with a cumulative investment of more than **10 billion**, and the profit model has not yet been verified. The repurchase rate of smart home scenario packages is only **~15%** (estimated).

**Opposite opinion**: The direction is right. The smart home penetration rate is currently only 15%, and there is huge room for growth in the future. Haier has a first-mover advantage in scene ecology.

**Contrary view**: Consumers are far more willing to pay for the functions of a single product than for "scenes". The smart ecosystems of Huawei and Xiaomi are more compatible and have lower switching costs. Haier's "scenario premium" may be a false proposition.

## Financial trends in the past five years: stable growth but worrying efficiency

| Year | Revenue (billion) | Net profit (billion) | Gross profit margin | Net profit margin | ROE |
|------|-----------|-------------|--------|--------|-----|
| 2021 | 2,271 | 130.8 | **31.23%** | 5.81% | 17.3% |
| 2022 | 2,436 | 147.1 | 31.33% | 6.05% | 16.8% |
| 2023 | 2,742 | 166.0 | **31.51%** | 6.40% | 16.9% |
| 2024 | 2,860 | 187.3 | 27.80% | 6.85% | 17.7% |
| 2025 | 3,023 | 195.5 | **26.66%** | 6.67% | 17.0% |
| 2026Q1 | 736.9 | 46.5 | **25.34%** | 6.52% | — |

Data source: Haier Smart Home 2021-2025 annual report, 2026Q1 quarterly report

Several key signals:

1. **Steady revenue growth**: 5-year compound growth rate **7.4%**, neither fast nor slow, the normal level of the white goods industry
2. **Net profit grows faster**: 5-year compound growth rate **10.6%**, indicating that profit margins are slowly improving
3. **The continued decline in gross profit margin is the biggest red light**: from **31.51%** to **26.66%**, a drop of **4.85 percentage points** in two years. This means that Haier’s products are losing pricing power, or that cost pressure cannot be transmitted downstream.
4. **Double decline in revenue and net profit in 2026Q1**: revenue **-6.87%**, net profit **-15.22%** - this is the first year-on-year decline in quarterly revenue in 6 years. Mainly affected by North American tariffs, but also exposed lack of business resilience

**Opposite view**: Profit growth in 2026Q1 is **113%** compared to 2025Q4, indicating that Q4 is the real bottom and Q1 is already picking up. You can’t just look at year-on-year growth, you have to look at trends.

Let’s put a set of valuation data first, and Part 04 will expand on it in detail:

| Valuation indicators | Haier Smart Home | Midea Group | Gree Electric Appliances |
|----------|---------|---------|---------|
| Price to earnings ratio (TTM) | **About 10 times** | About 13.5 times | About 7-8 times |
| Price to Book Ratio | **1.63x** | 2.68x | — |
| Dividend Yield | **About 5.6%** | About 5.1% | — |
| Free Cash Flow Yield | **About 8%** | — | — |
| 5-year P/E ratio | **2.58%** (very low) | Mid-low | Historical low |

Data source: Public market data from April to May 2026

Putting these together, the Haier Smart Home you see is:

- The world's largest white appliance company with the most complete brand matrix
- Casarte has a near-monopoly in the high-end market
- The P/E ratio is only 10 times, the lowest in 5 years
- The dividend yield exceeds 5%, and there is a three-year increase commitment
- **But** the net profit margin is only two-thirds of Midea
- **But** the gross profit margin has dropped by nearly 5 percentage points in two years
- **But** 2026Q1 revenue and net profit both dropped
- **But** the expense ratio is 19%, much higher than Midea’s 15.5%

**Cheap, but cheap for a reason. ** Whether the reason is "temporary headwinds" or "structural flaws" - this is the question to be answered in the next three articles.

## Compare with Midea and Gree in one sentence

Before you read the next three articles, remember the core differences between the three companies:

| Company | One sentence portrait | The essence of investment |
|------|-----------|---------|
| **Midea Group** | An efficiency-driven home appliance giant in all categories | What we buy is management efficiency and execution capabilities |
| **Gree Electric** | The cash cow that is unique in air conditioners | Buying a single category monopoly and dividends |
| **Haier Smart Home** | The most global brand matrix company | Buying global layout and high-end potential |

Each of the three companies has its own advantages, but the pricing given by the market is very different: Midea's price-to-earnings ratio is about **13.5 times**, Haier's is about **10 times**, and Gree's is about **7-8 times**. The logic behind this pricing difference is the most worthwhile thing to dig into in the entire series.
