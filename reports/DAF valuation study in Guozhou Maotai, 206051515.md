# Ten, reflections on valuation methods

The greatest gain of the study was not the calculation of how much the poo value was, but the understanding of the boundaries of the application of different valuation methods**.

**DCF illusion**: The most "science" seems to be the most vulnerable. The WACC changed from 9% to 4%, and the valuation jumped from $1,557 to $6,662 -- four times the difference. The final formula `FCF/(WACC-g)' makes the two parameters come close to the value limit and the valuation is endless. All "accuracy" is based on speculation about the WACC and the rate of growth of perpetuity, which are the most difficult to determine.

** WACC’s essence is opportunity cost**: discount rate is not a physical constant, but is “how much money you can make if you don’t put it on the roof.” Your alternative is a national debt (1.75%), which is worth a lot; you can make 15% a steady profit, which is not worth a price. The same company, the intrinsic value of the investors is different.

**Consequent constraints on growth rates**: GDP growth cannot be exceeded in the long term (otherwise, corporate profits will eventually exceed GDP of the country as a whole). The low-level 2-3% reflects "a rise in inflation, but the space for incremental growth is blocked by population decline".

** Better method than exclusion**: Rather than pursuing a precise figure, there are a number of simple ways to make a judgment. The profit rate of 4.8% is well over the national debt of 1.75%, the PE 20.8x is the lowest in history, the dividends rate of 3.8% and annual growth is more convincing than any DCF calculation.

**Buffett's wisdom**: "If something needs to be calculated precisely to be judged, it's not cheap. "The most valuable thing for a hut is not to calculate whether it's worth $1,557 or $1,717, but to determine whether it's a good business and whether the price is reasonable. The answer is "yes," and the answer for the latter is "optimal".
