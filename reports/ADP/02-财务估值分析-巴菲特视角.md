# ADP Financial and Valuation Analysis

> Analysis dimensions: financial quality, shareholder returns, and valuation attractiveness
> Data cutoff: FY2025 (June 2025)

---

## 1. Financial Overview: A Steady Compounding Machine

| Metric | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | Trend |
|------|--------|--------|--------|------|
| Total revenue | USD 18.1 billion | USD 19.2 billion | USD 20.6 billion | Stable growth |
| Revenue growth | 9.0% | 6.1% | 7.1% | High single digits |
| Net income | ~USD 3.4 billion | ~USD 3.8 billion | USD 4.1 billion | +8.7% |
| Net margin | ~18.8% | ~19.5% | 19.8% | Continued expansion |
| EPS | ~8.58 | ~9.10 | 9.98 | +9.7% |
| ROIC | ~40% | ~41% | 42% | Extremely high |

**Key observations:**

1. **Revenue growth has stayed around 7%**, with a three-year CAGR of 7.4%. For a mature company with a USD 79 billion market cap, that growth reflects genuine customer expansion and pricing power, not acquisition-driven growth.
2. **Net margin expanded from 18.8% to 19.8%**, improving for three straight years. The Employer Services segment delivered a 100-basis-point margin expansion for the full year, showing strong operating leverage.
3. **ROIC of 42%** means every USD 1 of capital invested generates USD 0.42 of return. That is elite by any industry standard.

## 2. Segment Performance

### Employer Services
- FY2025 revenue grew 7% on an organic, constant-currency basis
- Margin expanded by 100 basis points
- New-business bookings grew 3%
- Customer retention reached 92.1%, staying at a very high level

### PEO Services
- FY2025 revenue grew 7%
- Margin declined by 60 basis points, mainly because insurance costs rose
- The PEO business provides end-to-end HR outsourcing for small and mid-sized companies; in essence, it is a "co-employer" model

### Float Interest
- FY2025 interest income reached USD 1.2 billion, up 16% year over year
- Client cash balances were about USD 44.5 billion
- FY2026 guidance: USD 1.29-1.31 billion

## 3. Shareholder Returns: 51 Straight Years of Dividend Growth

ADP is a flagship name among "dividend aristocrats," with **51 consecutive years of dividend increases**:

- Most recent increase: in January 2025, the quarterly dividend was raised to USD 1.70 per share, up 10% year over year
- 10-year average dividend growth: 12% annually
- 5-year average dividend growth: 12% annually
- Current dividend yield: about 3.2% based on a USD 209 share price
- Payout ratio: about 61%, leaving room for further increases

**Buybacks:**
- In January 2026, ADP announced a new USD 6 billion repurchase authorization
- FY2025 first half buybacks totaled about USD 900 million
- FY2025 first half total shareholder returns were USD 2.1 billion (USD 1.3 billion in dividends + USD 900 million in buybacks)

## 4. Free Cash Flow

- FY2025 first-half operating cash flow was USD 1.8 billion, down 10% year over year, mainly because of working-capital swings
- Free cash flow per share was about USD 3.39
- Overall FCF conversion remains healthy, and the company has very limited capital-expenditure needs

ADP's asset-light model means most profits can be turned into cash. The company does not need to build factories or buy heavy equipment; its core assets are software platforms and compliance knowledge.

## 5. Valuation Analysis

**Current valuation snapshot (May 2026):**

| Metric | Value |
|------|------|
| Share price | ~USD 209 |
| Market cap | ~USD 79 billion |
| TTM P/E | 19x |
| Historical average P/E | 25-30x |
| Median analyst target price | USD 272 |
| DCF intrinsic value (base case) | USD 221 |
| DCF intrinsic value (growth case) | USD 291 |

**Valuation call:**

ADP's current 19x P/E sits at **the lowest level in nearly a decade**, and the stock is down about 40% from its high. Analyst consensus is "Hold," with a median target price of USD 272, implying roughly 30% upside.

The decline appears to be driven mainly by:
1. Market concerns about a slowdown in employment, since ADP revenue is correlated with employment levels
2. Expectations that lower interest rates could reduce float income
3. A broader multiple compression across tech and growth stocks

But from a Buffett perspective: **a company with 42% ROIC, 51 years of dividend growth, 92% retention, and stable 7% revenue growth trading at 19x earnings is historically cheap.** Even under a very conservative DCF assumption set (6% growth, 20x exit multiple), the current price still leaves a margin of safety.

## 6. Key Financial Risks

1. **Employment cyclicality**: in a recession, layoffs reduce per-employee revenue. Historically, though, ADP's revenue fell only about 2% during 2008-2009, showing strong resilience.
2. **Lower rates**: if rates fall sharply, float interest income will come under pressure. A laddered investment strategy helps cushion the impact.
3. **PEO insurance costs**: the PEO segment has already seen margin compression (-60bps), and this deserves ongoing monitoring.
