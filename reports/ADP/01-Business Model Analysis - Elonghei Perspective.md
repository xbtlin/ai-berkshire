# ADP Business Model Analysis

> Analysis dimensions: business essence, user value, competitive barriers
> Data cutoff: FY2025 (June 2025)

---

## 1. What Kind of Business Is This?

ADP (Automatic Data Processing) is fundamentally a business that **manages money on behalf of clients**. Every month, companies send employee wages and tax payments to ADP, and ADP completes the disbursement and tax remittance within the required compliance window. In the process, ADP earns three kinds of money:

1. **Processing fees** (charged per employee or per transaction): the core revenue source. Total revenue in FY2025 was USD 20.6 billion, up 7% year over year.
2. **Float interest income**: client cash balances of about USD 44.5 billion generated USD 1.2 billion of interest income in FY2025, up 16% year over year. This is essentially investment income produced by a zero-cost liability.
3. **Value-added services**: payroll outsourcing (PEO), compliance consulting, benefits administration, and other add-ons keep lifting ARPU.

This three-layer revenue structure gives ADP an economics model similar to an insurance company: it makes money using other people's cash while also charging a management fee.

## 2. User Value: Why Do Companies Depend on ADP?

**Payroll processing is one of the most unforgiving parts of running a business.** If wages are paid incorrectly or taxes are missed even once, the company faces fines and also damages employee trust. ADP serves more than 1 million businesses across over 140 countries and territories, building a vast compliance database that a single company could never replicate on its own.

From the user's perspective, ADP solves a problem that is "high pain, low glory" - when it works, nobody praises it; when it fails, it can be disastrous. Companies are willing to pay for certainty, and that is the fundamental reason ADP has maintained customer retention around **92%** for years.

## 3. Switching Costs: The Invisible Moat

ADP's moat is not branding or technology. It is **very high switching costs**. Specifically:

1. **Data migration complexity**: Companies accumulate years of payroll records, tax archives, employee benefit data, and related attachments inside ADP. Moving to a new system can take weeks or even months, and the risk of errors during migration is extremely high.
2. **Contract lock-in mechanics**: ADP contracts usually renew automatically every 2-3 years, and the cancellation window is only 60-90 days. Missing that window locks the customer in again. Early termination fees average three months of processing fees or 50% of the remaining contract value.
3. **Organizational inertia**: HR teams are already accustomed to ADP's interface and workflows. Switching systems means retraining the entire team.
4. **Compliance risk**: Any mistake during migration can trigger tax compliance problems.

Combined, these factors mean that **once a customer chooses ADP, the cost of switching away is far higher than the cost of staying**.

## 4. Float: The Essence of the Insurance-Like Model

ADP continuously holds about USD 44.5 billion of client funds on behalf of customers (mainly payroll taxes and wages before disbursement). In a high-rate environment, the interest income from this float is especially attractive:

- FY2024 interest income was about USD 1.0 billion
- FY2025 jumped to USD 1.2 billion (+16%)
- FY2026 guidance is USD 1.29-1.31 billion

Even more importantly, ADP uses a **laddered investment strategy**. That means even if rates fall, the high-yield bonds already locked in continue to generate income. Goldman Sachs has pointed out that even if central banks begin to cut rates moderately, ADP's portfolio can still maintain "higher for longer" yields.

The beauty of this model is that float grows naturally with customer count and payroll growth, with almost no additional capital investment required.

## 5. Overall Judgment on the Business

Using Duan Yongping's usual standards:

- **Is the user need real?** Yes. Payroll processing is a must-have, not a nice-to-have.
- **Is the business model simple?** Yes. Per-employee fees plus float interest is clear and predictable.
- **Is there pricing power?** Yes. ADP can raise prices modestly every year (3-5%), and customers rarely leave because of price alone.
- **Is the moat durable?** Switching costs, compliance barriers, and network effects from accumulated data all keep widening the moat.

**Conclusion: ADP is an excellent business.** It fits the standard of "doing the right thing" - creating real value for customers through compliance certainty while earning above-average returns through float. The only open question is whether AI will lower the barrier to payroll processing, but for now AI appears to strengthen ADP's data advantage rather than weaken it.
