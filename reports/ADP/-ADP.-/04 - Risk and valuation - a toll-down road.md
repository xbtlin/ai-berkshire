# Let's get all the bad news out of the world

The first three articles are all about the merits of ADP. The most taboo is the good side of investment – real judgment requires that risks be explained and price values not valued. This is a special case: bad news, then valuation.

Risk one: Employment cycle - ADP The biggest structural weakness

The ADP income pattern is "pay by head." This means:** corporate staff cutbacks <unk> ADP income **.

This is a risk that cannot be eliminated by business model design -- it is embedded in ADP DNA.

In three recessions, ADP registered the largest fall in revenue of only **2%**. This is a much more resilient company – but the share price drop is much greater (25-40%).

The reason is simple:** The market value multipliers for ADP contain a "stable growth" premium. Once growth expectations have shaken, the valuation multiples have collapsed.

In other words, ADP's business was stable in the recession, but stock prices were not necessarily stable.

** Why did the revenue fall by only 2%?** Because even when a business is retrenched, it still needs to deal with severance pay, severance pay, and tax clearance – all of which require ADP services. And SMEs tend to outsource pay processing in times of economic hardship to save costs.

** Inverse:** Data on the recession of the past are not necessarily applicable to the future. If a new recession like "AI causes massive white-collar unemployment," ADP is likely to face a "permanent decline in employment" rather than "defend the wave of people and then recover".

The buffer strategy of ADP is:** not all of the money is spent on short-term national debt**. It uses a ladder-based investment - spread funds across bonds of varying durations (1, 3, 5 years, etc.). This means that even short-end interest rates are falling, long-term bonds that have been locked up contribute high returns.

** But the buffer has a ceiling.** If the low interest rate lasts for more than three years, the maturity high interest rate bond will gradually be replaced by the low interest rate bond, which will eventually result in a significant decline in interest income. In the period 2020-2021, interest income from ADP fell to **less than $500 billion** during the ultra-low interest rate period.

** Inverse:** interest rate risk is overestimated. While interest on floating funds is not low, ADP’s core income is processing fees, and interest is only "accompanied." Even if interest income is cut, ADP remains a good company with 30% ROIC+.

Risk three: Increased competition - the fighting in the middle market

The previous detailed analysis shows that Paychex merges with Paycor and will create greater pressure in the middle market. Integration is expected to be completed in 2027.

ADP responds to the accelerated migration of the Next Gen platform - now **80%** new mid-end clients are deployed on the new platform with faster deployment and higher satisfaction.

This risk is that ** the controlled **-92% retention rate indicates that stock customers are stable and that competition occurs mainly in the incremental market. But competition in the incremental market is indeed increasing.

# # Risk four: Lyric HCM Implementation Risk -- Attacking Worldday whether or not to enter

ADP spent a great deal of resources on the development of the Lyric HCM platform and the acquisition of WorkForce Software ($1.2 billion), with the goal of entering the HCM market for large enterprises.

If successful, the ADP ' s reach market size will be expanded from pay treatment (~$20 billion) to HCM worldwide (~$60 billion).

But if the failed — $1.2 billion acquisition — could become a sunk cost, and large amounts of R& D resources were dispersed, it would affect the competitiveness of core pay operations.

** The current signal is positive:** Lyric HCM has taken over the largest corporate-level client in the company's history. But there is still a long way to go from taking the next client to getting his foot on the Worldday home.

# Risk five: Management -- competent but untested

CEO Maria Black took office in January 2023 and has now been in office for about three and a half years.

** Proven:** Strong Implementation - FY2025 Overall Compliance or Overexpected, FY2026 Continuous Upgrade Guidance. AI Strategic Directions are Right. Staff Satisfaction is America's number one.

** Unproven:** Crisis response capacity - not yet experiencing a major economic downturn in the tenure. As an insider working for 27 years in ADP, is there enough external perspective to face the subversive changes of the AI era?

** Overall evaluation:** Management is a sub-item, but not a sub-item "to keep your eyes closed."

# Why did the market give ADP discounts?

The stock price fell from $330 to $220, and it fell 33%. What was the market worried about?

1. ** Employment market slowdown forecast**. ADP ' s own monthly employment reports indicate that new jobs are slowing down and the market fears that this will drag ADP ' s head-to-head fees.
2. ** Lower interest rate forecast**. Although interest rate reductions have not yet occurred, market pricing pre-positions the potential for a decline in interest income on floating funds.
3. **The value of the Science and Technology Unit has been overall compressed**. The market environment in the first half of 2026 was unfriendly for "not sexy enough" technology companies.

** How many of these concerns have been reflected in the stock price?**

In the actual performance of Q3 SY2026:
- Income growth **7%**, over-expected
- EPS Growth **10%**, over-expected
- Profits expand **80 basis points**
- Management** upgrade**
- The retention rate is at a record high**

Performance is improving, valuation is getting lower. This "fundamental deviation from stock prices" is either the market seeing some hidden structural risk or the market is making a temporary mistake.

# The dividends as "waiting compensation"

A unique value of ADP is that:** It gives you 3.1% of the dividends per year while you wait for the valuation to return**.

And it's not a "can be cut" dividend -- ADP has been increasing dividends for 51 years ** and an average annual rate of increase of 10 ** 12% **. The current rate of distribution is about 61% ** and there is still plenty of room for improvement.

Even if the share price does not rise for a year, the 3.1% that you get wins most of the bank deposit rates. If the dividends continue to grow at 10%, the rate of dividends (based on the current purchase price) will exceed **5%** in five years.

** Inverse: ** 3.1% of dividends are not prominent in high interest rate settings – US government debt yields are also above 4%. And 10% of dividends increase is conditional on ADP profits growing – and the rate of increase could slow down if it is hit by recession.
