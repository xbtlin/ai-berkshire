## A company so "boring" that it makes $4.1 billion a year

Every company has a lifeline that must not be cut off - **payroll**. If a tax is sent one day late, employees will call HR. If a tax is reported incorrectly, a fine from the Federal Tax Agency will come at any time. There is zero room for error in this matter, but almost no one knows who is behind it.

That company is called **ADP** (Automatic Data Processing). It was founded in New Jersey in 1949. It is mainly engaged in one thing: **pay wages, file taxes, and manage human resources for companies**. It doesn’t make headlines and doesn’t create concepts, but more than 1 million companies across the United States rely on its system for their monthly payroll cycles. After reading the following set of numbers, you will understand how valuable this machine is:

| Indicators | Numerical values | Magnitude judgment |
|------|------|---------|
| FY2025 total revenue | **20.6 billion US dollars** | No. 1 in the global payroll processing industry |
| FY2025 Net Profit | **$4.1 billion** (+9%) | Continued growth |
| FY2025 EPS | **$9.98** (+10%) | Double-digit growth for many consecutive years |
| ROIC | **42%** | Top in the industry |
| Customer retention rate | **92%** | Customers almost never leave |
| Consecutive dividend increases | **51 years** | The benchmark for "Dividend Aristocrats" |
| Number of service companies | **Over 1 million** | Covering more than 140 countries |
| Client Fund Balances | **Approximately $48.3 billion** | Quasi-Insurance Float |
| Current Share Price (May 2026) | **~$220** | 52-week High $330, Low $188 |
| Current market capitalization | **~$88 billion** | |
| PE (TTM) | **~22 times** | Low level in the past decade |

(Data source: ADP FY2025 annual report, ADP Q3 FY2026 financial report, Yahoo Finance)

Put this all together - **$4.1 billion in annual earnings, 42% ROIC, 51 consecutive years of dividend increases, 92% customer retention, only ~22x PE, and a stock price down more than 30% from its highs**.

Good companies come across discounted prices? Or is it an old machine that is slowing down?

This series only does one thing: **break down ADP and let you judge whether it is worth it**.

## Three common misconceptions about ADP

### Misunderstanding 1: "ADP is just a payroll software"

This is the most superficial understanding. ADP does help businesses make payroll—but it does much more than that.

ADP's revenue comes from a three-tier structure:

| Income Tier | FY2025 Scale | Nature |
|--------|------------|------|
| Processing fee (charged per head/per time) | Accounting for the main body of total income | "Water, electricity and coal" that companies must pay every month |
| Float interest | **~$1.2 billion** (+16%) | Client funds are temporarily deposited with ADP before being released, and ADP uses the money to earn interest |
| Value-added services (PEO, compliance, benefits) | Continuous growth | Add more HR services on top of salary to increase unit price per customer |

The second level is the most interesting. ADP has been holding about $48.3 billion in customer funds all year round - these are salaries and taxes paid by companies to ADP, which are temporarily held in ADP's accounts until the compliance deadline arrives. ADP used the money to buy bonds to earn interest, and in FY2025 it earned **$1.2 billion** from interest alone.

Who does this model look like? **Warren Buffett’s Berkshire Hathaway**. The insurance company collects premiums and invests the money that it does not need to pay out for the time being - ADP collects payroll taxes and uses the money that it does not pay out for the time being to earn interest. It's all about "making money with other people's money".

So ADP is not "a payroll software". A more accurate definition is: **a compliance processing machine that charges per capita + an insurance float company**.

**Opposite view:** Float interest is highly dependent on the interest rate environment. This $1.2 billion in interest income will shrink if the Fed cuts interest rates significantly. ADP uses a ladder investment strategy (part of the funds locked in long-term high-interest bonds) to hedge, but it cannot completely eliminate interest rate risk. FY2026 guidance for interest income is **$1.34-1.35 billion**, indicating that management expects there is still room for growth in the short term.

### Misunderstanding 2: "The threshold for payroll processing is very low, AI will kill ADP"

Can large models be counted as salary? Theoretically yes. But calculating wages and paying wages in compliance with regulations are two different things.

The difficulty in salary processing is not "calculation" but "compliance":

- There are 50 U.S. states and thousands of local jurisdictions with varying and frequently changing tax laws
- More than 140 countries have completely different labor laws, social security, and provident fund rules
- Paying the wrong salary once will not only result in fines, but also the collapse of employee trust.

The compliance database ADP has accumulated over decades cannot be replaced by a large model. And ADP itself is also using AI to enhance its products - ADP Assist, launched in 2025, can automatically detect payroll anomalies and automatically update compliance rules, with early users reporting savings of more than **30 minutes per payroll period**. Q3 FY2026 financial report shows that AI tools have reduced customer call volume by **8%**.

**In the short term, AI is an accelerator for ADP, not a substitute. **

**Contrary view:** In the long term (more than 10 years), if AI capabilities continue to leap forward and universal tools for "one-click compliance" emerge, ADP's compliance barriers may be eroded. Technology giants may also use AI as an entry point to enter the salary market. The probability of disruption is currently low, but cannot be ignored.

### Misunderstanding 3: "ADP is a growth stock"

No. ADP is a **stable compound interest machine**, not a high-growth target.

FY2025 revenue growth **7%**, FY2026 guidance **6-7%**. Growth is not sexy, but it is better than stability. What’s more, ADP walks on three legs:

1. **Natural growth**: Economic growth → Enterprises hire more people → ADP charges more per head
2. **Price increase ability**: Moderate price increase of 3-5% every year, customers rarely leave because of price
3. **Penetration of value-added services**: Selling HR outsourcing, benefit management, and compliance consulting on the basis of salary

The advantage of this growth structure is: **No large capital investment is required, and most of the profits can be converted into cash**. Full-year free cash flow in FY2025 reached **4.78 billion**, a year-on-year increase of **20%**.

ADP doesn't chase 30% revenue growth - it chases **certainty**. 7% revenue growth + continued margin expansion + 51 consecutive years of dividend increases = a long-term compounding machine.

**Contrary view:** Because the growth rate is only 7%, ADP's valuation ceiling is limited. If the market continues to favor high-growth stocks, ADP may be ignored for a long time. Moreover, if the 7% growth rate is hit by the economic recession (company layoffs → reduction in capitation fees), it may slip to 2-3% in the short term.

## Understand ADP’s money-making logic with one picture

Condensing ADP’s business model into a cycle:

```
Enterprise customers → Pay monthly payroll processing fee (per head)
         → Also temporarily deposit salary/tax funds into ADP
         
ADP → Process payroll and tax compliance (earn processing fees)
    → Use temporary funds to buy bonds (earn interest on float)
    → Sell value-added services on the basis of salary (increase unit price per customer)
    
Results → Asset-light, high ROIC, strong cash flow
    →Customer retention 92%, average usage 11 years
    → Increased dividends for 51 consecutive years
```

The most subtle part of this cycle is: **The more customers → the more complete the compliance data → the more accurate the system → attracting more customers**. This is a positive feedback flywheel. Compliance data for 1 million enterprise customers and 140 countries is a barrier that would take any competitor starting from scratch and requiring billions of dollars and more than a decade to replicate.
