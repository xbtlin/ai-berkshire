# Why ADP makes so much money

There's a kind of business that the client can't run away after paying for. ADP is the business.

It helps companies pay their wages -- but it's just the entrance. The real thing about ADP making "comfort" is that it has built up two sources of revenue that cost almost zero, in addition to processing fees: one is to put the funds held in the hands of the client to pay interest on the bonds, and the other is to add more and more HR services to the pay system. Three layers are folded, each self-enhanced.

# First level: processing fees - "hydro-coal" by head

ADP core income is derived from two operational divisions:

# Employer Services (employer services) - Main business

- SY2025 Organic income growth **7%**
- Profit rate expansion **100 basis points**
- New business bookings increased by **3%** to **2.1 billion US$**
- Client retention rate **92.1%**

The fee is based on the number of persons, by head, by number, by month/year.

Translation into the white: your company has 500 employees, ADP pays you a monthly wage, tax, newspaper insurance. The more you pay on headlines, the more employees pay. One less, one more.

The benefits of this model are:** income is directly linked to the size of the client's workforce, and when the economy is good, it increases naturally, without the need for ADP to spend extra money to get paid**.

#PEO Services (Professional Employers Organization) - Shared Employers Model

- SY2025 Income growth **7%**
- Decline in profit margin **60 basis points** (due to increased insurance costs)

The S&T uses the word "share employer." The ADP has a job as a "single employer," and the ADP has a unified procurement of benefits like health insurance, pension schemes, etc. Because ADP is much larger than a single SME, it gets a more favourable price.

The value of the PEO is:** to help SMEs benefit from large enterprises at the cost of large ones**. This further enhances client stickivity - the cost of migration is higher once employees' health insurance and pension are covered by the ADP PEO system.

** Inverse: **The PEO profit margin for business fell by 60 basis points in SY2025, mainly because of rising insurance costs. If medical inflation persists, the PEO may become a profit-contributors drag. Management needs to demonstrate control over insurance costs.

# Second floor: Interest on floating cash -- "For money with someone else's money."

This is the most elaborate part of ADP business model**.

The company gives the employee’s wages and taxes to ADP, but ADP does not immediately issue – it deposits the money for a legal period, usually from days to weeks. In this time, ADP invests funds in fixed-income products such as bonds to earn interest.

**2. Step-by-step investment strategy reduces interest rate risk.** ADP does not invest all of its money in short-term national debt – it spreads funds across bonds of varying duration. This means that long-term high-interest-rate bonds that have been locked up contribute to the return even if short-term interest rates fall. ** Inverse:** Interest on floating funds is "false" but wind changes. And, the floating funds are essentially customer money – a profit source that may shrink if future regulation requires ADP to return some of the interest to the client.

# Third floor: value-added services - continuously increasing the price of a passenger unit

ADP is not satisfied with paying you for your salary. Its strategy is:** to add more services to the pay portal.**

What is already being done:
- HR Outsourcing (PEO)
- Welfare management (health insurance, pensions)
- Compliance advice
- Recruitment and management of talent
- Labour-force shifts and attendance

In progress:
- **Lyric HCM**: The cloud-based AI driver HCM platform released in 2024 directly against the Worldday, the main market for large enterprises.
- **ADP Assist**: AI Assistant, eight modules across pay, attendance, talent, welfare, recruitment, analysis, reporting, compliance.
- **WorkForce Software**: 2024 acquired **$1.2 billion** to complete the labour management puzzle covering more than 140 countries.

The logic of this strategy is clear:** clients have paid ADP, with a marginal decision-making cost of paying an additional HR function at a very low cost, but for each additional payment, the cost of moving is one higher **.

That's why ADP clients use on average **11 years** (Employer Services) and it's hard to get out once they get in.

# Conversion costs: ADP at the heart of the moat

The ADP moat is not brand, not price, but ** extremely expensive to convert**.

How high is it? Four floors:

##1. Data migration complexity

The enterprise has accumulated several years of payroll records, tax files, and employee benefits data in ADP.

#2. Contract locking mechanism

ADP contracts are usually automatically renewed every 2-3 years, with window periods cancelled only **60-90 days**. Missing window periods are again locked. The cost of early termination is on average **3 months of processing fees or 50 per cent of the residual contract value**.

Organizational inertia

HR team has become used to ADP interfaces and processes. A change of system means retraining the whole team. Who would take the risk of a change of system for a department that "dos well and nobody says it's wrong."

##4. Compliance risk

Any omission during the relocation process may lead to tax compliance problems — less fines and more legal proceedings.

Four layers of cumulative results:** Once a client chooses ADP, the cost of replacing it is far more than the cost of continuing to use it. This is not "the customer thinks ADP is good, so stay away" -- it is "the client can't walk if he wants to go."

**92% retention rate** is the best proof. Q3 SY2026's latest data show that ES retention rate is at an all-time high.

** Inverse:** Conversion costs are high, but not unlimited. New platforms in the cloud computing era (e.g. Paylocity) have made substantial optimizations in data migration tools and deployment speeds, and are lowering migration thresholds.

# Pricing authority: moderate but continuous capacity to raise prices

ADP can raise prices from clients **3-5% ** and very few customers leave as a result.

Why?

1. **The payroll processing fee is extremely low**. A 500-person company, ADP, may have only tens to a million dollars per year - a "zero" against millions of human costs. The 5% increase means that thousands more dollars per year are spent, and no CFO will take the risk of changing the system because of this.

2. ** Prices are not transparent**. The ADP pricing is "one-on-one" and is not like the SaaS company that carries a firm 's mark on the official network. This model of pricing gives ADP greater scope for price increases.

3. ** Compliance regulations are changing every year**. ADP can provide reasonable justification for price increases by packaging "update maintenance" as part of value-added services.

** This "moder but consistent" price increase, coupled with natural growth in client size, is the bottom logic of the ADP income steady growth of 6-7% per year**

# Light asset model: profits almost all turn into cash

Another financial advantage of ADP is that:** there is little need for a heavy asset investment**.

It does not require building, buying equipment, hoarding inventory. The core assets are software platforms and a knowledge base of compliance. This means:

- **FY2025 Free cash flow $4.78 billion**, FCF/net profit approximately **126%**
- Free cash flows as a percentage of income **23%**
- The company returns most of the profits to shareholders.

The shareholder returns:
- Interest expenditure **2.4 billion United States dollars**
- Stock buyback **1.28 billion United States dollars**
- Total return **3.38 billion United States dollars**, 90 per cent of net profit**

This "several points of money" is not uncommon in large market-market companies. The contrast is more numerous (a net profit of nearly 100 billion, a red buy-back of zero), and ADP is another extreme -- ** the money earned is almost entirely returned to shareholders.
