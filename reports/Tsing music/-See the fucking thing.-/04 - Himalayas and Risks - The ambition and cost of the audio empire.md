## A $2.9 billion acquisition and the three-layer logic behind it

On May 12, 2026, Tencent Music announced that it had received regulatory approval to acquire Himalaya for approximately $2.9 billion (approximately RMB 21 billion). This is one of the largest acquisitions in China’s Internet industry in recent years.

But this transaction is not "buy whatever you want" - the regulatory authorities have attached **5 restrictive conditions** before letting it go.

This article will answer two questions: First, is the strategic logic of acquiring Himalaya sound? Second, in addition to Himalaya, what other risks does Tencent Music face?

---

## 1. Himalaya Acquisition: Dismantling of Strategic Logic

### Why buy it?

Let’s first look at a set of numbers to understand the size of the Himalayas:

| Indicators | Himalayas (2025 data, estimated) | Description |
|------|------------------------|------|
| Monthly active users | ~280-300 million | China’s largest audio platform |
| Content type | Audio books + podcasts + paid knowledge + radio dramas | Full coverage of non-music audio |
| Revenue scale | ~6-7 billion yuan (estimated) | About 6.1 billion at the time of the 2023 prospectus |
| Profit status | Close to breakeven (estimated) | Long-term losses, narrowed significantly in recent years |

There are three levels of logic behind Tencent Music’s purchase of Himalaya:

**Level 1: Upgrade from "Music Platform" to "Audio Platform". **

There is a ceiling for music subscriptions – Chinese users are willing to pay 15-25 yuan per month for music, that’s all. But if audiobooks, podcasts, and knowledge payments are added, the payment ceiling for the same user can be significantly raised.

Analogy: Spotify began to vigorously develop podcasts in 2019, and currently podcast users account for more than 40% of total monthly active users. This strategy helped Spotify increase ARPPU from about $5 in the pure music era to a higher level, as advertising revenue opened up a second growth curve.

**Level 2: Hedging against soda music competition. **

What soda music can do is "pure music" - it has no audio books or podcasts. If Tencent Music becomes a comprehensive audio platform of "music + audio books + podcasts", the competition dimension will change from "who has more songs and who is cheaper" to "who has richer audio content" - the latter is difficult for soda to catch up in a short time.

**Level 3: Use cash to make high-return investments. **

Tencent Music has 38 billion in cash on its books. If you just leave it and earn interest, the annual return will be 2-3%. US$2.9 billion buys an audio platform with an annual revenue of 6-7 billion and is close to break-even. If it can achieve a profit margin of 10-15% after integration, it is equivalent to an additional profit of 600-1 billion per year. **Using 20% ​​of cash to buy an incremental profit engine is a mathematical bargain. **

### Opposite view: The risk of acquisition cannot be ignored

**First, the consideration of $2.9 billion is not cheap. ** Himalaya was valued at approximately RMB 25-30 billion ($3.5-4 billion) when IPOed in 2023. The public market valuation in 2024 has shrunk significantly. $2.9 billion is close to its IPO valuation – is Tencent Music overpriced?

**Second, integration is difficult. **Music subscriptions and audiobooks/podcasts are two completely different business models - the former relies on copyright procurement + algorithm recommendation, and the latter relies on content creation + community operation. The team genes and operational logic of the two are very different, and cases of integration failure are common in the Internet industry.

**Third, five restrictive conditions are attached to the supervision. ** These conditions may limit the synergy space between Tencent Music and Himalaya. Although the specific conditions are not fully disclosed, the "conditional approval" itself means that regulators are worried that the transaction may have a monopoly effect - and the restrictive conditions may just limit the most valuable synergy scenarios.

## 2. Integration Risks: Historical Lessons

The integration success rate of Internet acquisitions is not high. Here are some references:

| Acquisition | Consideration | Result |
|--------|------|------|
| Tencent acquires China Literature Group | Holdings | Success - China Literature continues to grow within the Tencent system |
| Baidu acquires YY Live | $3.6 billion | Failure - users continue to lose and the business is eventually marginalized |
| Alibaba acquires Youku | ~$4 billion | Controversy - continuous losses for many years |
| Spotify acquires Gimlet (podcasting) | $230 million | Success – podcasts become Spotify’s second growth curve |

Tencent has successful experience in the field of content integration (China Literature Group), but there are also many cases with medium results. The difficulty of integrating Himalaya is:

1. **Ximalaya's core assets are the creator ecosystem and user community** - these "soft assets" are easily lost during the merger and acquisition process
2. **Audiobook copyright and music copyright are two completely different systems** – it takes time to integrate the supply chain
3. **5 regulatory restrictions may prevent the most direct collaboration** - such as no forced bundling, no exclusive distribution, etc.

**Reasonable Expectation**: Integration will take 18-24 months to see initial results. Under optimistic conditions, Ximalaya can achieve a turnaround + content synergy within the Tencent Music system, increasing overall profits by 10-15%. In a pessimistic scenario, integration friction leads to the loss of users and the departure of creators, and it will take more than 5 years for the $2.9 billion investment to be repaid.

## 3. Other risks: not just soda music

In addition to Soda Music and Himalaya integration, Tencent Music also faces several risks that need to be taken seriously.

### Risk 1: Delisting of Chinese concept stocks

**This is not a problem unique to Tencent Music, but it is real. **

Tencent Music is dual-listed on the NYSE in the United States and the HKEX in Hong Kong. If Sino-US relations deteriorate and Chinese concept stocks are forced to delist from the U.S. stock market, the short-term impact on stock prices may be very severe - this has been demonstrated once during the panic selling of Chinese concept stocks in 2021-2022.

Mitigating factors:
- Tencent Music has been listed on the Hong Kong stock market for the second time - even if the US stock market is delisted, Hong Kong stocks can still be traded
- PCAOB audit compliance inspection passed
- Investors holding Hong Kong stocks will not be directly affected

**However, the delisting of US stocks will still cause liquidity losses and further compression of valuations. ** The current average daily trading volume of U.S. stocks is much higher than that of Hong Kong stocks - if the U.S. stock channel is closed, a large number of institutional funds forced to sell will hit lower prices in the short term.

### Risk 2: AI music subversion

In 2025-2026, AI music generation technology (such as Suno, Udio, etc.) will progress rapidly. AI can already generate songs of good quality - melody, arrangement, and even lyrics can all be completed by AI.

If AI brings the cost of music creation close to zero, what will it mean for Tencent Music?

**The bright side**: The cost of copyright acquisition may fall - if AI-generated music floods the market, the bargaining power of record companies will be weakened.

**The Bad**: The scarcity of music is diluted. When everyone can use AI to "compose" music, why should users pay to listen to other people's songs? In the long term, this could fundamentally change the basis of the value of music subscriptions.

**Current judgment**: Risks in the 5-10 year dimension. In the short term, the quality, emotional expression, and cultural significance of AI music are far inferior to human creation. But 10 years from now? uncertain.

### Risk 3: Narrow management information disclosure

Starting from Q1 of 2026, Tencent Music announced that it will no longer disclose user data on a quarterly basis (monthly active users, number of paying users, etc.).

This is a warning sign.

Good interpretation: Management believes that the indicator of monthly activity can no longer reflect the company's value - because user quality (payment rate, ARPPU) is more important than user quantity. The non-disclosure is to direct the market to focus on the right indicators.

Bad interpretation: Monthly activity may continue to decline at an accelerated pace, and management does not want the market to see this number every quarter. Stopping disclosure is a form of "information management" - selectively presenting good data to the market and hiding bad data.

**Both interpretations make sense, but as an investor, less information is never a good thing. ** The less information there is, the greater the "uncertainty discount" the market gives - which may be one of the reasons for the 68% plunge in 52 weeks.

### Risk 4: Risks associated with Spotify’s shareholding

Spotify holds about 9% of Tencent Music. In 2025, Spotify confirmed a fair value loss of approximately 975 million euros - that is to say, Spotify significantly reduced the value of its stake in Tencent Music.

This in itself does not affect the fundamentals of Tencent Music, but there is an implicit risk: **If Spotify decides to liquidate this 9% holding, it will cause huge selling pressure in the short term. ** 9% corresponds to a market value of approximately $1.2 billion - if the selling is concentrated, it may make the already sluggish stock price worse.

## 4. Risk summary

| Risk | Severity | Probability | Timeframe |
|------|---------|------|---------|
| Large-scale commercialization of soda music | High | Medium | 1-3 years |
| Himalayan integration failed | Medium to high | Medium | 2-3 years |
| Chinese concept stocks delisted | High (but buffered by Hong Kong stocks) | Low | Uncertain |
| AI Music Subversion | Medium | Medium Low | 5-10 years |
| Narrowing of information disclosure | Medium to low (but affecting valuation) | Already occurred | Continuing |
| Spotify liquidation holdings | Medium (short-term impact) | Medium-low | 1-2 years |

**The two biggest risks are soda music and Himalayan integration. ** The former determines how big the "offensive space" of Tencent Music is (whether it can continue to increase payment rates and ARPPU), and the latter determines whether the $2.9 billion investment will increase profits or be wasted.

Although the risk of delisting of Chinese concept stocks is high, the probability is relatively low - and the secondary listing of Hong Kong stocks provides a safety cushion. AI disruption is a more long-term risk.

---

## 5. Summary

The strategic logic of Himalaya's acquisition is clear - upgrade from a music platform to an audio platform, hedge against soda competition, and use cash to invest. However, the consideration of $2.9 billion is not cheap, integration is difficult, and regulatory restrictions are real -** the success or failure of this transaction may take 2-3 years to be seen. **

Among other risks, the delisting of Chinese concept stocks and AI disruption are "low probability and high impact" risks, while the narrowing of information disclosure and Spotify liquidation are "medium probability and medium impact" risks.

**Taken together, what Tencent Music currently faces is not a single fatal risk, but the superposition of multiple moderate risks. ** Each one taken individually will not "kill" the company, but when combined - soda is grabbing users, Himalaya is consuming resources, information is becoming less, and geopolitics is creating obstacles - it is no wonder that the market has given a "fear pricing" of about 6 times the cash PE discount.
