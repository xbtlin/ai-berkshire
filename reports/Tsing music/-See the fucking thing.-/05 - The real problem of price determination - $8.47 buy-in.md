# Two, where's the security margin?

The security margin is not a number, but a set of protective layers. The music is on three levels:

# First level: $38 billion in cash

The net cash on the books is $38 billion, or about 40% of the market value. This means that even if the business value is zero (extremely assumed), your principal loss is 60%.

In other words:** you bought $38 billion in cash with $96 billion + a $9.9 billion machine a year. Cash is partially zero risk, and the risk is all in the business.**

# Second layer: continuing output from free cash flows

Every year 11 billion free cash flows mean that even if the stock price never rises, the cash generated by the company increases its value per share. Cumulative buybacks have reached $2.5-26 billion (20 per cent of the market value), and $368 million in 2025 — management is feeding the cash back to shareholders, not hoarding it.

The contrast is more complex (42.23 billion cash, zero cents) and the capital feedback of music is significantly more powerful.

# Third floor: Business is still improving

The Māori rate is 44.2 per cent, the payer's $127.4 million (+5.3 per cent), the ARPU 11.9 (+7.2 per cent) - the core business indicator is all the better.** This is not a broken machine, but a changing machine — it is only a market that is not looking at it for the time being**

# Three, what's the "value trap"?

Valuation is cheap + high security margins, which does not mean it can't be a value trap. If three things ** happen simultaneously ** the current price may really not be the end:

# 1. The soda music erosion accelerates

If soda music is to be used in the next two years:
- The moon is over 250 million.
- Started large-scale development of pay-for-member (8 at the monthly card)
- The rate of increase in the payment of music by tweaks to zero or even negative growth

And the "pay rate + ARPPU" growth story of music breaks. Without growth, 10 times the price is not cheap.

#2. Himalayan integration failed

$2.9 billion to go if, 18-24 months later:
- The loss of users and creators of Himalayans
- Lack of coherence of content
- Integration losses drag down overall profit margins

That money was lost — $38 billion in cash turned to 35 billion and sustained investment in integration.

#3.3 The withdrawal of the stock market becomes a reality

If Chinese-American relations deteriorate to China’s capital stock, they are forced to withdraw:
- Loss of liquidity in the United States.
- Insufficient capacity to carry the Hong Kong stock (average daily turnover is much lower than the US share)
- The agency's money was sold out.

** The probability of three things being added is low, but if it really does -- there may be 30-40% downside space.**

# Four, what's the "gold pit"?

If any of the following three things ** happen, the current price will be a clear gold pit when it looks back:

# 1. Paying rate out of 30%

The rate of payment ranges from 24 to 30 per cent, meaning that the number of users of payment increased from 127.4 million to about 160 million (assuming monthly life to be stable at around 530 million). More than 30 million new users of payment × $11-12 per month <unk> generating an annual increase of $4-5 billion.

Adding ARPU up, online music revenue may increase from 26.7 billion to 35-40 billion. This scale rise is enough to support more than 15 times the PE.

##2. Super-intensity to 25%

From 20 million to 32 million (25% penetration), each person pays an additional $12-15 a month – an annual gain of about $1.72 billion. More importantly, the increase in the hyper penetration rate marks the success of the "high end" strategy – a multi-fold higher value for the market.

#3. The commercialization of soda music failed

Most of the users of soda music come for "free plus low." If the soda starts paying, it may be found:
- Eight-dollar card users have a very low willingness to pay -- they barely pay eight dollars.
- A 15-dollar raise would lead to a massive flow of users back to the troupe.
- The advertising income cannot cover copyright costs

In such cases, the stay of the soda concert in the position of the "squeak music player" does not constitute a fundamental threat to the payment of music by tether.

** If the rate of payment exceeds 30% + the failure of the commercialization of soda** — PE of tweak music from 10 to 15-18 times, the share price corresponds to $14-17. From $8.47, the increase is 65-100%.

# # Five, $8.47 worth it?

Not give the exact answer, but give a framework of judgement.

# Ask yourself five questions before buying

** First, what do you think about the next three years of soda music?** If you think that soda will break 300 million months of life and be commercialized successfully, the pay-for-growth logic of tweak music will be fundamentally shaken. If you think that soda will stop at around 200 million and make it difficult, the current stock price already oversaw this threat.

** Second, what do you judge about the threat to soda music?** If you think that soda will take out the payee on a massive scale -- the price may not be the bottom. If you think that soda can only be turned around at the lower end of the market -- the current price is already too much to fear.

** Third, how long would you like to wait for the Himalayas?** Integration takes at least two to three years to see. If you have less than three years of holding, this uncertainty is too big for you.

** Fourth, can you afford another 30-40%?** 52 weeks have already fallen from $26.7 to $8.47. If you fall further to $5-6 (because of the central retreat of panic or some black swan), can you not sell it?

** Fifth, is there a better choice for the same money?** At the same time, the PE is about 16 times higher, the Apple is about 30 times more, and the Spotify PE is about 37 times more. Ten times the PE is really the cheapest -- but the cheap is not the reason for buying it, unless you think "this company is worth a higher value."

When must I reevaluate

Some red lines - triggering any one requires a re-examination:

1. Quarterly decline in the rounding of pay-for-service users (core growth logic broken)
2. The soda music introduces mature payment patterns and fast-tracking (deteriorating competition patterns)
3. No synergy is visible 12 months after Himalayan integration ($2.9 billion possible to drift)
4. Substantive deterioration of the policy of retirement of the Central General Stock Exchange (SEC compulsory de-listing)
5. Two consecutive quarters of decline in Māori ratio (cost structure deterioration signal)

Conversely, a positive sign is: ** Monthly activity is steady or rising**. This is the most worrying indicator for the market — once it is stable, the narrative of "user loss" is broken and valuation repair may be quick.

# Six, last

Back to the paradox of the beginning of the series: profit rose by 25 per cent and equity prices fell by 68 per cent.

After five readings, this contradictory interpretation became clear -- ** The market is not pricing "the profits of the past" but "the uncertainty of the future".

The uncertainty arises from whether soda music can be blocked, whether Himalayan integration can be successful, whether the central stock will be withdrawn and when the monthly life will end.

About six times the cash PE, that's the market's price for these uncertainties. If you think these risks are over-magnified -- that $8.47 is a good price. If you think these risks are real and they could worsen -- then even about six times the PE does not constitute a security margin.

** Both judgements make sense.** The difference is that you have different probabilities for each variable.

A better company, a stronger opponent, a repricing market — three things combined — constituted the investment topic of tweak music.

There is no standard answer to this question. But at least after reading these five, you should know what the question is.
