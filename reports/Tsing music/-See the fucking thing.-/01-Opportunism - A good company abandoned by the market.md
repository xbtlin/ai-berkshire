## A set of data that allows you to re-examine Tencent Music

In 2025, Tencent Music delivered a good report card:

| Indicators | Data 2025 | Changes |
|------|-----------|------|
| Total revenue | 32.9 billion yuan | +15.8% |
| Non-IFRS net profit (profit after excluding one-time items such as equity incentives) | 9.6 billion yuan | **+25%** |
| Gross profit margin | 44.2% | Rising from 30% in 2021 |
| Free cash flow (operating cash flow minus capital expenditures, which can be understood as "actually earned cash") | ~9.9 billion yuan | Continue to be abundant |
| Net cash | 38 billion yuan | Accounting for about 40% of market capitalization |
| Paying users | 127.4 million | +5.3% |

Profit has increased by 25%, gross profit margin has increased for four consecutive years, and there is 38 billion in cash on the account. Logically speaking, the market should give it some applause.

However, during the same period, the stock price fell from a 52-week high of $26.70 to $8.47, a drop of **68%**.

Put these two things together: **A company that grew profits by 25% saw its stock price drop by 68%**.

What is the current valuation level?

| Valuation Metrics | Value | What It Means |
|----------|------|----------|
| Market Cap | ~96 billion RMB ($13.3 billion) | — |
| PE (Non-IFRS) | ~10x | Historical low |
| Net cash | 38 billion (accounting for 40% of market value) | The cash on hand accounts for almost half of the market value |
| PE after deducting cash | **~6 times** | Equivalent to spending 58 billion to buy a machine with an annual income of 9.6 billion |
| Cumulative repurchases | ~$2.5-2.6 billion | Accounting for about 20% of market value |
| Free cash flow yield | ~12% | Far superior to bank financing |

Translate it into vernacular: **The market's current pricing for Tencent Music is equivalent to "the cash on your account is given to you for free, and the business is only worth 6 years of profits"**.

Either the market has discovered a serious problem, or the market has made a mistake. Figuring out which situation is the case is the question this series aims to answer.

## Three common misconceptions about Tencent Music

Before we dismantle them, let’s break down three of the most widespread misconceptions.

### Illusion 1: Tencent Music is a "music player"

Let’s look at most people’s impressions of Tencent Music – QQ Music, Kuwo Music, and Kugou Music, three music-listening software. "Isn't it just a player?"

This understanding remains in 2019.

In 2019, Tencent Music’s revenue structure is as follows:

| Sector | Proportion in 2019 | Proportion in 2025 |
|------|----------|----------|
| Social entertainment (live streaming rewards, etc.) | **~70%** | 18.8% |
| Online music (subscription + advertising, etc.) | ~30% | **81.2%** |

**In 6 years, the income structure has completely flipped**. From a "pan-entertainment company" that makes money through live broadcast rewards, it has become a "Chinese version of a subscription platform" driven by music subscriptions.

What's more critical is that while social entertainment is shrinking, online music is growing at an annual rate of more than 20% - online music revenue will be 26.7 billion in 2025, +23% year-on-year. Overall profits rose instead of falling.

**This is not a story about a "player", but a story about a business structure that has been completely reborn. **

### Myth 2: The decline of social entertainment = the decline of the company

Social entertainment revenue has dropped from the peak in 2019, leaving only 6.2 billion in 2025, accounting for only 18.8% of total revenue. It sounds scary - a business that accounts for 70% has shrunk so much, can the company be okay?

The data gives a clear answer: **Yes. And it's better. **

| Indicators | 2021 | 2025 | Changes |
|------|--------|--------|------|
| Total revenue | 31.2 billion | 32.9 billion | +5% (recovered after experiencing a trough) |
| Gross profit margin | 30% | 44.2% | **+14 percentage points** |
| Non-IFRS net profit | ~5 billion (estimated) | 9.92 billion | **Nearly doubled** |

The gross profit margin increased from 30% to 44%, and the reason is simple: **Social entertainment is a low-margin business (needs to be shared with anchors), while online music is a high-margin business (subscription revenue is almost pure profit)**. Low-gross profit businesses shrink and high-margin businesses expand—mixed gross profit margins naturally rise.

Therefore, the decline of social entertainment is not so much "bad news" as it is that Tencent Music has completed a self-evolution - getting rid of low-quality income and replacing it with high-quality income**.

**Contrary view**: The decline of social entertainment is irreversible, but it has not completely returned to zero - currently, revenue of 6.2 billion continues to shrink every year. If the decline accelerates, it will still drag down overall growth in the short term.

### Illusion 3: Being so cheap must be a "value trap"

Excluding cash PE, it is only about 6 times - many people's first reaction is "cheap but not good".

The classic characteristics of a value trap are: it looks cheap, but profits are deteriorating, moats are disappearing, and cash is flowing out. Compare it:

| Value trap characteristics | The actual situation of Tencent Music |
|-------------|-------------------|
| Profits deteriorating? | Non-IFRS net profit +22% in 2025, Q1 2026 will continue to +4.8% |
| Cash flowing out? | Net cash is 38 billion, and free cash flow averages more than 10 billion per year |
| Is the moat disappearing? | This **needs careful discussion** - Chapter 03 Expand |
| Is the management messing up? | Cumulative repurchases of $2.5-2.6 billion (accounting for 20% of market value), dividends of $368 million in 2025 |

The first two of the four items are clearly not in line with the value trap, and the fourth item is also not in line - the management is actively repurchasing, not sitting on nothing.

**The only thing that needs further study is the third item: moat**. Monthly activity has declined for 16 consecutive quarters, soda music has 156 million monthly activity and is growing at an alarming rate - is this "it doesn't matter if a group of low-quality users are replaced", or is it a "sign that the moat is being eroded"?

This is one of the most crucial questions of the entire series. Chapter 03 will be specially dismantled.

## To understand Tencent Music, you need to answer four questions

If you could sum up the current situation of Tencent Music in one sentence:

> **A company that is getting better encounters a competitor that is getting stronger, and is trading in a market that is being repriced. **

The superposition of these three things resulted in the abnormal situation of "profit increased by 22% and stock price fell by 68%."

## A small preview

Leave a few numbers behind and will explain them in detail in subsequent chapters. Remember the magnitude first:

| Indicators | Data |
|------|------|
| China’s online music payment rate | 24% (global average 40-50%) |
| Tencent Music ARPPU | 11.9 yuan/month (Spotify is about $5.3/month, equivalent to about 38 yuan in RMB) |
| Soda Music monthly active users | 156 million (surpassed NetEase Cloud Music in March 2026) |
| Soda music library | 50 million songs (Tencent Music 260 million songs) |
| Himalaya acquisition consideration | $2.9 billion |
| Tencent Music’s cumulative buybacks | ~$2.5-2.6 billion (accounting for 20% of market value) |

Put these together, and what kind of company do you see:

- The payment rate is only half that of the world - meaning there is theoretically room for doubling
- Single-user revenue is only one-third of Spotify’s – there is plenty of room for price increases
- But the largest competitor has 156 million monthly active users and is still rising sharply - there is a spoiler on the way to raising prices.
- Just bought a Himalayan for $2.9 billion – either a smart move or an expensive gamble

**What do these contradictory signals mean together? Different judgments will give different answers. ** This is what the next 4 articles will answer.
