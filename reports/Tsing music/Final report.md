# 4. Summary of the analysis of dimensions

# Business model and moat river (infinity)

1. **The income structure transition was successful but forced** – online music rose from 30% (2019) to 82% (Q1,2026) and social entertainment from 70% to 18%. Not the proactive strategy, but the right path, forced by regulation and competition.
** The scale effect is the strongest moat** – the copyright cost-sharing advantage of 127.4 million payers is real (<$10 per month per person) and competition cannot replicate. But soda music does not require full copyright — blast.
3. ** The whole moat is becoming shallow** – after the exclusive copyright was broken, the flying wheels were changed from "licensed-driven" to "inert-driven". The conversion costs are derived from the sinking of the songsheet and the personalization of the algorithms, but the users are more "suffering" than "not wanting"
4. ** Stop disclosing MAU as a major red flag** — Sing Yongping: "Many doesn't want you to see data, usually the most deserving data."
5. **The Eternity of the Eternity Assessment**: "A good" business is not a "great" business.

# Finance and valuation (Buffett perspective)

** Cash fabrication machines** — FCF continuously exceeds net profits, Capex receives only 2-3%, five years of accumulated free cash flows ~ USD 5.8 billion, and the market value recovery period is ~2.5 years only
2. **The profit margin has expanded dramatically** — from 30% to 44% (five years + 14 ppt) and from ~7% to ~28% of business returns, driven by a core drive of low Maori social entertainment contraction + high Māori subscriptions increase
** Valuation is at a low level of multidimensional history** — PE 8.65x(GAAP)/10x(Non-IFRS), PB 0.57x, FCF Yield 10%, compared with Spotify PE 31-49x with a significant discount
** Net cash RMB 21 billion, or 22% of the market value** ** is equivalent to an automatic discount on purchase price, providing a solid value base
** But net interest ** = RMB 2.37 billion one-time gain (consequentialization of disposal), excluding real increase ~ 31% instead of 66% of GAAP
** ROE only 14% is a hard wound** – A large amount of cash idle and low capital returns, Buffett asks: Why not return the excess money to the shareholders?

# Industry and competition (Manger perspective)

1. ** Industry enters steady ** – online music (fluent media calibre) ~ 40 billion yuan market, increasing from 30% to 15%. TIE+ net-fluorescent aggregate income is close to market totals and is not as large as it could be expected.
2. **The soda music is a completely different business paradigm** - access to near zero (82% from the shivering flow), free mode, and algorithm recommended. 18 months from 50 million MAU to 140 million, with the second ladder of Nets and Clouds.
3. **TME moats are more accurately the moats of QQ music** – cool dog MAUs per year - 8.1%, cool me - 8%, "loose user" migration to soda music increasing by 64-73%.
** Non-consensus: Acquisition of Himalayan as a defensive acquisition** — market is generally negative (90 billion losses), but the core logic is to prevent byte-wide capture of music plus audio
** Comparability of the U.S.-American ARPU gap is overestimated** — Spotify has no good competition abroad and is completely free of charge, TIE has

# Risk and management (Li Su)

1. ** Management pass bias** — CEO beam pillar has undergone a difficult transition, with capital allocation (back-purchase + red + 6-7% market value), but not innovative entrepreneurs, but more career managers in the telecommunications system
2. **The greatest "invisible risk"** — not the soda music user (seen in the market), but the paradigm shift in the way music is consumed: when short video BGM becomes the dominant way for young people to listen to songs, the value of independent music APP is fundamentally questioned
3. ** Released risks** — Social entertainment dropped from 19.8 billion to 6.2 billion (-69 per cent) and anti-monopoly restructuring completed, two basic risks in
4. **Himalaya is the biggest M&A bet** – $1.260 billion to buy a long audio company with multiple IPO failures, with high integration risks, and "diworldification" risks that cannot be ignored
5. ** Stopping the disclosure of user data is a crisis of trust** – 25% of the market fell back to this signal.

#8. Summary

The good side is that the company earns real silver, values it at the bottom of history, and net cash provides a solid security cushion. The bad side is that users are losing, mooring the mound, management is starting to hide data, and the biggest competitors are rising rapidly with a completely different business paradigm.

This is not a "blind buy-in, long-term holding" target. It is better suited to build a disciplined warehouse when valuation is extremely low, with three core variables: ** the speed of the soda music, TEE's retention rate, Himalayan integration**. If user data are stable in the second half of 2026 and the Himalayan is starting to contribute revenue, stock prices are a reasonable path (+40% to +75%) from the current $9 to $13-16. If users continue to accelerate the loss plus the failure of the acquisitions, it is not surprising that less than $7.

The security margin is enough, depending on your judgment of whether the music consumption entrance will move from an independent APP to a short video ecology.
