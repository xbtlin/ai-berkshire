# V. Trends in industry

##5.1 Impact of short video on traditional music platforms (maximum structural threat)

This is the most important concern of Mang — the fundamental change in consumption habits.

** Fact:**
- 86% of the TOP 1000 hot song grew up in a tremor, 54% started a fire from a tremor cold.
- The portal to music consumption is going from "Opening the music App Search" to "Acceeding a song in a short video."
- Soda music users listen to songs for more than 80 minutes per day, 48 different scenes for the music.

** Deep impact:**

The core value of traditional music platforms is "Copy + Search" -- users know what they want to hear, come to the platform to find songs. But short videos change the logic: users don't know what they want to hear, algorithms recommend you. This means:

1. **The copyright barrier has depreciated**: "Is there a song" becomes less important when users are no longer actively searching but are passively recommended
2. ** Discover mechanism transfer**: The focus of the record company ' s campaign shifted from a music platform to a short video platform
3. ** User custom re-engineering**: The Z generation ' s music consumption patterns have been defined by short video, a trend that is irreversible

** But there is another side:** short video songs have a very short life cycle (usually only 2-4 weeks), while classic music on traditional music platforms has long tailing effects. "Scope a song and "collect and listen" are two different behaviours, the latter still completed on traditional platforms.

2021-2025: Net-flowing rights to motten sky, king, singer, Jay

** Non-consensual view:** Many analysts compare the TEE and Spotify ARPU gap to argue for "price-added spaces", but ignore a fundamental difference - Spotify has no free adverts (free but not experienced) overseas, while TIE faces a non-bad experience of completely free soda music. The comparability of US-American music payments is overestimated.

# VII. Summary of economic characteristics of the industry (Munge Framework)

# Is this business about winner-taker or how strong it is?

**Answer: Midway -- "One Power and How Weak" is evolving towards "How Strong and How Strong."**

Music streaming media have a network effect (the more users they use, the more recommended, the more accurate). But the network has a less powerful effect than social networks. The reason is that music consumption is mainly "one person listening to songs" and not "one person is using" like micro-mail. Thus, the moats of music platforms are more likely to come from: (1) user habits and sunk costs (songlists, collections); (2) integrity of copyright libraries; and (3) community stickyness (net-tubes).

Once exclusive copyright is removed, item (2) of the moat is weakened. The AI music may further reduce the scarcity of content. The rest depends on the inertia of user habits — a moat that can slowly erode.

# Will copyright costs always be high?

** Answer: No. Improvements are under way, but improvements are on the ceiling**

- TME cost/income ratio: 2018 ~ 89% ~ 2025 ~ 73%, decrease by 16 percentage points in 5 years
- Internet-leaf-coated copyright costs as a percentage of income: 2018 - 80 per cent ~ 2025 ~ 75 per cent, a decrease of 5 percentage points
- Reasons for improvement: (1) reduced competition after non-exclusive ownership; (2) increased ownership of content (original musician + AI generation); (3) scale effect
- Ceiling: the oligarchic status of the three major record companies will not disappear and copyright costs will not fall below 50%
