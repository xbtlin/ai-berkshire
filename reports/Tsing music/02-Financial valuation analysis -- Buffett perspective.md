#2.2 Buffett's perspective

** Positive**:
- Māori rate increased from 30% to 44% and by 14 percentage points in five years - a very rare increase in profit margins
- Core drivers: Social entertainment (low Maori, high-water) is shrinking and replaced by online music (high Maori, subscription)
- Visible leverage: revenue increased by only 16 per cent, but operating profits increased by 53 per cent

** The reverse test - can the profit margin expand?**:
- Social entertainment income has shrunk to a lower base, and the "positive drag-out" effect on profit margins will be reduced in the future
- The cost of music copyright may increase: the bargaining power of the copyrighter (the record company) after seeing an improvement in the TEE profit
- ROE is only 14.3 per cent, improved but well below the best (25 per cent+), and the root cause is that large cash on the balance sheet has reduced the return on capital

** Rating: <unk> **

> The profit rate expansion trend is excellent, with the transition from "pay for size" to "high Maori subscription model" nearing completion. But the low ROE is hard. Buffett asks: Why not return the excess cash to shareholders?

There must be a reason why the market gives such a low valuation.

1. **Concepted China stock discount**: geopolitical risks, VIE structure, de-market risks (although significantly reduced)
2. **Tet Holding Shadow**: TEE is a Tweet subsidiary, corporate governance may not be fully independent, associated transaction risks
3. ** Social entertainment continues to contract**: the ratio is decreasing but still lagging, with 2026Q1 social entertainment income of the same age - 11%
4. **Himalaya acquisition risk**: large mergers and acquisitions may consume net cash and consolidation risks are uncertain
**$26.70 a week high ** current $9.08**: share price re-echo, what are the negative expectations of the market pricing?
** Slower rate of growth**: 2026Q1 battalion received only 7.3 per cent (15.8 per cent for vs 2025) and subscriptions decreased to 6.6 per cent
