# Three levels of security margin

Even with caution about the fundamentals, the tweaking music has three layers of security margin at the current price:

** First tier: $34.5 billion in net cash.** This represents 37% of the market value. This means that even if the value of the business is zero (extremely assumed), you're only 63% short. Cash is the hardest.

** Second tier: the FCF continues to produce about 9.8 billion dollars a year.** Companies do not need external financing to produce nearly 10 billion dollars a year in cash. This cash flow engine will continue to function as long as business does not break off the cliff.

** Third tier: business fundamentals are still improving.** The trend is not reversed, with the rising share of subscription income, the increase in ARPPU (average monthly income per user paying) and the expansion of the Māori rate.

# Q1 Profits "brain" is a fake

If you see the net profit of the IFRS (International Financial Reporting Standards) of Q1 **2.09 billion yuan, 51 per cent below the same year **, do not panic.

This "blank beheading" is entirely due to** a lump sum of $2.37 billion in Q1 last year** - SWIFT shares are treated as paper profits from disposal (investments reclassified, not sold). This money has nothing to do with music subscriptions.

The disruption was removed and the adjusted net profit was $2.33 billion, an increase of **4.8 per cent** over the year.

Not surprising, but not "blank-heading."

One lesson is that China’s Internet companies are doing well ** to distinguish between GAAP and Non-GAAP, on a regular and one-time basis.** Title parties prefer to use the volatility of GAAP profits to create panic, but the actual performance is often much more stable.

# What's the "value trap"?

Cheap is not good. ** The real risk is not "Is it expensive now?" ** "Is it gonna be worse in the future?" **

If three things happen simultaneously**, the current $8.47 is still not safe:

**1. Large-scale commercialization of soda music**

If bytes jumps to push fee subscriptions strongly on soda (e.g., by launching 15-syllable membership + by expanding the pool) rather than continuing to treat them as tremor copyright centres and flow products, both payers of tsong music and ARPU will face a direct impact.

At present, bytes seem to still position the soda as an extension of the tremor ecology, rather than as an independent profit-making entity. But this judgement can change at any time.

**2. Failure of Himalayan integration**

The acquisition of $2.9 billion (approximately $18.6 billion in cash plus equity) is a huge capital misalignment if it does not produce meaningful synergies. The cash portion of the $2.1 billion ($1.26 billion) will directly consume cash reserves, weakening the company’s financial flexibility and buy-back capacity.

**3. The Central Chief is at extreme policy risk**

If China-US relations deteriorate further, resulting in China-China stock being forced to withdraw or severely restrict transactions, the valuation discounts will be further expanded rather than narrowed.

** The probability of simultaneous occurrence is low, but it is not zero.** If you judge that more than two of these three risks will become a reality, the current price may still be less expensive.

# What's the "gold pit"?

In turn, the current price will be a significant low if the following conditions are achieved:

**1. Pay rate out of 30% and SVIP penetration of 25%**

The current rates of payments are about 24%, SVIP 16%. If it is raised to 30% and 25%, respectively, there will be a significant non-linear increase in subscription income. This means that even if total users are falling, income and profits will continue to grow.

**2. Commercialization of soda music is hampered**

If bytes continue to position the soda as a vibrating copyright medium and user retention tool, rather than as a stand-alone profit product, the threat to the music of the tweaking is "the diversion of user attention" rather than "the replacement of business models" — the latter being a chronic effect, the latter being a deadly shock.

**3. Substantive progress in Himalayan integration**

After the two-year limit period (approximately 2028), $2.9 billion in acquisitions can prove value if members of music plus audio are successful, user retention rates are raised and ARPU (average income per user) are cross-excerpt.

# One of the most crucial signals

On the basis of all the complex analyses, one indicator is more important than any financial figure:

** Whether active monthly users are stable or up.**

This is the most anxious indicator of the market. Starting in the second half of 2021, the cumulative monthly loss of life exceeded 100 million – a figure too large for markets to have reason to suspect that this is not a cyclical one, but a structural recession.

Once the month is steady — if not growing, but not falling — ** the narrative "Users are losing" will be broken**

And narrative shifts tend to be faster and more intense in capital markets than in basics. A quarterly "mortal ring" may be more powerful in restoring equity prices than a quarterly "profit 20% increase".

The problem is that the company has stopped disclosing monthly data.

This makes validation more difficult and fear less difficult. Unless management chooses to redisclose at a node (which usually means data improvement), or third-party data sources provide credible alternative indicators, the "black hole" itself will continue to suppress valuations.

# No answers, no frames

And I'm not going to tell you "bought" or "not bought." Because the same data makes different conclusions -- and these conclusions may be right.

But before you make a decision, you need to answer three questions:

** Question 1: What are your judgments about the threat to soda music?**

If you think that soda is just a companion to the tremor, that the curry is not made up, that the eight-dollar card model is unsustainable — the threat of soda is overestimated and the current stock price is too high to reflect fear.

If you think that byte enforcement is always strong, that the gap between the curvatures will gradually narrow, and that free + advertising models will be enough to erode the willingness to pay — the threat of soda may just start, and the current stock price price may not be enough.

** Question two: Can you afford to fall by another 30%?**

Six times the cash PE is cheap. But "favour" is not "favourable." The discount on the average price can last for a long time in extreme cases. If you buy it, you'll have to pay 30 to $6 again, will you be stow or cut?

If the answer is meat cut, it means that your warehouse is probably too heavy, or your faith in this investment is not strong enough.

** Question three: Are you holding long enough?**

The regulatory restrictions in Himalayan are two years (by about 2028), the SVIP penetration rate takes time, and the final end of soda music is not clear in a single quarter or two.

If you need to see results within a year, tv music may not be suitable for you. If you have patience for more than three years, the current price offers at least a fairly attractive opportunity to pay — ** the core premise is that the fundamentals have not deteriorated to irreversible levels.**

** An operational observational indicator**: Quarterly attention to changes in the soda music MAU in third-party data (QuestMobile, etc.). If the soda MAU is slowing down to one-digit number, indicating that its natural flow pool (the shivering flow) is on the top, the pressure on the music is reduced; if the soda MAU continues to double and initiate large-scale fee promotion, it is reassessed.
