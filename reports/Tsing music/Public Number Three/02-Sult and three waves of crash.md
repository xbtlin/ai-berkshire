# 68% of the drop is not done in a day

The previous one saw a paradox: the profit from tactile music grew by 25 per cent, while the share price fell by 68 per cent.

Markets are not crazy – it's a fear of pricing. To understand that fear, it needs to go back to the stock price map, breaking 68% of the fall into three concentrated drops. Each drop has a specific catalyst behind it.

---

# First wave of crash: 25% in November 2025

** Trigger event**: Q3 financial disclosure + data exposure for monthly doubling of soda music

In November 2025, the music Q3 was released. The money itself is not bad -- both income and profits are increasing. But the market’s attention has been drawn to another figure:

** Monthly active users of soda music (MAU) reached 120 million, double the year-on-year.**

What is more disturbing to investors is the new word -- ** "loose user"**. Analysts found in their research that more than **21 million** users use both tweeting and soda music, and that the users spend more and more time on soda.

This is an ominous signal. The user does not unload QQ music directly, but steps on two boats and slowly migrates its usage habits to soda.

The market began to reassess a previously neglected risk:** byte beats seriously**.

Soda music is no longer an accessory function of tremors, but an independent, fast-growing competitor.

# Second wave of crash: 24.65 per cent in a single day on March 17, 2026

** Trigger event**: three bad news detonated on the same day

This is the worst day ever. ** The single day fell by 24.65%, the largest single day since the music was put on the market.

Three bombs landed at once:

♪ Bomb I: Profit "water" is exposed ♪

After the release of Q4 in 2025 and the year-round performance, analysts found that the GAP net profit included a share of **2.37 billion dollars in GWAP shares as disposal gains** (the amount recorded in Q1 2025) that were one-time paper gains - book profits from the reclassification of investments - and had nothing to do with the main business. Although the net profit of Non-GAAP has been cut off, the GAP net profit still misjudges some investors by the "beauty" figure, which is 66% higher.

** Bomb II: Declaration to stop disclosure of user data**

From 2026 Q1, tweak music will no longer disclose MAU and payee data in the financial statements.

This is the most deadly hit**.

The reason is simple:** companies choose to stop disclosure only when data are variable.**

If the monthly life is growing and the payee is accelerating, which management would have taken the initiative to hide its best figures? The cessation of disclosure is itself a signal — it suggests that management expects that the trend in these indicators will not improve and that instead of being asked by analysts every quarter, it would have been better to take them out.

** Bomb III: Low innovation for user fee growth**

In the last season, when disclosure stopped, the rate of increase for payers was only **5.3% ** ** ** ** – a low since the market was created. This certainly sits down the most worrying thing in the market: the growth engine is slowing down.

** Market response**: Three major investment parties (Golden, Morgan Chase, Citigroup) issued a downgrade report on the same day. Vendor analysts almost unanimously agreed that the cessation of disclosure of user data marked the end of the growth narrative.

# Third wave of crash: 30% in May 2026

** Trigger event**: approval of Himalayan conditionality + Q1 performance below expectations + defence cost rise

The third wave of crash is a combination of negative factors:

**1. Himalayans were approved, but it was equivalent to "do nothing."**

The AMA approval was approved on May 12, but five restrictions (no price increase, no exclusive, no co-sale, no anchoring, and no free content) allowed the market to expect any synergy. The $2.9 billion was spent without a short-term return path.

**2. Q1 Non-GAAP per share (EPS) below the expected $0.02**

The difference is small, but even a fraction of the difference is magnified when the market mood is extremely fragile.

**3. Marketing costs increased by 36 per cent over the same period**

Q1 marketing costs have increased significantly, and management has explained that "brand promotion and user access to inputs". But market translation is:** To protect against the erosion of soda music, tweaking music has to spend more money to keep users.**

The rising costs of defence are a dangerous signal — it means that competition is increasing and that the counterparty does not need to spend the same money (the user of the soda is from the shivering flow, with the cost of receiving the guest almost zero).

**4. Spotify confirmed a loss of Euro975 million on the share of tweak music**

Spotify holds about 9% of the share in tweak music. In his financial statement, he confirmed a **975 million euro ** investment loss. Although this is just a paper loss, market sentiment is difficult to resist when even your strategic shareholders are significantly devalued on the books.

# The core competitiveness of soda: zero cost for guests

The most horrific part of soda music is not the $8 monthly card, but** it does not require money to access users**.

**82% of users come directly from the shivering flow**

Users swipe to a beautiful BGM, clicking on "A full version with soda music" and doing a zero-cost hit. The vibrating is more than 700 million days, and this conduit is much more efficient than any traditional marketing tool.

By contrast, the cost of marketing Q1 music has increased by 36% over the same period, barely keeping the basics of the user. The more expensive one gets, the more expensive one spends, that is why the market is anxious.

# The short board of soda: the curve and the scene

But there are also obvious shorts for soda music:

**1. The gap in the stacks is huge**: 50 million vs. 260 million, **5 times less. Not for light users who listen freely, but for musicians, it is fatal to find no song they want to hear.

**2. No super-optimal rights**: SVIP, which is a music troupe, offers differential experiences such as soundless, masturbated and the like, and the soda is currently not a product of the label.

**4. Discovery has not yet been validated**: Whether the advertising-based model of soda supports long-term development is a question mark. The commercialization of music stream media is much less efficient than short video, and Spotify’s advertising supports free-of-charge margins are much less than the pay-for-the-couper.

From the second half of 2021, the number of active monthly users of telecommunication music continued to decline, with cumulative losses exceeding **100 million users**.

The number jumped briefly in 2023, with a change in Q1 calibre, which included IOT devices such as vehicle-borne audio boxes, smart audio boxes and so forth, but the trend remained unchanged.

** The company will not hide at the time of the data becoming better.** The cessation of disclosure is almost confirmed: the monthly life is still declining and management believes that it will not be reversed in the short term.

If it is a user who is either free or a light user (who leaves with a few songs per month), the impact on income is limited. The opposite is that today's free users are tomorrow's payers. Free user loss means that the "water tank" converted into a fee is shrinking. When the reservoir is small to a certain extent, the number of payers will eventually be the highest.

# Soda vs tweep music: What's the relationship?

In combination, the threat of soda music to tweak music can be summarized in three levels:

** First layer: seizing incremental users.** The new generation of young users (Z generation, after 00) has music portals that are no longer QQ but tremors. They naturally flow to soda music. The ability of tweak music to access new users is declining.

** Second floor: lower pay ceiling.** When $8 is able to listen to songs, $18 is expensive. Even if the pool of soda is far less expensive, users of "use it" will choose cheaper options. This suppresses the price-added space for tweaking music.

** Third floor: accelerating the loss of light users.** Those who are not willing to pay for QQ music because of habit, and with cheaper or even free alternatives, the motivation for migration increases.

** But soda is not a substitute for tweak music for the short term.** The pool is five times short of hard wounds, super-intelligent rights cannot be matched to the markers, and micro-social binding cannot be replicated for the short term.

What is the market afraid of?

The fear of the market became clear when three waves of crash and soda music analysis were put together:

** Not that the music will fall tomorrow, but that the chronic disease will worsen.**

Specifically:

1. ** User base plate is shrinking** and companies are preventing markets from judging the pace of contraction by stopping data disclosure
** Competition acquisition costs are approaching zero** while self-defence costs are rising
3. ** Synergy of Himalayan acquisitions are blocked by regulation** and $2.9 billion in investment is not yielding returns in the short term
4. **The price-up logic is being suppressed by the low-cost soda strategy** and the assumption that SVIP penetration may increase is overly optimistic

Each single one is not fatal. But when they are stacked together, they constitute a narrative that makes the market uncomfortable:** The moats of music are slowly being eroded, and management does not seem to have a particularly effective response**.

In summary:** soda music is a chronic disease of tweak music, not an emergency. But stock prices reflect the market's fear of whether chronic disease will deteriorate into incurable disease.**

Is this fear excessive?

Think of it differently:** If you spend 58.5 billion now on all the business of buying tweak music (butting cash), you can get back the free cash flow of 9.8 billion dollars a year -- six years. And the machine's profit rate is rising.**
