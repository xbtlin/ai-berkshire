#Tencent Music: Profit rose 25%, stock price fell 68%

> Reading time: about 8 minutes
> This article is one of three in the Tencent Music series

---

## A confusing set of numbers

Let’s look at a set of data first:

- Non-GAAP (non-GAAP) net profit for fiscal year 2025 **9.6 billion**, a year-on-year increase of **25%**
- During the same period, the stock price fell from **$26.70** to **$8.47**, a decrease of **68%**
- The current market value is approximately **93 billion yuan**, cash and equivalents on account are **38 billion yuan**, and net cash (excluding interest-bearing liabilities) is approximately **34.5 billion yuan**
- The PE (price-to-earnings ratio) after deducting net cash is only **6 times** - equivalent to spending 6 yuan to buy a business that earns 1 yuan a year

A company with rapidly growing profits and overflowing cash has seen its stock price fall by two-thirds.

This is not the story of a third-tier small-cap stock, this is **Tencent Music Entertainment Group** - China's largest online music platform, with three major brands: QQ Music, Kugou Music, and Kuwo Music, with a total of more than **127 million paying users**.

What happened? Before answering "Why did it fall?", we need to understand one thing first: What kind of company is Tencent Music? **

Because Tencent Music today and Tencent Music three years ago are two completely different companies.

## A quiet "blood exchange"

If your impression of Tencent Music is still limited to "live streaming rewards", then your understanding needs to be updated.

Look at a table of income structure changes:

| Year | Social entertainment revenue share | Online music revenue share |
|------|-----------------|-----------------|
| 2019 | **72%** | 28% |
| 2021 | **58%** | 42% |
| 2023 | 32% | **68%** |
| 2026 Q1 | 17.5% | **82.5%** |

The conclusion of this table is very clear: **Tencent Music is no longer a live broadcast company, but a subscription company. **

In 2019, social entertainment (mainly live streaming rewards) contributed 70% of revenue. By the first quarter of 2026, this proportion has shrunk to less than 20%, and online music (subscription + advertising) accounts for more than 80%.

This transformation is not forced by the market - the gross profit margin of live streaming rewards is much lower than that of subscriptions, and due to the dual suppression of regulation and user aesthetic fatigue, it is destined to be a shrinking business. Tencent Music chose to take the initiative to "cut the meat" and give up short-term income in exchange for long-term profit quality.

How effective is it? Look at the profit margin:

| Year | Gross profit margin |
|------|-------|
| 2021 | **30.1%** |
| 2023 | **36.7%** |
| 2026 Q1 | **44.9%** |

In four years, gross profit margin soared from 30% to nearly 45%. This improvement is quite rare among mature Internet companies.

There are two driving forces: first, exclusive copyrights will be canceled after anti-monopoly in 2021, and content costs will drop significantly; second, the proportion of high-gross-profit subscription revenue continues to increase, structurally improving the quality of earnings.

## The "second growth curve" of subscriptions: SVIP

The number of paying users of Tencent Music will reach **127 million** by the end of 2025, with a payment rate of approximately **24%**. This number still has room for improvement in global music streaming media - Spotify's payment rate is about **39%**, which is much higher than Tencent Music, but Spotify does not have as large a free user base as Tencent Music.

But what really deserves attention is not the total number of paying users, but SVIP (Super Membership).

The monthly fee of SVIP is **28-30 yuan**, which is twice that of ordinary members (about 15 yuan). It provides benefits such as lossless sound quality, premium mastering, in-vehicle scenes, and multi-terminal synchronization of smart devices. As of the latest data, SVIP has more than **20 million** users, with a penetration rate of approximately **16%**, and the management goal is to increase it to **25%**.

What does this mean? Do a simple calculation:

- The current ARPPU (average monthly revenue per paying user) is approximately **11.9 yuan**
- If the SVIP penetration rate increases from 16% to 25%, ARPPU is expected to approach **15 yuan**
- Without an increase in paying users, the increase in ARPPU alone can bring about a **26%** increase in subscription revenue.

This is why management still maintains confidence in revenue growth even as user growth slows down - the room for price increases is more certain than the room for new additions. **

Of course, the opposing view is also clear: SVIP's high penetration rate is based on the premise that "users are willing to pay for sound quality." However, the Chinese music market has been free for a long time, and no one really knows where the ceiling of users’ willingness to pay is. Moreover, when soda music disrupts the situation with an 8 yuan monthly card, the resistance to price increases will be even greater.

## Himalayas: A $2.9 billion bet

In June 2025, Tencent Music announced the acquisition of Ximalaya, China's largest online audio platform, for approximately US$2.9 billion (including cash + stock consideration).

The logic of this deal is clear: music and audio (podcasts, audiobooks, paid knowledge) are two adjacent categories with highly overlapping user scenarios (commuting, exercise, bedtime). After merging you can:
- Expand the content ecology, extending from "listening to songs" to "listening to everything"
- Cross-sell members to increase ARPU (average revenue per user)
- Share technology and recommendation algorithm resources

On May 12, 2026, the transaction received antitrust approval—but it came with **5 extremely harsh restrictions**:

1. **No price increase**: The price of audio services shall not be increased within two years
2. **No exclusivity**: Content providers must not be required to sign exclusive agreements with the platform
3. **No tying**: Music and audio memberships must not be forcibly bundled.
4. **Cannot restrict anchors**: Content creators must not be restricted from publishing content on other platforms
5. **Keep free content**: A certain percentage of free listening content must be maintained

In other words, supervision approved the deal, but blocked almost all means that could produce synergies. You can’t raise prices to monetize, you can’t exclusively lock content, you can’t bundle and cross-sell—so where do the synergies from acquisitions come from?

The optimistic view is that most of these restrictions have a time window (two years), and brand integration, technology sharing, and user data access are not within the scope of restrictions. The pessimistic view is that the market environment and competitive landscape may be completely different in two years. If we wait until the restrictions expire to discuss collaboration, the day lily will be gone.

## Cash: The Most Underrated Fact

Tencent Music has approximately 38 billion yuan in cash and equivalents on its books (including cash, time deposits and short-term investments), and its net cash after deducting 3.5 billion yuan in notes payable is approximately 34.5 billion yuan. This is not "paper assets", but real money.

**34.5 billion net cash accounts for 37% of current market capitalization. **

This means that you used a market value of 93 billion to buy Tencent Music, deducting the net cash of 34.5 billion - you actually only used about 58.5 billion to buy its business. The operating activities of this business in 2025 will generate approximately **10.2 billion** in cash flow, and the free cash flow after deducting a small amount of capital expenditures (FCF, that is, the freely disposable cash generated by the company's operating activities after deducting capital expenditures) will be approximately **9.8 billion**.

58.5 billion buys a business with an annual cash flow of 9.8 billion, and the return rate is about **17%**.

The company is indeed returning cash to shareholders:
- Cumulative share repurchases **$2.5-2.6 billion**
- 2025 dividend **$368 million**

But the 38 billion in cash also begs the question: Why not buy back more? If management really feels that the stock price is seriously undervalued, the most rational thing to do is to buy back the stock in large quantities. The current repurchase efforts are not aggressive relative to cash reserves.

One explanation is that the Himalaya acquisition requires reserved funds (the total consideration is approximately US$2.9 billion, of which the cash component is US$1.26 billion, which is approximately 9.1 billion yuan). Another explanation is that management may not be as confident in the business prospects as the valuation numbers suggest.

## So, what exactly is Tencent Music?

To summarize the portrait of the company today:

- **A music platform that is transforming from live broadcast rewards to subscription services**, the transformation is 80% completed
- **Margins are at an all-time high** and still improving
- **Extremely abundant cash**, with net cash of 34.5 billion accounting for 37% of the market value, and annual free cash flow of nearly 10 billion
- **Expanding audio territory through acquisition of Himalaya**, but regulatory conditions limit short-term synergy space
- **SVIP strategy provides price increase logic that does not rely on user growth**, but the ceiling is unknown

From a fundamental perspective, this is a company with strong profitability, a healthy balance sheet, and a business transformation in the right direction.

**But the market gave it 6 times cash PE. **

Why is such a profitable company priced by the market as if something is about to happen?

The answer lies in three words: **Soda Music. **
