## Core conclusion

**Overall Rating: ★★★★ (4/5)**

AIA is the largest independently listed life insurance group in Pan-Asia, with business covering 18 markets. The full-year value of new business (VONB) in 2025 is US$5.516 billion, a record high, a year-on-year increase of 15%; operating profit after tax (OPAT) is US$7.136 billion, a year-on-year increase per share of 12%; basic free earnings are US$6.765 billion, a year-on-year increase per share of 11%. EV equity increased by 18% to US$84.384 billion, with a shareholder capital ratio of 221%, indicating strong capital strength.

**Core contradiction: Mainland China’s slowdown in growth vs. regional growth resilience. ** In 2025, mainland China's full-year NBV will only grow by 2% year-on-year. In the second half of the year, the growth rate will pick up to 14%, and the growth momentum will fluctuate. However, the NBV in the Hong Kong market increased by 28% to US$2.256 billion and performed strongly, and the NBV in Thailand increased by 35% to US$522 million. P/EV is about 1.0 times, PE is about 14 times, and the valuation is at a historically low level. It is not extremely cheap but not expensive either.

**Key judgment:** AIA is a rare leader in the pan-Asian life insurance industry, with an excellent business model, outstanding management capabilities, and strict capital return discipline. The current valuation is reasonably low and has long-term holding value. The core variables are the pace of expansion in mainland China and the evolution of Asia's low interest rate environment.

**Main risks:** Mainland China's growth rate is lower than expected, falling interest rates in various Asian markets erode investment income, the world's lowest tax rate (Pillar Two) increases the effective tax rate, and capital market fluctuations in emerging markets such as Thailand.
