# AIA: Business Model Analysis - "Good Business" Perspective

> Core question: Is AIA a good business? Can you understand it? Can it be held for a long time?

---

## 1. Is this a good business?

### Conclusion: It is a good business, and it is among the best in the life insurance industry.

Duan Yongping’s criteria for a good business: a simple business model, pricing power, high customer departure costs, and good cash flow. Use this ruler to measure AIA and score higher.

**Positive arguments:**

1. **Life insurance is a classic "collect money first and service later" model. ** AIA’s underlying free earnings in 2025 will be US$6.765 billion, a year-on-year increase of 11% per share. Premiums are collected up front and claims are paid out behind – meaning there is a lot of long-term float available for investment. AIA mainly focuses on long-term protection products, with long policy duration and stable and predictable capital costs. Unlike domestic peers that rely on bancassurance channels to offset short-term scale, AIA’s liability-side quality is an industry benchmark.

2. **The differentiation of agent channels is the core barrier. ** In 2025, the agent channel will contribute NBV of US$4.273 billion, accounting for 73%, and the NBV profit margin will be as high as 71.5%, a year-on-year increase of 3.4 percentage points. AIA has topped the list of global MDRT (Million Dollar Round Table) members for 11 consecutive years. The number of agent supervisors increased by 7%, and the number of new agents increased by 8%. This "Premier Agency" model is not simply a pile of people, but an elite route with high productivity and high retention. The industry is generally reducing the number of agents, but AIA is growing with quality - this is evidence of the differentiation of the business model.

3. **Pan-Asia layout provides natural growth diversification. ** AIA’s business covers 18 Asia-Pacific markets: Hong Kong, Mainland China, Thailand, Singapore, Malaysia, South Korea, Vietnam, India, etc. In 2025, Hong Kong NBV will increase by 28% to US$2.256 billion, Thailand NBV will increase by 35% to US$522 million, Singapore and Malaysia will increase by 15% and 9% respectively. No single market can dictate a company's fortunes—when one market weakens, others can pick up the slack. This geographical diversification is almost unique among Asian life insurance companies.

4. **Strict discipline in capital return. ** The company has set a payout ratio target of 75% of annual net free earnings (through dividends + buybacks). The final dividend in 2025 is HK144.08 cents per share, and the full-year dividend is HK193.08 cents, a year-on-year increase of 10%. At the same time, a new round of repurchase plan of US$1.7 billion was announced. Total shareholder return is about 6%. Such return discipline is quite rare for a company still growing at a rapid pace.

**Negative Arguments:**

1. **The profits of insurance companies are always affected by the investment side. ** In the first half of 2025, fluctuations in Thailand's capital market led to losses of US$754 million due to differences in short-term investments and discount rates, and changes in the fair value of currency risk management derivatives also caused losses of US$354 million. Even if AIA’s liability-side management is the best in the industry, the fluctuations on the investment side cannot be completely eliminated.

2. ** Fluctuations in mainland China’s growth rate have caused market concerns. ** Mainland China's NBV will only grow by 2% year-on-year in 2025. Although the growth rate rebounded to 14% in the second half of the year, the weakness in the first half of the year has caused the market to worry about the sustainability of AIA's largest growth engine. In a downward interest rate environment, product attractiveness and spread space face structural pressure.

3. **Insurance is not the kind of "simple" business that Duan Yongping likes best. ** Actuarial assumptions, IFRS 17 standards, embedded value calculations – the financial statements of insurance companies are much more complex than those of consumer products companies. Duan Yongping has never been heavily invested in insurance stocks, partly for this reason.

## 2. Is the business model easy to understand?

### Conclusion: The core logic is clear, but the details are complex.

**Understandable part:**

AIA's business model can be summarized in one sentence: **Sell long-term protection insurance in Asia's richest and fastest-growing market to earn underwriting profits and investment income. **

Specifically, the sources of income can be broken down into:

| Business Segments | Performance in 2025 | Core Drivers |
|---------|-----------|----------|
| Hong Kong, China | NBV US$2.256 billion, +28% | Hong Kong insurance boom + mainland customers cross-border insurance |
| Mainland China | NBV +2% for the full year, H2 accelerated +14% | Provincial expansion + demand rebounded after interest rates fell |
| Thailand | NBV US$522 million, +35% | Health insurance demand + NBV rate 115.7% |
| Singapore | NBV +15% | High net worth customers + investment-linked insurance |
| Other Asia | Malaysia +9%, positive growth in all markets | Increased penetration of emerging markets |

**Partner distribution channels (bancassurance + brokerage)** contributed NBV of US$1.593 billion, a year-on-year increase of 22%. Among them, the NBV of the bancassurance channel increased by 20%, and the NBV profit margin increased by 3.5 percentage points to 45.4%. AIA's cooperation with Bank of East Asia, Postal Savings and other banks is broadening the distribution network and reducing reliance on a single agent channel.

**The difficult part to understand:**

1. **Actuarial assumptions for embedded value (EV). ** AIA’s EV equity in 2025 is US$84.384 billion, but the calculation of EV relies on a series of actuarial assumptions such as risk discount rate, investment return rate, and mortality rate. The impact of hypothetical adjustments on EV can be large - which increases valuation uncertainty.

2. **Exchange rate impact. ** AIA is priced in US dollars, but its business revenue comes from Hong Kong dollars, RMB, Thai baht, Singapore dollars and other currencies. During the 2025 reporting period, the difference between the fixed exchange rate and the actual exchange rate has an impact on various indicators. Investors need to distinguish between "real growth" and "exchange rate effects".

3. **Revenue recognition under IFRS 17 standards. ** The new standards change the way insurance revenue is recognized. The release pace of CSM (Contract Service Margin) and changes in risk adjustment all affect the presentation of current profits.

## 3. What is the quality of free cash flow?

### Conclusion: Cash generation capability is one of AIA’s most prominent strengths

**Positive indicators:**

1. **Net free surplus (Net FSG) is a key measure of an insurance company’s cash generation. ** Net FSG in 2025 is US$4.451 billion, a year-on-year increase of 14% per share. This means that after deducting new business investment and capital requirements, the company still generates a large amount of distributable cash.

2. **Basic free surplus (UFSG) was US$6.765 billion, a year-on-year increase of 11% per share. ** This indicator measures the cash continuously released by existing businesses - equivalent to "money that can be earned without doing new business." AIA's ability to release cash from existing businesses continues to increase.

3. **The continued growth of dividends + buybacks is the hardest evidence of cash flow. ** In 2025, shareholder dividends of US$2.427 billion + share repurchases of US$2.279 billion = total return of US$4.706 billion. A company that can continue to increase shareholder returns will not have poor cash flow quality. The company has clearly committed to a 75% net FSG payout ratio target, and management has fulfilled it for many consecutive years.

**Negative Indicators:**

1. **New business investment consumes capital. ** VONB of US$5.516 billion means that AIA is actively expanding. The costs of acquiring new business (commissions, marketing, etc.) consume cash in the current period, but the value created will be released in the future. This is a normal characteristic of insurance companies in the growth stage, but if growth slows down, the input-output ratio will decline.

2. **The impact of Pillar Two’s global lowest tax rate. ** The world's lowest tax rate, which takes effect from 2025, will have a negative impact on AIA's effective tax rate and may cut into some of its free cash flow. The specific range remains to be verified in subsequent financial reports.

## 4. Pricing power and customer stickiness

### Conclusion: We have pricing power on guaranteed products and strong customer stickiness.

**Evidence of Pricing Power:**

1. **NBV profit margin continues to increase. ** In 2025, the group's NBV Margin will reach 58.5%, a year-on-year increase of 4 percentage points. The NBV Margin of agent channels is as high as 71.5%. While premiums are increasing, profit margins are also increasing - this shows that AIA is not growing by "cutting prices in exchange for volume", but by selling higher value products.

2. **The product structure is tilted towards high value. ** AIA Hong Kong benefits from the participating insurance investment return cap policy (implemented in July 2025), because the displayed interest rates of most of its products are already within the scope of the new regulations, and competitors are forced to adjust product designs. This is a reflection of product pricing leadership.

**Evidence of customer stickiness:**

1. **Insurance products naturally have high switching costs. ** Once a long-term life insurance policy is purchased, surrender means the loss of premiums paid, and customers rarely change insurance companies on their own initiative. AIA mainly focuses on long-term protection products, with long policy duration and strong customer lock-in effect.

2. **Brand trust is crucial in the insurance industry. ** In the industry of "selling trust", AIA, as one of the most well-known life insurance brands in Asia, has a history of more than a century. Especially in the markets of Hong Kong, China and Southeast Asia, the brand recognition of "AIA" is a core intangible asset.

**But there are limitations:**

AIA’s pricing power is not unlimited. In an environment where interest rates continue to fall in mainland China, the predetermined interest rates of insurance products need to follow regulatory adjustments, and the spread space may be compressed. AIA only covers 12 provinces in the mainland (by the end of 2025). Compared with the national networks of China Life and Ping An, the channel scale is a hard constraint.

## 5. General comments from Duan Yongping’s perspective

### Business model rating: ★★★★ (4/5)

**Core judgment: AIA is a rare "good business" in the insurance industry. **

AIA has outstanding performance in the following aspects: differentiation of agent channels, continuous improvement of NBV Margin, diversified pan-Asia layout, and strict capital return discipline. It is not the kind of minimalist model that "anyone can make money by managing it" (such as Moutai), but in the insurance industry, the quality of AIA's business model is already top-notch.

**Reasons for deducting points:** First, insurance companies are naturally more complex and difficult to fully "understand". Second, fluctuations on the investment side cannot be eliminated, as evidenced by the losses in the Thai market in 2025. Third, the uncertainty of mainland China’s growth rate – this is AIA’s biggest growth story, but it is also the biggest variable.

> **Duan Yongping's possible opinion:** "AIA has indeed achieved differentiation in the insurance industry. The quality of its agent channels and the discipline of management are worthy of respect. But insurance is not my favorite kind of business. It is too complicated and the uncertainty on the investment side is too great. If you want to buy insurance stocks, AIA may be the best choice in Asia, but I will wait for a better price."
