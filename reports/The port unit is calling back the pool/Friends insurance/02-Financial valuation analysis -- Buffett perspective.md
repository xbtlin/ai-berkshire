## 2. Analysis of key financial ratios

### Profitability

| Indicators | Values | Evaluation |
|------|------|------|
| ROEV (EV operating return) | 15.8% | Industry-leading, indicating high efficiency in adding value to embedded value |
| Operating return on equity distributed to shareholders | 15.5% (new high) | Capital efficiency continues to improve |
| NBV Margin | 58.5% | Top in the industry, with agent channels as high as 71.5% |
| Agency channel NBV proportion | 73% | High profit margin channels dominate |

**Positive review:** AIA’s profitability index ranks first among global life insurance companies. An ROEV of 15.8% means that the annual new business and existing business together contribute 15.8% of the return to the embedded value - this is the core indicator for measuring the long-term value creation of life insurance companies. The NBV Margin of 58.5% indicates that nearly 60 cents of every dollar of premium sold is converted into value - this is a comprehensive reflection of product pricing power and channel efficiency.

**Negatives:** ROEV 15.8% includes the impact of some changes in economic assumptions. If the divestiture assumption is adjusted, the core operating ROEV will be about 13-14%, which is still excellent but not as impressive as the surface number. In addition, OPAT's growth rate (+12%/share) is faster than that of UFSG (+11%/share), indicating that part of the profit growth comes from accounting recognition rather than cash creation.

### Capital adequacy and safety margin

| Indicators | Values | Evaluation |
|------|------|------|
| Shareholders’ capital ratio | 221% | Far exceeding regulatory requirements, capital is very sufficient |
| EV Equity/Total Market Capitalization | ~1.0x | P/EV is about 1.0x, historically low |
| Net FSG/dividend + buyback | ~0.95 times | Full coverage of dividend buyback, sustainable payment |

**When Buffett looks at insurance companies, he is most concerned about two things: the cost of float and the safety of capital. ** AIA's shareholder capital ratio of 221% indicates that the company still has sufficient capital buffers even if it encounters a large market shock. At the same time, the net FSG of US$4.451 billion basically matches the shareholder return (dividends + buybacks) of US$4.706 billion, indicating that the current return policy can be maintained in the long term.

## 3. Valuation analysis

### Current valuation level

Based on 2025 annual report data and current market price (approximately HK$80, as of May 2026):

| Valuation Metrics | Value | Historical Median | Evaluation |
|---------|------|---------|------|
| P/EV | ~1.0x | 1.2-1.5x | Lower than historical median, but not extreme |
| PE (based on OPAT) | ~14x | 15-20x | Reasonably low |
| Dividend yield | ~2.5% | 1.5-2.0% | Slightly higher than historical average |
| Total shareholder return | ~6% | — | Dividend + buyback combined return |

### Core thinking of P/EV valuation

**P/EV is the most important valuation anchor for life insurance companies. ** 1.0x P/EV means the market is pricing AIA at book embedded value - neither a premium nor a discount.

**Why can we give a premium? **

1. VONB’s rapid growth (+15%) means that future EV growth is guaranteed
2. ROEV 15.8% shows that EV itself continues to increase in value
3. Management rewards shareholders in a disciplined manner (75% payout rate + buyback)

**Why is the market reluctant to give a premium? **

1. Hidden concerns about China’s slowdown in growth
2. Asia’s low interest rate environment may erode investment returns
3. The uncertain impact of Pillar Two’s global minimum tax rate
4. The overall valuation of the Hong Kong stock market is under pressure

**Reasonable valuation range:** Based on AIA’s growth quality and capital returns, a reasonable P/EV should be 1.0-1.5 times. The current 1.0x is at the lower edge of the range. If mainland growth accelerates again (it has exceeded 20% growth in the first two months of 2026), P/EV is expected to recover to 1.2-1.3x.

### Cross-validation of PE valuation

14 times PE (based on OPAT) is not expensive among high-quality life insurance companies in the world. For comparison:

| Company | PE valuation | P/EV | Growth rate |
|------|--------|------|---------|
| AIA | ~14x | ~1.0x | VONB +15% |
| Ping An of China (Hong Kong stocks) | ~5-9 times | ~0.6 times | NBV +29% |
| Prudential Group | ~8-10 times | ~0.6-0.7 times | NBV double-digit growth |

AIA's valuation premium reflects higher growth quality (NBV Margin of 58.5% far exceeds peers) and a purer life insurance business model. Ping An's low valuation comes in part from the drag of integrated financial complexities and real estate exposure -- which friends don't have.

## 5. Safety Margin Assessment

### Buffett’s Margin of Safety Framework

| Dimensions | Assessment | Conclusion |
|------|------|------|
| Asset quality | EV equity real asset support, 221% capital ratio | ★★★★ |
| Profit security | OPAT and UFSG continue to grow, covering dividends + buybacks | ★★★★ |
| Valuation safety | P/EV 1.0 times is not extremely cheap, but there is room for upside | ★★★ |
| Growth is safe | VONB +15%, but the growth rate in the mainland fluctuates | ★★★ |

**Comprehensive safety margin: above average. ** AIA's current valuation does not have the opportunity for "extreme discount" like Ping An's. 1.0x P/EV is a "reasonable" price, not a "discounted" price. The margin of safety mainly comes from: (1) 6% total shareholder return provides downside protection; (2) VONB's continued growth will promote EV value appreciation; (3) If mainland growth accelerates, there is room for valuation repair.

**Risk scenario:** If interest rates in Asia continue to decline, mainland expansion is blocked, and VONB growth falls to single digits, P/EV may drop to 0.8 times, corresponding to a stock price drop of about 20%. But even so, a 6% shareholder return can still cover most of the paper losses in 2-3 years.

## 6. General review from the perspective of value investing

### Financial Valuation Rating: ★★★★ (4/5)

**Core judgment: AIA's financial quality is excellent and its valuation is reasonably low, but this is not an opportunity to "pick up bargains." **

AIA's financial performance has no obvious shortcomings. VONB has reached a new high, NBV Margin continues to increase, capital is sufficient, and dividends have grown steadily. The only regret is that the valuation is not extremely low - 1.0 times P/EV is a "fair price" rather than a "discount price".

> **What Buffett may think:** "AIA is one of the best Asian life insurance companies I have seen. Its agent channel is like GEICO - low-cost, high-efficiency, and consistently generating profits. The capital management discipline also reassures me. My only hesitation is the price: 1x P/EV means I paid the full embedded value and received no discount. I would prefer to sell at 0.8x P/EV. But if it can maintain 15% VONB growth, even buying at the current price, the long-term returns will be considerable."
