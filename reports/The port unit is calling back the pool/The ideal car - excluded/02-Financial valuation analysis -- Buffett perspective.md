# Li Auto (2015.HK) Financial Valuation Analysis

> Analysis dimensions: profitability, capital efficiency, free cash flow, margin of safety
> Study date: May 16, 2026

## 1. Panorama of financial fundamentals

### 1.1 Revenue and Profit Trends

| Year | Revenue (100 million yuan) | Year-on-year growth rate | Net profit (100 million yuan) | Net profit rate |
|------|-------------|---------|--------------|--------|
| 2022 | 452 | +67% | -20 | -4.4% |
| 2023 | 1,239 | +174% | 118 | 9.5% |
| 2024 | 1,445 | +17% | ~80 | ~5.5% |
| 2025 | 1,123 | -22% | 11.39 | 1.0% |

This set of data presents a typical "growth stock trap": after a surge in 2023, the growth rate slowed down sharply in 2024, and negative growth occurred in 2025. Net profit plummeted from 11.8 billion to 1.1 billion, which is alarming.

### 1.2 Quarterly profit fluctuations

Performance across quarters in 2025 is highly uneven:
- Q1: Profitable (benefiting from L series sales)
- Q3: Loss of 624 million yuan (ending 11 consecutive quarters of profit)
- Q4: Revenue of 28.8 billion, profit is meager

Such large fluctuations between quarters indicate that the company's profitability is extremely unstable and lacks the certainty of "sustainably making money".

### 1.3 Gross profit margin analysis

| Indicators | 2024 | 2025 |
|------|--------|--------|
| Comprehensive gross profit margin | 20.5% | 18.7% |
| Vehicle gross profit margin | 21.5% | 17.9% |

Vehicle gross profit margin fell from 21.5% to 17.9% due to:
- Increased discounts for L series terminals
- The pure electric i series "trades price for volume", lowering the average price
- The average price of a bicycle dropped from 278,000 to 250,000

The "moat" that Buffett values ​​is reflected in the stability of gross profit margins. Ideal's gross profit margin fell by 3.6 percentage points within one year, indicating that its pricing power is being eroded by competition.

## 2. Capital efficiency and cash flow

### 2.1 Capital Allocation

The ideal balance sheet as of the end of 2025:
- Cash and equivalents: 101.2 billion yuan
- Total liabilities: The data is yet to be confirmed, but the asset-liability ratio is relatively healthy
- R&D investment in 2025: 11.3 billion yuan (accounting for 10.1% of revenue)
-Capital expenditure in 2025: estimated to be 6-8 billion yuan (factories, supercharging stations)

**Key Question: Opportunity Cost of $101.2 Billion in Cash**

It holds more than 100 billion yuan in cash and has an annual interest income of about 3-4 billion yuan. But if the return on investment (ROIC) of this cash is lower than the weighted average cost of capital (WACC), it is actually "consuming" shareholder value.

### 2.2 Return on Capital (ROIC)

Rough estimate:
- After-tax operating profit in 2025 ≈ 1.5-2 billion yuan
- Invested capital (equity + interest-bearing liabilities - cash) ≈ 30-40 billion yuan
- ROIC ≈ 4-6%

This rate of return barely covers the cost of capital (WACC for new energy vehicle companies is usually 8-12%). It is far from the "15% + ROIC for excellent companies" required by Buffett.

### 2.3 Free cash flow

A structural problem in the auto manufacturing industry is: **capital spending will never stop**.

Ideally each year requires:
- Invest 10 billion+ in R&D (maintaining intelligent competitiveness)
- Investment in production line is RMB 3 billion to RMB 5 billion (new pure electric models)
- Supercharging network construction of RMB 2-3 billion (4077 supercharging stations are still being expanded)
- Retail network maintenance (511 stores)

Even if revenue exceeds RMB 100 billion, high ongoing investment means free cash flow may be zero or even negative. This is completely inconsistent with Buffett's "cash cow" investment criteria.

## 3. Valuation analysis

### 3.1 Current Valuation Level

As of May 2026:
- Stock price: approximately HK$70
- Market value: approximately HKD 134 billion (approximately RMB 120 billion)
- PE (TTM, based on 2025 profit of 1.139 billion): approximately 105 times
- PE (based on a more optimistic caliber): 25-30 times

**Explanation of the huge difference in PE:** The market's ideal valuation depends entirely on expectations of future profits, not current profits. If calculated based on actual profits in 2025, a PE of 100+ times is obviously extremely expensive; if the market expects profits to return to 5-8 billion in 2026-2027, then a forward-looking PE of 15-25 times seems reasonable.

### 3.2 Valuation comparison with peers

| Company | Market value (100 million yuan) | Delivery volume in 2025 | PE (TTM) |
|------|----------------|-------------|-----------|
| BYD | ~9,000 | 4.25 million+ | ~25 times |
| Li Auto | ~1,200 | 406,000 | ~105 times |
| NIO | ~1,000 | ~220,000 | Loss |
| Xiaopeng | ~800 | ~190,000 | Loss |

BYD trades at 25x PE, delivers an ideal 10x, and has higher and more stable profit margins. From the perspective of "what should be bought with the same amount of money", the ideal valuation premium needs to be supported by extremely high growth expectations.

### 3.3 DCF Valuation Framework

**Optimistic Assumption (Scenario A):**
- Delivery volume from 2026 to 2028: 480,000 → 650,000 → 800,000
- Net interest rate gradually recovers to 4-5%
- Net profit in 2028: 6-8 billion yuan
- Give 20 times PE → Reasonable market value 120-160 billion

**Neutral Assumption (Scenario B):**
- Delivery volume from 2026 to 2028: 450,000 → 550,000 → 650,000
- Net profit margin maintained at 2-3% (price war continues)
- Net profit in 2028: 3 billion to 5 billion yuan
- Give 15 times PE → Reasonable market value 45-75 billion

**Pessimistic Assumption (Scenario C):**
- Pure electric transformation continues to burn money, and range extension share continues to be lost
- Continuous small profits or losses in 2026-2027
- The market gives PS (price-to-sales ratio) valuation → 0.5-1x → market value 60-100 billion

The current market capitalization is RMB 120 billion, which is at the lower limit of optimistic assumptions. The market's implicit expectation is that Ideal can return to an annual profit of 5 billion+ within 2-3 years. This expectation is not impossible, but the certainty is not high.

## 4. Buffett-style "Circle of Competence" Test

### 4.1 Can I understand this business?

Buffett never invests in auto stocks, and the reason is very clear:
- High capital intensity
- Rapid technology iteration
- Low consumer brand loyalty
- Competition is extremely fierce
- Union and regulatory pressure

Although Li Auto has no labor union issues in the Chinese market, it has many other "bad business" characteristics. From Buffett's perspective, this is not within the circle of competence.

### 4.2 Moat inspection

Buffett’s four types of moats:

| Moat type | Whether the ideal is present | Assessment |
|-----------|-------------|------|
| Brand premium | ❌ | The terminal price reduction of 70,000+ indicates that the brand premium is limited |
| Switching cost | ❌ | The user replacement cycle is 3-5 years, no lock-in effect |
| Network effects | ❌ | Does not exist |
| Cost advantage | ❌ | Not as vertically integrated as BYD |

Ideal currently does not have a moat in any classic sense. What it has are "temporary competitive advantages" - product definition capabilities and range-extending first-mover advantages, but both are being rapidly caught up by competitors.

### 4.3 Management’s capital allocation capability

Buffett values the CEO's capital allocation ability most. How is Li Xiang's performance?

**Bonus points:**
- 101.2 billion cash reserves, good financial discipline, no blind expansion
- Efficient operations in 2023-2024 demonstrate cost control capabilities
- Stop losses on MEGA promptly and do not continue to invest in failed products

**Minus points:**
- MEGA invested billions in research and development, but ultimately delivered only 18,500 vehicles per year
- The i8 pricing strategy was wrong and the price was reduced as soon as it was launched.
- Announcing the transformation to "embodied intelligence", the malleability of the circle of competence has not been proven yet

## 5. Safety Margin Assessment

### 5.1 Downstream protection

The ideal "safety pad":
- 101.2 billion in cash = approximately RMB 47 per share (approximately HKD 51)
- The current stock price is HK$70. After deducting cash, the actual payment for the automobile business is only about HK$19/share.
- Based on this calculation, the market values Li Auto’s business at only about HK$40 billion

This means that if Ideal ceases all business, lays off all employees, and distributes 101.2 billion in cash to shareholders, investors can get back about 72% of the stock price. This is relatively good "downside protection".

### 5.2 But is the margin of safety enough?

The problem is:
- All cash will not be returned to shareholders (management will use it to develop AI and embodied intelligence)
- If the automotive business continues to lose money, it will consume cash reserves
- Ideal has no tradition of dividends and no large-scale buyback plan

Buffett said, "I would rather buy a good company at a reasonable price than a mediocre company at a cheap price." Ideal may be inexpensive at its current price, but it is far from a "good company" (in terms of quality of business).

## 7. Summary of Buffett’s Perspective

> If Buffett looks at Li Auto, he will most likely say: "This is a company run by smart people. It has temporarily made a little money in an industry where it is difficult to make money. But I am not sure whether its competitive position will be stronger or weaker in five years than it is today."

**Core Contradiction:** Ideal has an extremely abundant amount of cash (101.2 billion), but this money is being invested in highly uncertain directions (pure electric transformation, AI chips, embodied intelligence). If the investment pays off, Ideal could become a great company; if not, the money will be slowly burned.

**Advice to ordinary investors:** Unless you have a deep understanding of the new energy vehicle industry and are willing to bear high volatility, Ideal is not a "buy and hold for ten years with peace of mind" target. It is more suitable for investors with industry knowledge as a stage investment rather than a Buffett-style permanent holding.
