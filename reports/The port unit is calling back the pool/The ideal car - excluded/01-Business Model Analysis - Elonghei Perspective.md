# Li Auto (2015.HK) Business Model Analysis

> Analysis dimensions: user value, product definition, business essence, long-termism
> Study date: May 16, 2026

---

## 1. The essence of business model

### 1.1 What did Ideal do right?

The start of Li Auto can be called a textbook "user value orientation" - Li Xiang accurately identified the core needs of Chinese family users for large-space SUVs:

- **Solution to mileage anxiety**: The extended-range architecture is an "imperfect but practical" solution that gives users the lowest threshold for use during the transition period when pure electric infrastructure is imperfect.
- **Family scene definition**: Refrigerators, color TVs, and large sofas are not gimmicks, but a deep understanding of the "first car/second car in the family" scene
- **Price with accurate card position**: L7/L8/L9 cover the family SUV market of 250,000-500,000 yuan, and L6 is lowered to 200,000 yuan level

From the perspective of Duan Yongping's "duty", what Ideal did in the early days was to "think clearly about user needs before taking action", rather than building a car first and then finding users. This is highly consistent with OPPO/vivo’s product philosophy.

### 1.2 Core logic of business model

The ideal business model can be summarized as: **Accurate product definition + efficient operation = achieving economies of scale with limited SKUs**.

Compared to competitors:
- Weilai relies on services and communities, with many models but a small scale of bicycles
-Xpeng relies on technology labels, but its product definition is wavering
- Ideal relies on "less is more", 4 L series vehicles cover the full price range of family SUVs

This allowed Ideal to achieve amazing operational efficiency from 2023 to 2024: when more than 130,000 vehicles were delivered in a single quarter, the operating profit margin once reached more than 8%, far exceeding that of its peers.

### 1.3 Income structure

Throughout 2025:
- Vehicle sales revenue: 106.7 billion yuan (accounting for 95%)
- Other income (charging, accessories, services): approximately 5.6 billion yuan (accounting for 5%)

Revenue is highly dependent on vehicle sales, and monetization of services and software has not yet reached scale. This is a typical "hardware company" model, not a "platform company" or "software company".

## 2. User value analysis

### 2.1 User portrait and demand insight

The ideal core users are:
- Families with children in first- and second-tier cities
- Annual income 300,000-1 million
- Need a large-space SUV that can accommodate commuting and long-distance travel
- Have a certain pursuit of a sense of technology, but not a geek

The core appeal of this group is to "enjoy the benefits of electrification without anxiety" - quietness, good acceleration, smart cockpit, and no need to worry about charging. The range extender perfectly matches this need.

### 2.2 User reputation and repurchase

The ideal NPS (Net Promoter Score) ranks among the best among new forces for a long time, and has a high proportion of referrals from old car owners. But there are some changes in 2025:

**Positive Signs:**
-L series user satisfaction continues to be high
- Product strength in the home scene is still the industry benchmark
- OTA upgrades maintain a high frequency

**Negative Signs:**
- MEGA’s reputation collapsed after its launch, exposing mistakes in product definition
- The initial sales of the i8 pure electric SUV were lower than expected, and the pricing strategy was questioned
- L series terminal discounts are increased (up to over 70,000 yuan), and the brand value is diluted

### 2.3 "Sustainability" issue of product definition capabilities

Duan Yongping said, "Dare to be the queen of the world, and those who are behind will strive to be the first." The ideal problem is: **Product definition ability is highly dependent on Li Xiang's personal market intuition**.

- L series success = Li Xiang’s deep understanding of the family SUV market
- MEGA failure = Li Xiang’s misjudgment of the MPV market (the demand for high-end pure electric MPVs is far less than expected)
- i8’s setback = misjudgment of the pace of the pure electric SUV market

When the scale of an enterprise increases from annual delivery of 100,000 vehicles to 500,000 vehicles, the complexity of managing the product matrix increases exponentially. Relying on one's intuition is extremely risky.

## 3. "Quality" of business model

### 3.1 Profitability Assessment ★★☆☆☆

The 2025 data reveals a cruel reality:

| Indicators | 2024 | 2025 |
|------|--------|--------|
| Revenue | 144.5 billion | 112.3 billion |
| Net profit | ~8 billion | 1.139 billion |
| Net profit margin | ~5.5% | ~1.0% |
| Delivery volume | 500,000+ | 406,000 |

The net profit margin plummeted from 5.5% to 1%, indicating that ideal profitability is extremely fragile - if sales fall by 20%, profits will evaporate by 85%. This is a typical manufacturing characteristic of "high fixed costs, low profit margins" and does not have the attributes of a "money printing machine" as Duan Yongping calls it.

### 3.2 Pricing power evaluation ★★☆☆☆

Ideal will be forced to join the price war in 2025:
- L series terminal discounts up to 70,000 yuan
-The average price of bicycles dropped from 278,000 to 250,000
- Pure electric i series "price-for-volume" strategy

A brand that truly has pricing power should not be forced to cut prices significantly when competition intensifies. This shows that the Ideal brand premium is limited, and users choose Ideal more because of "cost-effectiveness" than "brand belief."

### 3.3 Predictability Assessment ★★☆☆☆

The predictability of the new energy vehicle industry is naturally low:
- Rapid iteration of technical routes (extended range vs pure electric vs plug-in hybrid)
- The competitive landscape changes every three years (from zero to monthly sales of 50,000)
- Policy subsidies can be adjusted at any time
- Rapid changes in consumer preferences

Ideal's revenue comes almost 100% from vehicle sales in the China market, without the buffer of geographic dispersion and revenue diversification.

## 4. Long-term perspective

### 4.1 "Doing the right thing" vs "doing things right"

Duan Yongping emphasized that "doing the right thing" is much more important than "doing things correctly". The core strategic choices Ideal currently faces:

**Do the right thing:**
- The program extension program has indeed solved real user needs during the transition period
- Focus on the family SUV track, don’t be greedy for too much
- R&D investment of 11.3 billion, accounting for 10% of revenue, to maintain technology investment

**Questionable direction:**
- Announcing the transformation into an "embodied intelligence" enterprise - does it deviate from the main automotive business?
- Self-developed chips (Mach 100) - Are resources dispersed?
- The goal has changed from "making good cars" to "a terminal enterprise in the AI era" - the vision is grand but the path to implementation is unclear.

### 4.2 Where will your ideal be in ten years?

This is the core issue of Duan Yongping’s thinking. Frankly, the answer is highly uncertain:

**Optimistic scenario:** With a cash reserve of 101.2 billion, Lideal has successfully completed the pure electric transformation, established a firm foothold in the 300,000-500,000 family SUV market, delivered 800,000-1 million vehicles annually, and the net profit margin has returned to 5%+, becoming the Chinese version of "Electric Toyota".

**Pessimistic Scenario:** The pure electric transformation continues to burn money, the extended-range market share is eroded by BYD/Wenjie, and the price war leads to a continued decline in gross profit margin. It will eventually become a second-tier car company that delivers 300,000-400,000 vehicles a year and makes a small profit or loses money.

**Key variable:** Can the success of the L series be replicated on pure electric products such as i6/i7? It is a positive sign that the cumulative orders for the i series will exceed 100,000 in 2025, but whether the monthly delivery volume can be stabilized at 20,000-30,000 units remains to be verified.

## 6. Summary of Duan Yongping’s Perspective

> "Good company" does not equal "good business". Ideal is a company with strong product capabilities, but the industry it is in (automobile manufacturing) is naturally not a good business - heavy assets, low profit margins, fierce competition, and rapid technology iteration. Even the best car company (Toyota) has a long-term net profit margin of only 5-8%. Ideal's current net profit margin is only 1%, which is far from proving that it can continue to make money in the face of fierce competition.

From the perspective of "duty", Li Xiang has a very good quality - he is willing to admit mistakes and correct them quickly (reflection after MEGA's failure, organizational structure returning to entrepreneurial mode). However, the automotive industry has very little room for error. Failure of a product may waste 2-3 years and billions of funds.

**Core conclusion:** The ideal product definition ability is first-rate, but the "quality" of the business model is not high. Unless you have extremely high trust in Li Xiang personally and believe that he can prove himself again in the pure electric transformation, this is not an investment that makes people "sleep well".
