## 2. Investment thesis: one sentence summary

**China Shenhua is a "high-interest treasury bond" - as the coal industry gradually shrinks, as the ultimate survivor with the lowest cost, it will continue to reward shareholders through high dividends until the industry completes its historical mission. **

This is not a "doubling" story, but a story of "stably receiving 4-5% dividends every year, with a high probability of not losing the principal."

## 3. Core findings

### 3.1 The part correctly priced by the market

- **"bond-like" attributes of the coal industry**: PE 12-13 times, dividend rate 4.9%, the market has priced Shenhua as a "sustainable dividend fund".
- **Stability of the integrated business model**: The coal and electricity hedging mechanism makes profit fluctuations much smaller than those of pure coal companies, and this advantage has been reflected in the valuation premium.
- **Reliability of dividends from central enterprises**: 79% dividend ratio + 65% minimum commitment + net cash status. The safety of dividends has been fully recognized by the market.

### 3.2 Parts that may be undervalued by the market

- **Mid-term synergies from the 100 billion asset restructuring**: After the restructuring, coal production capacity increased by 57% and power installed capacity increased by 28%. After integration and digestion (expected in 2027-2028), the scale effect may push ROE back to more than 13%. The current market may be overly focused on short-term consolidation pains.
- **"Final Winner" Logic**: Industry contraction is often good for the leader. As small and medium-sized coal companies exit, Shenhua's market share will naturally grow. The current market share of 11% may increase to 15-20% in the next 10 years.
- **The "hidden value" of coal reserves**: 68.5 billion tons of resources and 34.5 billion tons of recoverable reserves. According to any reasonable unit valuation, it far exceeds the current market value. These reserves are "invisible safety cushions."

### 3.3 Parts that may be overvalued by the market

- **Absolute expectations of dividend sustainability**: The 79% dividend ratio is "excess dividends" and is not the norm. If profits continue to decline, the total amount of dividends will decline, even if the dividend ratio remains high.
- **Perfect Hedging Hypothesis for Coal and Power Integration**: The power business can only partially hedge against the decline in coal prices, but cannot completely offset it. When coal prices fall sharply (for example, by more than 20%), overall profits will still decline significantly.
- **Positive imagination of the reform of central enterprises**: The market's expectations for the reform of central enterprises, market value management and other topics have limited room for actual implementation on Shenhua.

## 4. Key variables and scenario analysis

### 4.1 Three key variables that determine return on investment

**Variable 1: Coal price trend**
- For every 100 yuan/ton change in the average thermal coal price, Shenhua’s net profit changes by approximately 20 billion yuan (after accounting for coal and electricity hedging)
- The current Changxie coal price is about 700 yuan/ton. If it falls to 600 yuan/ton, the net profit will be about 40 billion yuan; if it falls to 500 yuan/ton, the net profit will be about 30 billion yuan.

**Variable 2: Asset restructuring and integration effect**
- Optimistic scenario: The injected assets will reach the profitability level of Shenhua’s existing assets within 2 years, and ROE will rise to 13%+
- Baseline scenario: 3-4 years of integration, some inefficient assets drag down ROE to 11-12%
- Pessimistic scenario: Integration is difficult, inefficient assets continue to be a drag, and ROE drops below 10%

**Variable three: Energy transition speed**
- Slow transformation: coal consumption will not peak until 2030, and will fall by 1% annually thereafter
- Baseline transformation: Coal consumption peaks in 2028, and then falls by 2% annually
- Rapid transformation: Coal consumption has peaked and will fall by 3-5% annually thereafter

## 8. Final conclusion

### One sentence summary

China Shenhua is the "ultimate winner" in the coal industry, but the meaning of "winner" is not that the stock price doubles, but "the last one standing and continuing to pay dividends to the end in the process of the industry's slow shrinkage."

### Investment value judgment

**The current valuation (PE 12-13 times, dividend yield 4.9%) is in the "reasonable" range-neither cheap enough to be excited, nor expensive enough to avoid. **

For investors who need stable cash returns, Shenhua is one of the most reliable dividend stocks in the Hong Kong stock market. A dividend ratio of 79%, an asset-liability ratio of 23%, and an operating cash flow of 75 billion yuan—these three figures form the "iron triangle" of dividend safety.

For investors seeking excess returns, Shenhua's appeal is limited. 12-13 times PE means that even if there is zero performance growth, the annualized return of buying and holding is only 4-5% of the dividend rate plus possible valuation repair (or valuation compression). In an industry that is certain to shrink, there is not much room for upward repair in valuations.

### Core Contradiction

There is a fundamental contradiction in Shenhua: **It is the best coal company, but coal is an industry that is coming to an end**.

Buying Shenhua is essentially betting that "the end will come slower than the market expects." If coal consumption peaks later and declines more slowly than expected, then a 4.9% dividend yield + possible valuation repair = a decent return. If the energy transition accelerates and demand for coal shrinks rapidly, even the best coal companies will not be immune to falling share prices.

### Recommended position positioning

If you want to include it in your investment portfolio, it is recommended to position it as a "bottom defensive" position, accounting for 5-10% of the total position. The time to buy is to wait for Hong Kong stock PE to fall back to 10-11 times (corresponding to a dividend rate of 5.5-6%), providing a more adequate margin of safety. It is not recommended as a core holding or heavy position target - in the context of energy transformation, allocating too much funds in the coal industry is not in line with the direction of long-term asset allocation.
