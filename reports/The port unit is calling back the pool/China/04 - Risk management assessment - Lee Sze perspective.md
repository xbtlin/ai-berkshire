# I. Risk panorama scan: sorted by lethal force

# Risk I: Long-term structural risks of energy transformation

** Risk description**: China’s carbon median and target for 2060 means that coal must have a significant decline in its share of the energy structure. Coal consumption is expected to peak around 2028, then enter a long-term slow decline route. This is not a “can happen” risk, but a “can happen” trend.

**Quantified impact**:
- Currently, China consumes approximately 4.7 billion tons/year of coal
- Some 4.4-46 billion tons (- 2 per cent to 6 per cent) are projected for 2030
- Some 3.5 to 40 billion tons (-15 to 25 per cent) are projected for 2035
- Some 1.5 to 2.5 billion tons (-47 to-68 per cent) are projected for 2050

** Impact on the gods**: Short term (in five years) has limited impact and coal demand is almost equal.

** Deductive factors**:
- God is the lowest-cost coal company, and the industry is finally eliminated when it shrinks.
- Coal power integration can affect the vortex.
- The assets reorganization of hundreds of millions of dollars has increased the share of stock cakes.
- Management has begun to deploy new energy sources (but on a small scale)

** Core judgement**: This is the biggest long-term risk and the most identified risk. But the time horizon (20-30 years) gives companies sufficient time to adjust. For medium-term (5-10 years), this risk is more a "value-stressed" factor than a "survival threat".

# Risk two: Coal prices fall periodically

** Risk description**: Coal prices have been falling steadily since the end of the 2022 coal supercycle. Average power coal prices in 2025 were about 700 yuan per tonne, down by about 30 per cent from the 2022 high. Combined coal prices continued to fall by 3.8 to 487 yuan per tonne in the first quarter of 2026.

**Quantitative impact**: The profits from the Sino-Chinese coal business will be reduced by about $30-35 billion per 100 ton of coal prices. But the profits from the power business will increase by $10-15 billion as fuel costs fall, with a net impact of about $20 billion.

** Deductive factors**:
- The JCJ coal mechanism locks around 87% of sales in the "reasonable price zone."
- The policy base (570 ton) supports the price of coal
- The supply-side reform of the capacity constraints has limited the price collapse.
- Natural hedges for coal-fired electricity integration

** Core judgement**: The fall in coal prices is a cyclical risk, not a structural one. With the cost of self-produced coal at the divine $179/t, even if the price drops to $500/t, there is still a very high profit. The risk effect is "how much money" instead of "how much money is earned."

# Risk 3: Integration risk of asset restructuring of 100 billion

** Risk description**: 13.36 billion yuan in asset restructuring completed in 2025-2026, covering 13 enterprises covering a number of areas such as coal, electricity, chemicals, shipping, etc. The management levels, safety standards, profitability of these enterprises are uneven.

** Specific risk points**:
- The historical underprofitness of some injections of assets (e.g., UGC, CHE) may reduce the overall ROE
- 13 enterprises require 2-3 years for personnel, culture and system integration
- Reorganization has resulted in a dilution of the rights and interests of former shareholders (approximately 15-20 per cent)
- $20 billion in matching finance increases financial costs

** Deductive factors**:
- The profitability of core assets such as domestic power
- 57 per cent increase in coal production after reorganization, with a scale effect that will gradually be reflected
- Reorganization of the company with policy and administrative support, which makes integration less difficult than market-based M & As
- Management has extensive experience in operating mega-coal enterprises

** Core judgement**: In the short term (1-2 years) integration pains are inevitable, ROE may be under pressure. But in the medium term (3-5 years) the reorganization will be a net positive event when the advantage of scale is released. The key variable is the ratio and speed of improvement in the injection of inefficient assets into the asset.

# Risk IV: Policy and Regulatory Risks #

** Risk description**: The coal industry is highly influenced by policies - production approval, safety checks, environmental standards, long-term agreement prices, carbon emissions control, etc. - each policy change may affect the company's operations.

** Specific risk points**:
- The JCLU coal pricing policy may further push down the JCCP prices
- The possible increase in operating costs due to the expansion of the carbon market (current carbon price 80-100/tCO2)
- Whole industry lockout after a security accident
- Local government taxation and social responsibility requirements for coal companies

** Deductive factors**:
- As an elite, there's a voice in policy making.
- Energy Security State policy protects the bottom line of the coal industry
- Carbon market is moving at a prudent pace, with limited short-term shocks

** Core judgement**: Policy risk is a "sustained but manageable" risk. As an elite, the Senghua is both a policy implementer and a policy influencer. The greatest policy risk is that the price of coal is further reduced by the LCCP, which is expected to be within 5-10%.

# Risk five: safe production risk # (middle and low)

** Risk description**: Safety accidents in coal mines are inherent risks in the coal industry. If a major accident occurs, it will not only face human and economic losses, but may also lead to a shutdown of production across the industry.

** Deductive factors**:
- The Shenghua mine is dominated by open air and better-quality well-workers, with better safety conditions than industry averages
- Adequate production and smart mining leading.
- Better history security records.

** Core judgement**: Low probability, high impact events. For investment analysis, risks are "black swan" categories that cannot be predicted but need to be taken into account.

# Risk six: Red-score policy change risk # (middle-low)

** Risk description**: The current 79.1% share of red is "exceeding red" and is well above the 65% minimum commitment. If capital expenditure increases or profits fall in the future, the share of red red will fall.

** Deductive factors**:
- 65% of the minimum red mark is in the company charter.
- Affordable net cash on the books and red capacity not affected by short-term profit fluctuations
- The trend in the Chinese policy is "no drop."

** Core judgement**: The decline in the share share of the red from 79% to 65-70% is possible, but the probability of a "severe reduction of the share of the red" is extremely low. Even at 65%, the rate of the share of the Hong Kong stock is still around 4%.

##2.2 Capital allocation capacity

Capital allocation is a central dimension of management assessment. How is China’s capital allocation record?

** Positive record**:
- The proportion of red points increased from 60 per cent to 79 per cent, showing a focus on shareholder returns
- Asset and liability rates remained at 23 per cent, without blind leverage expansion
- The hundreds of millions of asset restructurings are the promise of securitization of the group assets, not the management of "imperialist construction."
- No significant additional investment at the high coal prices (2022), showing a sense of cyclicality

** Negative records**:
- Undesirable returns on some of the investments (e.g., overseas coal mines, partial chemical projects) in history
- Capital expenditure increased from 33.5 billion in 2024 to 44.7 billion in 2025, a larger increase
- The new energy transformation is slow, and the share of new energy installed is extremely low
- As an elite, it is difficult to avoid non-economic investments of a "social responsibility" nature.

** Comprehensive evaluation**: Capital allocation capacity is at the "top-down" level. No big mistake, red-score policy implementation, but no surprise of "better than market expectations." Management is more like "stable butler" than "entrepreneurship entrepreneurs."

# III. Associated transaction risk: the boundary of interest between major shareholders and listed companies

There are a number of related transactions between the NEG, which holds about 69% of the Chinese Senghua shares and is an absolute shareholder:

- Public companies buy coal from the group for sale
- Some of the railways and ports shared by listed companies with the consortium
- A billion asset restructurings are the biggest associated transactions in their own right.

** Core risk**: Groups may be “transferring profits” through associated transaction pricing - sold to listed companies at high prices and bought from listed companies at low prices.

** Actual**: In historical records, associated transaction pricing is based on market prices and no apparent transfer of benefits has been observed. The pricing of hundreds of millions of asset restructurings has been independently evaluated, although some investors consider it to be high, but the whole is within reasonable limits.

** Core judgement**: Associated transaction risk is "already but manageable". Port stock listing and the dual constraints of A-stock regulation limit the space for major shareholders’ interests. But, as a minority shareholder, continued attention needs to be paid to the pricing fairness of associated transactions.

# Six, core conclusion

The Chinese Xia’s risk portrait can be summarized as:** short-term controllable, long-term defined, medium-term blurred**.

** Short-term control**: Coal price fluctuations, restructuring consolidation, policy changes - these are quantifiable and manageable risks. With the power of the divine and market position, there is no threat to survival in the short term.

** Long-term determination**: Energy transformation reduces coal demand is certain. But the process (20-30 years) is long, and the last time the Gods as the least costly producers will be out.

** Medium term blur **: The next 5-10 years are the most critical window period – coal demand is high but not yet falling significantly, and new energy costs are falling steadily but not yet sufficient to replace the spark entirely. During this window period, the ultimate return on investment is determined by whether the god will be able to pay off the shareholders with a high share in the red and at the same time to do well for the long-term transformation.

The role of management is "guardy" rather than "start-up." That is enough for a mature resource company. Investors do not need management to create growth miracles, but they need to keep their current strengths, split reds on time, and make no big mistakes.
