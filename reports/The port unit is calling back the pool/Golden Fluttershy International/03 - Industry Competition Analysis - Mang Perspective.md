# Kingdee International (0268.HK) industry competition analysis

> Analysis dimensions: industry ecology, competition landscape, moat depth, sustainability of long-term competitive advantages
> Core question: How wide is Kingdee’s moat? Is the competitive landscape conducive to long-term success?

---

## 1. Industry Panorama: Structural Characteristics of Chinese Enterprise SaaS

### Market size and growth

China's enterprise-level SaaS market will be approximately 186 billion yuan in 2025, a year-on-year growth of 22.3%. The AI ​​SaaS sub-track is growing at a higher rate, with a compound annual growth rate of 29.5% expected from 2024 to 2027, and will exceed 150 billion yuan in 2027. The Xinchuang market is also in an explosive period. It is expected to exceed 100 billion yuan in 2025 and reach 146.2 billion yuan, and is expected to reach 322.8 billion yuan in 2027.

However, the growth rate has slowed down from 29.7% in 2024 to 22.3%, and the industry is transitioning from "barbaric growth" to "intensive farming."

### The deep dilemma of China’s SaaS industry

We must face up to a reality: the development level of China's enterprise SaaS industry lags far behind that of the United States.

**Structural Barriers:**

1. **Low willingness to pay**: Chinese companies, especially small and medium-sized enterprises, are much less willing to pay for software than European and American companies. The culture of "pay for service" is not yet fully established.
2. **Customized demand is heavy**: The management processes of Chinese enterprises are very different, and standardized SaaS products cannot meet all needs, resulting in high implementation costs and low gross profit margins.
3. **Short customer life cycle**: The average survival life of small and medium-sized enterprises is shorter than that in Europe and the United States, resulting in a naturally high customer churn rate.
4. **Excessive fragmentation of competition**: In 2025, there will still be hundreds of SaaS vendors fighting in various segments, with serious price involution.

These difficulties are not a problem for Kingdee alone, but a systemic challenge for the entire industry.

### Future trends of the industry

"In the next three years, China's SaaS market will move from a hundred schools of thought to ten companies competing for hegemony." AI is becoming a new watershed:
- Over 60% of SaaS vendors simply encapsulate the "intelligent question and answer" function by calling large model APIs, which lacks depth.
- The valuation multiple of AI-native SaaS can reach 3-4 times that of traditional tool-based SaaS
- "No AI, no SaaS" has become a new business paradigm, and companies that cannot prove the value of AI will be eliminated

### Kingdee vs UFIDA: Thirty Years of Fighting

The competition between Kingdee and UFIDA is the longest struggle for hegemony in the history of Chinese enterprise software. Differences in strategies between the two companies:

**UF’s Advantages:**
- Leading the market share of large enterprises (ERP SaaS market share is 31.5%, ranking first for 5 consecutive years)
- Deeper relationship network between government and state-owned enterprises
- Brand awareness is higher among large customers

**Kingdee’s advantages:**
- Cloud transformation progress is leading (cloud revenue accounted for 82.5% vs UFIDA ~70%)
- Be the first to achieve profitability in 2025 (UFIDA is still losing money)
- The SME market has more advantages
- Product experience and user reputation are relatively better

**Key Judgment:** This is not a "winner takes all" market. The two companies have formed a misaligned competition pattern in terms of customer scale and industry. Kingdee's leading advantages in small and medium-sized enterprises and cloud transformation are meaningful, but they do not constitute a crushing advantage.

### SAP/Oracle Xinchuang alternative opportunities

The domestic replacement process of Xinchuang, a central state-owned enterprise, is accelerating, and SAP and Oracle are facing replacement pressure. In the domestic high-end ERP market, SAP accounts for 33%, Oracle accounts for 20%, IBM accounts for 8%, and Kingdee accounts for only 6%.

This means:
1. There is huge room for substitution, but Kingdee’s current share is very low
2. Replacing SAP/Oracle requires strong product capabilities and industry experience
3. This is a long-term and ongoing process and will not happen overnight.
4. Kingdee and UFIDA are more collaborators (jointly replacing foreign capital) than competitors in this market.

## 3. Moat analysis

### 1. Conversion cost moat ★★★★☆

**This is the most important moat of Kingdee. **

The ERP system is deeply embedded in the company's core processes such as finance, procurement, production, and human resources. Replacing the system means:
- Data migration risk (historical data loss or incompatibility)
- Business interruption risk (operations affected during switchover)
- Personnel retraining costs
- Reintegrate with upstream and downstream systems

But please note: the level of switching costs is positively related to customer size. The switching costs of large enterprises are extremely high (Xinghan NDR 103%), while the switching costs of small and medium-sized enterprises are relatively low (Xingchen NDR 94%). Kingdee's customer base is mainly small and medium-sized enterprises, so the overall conversion cost moat is "medium width".

### 2. Network effect moat ★★☆☆☆

**Weaker. **

As a PaaS platform, Kingdee Cloud can theoretically form a network effect for the developer ecosystem and application market. However, the current scale and activity of the third-party developer ecosystem is far less than that of Salesforce's AppExchange. It's still early days on this front.

### 3. Brand moat ★★★☆☆

**Medium. **

Kingdee has 30 years of brand accumulation in China's financial software and ERP fields, and is particularly well-known in the small and medium-sized enterprise market. Major customer cases such as China Merchants Group, Huawei, China Construction Bank, and CRRC endorse the brand. However, in the large enterprise market, the brand power is still weaker than that of SAP/Oracle and UFIDA.

### 4. Economies of scale moat ★★★☆☆

**Initially formed but not yet fully developed. **

Kingdee has an annual R&D investment of approximately 1.4 billion yuan, which is among the top levels in the field of Chinese enterprise SaaS. The scale effect of R&D investment is that one product code can serve tens of thousands or even hundreds of thousands of customers. However, the sales expense ratio of 38.9% shows that the economies of scale of the client have not yet formed.

### 5. AI first-mover advantage ★★★☆☆

**Potential moat, but not yet verified. **

Kingdee proposed the concept of "Enterprise Management AI" (EMAI) earlier and connected it to the DeepSeek large model. The contract value of the AI ​​suite in 2025 is 356 million yuan, and 230 million yuan has been signed in 2026Q1. If AI can truly improve product stickiness and willingness to pay, it will form new competition barriers. However, the current actual ARR in the AI ​​SaaS field accounts for less than 15% of the market, and most of them are still in the "demonstration stage."

## 4. Key variables of the industry

### Positive variables

1. **Innovation policy continues to advance**: high policy certainty and large room for substitution
2. **AI reshapes the industry landscape**: SaaS platforms with data and scenarios are most likely to benefit
3. **Industry concentration increases**: After the tail companies are cleared, the bargaining power of the head companies increases
4. **Enterprise digitalization is irreversible**: There is still room for significant improvement in the digital penetration rate of Chinese enterprises

### Negative variables

1. **Macroeconomic Downturn**: Small and medium-sized enterprises bear the brunt, IT budgets shrink
2. **Large models lower the threshold**: AI may make it easier for new entrants to develop ERP products
3. **Customer concentration risk**: The demand for central state-owned enterprise Xinchuang may fluctuate in stages.
4. **Internationalization Difficulties**: Kingdee’s vision of “making China’s management model rise globally” faces cultural and compliance barriers

## 6. Key conclusions

**Thinking like Munger - thinking the other way around:**

"If Kingdee fails, what is the most likely reason?"

1. **Chinese enterprise SaaS industry as a whole does not meet expectations** - willingness to pay has not increased for a long time, and the SaaS model in China has never been able to replicate the success of the United States. This is the largest systemic risk and cannot be diversified.

2. **AI breaks the existing pattern** - Large models allow new players to quickly develop "enough" ERP products, weakening the accumulated advantages of traditional manufacturers.

3. **The attrition war with UFIDA continues** - the two companies compete comprehensively in Xinchuang, AI, and large customer markets, resulting in long-term suppression of industry profit margins.

**Conversely, if Kingdee succeeds, what is the most likely path? **

1. Xinchuang substitution continues to advance + differentiated monetization of AI suites
2. Breakthroughs in the major customer market drive NDR to over 100%
3. Small and medium-sized clients use AI to reduce customer acquisition and service costs
4. Enhanced pricing power after industry consolidation

Kingdee is in a "good position in a good industry", but the moat is not wide enough and the competitive landscape is not stable enough. This is not a target that can be "bought with eyes closed". It is necessary to continuously track changes in the competitive situation.
