## 1. Profitability analysis

### 1.1 Quality of revenue growth

Hansoh Pharma’s performance growth over the past three years has shown a clear acceleration:

| Year | Operating income (100 million yuan) | Year-on-year growth rate | Net profit (100 million yuan) | Year-on-year growth rate |
|------|------------------|----------|----------------|----------|
| 2023 | 101.06 | +5.2% | 32.77 | +10.8% |
| 2024 | 122.61 | +21.3% | 43.72 | +33.4% |
| 2025 | 150.28 | +22.6% | 55.55 | +27.1% |

Several features worth noting:

**Growth quality is higher than growth rate. ** Revenue growth jumped from 5.2% to 22.6%, and the core driver was innovative drug sales (+30.4%) rather than one-time license-out revenue. The proportion of innovative drugs will increase from approximately 67% in 2023 to 82.2% in 2025, indicating that the growth structure continues to be optimized.

**Profit growth is faster than revenue growth. ** The net profit growth rate (33.4%) in 2024 will significantly exceed the revenue growth rate (21.3%), reflecting the operating leverage effect-in the process of increasing the volume of innovative drugs, fixed costs are diluted and profitability is accelerated. The profit growth rate in 2025 (27.1%) is slightly higher than the revenue growth rate (22.6%). This pattern of "profit growth > revenue growth" is a sign of high-quality growth.

### 1.2 Profit rate structure

| Indicators | 2024 | 2025 | Trends |
|------|------|------|------|
| Gross profit margin | ~90% | ~90% | Stable |
| Net profit margin | ~35.7% | ~37.0% | Improvement |
| R&D expense ratio | ~22.0% | ~22.3% | Stable |
| Selling expense ratio | ~31% | Expected ~30% | Improvement |

**Gross profit margin is about 90%** which is almost the highest level among A/H-share pharmaceutical companies. This means that for every 100 yuan of drugs sold, the production cost is only about 10 yuan, and the remaining 90 yuan is used to cover research and development, sales and profits. Such a high gross profit margin reflects the pricing power of innovative drugs.

**Net profit margin is about 37%** which is also at the upper level in the global pharmaceutical industry. For reference, the net profit margins of major global pharmaceutical companies (Pfizer, Novartis, Roche, etc.) are usually between 20-30%. Hansen's higher net profit margin is partly due to the relatively low operating costs in the Chinese market.

**It should be noted that the sales expense ratio is still high (~30%). ** This is a common phenomenon in China's pharmaceutical industry - coverage of hospital channels requires a large number of sales personnel. As the proportion of innovative drugs increases and the efficiency of academic promotion improves, this ratio has a downward trend, but it is difficult to decrease significantly in the short term.

### 1.3 ROE analysis

Hansoh Pharma's ROE is about 18%, which may not seem particularly outstanding, but its background needs to be understood:

- The company has more than 27.1 billion yuan in cash on hand, almost zero debt, and the asset-liability ratio is only 11.33%
-Such a low leverage ratio significantly lowers the denominator effect of ROE
- If excess cash is excluded, "Operating Assets ROE" would be significantly higher

In other words, an ROE of 18% was achieved with almost zero leverage, indicating that its actual capital return capability is much stronger than its numerical performance. This is the type of business Buffett prefers—a business that can generate high returns without borrowing money.

## 2. Cash flow analysis

### 2.1 Operating cash flow

In 2025, cash inflow from operating activities will be 6.738 billion yuan, far exceeding net profit (5.555 billion yuan), with a cash-to-profit ratio of approximately 1.21. This means that the company's profits are not "paper profits", but actually converted into cash.

**Operating cash flow > Net profit** is a typical characteristic of high-quality companies, explaining:

- Good accounts receivable management and high collection efficiency
- No significant capitalization to cover expenses
- Profit quality is real and reliable

### 2.2 Free cash flow

| Project | 2025 (100 million yuan) |
|------|-----------------|
| Operating cash flow | 67.38 |
| Capital expenditures | 4.58 |
| **Free Cash Flow** | **~62.80** |

Capital expenditure of only 458 million yuan was mainly used for new R&D centers and production facilities. This is a capital-light operation business - it can drive performance growth without a large investment in fixed assets. Free cash flow is approximately 6.28 billion yuan, and the free cash flow conversion rate is extremely high.

### 2.3 Cash Reserves

As of the end of 2025, the company's cash and bank deposits were approximately 27.1 billion yuan, and it had almost zero interest-bearing liabilities. This cash is equivalent to:

- About 8 years of R&D expenditure (3.358 billion/year)
- Approximately 4.9 years of net profit
- Approximately 12-13% of total market capitalization

**Positive Interpretation:** This provides an extremely strong margin of financial safety. Even if it encounters a "black swan" event, the company has sufficient ammunition to continue advancing its R&D pipeline and pursuing BD opportunities.

**Critical thinking:** 27.1 billion yuan in cash is lying on the account, and the efficiency of capital use is not high. As a pharmaceutical company, we should be more active in deploying capital through internal R&D, external introduction (License-in) or mergers and acquisitions. Or, if you can't find suitable investment opportunities for the time being, you should increase dividends or buybacks to reward shareholders. At the end of 2025, the dividend will be only HK$0.2 per share, which is a low dividend ratio.

## 3. Capital Allocation Assessment

### 3.1 R&D investment

R&D expenses will increase from 1.121 billion yuan in 2019 to 3.358 billion yuan in 2025, with an average annual compound growth rate of approximately 20%. R&D expense ratio remains stable at around 22%. This level of investment is at the forefront among Chinese pharmaceutical companies, but is above average compared with global large pharmaceutical companies (R&D expense ratios are usually 15-25%).

More important is R&D efficiency. Hansoh has already had a number of innovative drugs approved for marketing and successful commercialization (annual sales of Ametinib are 5.5 billion), and its ADC pipeline assets have been verified by GSK’s multi-billion dollar cooperation. This shows that the output efficiency of R&D investment is relatively high.

### 3.2 BD Strategy

Hanson’s License-out strategy has been quite successful:

The deals have cumulative down payments of more than $500 million and potential milestone payments totaling nearly $8 billion. Even assuming only some milestones are met, this is a huge amount of potential revenue.

### 3.3 Dividends and buybacks

The company will pay a dividend of HK$0.2 per share in 2025, corresponding to a total dividend of approximately HK$1.2 billion, with a dividend ratio of approximately 19% of net profit. This dividend level is low for a company that is flush with cash and has strong free cash flow. No information on recent share repurchases was found.

**Capital Allocation Overall Comment:** R&D investment and BD strategy execution are excellent, but insufficient emphasis is placed on shareholder returns. A large amount of cash is sitting idle on the books, reducing overall capital efficiency.

### 4.2 Discussion on the rationality of valuation

**Is 32 times PE expensive? **

Horizontal comparison:

- Hengrui Pharmaceutical (A shares): about 50-60 times PE
- BeiGene: Still in the early stages of turning losses into profits, PE is not comparable
- Innovent Biologics: about 30-40 times PE
- Global pharmaceutical companies (Pfizer, Novartis, etc.): about 15-20 times PE

Hansoh's PE of 32 times is at a medium to low level in China's innovative drug sector, lower than Hengrui Medicine, but higher than that of global mature pharmaceutical companies. Considering Hanson's 20%+ growth and 37% net profit margin, this valuation is not outrageous.

**PEG perspective:** Assuming that net profit will grow at an average annual rate of 20-25% in the next three years, PEG will be approximately 1.3-1.6 times. For high-quality growth stocks, PEG 1.0-1.5 is a reasonable range, and PEG 1.3-1.6 is slightly expensive but not a bubble.

### 4.3 Safety Margin Assessment

**Factors in favor of margin of safety:**

- Cash of 27.1 billion yuan, zero debt, and extremely high financial security
- Strong free cash flow (6.28 billion yuan/year), continuing to generate real money
- License-out milestone payments for multiple pipelines provide an additional revenue safety cushion
- Shares are trading below the middle of their 52-week range, not in a crazy position

**Factors that are not conducive to the margin of safety:**

- 32x PE means the market has priced in considerable growth expectations
- Amitinib accounts for too high a proportion of revenue, and valuation may shrink if growth slows due to intensified competition
- There are inherent risks of failure in the research and development of innovative drugs, and the value of the pipeline is uncertain.
- China’s medical insurance negotiations may reduce the pricing space for innovative drugs
