## 1. What kind of business is this?

### 1.1 The essence of business

Hansoh Pharmaceutical's core business is the R&D, production and commercialization of innovative drugs**. Unlike other pharmaceutical companies, Hansoh has completed the strategic transformation from "generic drug-based" to "innovative drug-driven". In 2025, revenue from innovative drugs and cooperative products will reach 12.354 billion yuan, accounting for 82.2% of total revenue, maintaining a growth rate of over 30% for three consecutive reporting periods.

The economic characteristics of this business are very prominent:

- **Super high gross profit margin (~90%):** Once a drug is successfully developed and approved for marketing, the marginal production cost is extremely low. The cost of sales in 2025 will be only 1.498 billion yuan, accounting for less than 10% of revenue. This gross profit margin level ranks in the first echelon in the global pharmaceutical industry, comparable to Moutai.
- **High R&D investment threshold:** R&D expenditure in 2025 will be 3.358 billion yuan, a year-on-year increase of 24.3%, accounting for 22.3% of revenue. R&D is a continuous and huge investment, which constitutes a natural barrier to entry.
- **Long cycle, high risk, high return:** It usually takes 10-15 years for an innovative drug to go from project establishment to launch, with a success rate of less than 10%. However, once successful, it may create billions or even tens of billions of revenue.

### 1.2 Essential differences from generic drugs

Generic drugs are a "manufacturing" business - competing for costs and channels, with low profit margins, and are greatly affected by centralized procurement policies. Innovative drugs are an "intellectual property" business - competing in research and development capabilities and clinical data, and enjoying pricing rights during the patent protection period.

Hansoh's transformation from generic drugs to innovative drugs is essentially an upgrade from "manufacturing" to "knowledge economy". During the centralized procurement period in 2019, the company's core generic drug products (pemetrexed, olanzapine, etc.) suffered heavy losses, but the management decisively accelerated innovation and transformation. By 2025, the proportion of generic drugs revenue has dropped to less than 18%, and the company's growth engine has been completely "decoupled" from the policy risks of generic drugs.

## 2. Where is the moat?

### 2.1 Depth and breadth of R&D pipeline

Hansoh is currently simultaneously advancing more than 40 innovative drug candidates and more than 70 clinical trials, covering multiple fields such as oncology, autoimmunity, metabolism, and central nervous system. The scale of this pipeline ranks among the top among Chinese pharmaceutical companies.

Key pipeline assets include:

- **Aumolertinib:** China's first original third-generation EGFR-TKI, with sales of 5.53 billion yuan in 2025, is the best-selling innovative drug in the Chinese market. It has been approved for marketing by the British MHRA and is being promoted internationally.
- **B7-H3 ADC (HS-20093):** The world's leading B7-H3-targeted ADC drug, has authorized GSK for global (excluding China) development and commercialization.
- **B7-H4 ADC (HS-20089):** Also authorized to GSK, with an upfront payment of $185 million and milestone payments of up to $1.525 billion.
- **GLP-1/GIP Dual Target Agonist (HS-20094):** Licensed to Regeneron for $80 million upfront and up to $1.93 billion in milestones.

### 2.2 Signal meaning of License-out

In recent years, Hansoh has reached a number of major cooperation with international giants such as GSK, Regeneron, Merck, Roche, and Glenmark. The significance of these transactions is not just financial – they validate the international competitiveness of Hansen’s innovation pipeline. When the world's top ten pharmaceutical companies are willing to pay hundreds of millions of dollars in down payments and billions of dollars in milestones to acquire Hanssen's pipeline assets, this in itself is the most powerful endorsement of its R&D capabilities.

### 2.3 Commercialization capabilities

It has a mature sales network covering the whole country, which many Biotech companies do not have. Hansoh has accumulated deep channel resources in the era of generic drugs, and these channels can be directly reused in the process of transforming into innovative drugs. At the same time, the sales expense ratio continued to decline from 38% in 2019 to approximately 31%, indicating that the commercialization efficiency of innovative drugs is improving.

## 3. User value and differentiation

### 3.1 Who does it create value for?

Hansoh Pharma's end users are patients and doctors. In the anti-tumor field (revenue accounting for 66.4%), ametinib provides a domestic alternative for patients with EGFR-mutated non-small cell lung cancer. The efficacy is equivalent to AstraZeneca’s osimertinib, but the price is more advantageous. This positioning of "same efficacy, lower cost" has a natural competitive advantage in the Chinese pharmaceutical market.

### 3.2 Differentiated positioning

Unlike BeiGene, Innovent Biologics and other companies that prefer the "Biotech" route, Hansen's differentiation lies in:

- **"Pharma" gene:** Have a mature commercialization system and stable cash flow, and do not rely on external financing
- **Platform layout:** Covering multiple technology platforms such as small molecules, large molecules, and ADCs, without betting on a single technology route
- **Pragmatic strategy of "combination of generics and innovative drugs":** Generic drug business provides a cash flow base, while innovative drugs drive growth

## 4. Corporate Culture and Management Philosophy

### 4.1 Zhong Huijuan’s management style

Zhong Huijuan has transformed from a teacher into an entrepreneur. She has been in charge of Hansen for nearly 30 years and has a pragmatic and low-key style. She adheres to the principles of "fairness, openness, and talent only" in employing people, and has formed a management culture of "loose environment and strict system". The company's R&D team has more than 1,800 people and stable core technical personnel.

### 4.2 The embodiment of long-termism

Hansen did not reduce its R&D investment in 2019-2020, when the impact of centralized purchasing was the most serious. Instead, it continued to increase investment. R&D expenses will increase from 1.121 billion yuan in 2019 to 3.358 billion yuan in 2025, a three-fold increase in 6 years. This kind of "countercyclical investment" is a typical manifestation of long-termism.

At the same time, the company maintains zero leverage and does not engage in aggressive capital operations despite being extremely cash-rich (27.1 billion yuan), which also reflects the management's prudence.

### 4.3 Hidden concerns: family governance

Hansen is 75.66% owned by the Sunrise Family Trust, with the Chung family having absolute control. Her husband, Sun Piaoyang, is the actual controller of Hengrui Medicine. Although the two have been legally separated, the label of "mom-and-pop shop" cannot be completely eliminated. Daughter Sun Yuan has entered the management as executive director, responsible for R&D strategy and business development. The intergenerational inheritance is in progress, but her ability still needs time to be verified.

## 5. Business model assessment

### Positive factors

- Innovative drugs accounted for 82.2% of the revenue, the transformation was basically completed, and the business model was successfully upgraded.
- Ultra-high gross profit margin (~90%) + zero leverage + abundant cash (27.1 billion yuan), extremely healthy financial structure
- License-out cooperation with major international pharmaceutical companies to verify pipeline quality
- The annual sales of Ametinib is 5.5 billion, which has proven the commercialization ability of large-scale products.
- Multi-technology platform layout (small molecules, ADCs, large molecules) reduces the risk of a single technology route

### Negative factors

- Still highly dependent on the Chinese market, internationalization is still at an early stage
- Amitinib's revenue share is too high, and there is a risk of dependence on large single products
- Uncertainty in the intergenerational inheritance of family governance structures
- The sales expense ratio of 31% is still high, indicating that the channel moat is not irreplaceable
- The success rate of innovative drug research and development is naturally low, and the value of the pipeline is uncertain.
