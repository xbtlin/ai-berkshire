# I. KEY RISK INVESTMENT AND ASSESSMENT

# 1.1 Large monolithic dependency risk

Americoni sold $5.13 billion in 2025, the best-selling innovation in China’s market and the largest single contributor to Hansen’s income. Of the total revenue of $15.28 billion, 66.4% (approximately 99.80 billion) was in the field of anti-cancer alone, with Amételleni as the absolute core.

** Risk is at:**

- Amétini faces competition from AstraZeneca Osiètini and other generations of national production, EGFR-TKI
- Negotiations on the renewal of health insurance may further reduce price space
- Once the competition pattern deteriorates or a new generation of EGFR drugs emerges, there may be a diversion of Amedaloni's income.
- The excessive contribution of a single product means a concentration of risk from fluctuations in performance

** Mitigation:**

- Amédini has the most approved treatment and the widest range of treatments, with the advantage of pre-emptiveity
- In the process of internationalization (UK approval, working with Glenmark in a $1 billion+dollar) opens up incremental space
- Hanson ' s follow-on pipelines (ADC, GLP-1 etc.) are accelerating, with potential for the medium to long term to diversify sources of income

** Risk assessment:** Controllable in the short term, but the risk will increase significantly after 2-3 years if the follow-up large single items fail to relay.

##1.2 Risk of failure of R & D pipeline # (medium)

Hanson is simultaneously pushing 40+ innovative drugs projects, 70+ clinical trials. The inherent feature of innovation drug development is a low success rate – typically less than 10% of the combined success rate from I-Clinic to marketing approval.

** Critical pipe risk point:**

- **B7-H3 ADC (HS-20093);** Already in clinical phase III, is Hanson’s core asset for working with GSK. If clinical failure in phase III results in not only the loss of a heavy-pound pipe, but also the possible impact on subsequent milestone payments.
- **GLP-1/GIP Double Target (HS-20094);** Authorized Regeneron, currently conducting the III phase in China. The GLP-1 track is highly competitive, with the pre-emptive advantages of Noor and Nord and Reed.
- ** Free-of-charge lines (IL-23, TYK2 etc.):** In the clinical stage of the III stage, it is an important pillar of Hansen’s outreach beyond neoplasm.

** Mitigation:**

- There are enough pipes. A single project failure is not fatal.
- License-out strategy transfers some of the risks to the partners.
- Research and development team 1800+ people, technology platform covering small molecules, big molecules, ADC, etc.

**Risk evaluation:** This is an inherent risk of an innovative pharmaceutical enterprise that cannot be completely eliminated, but Hansen has done a reasonable risk dispersion through the diversification of the pipeline and the License-out strategy.

# 1.3 Policy and regulatory risks (medium)

Each renewal may face further price reductions. While prices for innovative medicines are usually lower than for generic collections, continued price compression will erode profit margins.

** Medicines against Corruption:** The drug-related anti-corruption storm since 2023 has had a profound impact on the marketing patterns of the entire industry. Although Hansen has not been exposed to major problems in the course of his anti-corruption operations, the ~30% level of marketing costs still indicates that his business model is more dependent on the distribution channels.

**The international policy environment:** The United States ' Biosecurity Act and other policies may affect the prospects of Chinese pharmaceutical companies in the United States. Although Hansen is now primarily entering the United States market through License-out, rather than directly, the attitude of the partners may change if the international political environment deteriorates further.

#1.4 Competition increases risk

China’s innovation medicine industry is rapidly "introverting." In hotgates like tumors, GLP-1s, ADCs, companies are pushing a pipe line with similar targets. This may lead to:

- There are multiple competitions for the same adaptive disorder, and price wars are inevitable.
- Increased competition for recruitment to clinical trials, and higher costs and time for research and development
- The market share is fragmented, and it's hard to get a complete dragon like Amétini.

Hansen’s response strategy is to avoid positive competition by differentiating the target selection (e.g. B7-H3, B7-H4) and the License-out model. The strategy is effective, but its sustainability depends on the continued innovation of the pipeline.

# # 1.5 Exchange rate and macro-risk # (lower)

Hanson now has a majority of revenues in renminbi, but License-out’s initial payments and milestones are in United States dollars. The renminbi appreciation may reduce the value of License-out revenues.

# II. Management assessment

# 2.1. Hye-chan: From teacher to "Medical Queen"

The career experience of Chung is a very special case. From a teacher to an entrepreneur, she has developed a regional counterfeiting plant into an innovative drug company with a market value of over HK$ 200 billion over the last 30 years.

** Capacity assessment:**

- ** Strategic judgement (<unk> ):** The transformation of innovative medicines started when counterfeiting was still profitable, and the decisive acceleration of transformation rather than the imposition of counterfeiting in the face of aggregate shocks — two key decisions have shown a strategic forward-looking approach.
- ** Implementation (<unk> ):** Income from innovative medicines increased from less than 30% to 82.2% and sustained profitable growth in the transition process, indicating a strong strategic landing capacity.
- ** International Perspective:** Success in achieving a number of heavy-pound License-out deals, but the layout of autonomous globalization is still early. Hansen’s internationalization strategy is conservative compared to the globalization ambitions of the state of Chisminsk.
- ** Capital allocation (<unk> ):** R & D inputs and BD strategies are well implemented, but 27.1 billion cash is idle and under-red, indicating that there is room for greater efficiency in capital use.

** Assessment of integrity:**

Hanson’s disclosure of information in the financial statements for the years was relatively transparent, and no major financial fraud or information fraud was detected. But it is important to note that company-related transactions (with historical links to Hensel’s medicine) have always been a source of doubt in the market.

#2.2 Sun: Keys of Generations

The daughter of Chung's daughter, Sun, has become the Executive Director of Hensen Pharmaceuticals, primarily responsible for research and development strategies, business development, investment strategies, and scientific development. As the "Creative Generations," her performance will have a direct impact on Hansen's long-term course.

** Positive signal:** Sun is placed in two core posts, R & D and business development, which demonstrates the overall capacity-building of management in a conscious manner.

** Uncertainty:** There is a lack of sufficient public information to assess Sun-Yan’s independent decision-making and team leadership. Intergenerational transmission is one of the greatest risks for family businesses, with success cases (such as LVMH) and failure cases (such as many family businesses) occurring in large numbers.

#2.3 Overall assessment of the management team

Hanson has a 1,800+ research and development team, with core managers and research and development cadres relatively stable. Given the high turnover of talent in the Chinese pharmaceutical industry, Hansen is able to maintain team stability, suggesting that his corporate culture and incentives are attractive.

But it is worth noting that Hanson’s management style is biased in favour of “centralization” – the CEO and CEO, the family trust holding 75.66%. This governance structure can be efficient in the “era of power” but also means overreliance on a single decision maker.

# IV. Long-term vision assessment

# 4.1 What kind of company does Hanson want to be?

In the light of management action (rather than mere public pronouncements), Hansen ' s long-term vision is:

** Become an innovative pharmaceutical industry with international influence.**

The evidence that supports this judgment:

1. High and sustained growth in R & D inputs, 6 years 3 times
2. Income from innovation medicines increased from less than 30 per cent to 82 per cent
3. Multiple pounds of cooperation with top 10 pharmaceutical companies around the world
4. Amedeni was approved in England to launch the international chemical expedition
5. Pipe cover multiple areas such as tumors, self-preservation, metabolism and avoiding single track dependency

##4.2 Probability assessment for realizing the vision

** Favourable:**

- Adequate financial ammunition (27.1 billion cash + 6.2 billion free cash flows) free of financial constraints
- Proven capacity for research and development and commercialization of innovative medicines (Amedaloni annual sales 5.5 billion)
- Multiple tubes into III. Clinical. 2026-2028.
- License-out network has been established and internationalization routes are clear

** Disadvantages:**

- Lack of capacity to globalize and limited experience in taking a free trip to the sea
- Aging management (Gong Huijun 60+) with uncertainty in intergenerational transmission
- Industry is competing. New targets are getting shorter.
- Geopolitics may affect international cooperation

** Probability judgement:** Hanson is moderately more likely to be a "China version of Abbvie" (a large pharmaceutical company with innovation as its core and a stable profitability); and is less likely to be a "China version of Roche" (the top 10 pharmaceutical companies in a globalizing world). More likely is the path to being a regional innovation firm that takes the lead in China and benefits from cooperation in the international market through License-out+ selective autonomous commercialization.
