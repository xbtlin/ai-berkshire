# I. PC Industry: A sector that is structurally unfavorable to OEM for money

#1.1 Profit distribution in the industrial chain

To understand the competitive situation that is associated with it, first of all the power structure of the PC industrial chain:

- **Intel/AMD** (CPU): mastering core computing and long-term high Maori (50-60%), which, despite increasing competition in recent years, still occupies the strongest bargaining position
- ** Yin Wei Da** (GPU): The biggest winner in the AI era, 70% Māori +
- **Microsoft** (operative system): Windows monopoly desktop operating systems, 70% Māori +
- ** Samsung/Helsi** (RAM/storage): cyclical industry, good years earn a lot and bad years lose
- **Us/HP/Del** (OEM assembly): between upstream and downstream, Māori ratio 15-25%

This is the classic "smilt curve" dilemma -- the two ends of the value chain (upstream core parts, downstream brands/software) earn most of their profits, and the middle assembly is the least profitable. The association is at the lowest point in the curve.

** Analogous**: It's like a restaurant where people grow vegetables, people feed meat, and sell them to customers.

# # 1.2 Industry concentrated but not profited

The top five producers in the global PC market together account for more than 77 per cent of the share: conjuncture (25.4 per cent), Hewlett-Packard (20.6 per cent), Dale (15.0 per cent), Apple (9.9 per cent) and Huasung (7.2 per cent).

Industry concentration is already high, but - high concentration is not equal to high profit margins. This is because:

1. ** High homogenization of products**: Except for apples (with self-research chips plus operating systems plus ecology), the products of the remaining PC manufacturers are very fewly differentiated. The same CPU, the same operating systems, similar panels are used. Consumers/businesses often choose between the association and Hewlett-Packard on the basis of price and channel convenience.
2. ** Price wars are normal**: in homogenous markets, share competition is mainly based on price competition and channel efficiency. No one dares to raise prices significantly, because customers can switch easily.
**Symmetrical upstream bargaining**: Intel/Investak faces multiple OEM clients, but OEM manufacturers have only limited alternatives to CPU/GPU. Power is naturally upstream.

# Evaluation: # # assessment: # # assessment: #

The structure of the industry is not naturally conducive to over-profiting by OEM manufacturers.

# III. Server/AI infrastructure market: competition in new battlefields

#3.1 Market patterns

The global server market pattern: Dale (about 30%) — Hewlett-Packard HPE (about 15%) — Association (about 8-10%) — Micro-micro/wave, etc.

The market is a catch-up rather than a leader. Although ISG camp is growing rapidly (Q3-year-olds +31%), its share is still far below Dale.

#3.2 Real competitiveness of the AI server

The AI server market is currently in the "arms race" phase, with a huge demand for super-large customers (Microsoft, Meta, Google, etc.). The $15.5 billion order line that ISG has in common looks beautiful, but needs to be followed up:

The core cost of the AI server is the British Weilda GPU (50-70% of total costs), and the profit space available as a "assembly plus configuration plus delivery" is very limited. This is exactly the same as the PC's dilemma -- core technology is in the hands of others.
** Where is the difference?** The associated liquid cold technology (Neptune) is a potentially differentiated direction - the 300% increase in the income from liquid cold Q3. In the age of high-power-consuming AI servers, the solution to the heat radiation is indeed a client’s focus. But the current share of the whole ISG collection is still very small.
- ** Competing is also accelerating **: Dale, HPE, and Microbust are storming the AI server market.

# 3.3 Relationship with mega-clients

One alarming trend is that super-large clients like Microsoft, Google, Amazon are developing their own servers (e.g. Google's TPU server, Microsoft's Maia chip). In the long run, if the biggest customers do their own hardware, the market space that they want to get is being suppressed.

# Four. Long-term trends in industry: what are the advantages and the disadvantages of conjecture?

# 4.1 AI PC change cycle (short term good)

The IDC projects a switch driven by AIPC in 2025-2027. Windows 10 de-support (October 2025) will also drive business customers to upgrade their equipment.

** But note **: The change cycle is cyclical - it is a wave-end. The incremental demand generated by AI PC is one-time and not permanent. And the current AI PC "AI" function has limited real value for ordinary users and it is still to be tested whether it can stimulate premium purchases.

##4.2 Mixed AI Trends (Middle-term Opportunities)

The joint bet "mixed AI" -- not all AI reasoning is done on the cloud, but some of it is done on the terminal (PC, mobile phone) and peripheral servers. If this is correct, the association can benefit from being the provider of terminal plus edge devices.

**Risk**: The debate on the hybrid AI vs pure cloud level AI is not yet conclusive. If AI ultimately concentrates on the cloud end (because it is cheaper and easier to manage), the story of the joint "equipment end AI" is compromised.

##4.3 Business digital transformation (long-term benefits)

Global corporate IT spending has continued to grow, with digital transformations, and AI deployments a long-term trend. The SSG service operation and the ISG infrastructure operation that we have associated with it can benefit. But there are also a lot of competitors in this track – from IT consulting firms like Essensell to cloud vendors like AWS/Azure – the competitiveness of the idea is "hardware plus service together," but whether the combination is attractive enough is yet to be tested.

## 4.4 Geopolitical risks (long-term uncertainty)

The Chinese context is a double-edged sword. In China, it has a local advantage; but in the United States, it faces tariff and geopolitical risks (about 25-30% of the revenue).

** Recent developments**:
- US tariffs have affected product pricing.
- Associated to accelerate exports before customs duties are enforced — 20% surge in USPC Q1 shipments in 2025
- Convergence to spread risk through multi-point global manufacturing (India, Brazil, Hungary, Saudi Arabia)
- But the label "China brand" is still a sub-item in the current geo-environment.

# Evaluation: # # assessment: # # assessment: #

There is a short-term catalytic shift cycle and an opportunity for AI in the medium term, but a long-term dual repression of the industry ' s profit structure plus geo-risk.

# Five, the industry’s most central question: Who can break the profit margin spell?

The profitability of the PC industry OEM plant, the Curse, has existed for more than 20 years - regardless of size and share increase, OEM's net interest rate has been hovering at 2-5% (symmetrical 2-3%, Hewlett-Packard 3-4%, Dale 4-5%).

The only way to break the spell is to move from "sell hardware" to "sell services and solutions." Microsoft's transformation (from selling Windows License to selling Azure Clouds and subscriptions) is a success story.

The SSG of the Union is on this path – 22% of operating profits demonstrate that the profit margin for service businesses can be several times higher than for hardware. But SSG currently accounts for only 12% of the revenue. If it is raised to 25-30% in the next five years, the group profit rate will improve qualitatively.

But it is also important to see that IT services are a highly competitive market (IBM, Essenzel, Infosys, local systems integrator), and that the core competitiveness of SSG is "hardware plus service bundles" - if customers do not need to buy hardware and services from the same manufacturer. Whether this assumption is established will require time to be validated.
