## 2. Core Investment Discussion

### 2.1 Bull argument (why it might be worth buying)

1. **Cheap valuation**: 8.8 times TTM PE + 3% dividend yield, which is a low valuation among global technology hardware stocks. If the AI ​​cycle continues to drive profit growth, there is room for repair in valuations.

2. **There are many AI catalysts**: AI PC replacement wave (2025-2027) + ISG AI server high growth (USD 15.5 billion order pipeline) + SSG service business continues to grow. Multiple AI-related catalysts are superimposed.

3. **Profit rate inflection point is approaching**: ISG is close to breakeven + SSG continues to expand + ISG restructuring saves 200 million +/year cost. There may be an inflection point in 2026-2027 when profit margins jump from 2-3% to 4-5%.

4. **Mature globalization layout**: Operation network in 180 markets, Gartner supply chain Top 8, multi-point manufacturing layout - in the era of tariffs, this globalization capability is a real competitive advantage.

5. **Stable Dividends**: The dividend payout rate of 40% and the annual dividend growth rate of 6.7% are attractive to investors pursuing cash returns.

### 2.2 Short Discourse (Why It May Be a Trap)

1. **Structural low profit margin**: The net profit margin ceiling of the PC assembly business is about 3-5% (just look at Dell), and the core technology is in the hands of upstream chip and operating system companies. Lenovo essentially makes "hard-working money" rather than "smart money."

2. **The illusion of high ROE**: 20%+ ROE is driven by 4-5 times leverage, not by brand premium or technical barriers. Once the cycle goes down, high leverage will amplify losses.

3. **Low PE is a classic feature of a value trap**: Low PE + cyclical industries + profits at cycle highs - this is exactly the time to sell rather than buy. PE will skyrocket when the cycle goes down (because profits fall faster than stock prices).

4. **ISG’s profit is far away**: It has been losing money for 10 years since its acquisition in 2014. Even though ISG's revenue is growing rapidly, most of the profits from AI servers are eaten up by NVIDIA GPU costs. "Double revenue but zero profits" growth means nothing.

5. **Geopolitics is the Sword of Damocles**: The United States accounts for 25-30% of revenue. Once sanctions intensify or Sino-US technology is completely decoupled, Lenovo's globalization model will face fundamental challenges. This is a systemic risk that cannot be hedged.

6. **Lack of a real moat**: There is no self-developed chip, no own operating system, and no highly sticky software ecosystem. Product standardization and low customer switching costs. Lenovo's competitive advantage in five years' time may be exactly the same as it is today - this is not a company that can "get stronger and stronger".

**Observation**: Lenovo has the lowest PE, but also the lowest net profit margin. It makes sense for the market to give Lenovo a discount - low profit margins mean high profit elasticity (large upside potential, but also large downside risks) and poor predictability. Dell and HP can obtain higher PE, on the one hand because of better profit margins, and on the other hand because there is no geopolitical discount for American companies.

## 4. Investment decision-making framework

### 4.1 What type of investors is it suitable for?

- **Suitable**: Value/income investors who pursue stable dividends + moderate growth; short- and medium-term traders who are optimistic about the AI hardware cycle
- **Not suitable**: value investors pursuing long-term compound interest growth (the moat is too thin); risk-averse investors (geographic risk + high leverage)

### 4.2 Buying conditions (if considered)

If you want to consider buying Lenovo in the future, it is recommended to wait until the following conditions are met at the same time:

1. **Valuation**: PE returns to less than 8 times (corresponding to a stock price of less than 10 Hong Kong dollars), providing a sufficient margin of safety
2. **ISG Profit**: Operating profit has been positive for two consecutive quarters, proving that the AI server business can make money.
3. **Group net profit margin**: reaching above 3.5% for at least one quarter, proving that the profit structure is improving
4. **Geographic easing**: Sino-US relations show signs of substantial easing, and tariff risks decrease

### 4.3 Reasons not to buy now

- The stock price of HK$12.94 is close to the 52-week high (13.46), lacking a margin of safety
- ISG has yet to prove sustained profitability
- Geopolitical risks have not subsided
- The benefits of the AI PC replacement cycle may have been priced in by the market

## 5. Final conclusion

### Overall rating: ★★½☆☆ (2.5/5)

### Investment advice: **Observe, no action yet**

Lenovo is a "make do" company - not bad, but not good enough. The business profits it does are too thin, the moat is too shallow, and the external risks are too high, so it is not suitable as a core position in long-term value investment.

The AI wave has indeed brought growth catalysts to Lenovo, but the "quality" of growth is the key. It is not difficult to increase revenue from US$80 billion to US$100 billion. What is difficult is to increase the net profit margin from 2-3% to 5%. If the latter cannot be achieved, Lenovo will just be a company that is "getting bigger but not getting stronger" - with limited returns to shareholders.

The current price of HK$12.94 is at the upper end of the reasonable valuation range, with no obvious margin of safety. It is not recommended to open a position at this price. If the stock price falls below HK$10 (corresponding to a PE of about 7 times) and ISG starts to make profits, it can be reconsidered.

**Final warning**: Don't be fooled by the combination of "AI concept + low PE". A low PE may be an underestimation, or it may be the market's correct pricing of the "nature of the business." The core reason for Lenovo's 8.8 times PE is not that the market ignores it, but that the market clearly knows that this is an assembly business with a profit margin of only 2-3%, leverage to drive ROE, and lack of a moat. Low valuations are a reasonable reflection of low quality.
