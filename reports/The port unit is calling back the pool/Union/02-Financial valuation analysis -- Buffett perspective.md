# Lenovo Group (0992.HK) Financial and Valuation Analysis

> Analysis dimensions: financial quality, capital return, cash flow, valuation rationality
> Core question: Are you making real money? Is high ROE sustainable? Are the current prices cheap or expensive?

---

## 1. Revenue and profit: huge scale, thin profits

### 1.1 Revenue Trends

Lenovo Group's strong performance in the first three quarters of fiscal year 2025/26:

| Quarterly | Revenue (USD) | Year-over-year growth | Adjusted net income | Year-over-year growth |
|------|-------------|----------|-------------|----------|
| Q1 FY25/26 | About 18.8 billion | 22% | About 390 million | 22% |
| Q2 FY25/26 | 20.5 billion | 15% | 512 million | 25% |
| Q3 FY25/26 | 22.2 billion | 18% | 589 million | 36% |

Full-year revenue is expected to exceed US$78 billion, heading towards the US$100 billion target. The driving force for revenue growth mainly comes from three aspects: the moderate recovery of the PC market, the explosive growth of the ISG server business, and the steady expansion of the SSG service business.

### 1.2 Profit rate: structural dilemma

Although revenue growth is impressive, the "thin" profit margin is a structural problem:

- **Gross profit margin**: approximately 15.4%, fluctuating between 15-17% in recent years. This level is relatively low in the technology hardware industry - Apple's gross profit margin is 45%+, Dell is 25%+, and HP is 20%+. The fundamental reason for Lenovo's low gross profit margin is that "pure hardware assembly" accounts for too high a proportion in its product portfolio.
- **Operating profit margin**: approximately 3.5-4%.
- **Net interest rate**: about 2-3%. Q2 net profit margin was only 2.15%.

**Key Question**: What does a 2-3% net profit margin mean? This means that for every $100 of product sold, you only earn $2-3. Under this kind of profit margin, any external shock (raw material price increase of 5%, exchange rate fluctuation of 3%, tariff increase of 10%) may eat up half or more of the profit. This is the fragility of the small profit model.

### 1.3 points business profit contribution

The profit quality of the three major businesses is very different:

| Business | Revenue proportion | Operating profit margin | Profit contribution |
|------|----------|-----------|----------|
| IDG (Intelligent Devices) | ~53% | ~7% | Main source of profit |
| ISG (Infrastructure) | ~35% | About -0.2% (near breakeven) | Contributes almost no profit |
| SSG (Solution Service) | ~12% | ~22% | High quality but small volume |

**Straightforward statement**: Lenovo’s current profits mainly come from selling PCs. Although ISG accounts for one-third of revenue, it barely makes any money. SSG has the best profit margin, but its volume only accounts for more than 10%. The improvement of the group's profit structure is highly dependent on when ISG can stabilize its profits and how quickly SSG can grow bigger.

### Evaluation: ★★☆☆☆

Revenue growth is good, but profit margins are structurally low, profit comes from a single source (reliance on IDG), and risk resistance is weak.

## 2. ROE dismantling: the truth about high ROE

Lenovo's ROE has been above 20% for a long time, which looks very good. But ROE needs to be broken down to look at:

**ROE = Net profit rate x Asset turnover rate x Equity multiplier**

Lenovo’s situation is roughly as follows:

- Net profit margin: about 2.5% → **very low**
- Asset turnover rate: approximately 2.0x → **medium to high** (characteristics of hardware distribution business)
- Equity multiplier: ~4-5x → **very high**

**Interpretation**: Lenovo's high ROE is almost entirely driven by high leverage. The net interest rate is only 2.5%. Without 4-5 times leverage, ROE is only 5-6%, which is completely unattractive.

**How does high leverage come from? **Lenovo's balance sheet has significant accounts payable (credit to suppliers) and interest-bearing liabilities. As of March 2025, total liabilities were approximately $5.6 billion. The debt-to-equity ratio is approximately 0.59. Lenovo's leverage comes partly from operating liabilities (accounts payable, which is a common feature of the PC industry - sell goods first and then pay suppliers), and partly from interest-bearing debt.

**Risk**: High leverage amplifies gains during good times and losses during headwinds. When Lenovo experienced a trough in 2018-2019, net profits shrank significantly, but while leverage remained unchanged, ROE dropped sharply. A 20% ROE driven by leverage and a 20% ROE driven by product premiums (such as Moutai) have completely different qualities.

### Evaluation: ★★☆☆☆

The ROE figure is good-looking but the quality is not high. It is driven by leverage rather than product competitiveness, and its sustainability is questionable.

## 3. Cash flow: better looking than the income statement

For businesses with low profit margins, cash flow is more important than profit. Lenovo's performance in this regard is acceptable:

- **Operating cash flow**: Lenovo benefits from the "negative working capital" model of the PC industry - collecting money from customers first and paying suppliers later. This makes operating cash flow generally better than net income.
- **Free Cash Flow**: Because capital expenditures are relatively low (no fabs are needed), free cash flow is positive and relatively stable.
- **Dividend Distribution**: The annual dividend is approximately HK$0.39 per share, the dividend payout ratio is approximately 40%, and the dividend rate is approximately 3%. Lenovo's dividend growth rate over the past five years was 6.7%, indicating that management intends to maintain a stable dividend policy.

**Positive**: This is not a money-burning business. Lenovo generates positive free cash flow and has the ability to pay consistent dividends.

**Negative**: The absolute amount of free cash flow is constrained by low margins. For a company with a net profit margin of only 2-3%, even if the cash flow conversion rate is 100%, the ceiling of free cash flow is very low. Moreover, if Lenovo wants to increase investment in AI infrastructure (more R&D, expansion of ISG), free cash flow may be further pressured.

### Evaluation: ★★★☆☆

Cash flow is positive and dividends can continue to be paid, but the absolute amount is limited by low profit margins, and future AI investment may squeeze free cash flow.

## 4. Balance sheet: There are hidden concerns

### 4.1 Goodwill and intangible assets

Lenovo has accumulated a lot of goodwill through three large-scale mergers and acquisitions (IBM PC, IBM x86 server, Motorola). As of recently, the goodwill on the balance sheet was approximately $4-5 billion. This is a risk that needs attention - if the performance of an acquired business is not as good as expected (for example, Motorola's mobile phone business is in a long-term slump), goodwill impairment will directly impact profits.

### 4.2 Interest-bearing liabilities

Lenovo's interest-bearing liabilities are approximately US$5.6 billion, and together with operating liabilities, the total debt scale is considerable. In an environment of rising interest rates, rising financing costs will directly erode the already meager profits. A one-off charge of $285 million for ISG's restructuring in 2025/26 also adds to short-term financial pressures.

### 4.3 Net Worth

Due to highly leveraged operations, Lenovo's net assets (shareholders' equity) are relatively low relative to total assets. This means that the company's "safety cushion" is not thick - in extreme situations (such as a sharp decline in global PC demand coupled with huge losses in the server business), financial flexibility is limited.

### Evaluation: ★★☆☆☆

A large amount of goodwill, high interest-bearing liabilities, thin net assets, and insufficient financial safety margin.

## 5. Valuation analysis

### 5.1 Current Valuation Level

| Valuation indicators | Lenovo (0992.HK) | Industry reference |
|----------|-----------------|----------|
| PE (TTM) | 8.8x | Dell ~20x, HP ~12x |
| Forward PE | 11.6x | — |
| PB | ~1.8x | — |
| EV/EBITDA | ~6x | — |
| Dividend Yield | ~3.0% | — |

Lenovo's PE looks very low - 8.8 times TTM PE, which falls into the "value stock" range among technology stocks. But low PE does not mean cheap, you need to understand "why it is low".

### 5.2 Reasons for low PE

The market gives Lenovo a low valuation. The core logic is:

1. **Low profit margin business is not worth high PE**: A company with a net profit margin of only 2-3% has high profit volatility and poor predictability. The market is unwilling to pay a premium for uncertain profits.
2. **Cyclic**: PC is a cyclical industry and is currently in the upward stage of the AI replacement cycle. The market often discounts cyclical stocks because high-point profits are unsustainable.
3. **ISG business is not profitable**: The business accounting for 35% of revenue barely contributes profits, and the market has doubts about its prospects.
4. **China background + geopolitical risks**: As China’s largest PC company, Lenovo faces discounts from tariffs and geopolitical risks.

### 5.3 Is the valuation reasonable?

**Reasons for Cheap**:
- 8.8 times PE corresponds to an earnings yield of about 11% (1/PE), plus a 3% dividend rate, the total return looks good
- If ISG can turn a profit, profits may grow non-linearly
- AI PC replacement cycle may drive IDG profits upward
- Analysts’ average target price is HK$12.18, which is a slight premium to the current price, but the highest target price is HK$19.

**Reasons for not being cheap**:
- Low PE may be a "value trap" - if profit margins cannot continue to increase, PE will not increase
- Buying cyclical stocks with low PE at cycle highs is a classic investment trap
- If net profit margin cannot be improved from 2-3% to 5%, revenue growth will have limited meaning to shareholders
- A forward-looking PE of 11.6 times is no longer particularly cheap.

### 5.4 Safety Margin Assessment

According to the current stock price of HK$12.94, relative to the 52-week range (8.52-13.46), the stock price is in a high range (near the 52-week high). This is not a buying time with a clear margin of safety.

**Simple discounted cash flow thinking**: Assuming that Lenovo's full-year net profit is about 1.8-2 billion US dollars, giving 10 times PE, the reasonable market value is about 18-20 billion US dollars (140-156 billion Hong Kong dollars). The current market value is approximately HK$160 billion, which is basically within a reasonable valuation range and is not obviously undervalued.

### Evaluation: ★★★☆☆

The valuation is not expensive but not cheap either, and is within a reasonable range. Lack of clear margin of safety.

## 6. Summary

| Dimensions | Ratings | Core Judgments |
|------|------|----------|
| Revenue growth | ★★★★☆ | The three major businesses are growing, and AI-driven growth is accelerating |
| Profit margin | ★★☆☆☆ | Structurally low profit margin, room for improvement exists but has not yet been realized |
| ROE quality | ★★☆☆☆ | High ROE is driven by leverage, not product premiums |
| Cash flow | ★★★☆☆ | Positive free cash flow, able to distribute dividends, but the absolute amount is limited |
| Balance sheet | ★★☆☆☆ | High goodwill, high leverage, thin safety cushion |
| Valuation | ★★★☆☆ | Reasonable range, no obvious undervaluation or safety margin |
| **Comprehensive Rating** | **★★½☆☆** | **Growth is not bad but profit quality is poor, valuation is reasonable but no margin of safety** |

**One sentence conclusion**: Lenovo's financial data shows the typical characteristics of "good on the revenue side, mediocre on the profit side". High ROE is driven by leverage rather than brand premium, and the structurally low profit margin limits the intrinsic value of the company. Current valuations are reasonable but lack a margin of safety -- not a price that will excite value investors. If you want to invest in Lenovo, the core bet is whether ISG can be profitable and whether profit margins can be systematically improved, rather than simply "low PE equals cheap".
