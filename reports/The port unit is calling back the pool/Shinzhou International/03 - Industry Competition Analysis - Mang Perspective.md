## 1. Understanding the Industry: The Nature of Textile OEM

### 1.1 The economic nature of the industry

The textile and garment OEM industry has a seemingly contradictory feature: the overall profit of the industry is meager, but leading companies can obtain excess returns**.

The global apparel OEM market is worth hundreds of billions of dollars, with tens of thousands of participants. Most small factories have profit margins of only 3%-5%. However, Shenzhou International's net profit margin is as high as 18%-22%, and Huali Group's net profit margin is also over 15%. This pattern of "industry mediocrity but leader excellence" points to a core insight: **Textile OEM is evolving from "labor-intensive low-end manufacturing" to "technology-intensive lean manufacturing"**.

Brand owners are increasingly concentrated (the top ten brands such as Nike, Adidas, and Uniqlo occupy more and more market shares), and their requirements for suppliers are also upgrading: not only low cost, but also fabric research and development capabilities, rapid response, environmental compliance, and global production capacity. There are only a handful of suppliers that can meet these comprehensive requirements, and orders naturally focus on the leading ones.

### 1.2 Use the "Five Forces Model" to examine the industry

| Power | Strength | Analysis |
|------|------|------|
| Threat of new entrants | Low | It takes more than 10 years to accumulate capital, technology, and customer trust required for vertical integration |
| Bargaining power of suppliers | Medium | Price fluctuations of raw materials such as cotton affect costs, but Shenzhou's self-supplied fabrics alleviate this pressure |
| Buyer’s bargaining power | High | The four major customers account for 80% of the revenue, and the brand has a strong voice |
| Threat of substitutes | Low | Functional fabrics for sportswear are difficult to replace by low-end substitutes |
| Competition within the industry | Medium-low | There are few competitors in the high-end foundry field, but the mid- and low-end are still highly fragmented |

**Key conclusion**: The competition barriers in the industry are constantly increasing (environmental protection, technology, scale), which is beneficial to leading companies that have established advantages. The biggest pressure comes from downstream buyers - brands always have strong bargaining power.

## 2. Competitive landscape: Who is Shenzhou’s opponent?

### 2.1 Direct Competitor Comparison

**Important Findings**:

1. **Shenzhou’s net profit margin is far ahead in the foundry industry**. The net profit margin of 21.8% even exceeds the downstream brand Nike (about 12%), which is extremely rare in the global OEM industry.
2. **Huali Group is the most noteworthy opponent**. Although the main business is footwear rather than clothing, the two are highly similar in terms of customer resources, management models, and production capacity layout.
3. **Lutai** focuses on the woven fabric market segment, which is different from Shenzhou’s knitted fabric track and has limited competition.
4. **Yueyuan/Fengtai** mainly manufactures footwear, and its net profit margin is much lower than that of Shenzhou and Huali, indicating that the internal differentiation of the foundry industry is serious.

### 2.2 Shenzhou’s competitive positioning

In the global sportswear OEM market, Shenzhou has a market share of approximately 3.8%. This number may not seem high, but considering the extremely fragmented nature of the industry, 3.8% is already the absolute number one. More importantly, in the market segment of "providing high-end fabrics + one-stop clothing services for Nike and Adidas", Shenzhou's actual market share is much higher than this.

There are almost no competitors in the world that truly have vertical integration capabilities and fabric research and development capabilities that reach Shenzhou level. This cannot be achieved with capital - it requires decades of process accumulation, hundreds of patents, and the experience of tens of thousands of skilled workers.

## 3. Industry chain analysis

### 3.1 Upstream: raw materials

Shenzhou's main raw materials are cotton yarn, chemical fiber and other textile raw materials. Because the company has the ability to develop and produce fabrics by itself, its reliance on upstream raw materials is lower than that of pure OEM companies. The fabric link itself is the most profitable link, and Shenzhou has gained more value chain profits through "upward extension".

### 3.2 Midstream: Manufacturing

This is Shenzhou’s core battlefield. The vertical integration model (weaving - dyeing and finishing - cutting - printing, embroidery - sewing) allows the company to complete the entire process from fabrics to garments internally, reducing outsourced losses and transaction costs.

Globalization of production capacity (China 47% + Vietnam 27% + Cambodia 26%) is an important strategy to deal with geopolitical risks. After the new base in Cambodia is put into operation in 2025, the total annual production capacity will reach 550 million pieces.

### 3.3 Downstream: brand customers

| Customers | Proportion in 2024 (estimated) | Growth |
|------|------------------|---------|
| Uniqlo | ~28% | Strong growth (+33%) |
| Nike | ~25% | Slight decrease (-3.9%) |
| Adidas | ~17% | Strong growth (+35%) |
| Puma | ~11% | Steady growth (+11%) |
| Others | ~19% | - |

A positive change in 2024 is that the growth of Uniqlo and Adidas makes up for Nike's decline, and the rebalancing of the customer mix reduces dependence on a single customer.

## 4. Industry evolution trends

### 4.1 Orders are concentrated towards the leader (high certainty ★★★★★)

This is the most certain trend. Reasons include:
- Brand owners streamline the number of suppliers and improve management efficiency
- The threshold for environmental protection compliance has increased, and small factories have been eliminated
- Under ESG requirements, brands prefer large suppliers with standardized governance
- The demand for functional fabrics is increasing, and only a few suppliers have R&D capabilities

Shenzhou's market share of 3.8% still has a lot of room for improvement, which is the core driving force for growth in the next few years.

### 4.2 The production base will be further transferred to Southeast Asia (high certainty ★★★★☆)

Labor costs in China's manufacturing industry continue to rise. Coupled with Sino-US trade frictions, the transfer of production capacity to Vietnam, Cambodia and other places is an irreversible trend. Shenzhou is already in the lead (overseas garment production capacity accounts for 55%), and has obvious first-mover advantage.

But attention needs to be paid: The labor supply in Vietnam and Cambodia is not unlimited, and local wages are also rising. In the long run, the game of "low cost" will eventually see its ceiling.

### 4.3 ESG and sustainable development (high certainty ★★★★☆)

The ESG requirements of the global fashion industry are changing from "plus points" to "access items". Markets such as the European Union have increasingly stringent requirements for supply chain transparency, carbon emissions, and circular economy. Shenzhou's continued investment in wastewater treatment, energy conservation and emission reduction will become a new competitive barrier.

### 4.4 Automation and intelligent manufacturing (high uncertainty ★★★☆☆)

AI and robotics are penetrating the textile manufacturing sector. At present, it is mainly used in sorting, quality inspection and other aspects, and the degree of automation of core processes such as sewing is still very low. But in the long term (10-year perspective), if sewing automation achieves a breakthrough, it will fundamentally change the competitive elements of the industry - from "who has the most skilled workers" to "who has the best automation system."

This is both an opportunity and a threat to Shenzhou: If Shenzhou can take the lead in realizing automation transformation, it will further consolidate its advantages; but if it is surpassed by new technological entrants, the accumulation of labor management will be devalued.

## 5. Reverse thinking: Under what circumstances will investment logic fail?

1. **Large-scale decentralization of supply chains by brands**: If Nike and Adidas decide to disperse orders from a few large factories to multiple small and medium-sized factories, Shenzhou's bargaining power and growth logic will be damaged. The current trend is opposite, but it needs to be continued to follow
2. **New technologies subvert the manufacturing model**: For example, the large-scale commercial use of 3D printed clothing and fully automatic sewing robots may weaken traditional manufacturing advantages.
3. **Geopolitical Extreme Scenario**: If Sino-US relations deteriorate sharply and lead to a complete decoupling, Shenzhou’s global layout may not be enough to cope with it.
4. **Changes in consumption paradigm**: For example, the demand for sportswear shrinks for a long time (the possibility is extremely low but needs to be considered)
5. **Single major customer slashes orders**: Nike itself has faced growth difficulties in recent years. If it continues to reduce its outsourcing ratio, the impact cannot be ignored.
