# II. Corporate cultural assessment

#2.1 Employees-led culture

Ma Jianyong has a frequently cited view: "Sen's biggest asset is the employee." This is not a hollow word for a labour-intensive enterprise with about 80,000 employees, but rather a real input:

- Annual wage increases of 10-13 per cent, lasting for many years
- Provision of spring charter vehicles for six consecutive years, at an annual cost of approximately $17 million
- 100 million yuan in 2016 to build the largest intra-business kitchen distribution centre in Zhedong
- Hundreds of millions of dollars per year are invested in improving the living environment of employees

The result is:** The mobility rate of people is only 3.04%**, which is extremely rare in manufacturing. Low mobility means that workers have accumulated experience, increased productivity and lower training costs - all of which are "invisible" but extremely important competitive advantages.

#2.2 Practical low-key style

Ma Jianyong rarely gives media interviews, and companies rarely do branding. This pragmatic style is highly compatible with his manufacturing genes -- spending on products and customers, not on the "studies" of capital markets.

In contrast, many A-stock companies are keen on conceptual and cross-border acquisitions, and Shinzhou International has been focusing on knitting for more than 20 years, never departing from the main industry. This "do nothing" fix is often more important than "do what."

#2.3 Cultural risk

The other side of family culture is "exclusivity." The core management comes almost entirely from within the family or from early entrepreneurial partners, and the way to the growth of external talent is open. Is intercultural management adequate as business becomes globalized (Viet Nam, Cambodian factories)?

# Three, risk panorama

#3.1 Client concentration risk # (high concern)

** Status**: 80.7% of the revenues were received by four major clients (Nek, Adi, Uygur, Bhor).

** Bipolar analysis**:

Positive - Four clients are top brands worldwide, with high stability and low risk of default. Deep partnerships make the transition costly and the client is sticky.

Conversely, if any client were to cut orders significantly (e.g. Nike’s growth slowed in recent years, with a 3.9% drop in the 2024 Nike order year-on-year) there would be a significant impact on Shinzhou’s collection and capacity utilization. The 80% concentration means that Shinzhou’s fate depends largely on the brand’s business decisions.

** Depressive **: The strong growth in 2024 in the Queen's Clothes and Adi has made up for Nike's decline, suggesting that the client portfolio has some complementarities. Companies are also actively developing “other customers” (about 19%), but it is difficult to change structures significantly in the short term.

#3.2 Geopolitics and tariff risks (high concern)

- The decline in the Māori rate by 1.8 percentage points in 2025 was partly due to the United States share of the cost of customs duties

#3.3 New plant risk of climbing slopes

The new plant is facing:
- The recruitment and training of workers takes time
- Production efficiency at mature plant levels usually takes 1-2 years
- Inadequate allocation of fixed costs during the slope period, which has resulted in a loss of profit margin

This is one of the reasons for the decline in the Māori rate in 2025, but it is a short-term risk that can be controlled.

#3.4 Risk of labour cost escalation <unk> (medium concern)

Labour costs are rising continuously in China and Viet Nam and Cambodia. The annual wage increase in Shinzhou is 10-13%. Although it has been partially absorbed through efficiency gains and scale expansion, the rigid increase in labour costs will continue to shrink profit margins over the long term.

This is a structural challenge for manufacturing that cannot be avoided. The only long-term solution is automation — but it is slow to automate the textile industry (especially the stitching chain).

#3.5 Exchange rate risk # (low-medium concern)

The renminbi’s appreciation will reduce profits (the same dollar income will be exchanged for less) and the renminbi will be depreciated. Companies have a natural hedge (Viet Nam’s cost is denominated in local currency), but exchange rate fluctuations will affect quarterly profits.

# 3.6 ESG and compliance risk (low concern)

As a core supplier of global brands, Shinzhou has maintained high standards of labour rights and environmental compliance. Negative events (e.g. labour disputes, environmental violations) may lead to a reduction in orders by brand parties.

# V. Management and risk combined

#5.1 Does management have confidence?

** Conclusion: highly credible, but with attention to the limitations of family governance**

Basis of trust:
- Over 53 per cent of management holdings with high interest binding
- Business records for more than 20 years, never deviated from the main business
- Continuous staff input and low mobility confirm the convergence of management concepts
- The balance sheet is extremely solid, uninvited, unradical.

Attention is needed:
- The ceiling for family corporate governance - how to introduce and motivate outside talent
- Ma Jianyong personally has a huge influence on the company.
- The succession arrangement (double CEO architecture) has been completed initially, but the operational effectiveness of the new architecture will take time to validate

#5.2 Are the risks manageable?

** Conclusion: Main risks are manageable but tail risks exist**

Short term (1-2): New plant landslides and tariff shocks are the main drags, but they are both predictable and manageable. The return in 2026 was a rough one.

Medium-term (3-5 years): Client concentration and geopolitics are core risks, but the global organization of companies and the efforts of client diversification are in the right direction.

Long term (5-10 years): Automated technology change and succession governance are the most uncertain. Neither of these factors is fully controlled by Shinzhou and requires continuous follow-up.
