# Analysis of Shenzhou International (2313.HK) business model

> Analysis Dimension: Business Nature and Moat Depth
> Core question: Is this a good business? How long will a moat last?

---

## 1. The essence of business model

### 1.1 Positioning of "TSMC in Textile Industry"

Shenzhou International's business model can be summarized in one sentence: **Provide one-stop vertically integrated OEM services from fabric research and development to garment production** for the world's top sports brands. This is not a simple "processing with supplied materials", but an ODM (Original Design Manufacturer) model that is deeply embedded in the brand's product development chain.

Traditional OEMs (OEMs) only do "construction according to drawings", with meager profits and easy to be replaced. Shenzhou International began its transformation to ODM in 1997. The core breakthrough lies in its fabric research and development capabilities. The company has developed Dri-FIT moisture-wicking fabrics, TechFleece technical velvet fabrics, and Flyknit upper fly-woven fabrics for Nike, and has developed AIRism series fabrics for Uniqlo. These fabric technologies have become the brand's iconic product lines, which in turn deeply bind the brand to Shenzhou.

As of the end of 2024, the company has more than 143 new material fabric patents, and fabric research and development capabilities are the core competitive barrier.

### 1.2 Complete chain of vertical integration

Shenzhou International's production chain covers: **weaving-dying and finishing-cutting-printing and embroidery-sewing** the entire process. This vertical integration brings three key advantages:

- **Efficiency Advantage**: Internal collaboration reduces intermediate links, and the delivery cycle is 30%-40% faster than the industry average
- **Quality Control**: Full chain quality control, yield rate is higher than industry level
- **Cost Advantage**: Internalized intermediate profits, a high proportion of self-supplied fabrics, and comprehensive costs significantly lower than the decentralized outsourcing model

Fabric weaving is the link with the highest technical barriers and the greatest added value in the entire chain. There are only a handful of OEM companies in the world that can independently develop fabrics. This is the fundamental reason why Shenzhou’s gross profit margin can be maintained at 26%-31%.

### 1.3 Foresight in production capacity layout

The global layout of the company's production base has a strategic vision:

| Region | Capacity share | Main functions |
|------|---------|---------|
| China (Ningbo/Anhui) | About 47% | Fabric production (50%) + ready-made garments |
| Vietnam | About 27% | Fabric production (50%) + ready-made garments |
| Cambodia | About 26% | Mainly ready-made garments, new base is expanding production |

Among ready-made garment production capacity, overseas accounts for 55%. This layout reflects extremely high strategic value in the context of Sino-US trade frictions and tariff barriers. After the new base in Cambodia is put into operation in 2025, it is expected to increase production capacity by 10%, and the total annual production capacity will reach 550 million pieces.

## 2. Moat analysis

### 2.1 Barriers to fabric research and development ★★★★★

Fabric research and development is the core moat of Shenzhou. Fabric weaving is a technology-intensive industry that requires long-term process accumulation and equipment investment. Even if competitors know the direction, it will be difficult for them to copy it in the short term. Once a brand's core fabric technology is successfully developed in Shenzhou and forms a series of product lines, the transfer cost is extremely high - this is not a problem that can be solved by changing suppliers, and involves a large amount of tacit knowledge such as fabric formula, process parameters, production line debugging, etc.

**Positive evidence**: Iconic fabrics such as Nike Dri-FIT, TechFleece, Flyknit, and Uniqlo AIRism were all developed by Shenzhou. These product lines contributed a large amount of sales to the brand, forming a strong interdependence.

**Risk factors**: If the brand invests in building its own fabric research and development capabilities (the possibility is very low and does not conform to the light asset strategy), disruptive new material technology may emerge.

### 2.2 Scale and efficiency barriers ★★★★☆

The global annual production capacity is more than 500 million pieces, with significant scale effect. More importantly, the company's operating cash flow/revenue ratio averaged 19.21%, significantly ahead of the industry average of 12%, indicating that scale advantages have been transformed into tangible cash flow creation capabilities.

There are about 80,000 employees, and the average annual salary increase is 10%-13%, but the turnover rate is only 3.04% (2020 data), which is far lower than the industry average. Low turnover means the accumulation of experience among skilled workers, which is a core source of manufacturing efficiency.

### 2.3 Customer relationship barriers ★★★★☆

The four major customers (Nike, Adidas, Uniqlo, and Puma) contributed 80.7% of revenue, and the cooperative relationships have exceeded 15 years. Customers rely on Shenzhou not only for its production capacity, but also for its comprehensive capabilities in fabric research and development collaboration, rapid response, and quality assurance.

**Positive**: The four major customers are all the world's top brands, with high order stability, and Shenzhou's purchasing share in each brand continues to increase. In 2024, the revenue share of the four major customers will increase by 1.1 percentage points compared with the previous year.

**Hidden concerns**: Excessive customer concentration is a double-edged sword (see risk analysis section for details).

### 2.4 Environmental Compliance Barriers ★★★★☆

The environmental protection threshold of the textile industry continues to increase. Shenzhou's long-term investment in wastewater treatment, energy conservation and emission reduction has created compliance barriers. Small and medium-sized OEM companies have been eliminated due to environmental protection costs, and the concentration of orders towards leading companies has become a long-term trend. In the context of increasingly stringent global ESG requirements, brands are more inclined to choose suppliers with excellent ESG performance, and Shenzhou will continue to benefit from its first-mover advantage.

## 3. "Good" and "bad" business models

### Good things

1. **Stable demand side**: Sportswear is a long-term trend in consumption upgrading and has certain functional and rigid needs.
2. **The competitive landscape is clear**: Industry concentration continues to increase, and the leading share expands (Shenzhou’s global market share is only 3.8%, and there is still room for improvement)
3. **Light capital and high returns**: ROE maintained at 15%-18% for a long time, asset-liability ratio only 32%, no major financing needs
4. **Excellent Cash Flow**: High matching between operating cash flow and net profit, and excellent profit quality
5. **Generous dividend payout**: The dividend payout rate has been 55%-61% in recent years, and shareholders have a strong sense of return.

### Bad aspects

1. **It’s still an OEM business**: Even for the most high-end OEM, the bargaining power is still limited by the downstream brands and they cannot set their own prices.
2. **Labour-intensive nature remains unchanged**: The management complexity of 80,000 people is high, and labor costs are rising rigidly.
3. **Growth ceiling**: The scale of the global sportswear OEM market is limited, and Shenzhou is already the largest. The growth rate will gradually slow down in the future.
4. **Geopolitical Risk**: Production capacity is distributed in China, Vietnam, and Cambodia, all of which are sensitive areas for Sino-US competition.

---

## 4. Judgment of business model sustainability

### Core question: Can OEM companies continue to create excess returns?

From a historical perspective, Shenzhou International has spent 20 years proving that "high-end OEM" can achieve a net profit rate that exceeds that of the brand (Shenzhou's net profit rate in 2024 is 21.8%, higher than Nike's approximately 12%). Its core lies in: the irreplaceability of fabric research and development + the efficiency advantage of vertical integration + the cost dilution brought about by economies of scale.

However, there is a signal worth paying attention to in the performance in 2025: **The gross profit margin dropped from 28.1% to 26.3%, and the net profit fell by 6.7% year-on-year**. Reasons include rising employee wages, ramp-up costs for new Cambodian factories, and U.S. tariff sharing. This reminds us that the foundry model has limited buffering capacity in the face of rising costs - you can optimize efficiency, but it is difficult to completely pass on the cost to the brand.

### Long-term outlook

Short to medium term (3-5 years): The business model remains solid. The global sportswear industry continues to grow, the trend of orders concentrating on leaders remains unchanged, and the release of new production capacity in Cambodia will bring about growth. Revenue is expected to reach 34.8 billion yuan in 2026, with net profit recovering to more than 7.4 billion yuan.

Long-term (5-10 years): It is necessary to pay attention to the impact of automation technology on labor-intensive manufacturing, brand supply chain diversification strategies, and the impact of geopolitics on production capacity layout. Shenzhou's fabric research and development barriers are its most durable moat, but its competitive advantages in manufacturing may be eroded by technological changes.

Fabric R&D barriers are the core moat, which is more important than scale and efficiency. What investors need to judge is whether this moat will widen or narrow over the next 10 years.
