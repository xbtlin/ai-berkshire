# Seven, Lee Siu's perspective core judgement

Lee Siu's investment philosophy emphasizes three cores: (1) understanding the "frontiers of knowledge"; (2) investing in his own "competence circle"; and (3) investing in a "buy business" rather than "buy stock".

** If this is the framework for looking at the drug Mind:

# "The boundaries of knowledge"

The ultimate enforcement of the BIOSECURE Act is beyond our "knowledge boundaries." The OMB list, the White House attitude, the direction of the US-China relationship -- these variables are beyond the ability of any corporate analyst to predict. When the core variables are unknown, the way Li will be to demand a greater margin of security, or simply not to do it.

# "Cluster of Capability"

The business of Pharmacério Condé is understandable in itself – outsourcing research and development and production for pharmaceutical companies. Its fate is increasingly dependent on political decision-making in Washington rather than on the efficiency of a non-tiny laboratory.** When an investment is largely political, not commercial, it deviates from the circle of competence.**

# The way "buying the business" feels #

If you think of the "buy the whole drug" mentality: would you like to spend $330 billion (current market value) on a business that makes $13 billion a year, and that has an order of $58 billion, but faces legal restrictions in the United States?

** The answer depends on your judgement on the BIOSECURE Act**:

- If you think that the bill will not substantially affect the drug Mindkandre (20-30% probability), the current valuation is reasonable
- If you think the bill would cause a 20-30% loss in US business (50-60% probability), the current valuation is high
- If you think that China-American decoupling will undermine the drug Mind-Kander business model (<5% probability), any price is unsafe.

# Final evaluation

** Management assessment: <unk> - overcapacity but insufficient trust**

Li Gu, an outstanding entrepreneur and strategist, created the most competitive CXO platform in China and the world. But the continued substantial decline in ownership makes it difficult for investors to see him as a manager "in full alignment with shareholders' interests." Together, these risks require investors to enter at a sufficiently low price.
