# Analysis of the business model of Cheung Kong Infrastructure (1038.HK)

## 1. The essence of business

CK Infrastructure is in the business of owning and operating regulated infrastructure assets - power grids, gas distribution networks, water, transport, etc. The income from these assets has a rate of return set by government regulators, similar to "capital guaranteed financing" - the rate of return is not high but extremely stable.

From Duan Yongping's perspective, this is a "low return but high certainty" business: you don't need innovation, marketing, or bargaining with customers. Regulators guarantee that you will earn a return on assets of 6-8% per year. The price is: excess returns are never possible.

## 2. Moat assessment

**Rating: ★★★★☆ (4/5)**

### Existing moat:
1. **Natural monopoly**: Infrastructure such as power grids and gas distribution networks are natural monopolies - a city does not need two sets of power grids
2. **Regulatory Barriers**: Obtaining a utility operating license is extremely difficult
3. **Assets cannot be copied**: Built infrastructure assets cannot be rebuilt by latecomers
4. **Long-term contract lock-in**: Most assets have a 20-50 year franchise period

### Moat defects:
1. The rate of return is locked in by regulations – even if you do well, you can’t make more
2. Assets are dependent on the policies of the country in which they are located - if the regulatory framework changes, returns may be depressed
3. Growth can only come from acquiring new assets – organic growth is limited

## 3. Core logic of business model

Cheung Kong Infrastructure's "money-making formula":
1. Raise low-cost capital (leverage high credit rating and Yangtze River Group’s financing capabilities)
2. Acquisition of regulated infrastructure assets (buy at reasonable prices)
3. Operate and obtain returns permitted by regulations
4. Obtain additional profits through efficiency improvements
5. Stable dividend distribution to shareholders

This is a game of "capital allocation + operational efficiency" rather than an "innovation-driven" game.

## 4. User value analysis

The "users" of Yangtze River Infrastructure are millions of households and businesses in the UK, Australia and Canada - they pay monthly water, electricity and gas bills. User characteristics: no choice (monopoly), must use, price set by regulators.

From the perspective of Duan Yongping's "user value": Yangtze River Infrastructure does not need to "make users happy" to make money. It only needs "normal operations without incident" to make stable profits.

## 5. Whether management is "doing the right thing"

### Very correct decision:
1. Continuously increasing distributions for 28 years: Very few companies can achieve this
2. Globalization and diversification: expanding from Hong Kong to the United Kingdom, Australia, Canada, and Europe
3. Countercyclical acquisitions: buying when others are fearful
4. Sell at the right time: It is recommended to sell UKPN in 2025 and lock in profits when the price is high

The Li Ka-shing/Li Zeju family has demonstrated a strong long-term thinking in the management of the infrastructure business.

## 6. Is it a good business?

**Rating: ★★★★☆ (3.5/5)**

Advantages: extremely high certainty, natural monopoly moat, inflation hedging, low operating risk. Disadvantages: The rate of return is locked at 6-8%, growth relies on acquisitions, and the asset-heavy model.

Duan Yongping's possible evaluation: This is a "sleep-worthy" business - but not an "exciting" business.

## 7. Core Conclusions

Cheung Kong Infrastructure is a "super bond" - a deterministic return similar to long-term government bonds (4-5% dividend rate + 2-3% growth), but lacks the upside flexibility that equity investments should have. Best suited for conservative investors seeking stable cash flow. The core problem is valuation: PE 20 times is too expensive for ROE 6%.
