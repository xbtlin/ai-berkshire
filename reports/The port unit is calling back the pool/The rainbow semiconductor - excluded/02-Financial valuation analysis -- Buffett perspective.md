# Hua Hong Semiconductor (1347.HK) – Financial Valuation Analysis

> Analysis dimensions: financial quality, profitability trend, capital allocation efficiency, valuation rationality, safety margin

---

## 1. Financial panoramic scan

### 1.1 Revenue growth trend

| Annual | Revenue (100 million US dollars) | YoY growth | Gross profit margin | Net profit margin |
|------|---------------|---------|--------|--------|
| 2023 | 19.29 | -8.5% | 16.2% | 10.5% |
| 2024 | 20.04 | +3.9% | 10.2% | 2.8% |
| 2025 | 24.02 | +19.9% | 11.8% | ~2% |
| 2026E | 28.49 | +18.6% | 16.1% | ~7.7% |

**Key Findings**:
- Revenue growth momentum is strong, maintaining a growth rate of nearly 20% in 2025-2026
- But the performance on the profit side is extremely poor: despite significant revenue growth, net profit margin plummeted from 10.5% in 2023 to about 2% in 2025
- The reason is clear: huge depreciation during FAB9’s capacity ramp-up period (estimated annual depreciation of US$170-180 million) erodes profits

### 1.2 In-depth analysis of profitability

**Gross profit margin breakdown**:
- 8-inch production line (mature and stable): gross profit margin is about 30%, but there is limited room for capacity growth
- 12-inch production line (FAB9 new line): just started above the break-even line, the target is increased to 10%
- Mixed gross profit margin is dragged down by new lines, overall only 11.8% (2025)

**Why is the net profit margin so low**:
- Q1 net profit in 2025 will fall by 89.73% year-on-year, even if revenue increases by 18.66%
- Huge depreciation is the main reason: the total investment in FAB9 is approximately US$6.7 billion, which is depreciated over 10-15 years, and the annual depreciation amount is huge.
- This is a "necessary pain" in the period of capacity expansion - spend money first and make money later

### 1.3 ROE analysis

The current ROE is about 1-2%, which is extremely unsatisfactory. Break it down:
- Net profit margin: ~2% (depreciation pressure)
- Asset turnover rate: low (characteristics of asset-heavy industries)
- Leverage ratio: Moderate (interest-bearing liabilities are controllable)

**ROE recovery path**:
- In 2026, as FAB9 utilization rate increases, the net interest rate is expected to return to 7-8%
- If gross profit margin increases to 20%+ in 2027-2028, ROE is expected to rise to 8-10%
- But even if it recovers completely, this is not a high ROE business (TSMC ROE is about 25-30%)

## 2. Capital allocation efficiency

### 2.1 Capital expenditure cycle

Huahong is in the midst of its largest capital expenditure cycle in history:
- FAB9 Phase I investment: approximately US$6.7 billion
- 2024-2025 is the peak period of capital expenditure
-Capital expenditure is expected to decline in 2026 and enter the "harvest period"

### 2.2 Free cash flow

| Annual | Operating cash flow | Capital expenditures | Free cash flow |
|------|-------------|---------|-----------|
| 2024 | Positive | Huge | Huge Negative |
| 2025 | Positive growth | High | Still negative |
| 2026E | Continued growth | Decline | Expected to turn positive |

**Key Judgment**: The company is currently in the "cash burning" stage, with negative free cash flow. As a value investor, this means that DCF cannot be used to give a reliable valuation and can only be judged based on expectations of future earnings recovery.

### 2.3 Balance sheet health

- The company is a state-owned enterprise (controlled by Shanghai State-owned Assets Supervision and Administration Commission) with strong financing capabilities
- Obtain sufficient funds through A+H dual listing
- The interest-bearing debt ratio is controllable and there is no short-term debt repayment risk
- But attention needs to be paid: If production capacity ramps up less than expected, depreciation + interest may continue to erode net profits

## 3. Valuation analysis

### 3.1 Current Valuation Level

| Indicators | Current Value | Industry Average | Judgment |
|------|--------|---------|------|
| Dynamic PE | ~52 times | 40-45 times | High |
| PB | ~5.86 times | 3-4 times | Obviously high |
| PS | ~8 times | 5-6 times | High |
| EV/EBITDA | ~25x | 15-20x | High |

The stock price of Hong Kong stocks is approximately HK$141 (May 7, 2026), corresponding to a market value of approximately RMB 220 billion.

### 3.2 Is the valuation reasonable?

**Opposite view (valuation is reasonable/low)**:
1. The current low profit margin is temporary and the depreciation peak will pass.
2. After the ramp-up of production capacity is completed, revenue may double while depreciation remains basically unchanged.
3. Domestic alternative tracks provide reasonable valuation premiums
4. Benchmark SMIC’s PB valuation level
5. Analysts have given a target price of 152 yuan (A shares), which means there is still room for upside.

**Opposite view (valuation is too high)**:
1. 52 times PE corresponds to an ROE of less than 2%, and investors pay for the "future"
2. Even if profits return to normal levels (assuming net profit of US$400 million in 2028), the PE implied by the current market value will still exceed 30 times
3. The long-term ROE ceiling of mature process OEMs is limited (refer to UMC’s long-term ROE of about 12-15%)
4. Equipment supply interruptions as geopolitical risks intensify may delay production capacity ramp-up
5. Mainland China’s mature process capacity is being released intensively, and ASP may face downward pressure.

### 3.3 Scenario Valuation

**Optimistic scenario (normal profit status in 2028)**:
- Revenue of US$4 billion, net profit margin of 12%, and net profit of US$480 million
- Give 25 times PE → market value of US$12 billion → corresponding to the current market value, there is some room for upside
- Annualized rate of return is about 15-20%

**Neutral Scenario**:
- Revenue of US$3.5 billion, net profit margin of 8%, and net profit of US$280 million
- Give 20 times PE → market value of US$5.6 billion → correspond to current market value basically parity
- Annualized rate of return is about 0-5%

**Pessimistic Scenario**:
- Overcapacity has led to a decline in ASP, and equipment supply interruptions have delayed FAB9’s climb
- Revenue of US$3 billion, net profit margin of 5%, net profit of US$150 million
- Give 15 times PE → Market value is US$2.25 billion → There is significant downside risk corresponding to the current market value
- Negative annualized return

## 4. Safety Margin Assessment

### 4.1 Net asset support

PB 5.86 times means that the stock price is much higher than the net assets. From a liquidation perspective, there is no margin of safety. However, liquidation valuation methods are generally not applicable to semiconductor companies.

### 4.2 Profit certainty

- The certainty of revenue growth is relatively high (production capacity is on hand, domestic substitution trend is determined)
- But the certainty of profit is low (depending on the recovery speed of gross profit margin, ASP trend, and competitive landscape)
- Key time node: After FAB9 reaches full production capacity in the second half of 2026, can we see a profit inflection point?

### 4.3 Credibility of management guidance

- The company guides Q2 gross profit margin to be 15-17%, continuing to improve quarter-on-quarter
- Historically, company guidance has been delivered relatively well
- But there is great uncertainty in long-term profit forecasts

## 7. Core Conclusions

From a strict value investment perspective, Hua Hong Semiconductor currently does not have sufficient safety margin:

1. **"Use today's high price to buy tomorrow's profits"**: 52 times PE to buy a company with an ROE of only 1-2% requires extremely high confidence in future profit recovery
2. **The profit turning point has not yet been confirmed**: Although gross profit margin is improving quarter by quarter, the path from 12% to 20% is not linear.
3. **The best buying point may have passed or not yet**: If you buy when the stock price is low in 2024 (capacity expansion is confirmed but valuation is lower), the return will be better; if you wait until the profit inflection point is confirmed before buying, the risk will be lower
4. **Valuation trap in asset-heavy industries**: High PE does not mean growth, it may just be an illusion that profits are temporarily suppressed by depreciation - the key depends on whether the steady-state PE is still attractive after profits recover.

**If you must invest**, the reasonable strategy is: wait for confirmation of the profit turning point (gross profit margin exceeds 18% for 2-3 consecutive quarters), or look for opportunities in panic decline caused by geopolitical risks. The risk-benefit ratio at current prices is less than ideal.
