# Hua Hong Semiconductor (1347.HK)—Business Model Analysis

> Analysis dimensions: nature of business, user value, moat, pricing power, business model sustainability

---

## 1. What kind of business is this?

### 1.1 The essence of business model

Huahong Semiconductor is a pure wafer foundry company (Foundry). It does not design chips and is only responsible for manufacturing chips according to customer design drawings. Specifically, it focuses on "featured processes" - that is, it does not pursue the most advanced processes (7nm, 5nm, etc.), but maximizes the physical characteristics of power devices, memories, analog chips and other products by optimizing device structures and manufacturing processes in mature processes of 55nm and above.

The core logic of this positioning is: not all chips require advanced processes. Chips in areas such as automotive electronics, industrial control, power management, and the Internet of Things focus more on reliability, cost, and specific performance indicators than on transistor density.

### 1.2 Revenue model

The company earns revenue by charging wafer processing fees to IC design companies. There are three main drivers of revenue:
- **Production Capacity Scale**: How many wafers are produced per month?
- **Capacity Utilization**: Equipment operating rate
- **Average Selling Price (ASP)**: The processing unit prices of different process platforms are different

Full-year revenue in 2025 is US$2.402 billion, a year-on-year increase of 19.9%, mainly driven by FAB9 production capacity ramping and ASP improvement.

### 1.3 Customer Structure

By end market (2023 data):
- Consumer electronics: 56.6% (the proportion is too high and there are cyclical risks)
- Industrial and automotive electronics: 29.5% (high-value fields, increasing share)
- Communications: 11.2%
- Calculation: 2.7%

**Key Observation**: Consumer electronics accounts for 64.3%, resulting in large fluctuations in the company's revenue, and gross profit margin is easily affected by downstream prosperity. In contrast, UMC has achieved a more stable gross profit margin of 35%+ by binding automotive customers such as Infineon and TI. Huahong is transforming into automotive and industrial directions, but the transformation takes time.

## 2. User value and customer stickiness

### 2.1 Why do customers choose Huahong?

1. **The most comprehensive coverage of featured processes**: Huahong is the wafer foundry company with the most comprehensive coverage of featured process platforms in the industry, meeting customers’ foundry needs for a variety of chips in one stop
2. **Leading power device foundry capabilities**: The world’s largest 12-inch power device foundry base (FAB7, 94,500 pieces/month)
3. **Automotive-grade certification**: 20 years of automotive-grade chip foundry experience, passed AEC-Q100 Grade0 certification
4. **Cost-performance**: Compared with TSMC, UMC, etc., Huahong has a cost advantage in mature manufacturing processes.
5. **Geosecurity**: For IC design companies in mainland China, choosing local foundries is an imperative for supply chain security.

### 2.2 Customer switching cost

The customer switching costs of foundry are higher:
- From design to mass production, chips need to go through process adaptation, tape-out verification, reliability testing, etc., which usually takes 12-18 months.
- The certification cycle for automotive-grade chips is longer, usually taking 2-3 years
- Once mass production is stable, customers will not easily change foundries

**But please note**: In mature process fields, customers may also allocate orders among multiple foundries in order to spread risks. Hua Hong's customer stickiness is not as "irreplaceable" as TSMC's in the advanced process field.

## 3. Moat assessment

### 3.1 Scale and cost advantages ★★★☆☆

Huahong’s production capacity ranks second in mainland China, but there is still a gap compared with global leaders:
- TSMC: monthly production exceeds 2 million 12-inch equivalent wafers
- SMIC: Production capacity in 2026 is expected to be 1.17 million pieces/month
- Huahong: about 300,000 pieces/month (including 8-inch and 12-inch)

Insufficient scale means limited unit cost allocation capabilities, especially during the ramp-up period of FAB9, where depreciation pressure is huge.

### 3.2 Technical Barriers ★★★★☆

The technical barrier of characteristic craftsmanship lies in "knowing what it is and why it is what it is":
- Different from the pursuit of line width reduction in advanced processes, special processes require an in-depth understanding of device physics
- Huahong has accumulated 20+ years of process knowledge base in IGBT, super junction MOSFET, embedded memory and other fields
- A total of 10,360 patent applications and 4,900+ authorizations
- R&D investment accounts for 11.53% of revenue, with continuous iteration

### 3.3 Differentiated positioning ★★★★☆

Huahong has successfully avoided head-on competition with TSMC and Samsung in advanced manufacturing processes, and has also formed a certain differentiation from SMIC:
- SMIC: Wider coverage, covering everything from mature to advanced processes
- Huahong: Focus on unique processes, especially power devices
- This focus strategy allows Huahong to establish deeper competitive barriers in niche areas

### 3.4 Customer Relationship ★★★☆☆

It has established in-depth cooperative relations with leading power device design companies such as Star Semiconductor to jointly develop automotive-grade IGBT chips. However, there is still room for improvement in overall customer concentration and binding depth.

## 4. Pricing power analysis

### 4.1 Current pricing power: weak

Looking at Huahong’s pricing power from the perspective of gross profit margin:
- Gross profit margin for 2025 is only 11.8%
- Compare TSMC (55%+), UMC (35%+), SMIC (15-20%)
- Huahong’s pricing power is at a relatively low level in the foundry industry

Reason analysis:
1. There are many competitors in mature manufacturing processes. SMIC, UMC, and GlobalFoundries are all competing for the same customers.
2. Consumer electronics customers are highly price sensitive
3. FAB9’s production capacity ramp-up period requires exchanging volume for price and sacrificing short-term profit margins.
4. Mainland China’s peers have expanded their production capacity on a large scale and competition on the supply side has intensified.

### 4.2 Room for improvement of pricing power in the future

- Car-grade chip certification has high barriers and strong customer stickiness, which can support higher ASP
- Power devices (IGBT, SiC) are in tight supply and demand
- Under the background of domestic substitution, customers are less sensitive to price and pay more attention to supply security.

## 5. Business model sustainability assessment

### 5.1 Positive factors

1. **High certainty of domestic substitution**: IC design companies in mainland China are growing rapidly, and the demand for local foundry capacity is rigid.
2. **Terminal markets continue to expand**: The demand for power devices such as new energy vehicles, photovoltaics, and energy storage has exploded.
3. **Technical routes are not easily obsolete**: Featured processes do not have the iteration anxiety of Moore’s Law, and a production line can run for 10-20 years.
4. **The peak of capital expenditure will pass**: The main investment of FAB9 has been completed, and the subsequent period is the production capacity ramp-up period.

### 5.2 Negative factors

1. **Capital-intensive business**: Wafer foundry is a typical asset-heavy industry, and ROE may be limited in the long term.
2. **Overcapacity Risk**: Mainland China’s mature process production capacity is being released intensively, and it may face oversupply from 2025 to 2027.
3. **Technical ceiling**: Although the characteristic technology is stable, the growth space is limited and it is difficult to achieve exponential growth.
4. **Geopolitical Risk**: The United States will cut off the supply of equipment to Huahong in April 2026. Although it mainly affects advanced manufacturing processes, the signal is negative.

## 7. Core Conclusions

Hua Hong Semiconductor's business model itself is healthy - the foundry model emphasizes R&D and manufacturing, customer switching costs are high, and terminal demand has long-term growth logic. But it faces a fundamental problem: **This is a business that requires scale to make money, and Huahong is currently in the stage of "spending money to expand production capacity but not making money yet"**.

If we look at it from Duan Yongping's perspective: "Can this business earn excess profits in the long term?" the answer is uncertain. Although special processes have avoided the arms race of advanced processes, the competition for mature processes is equally fierce. Huahong's differentiated positioning deserves recognition, but before it truly establishes a car-level customer matrix and achieves high ASP coverage, it is more like an investment target that "has a good story but has not yet materialized."

Key observation points: Whether the gross profit margin can be increased from the current 12-14% to 20%+ in the next 2-3 years will be the core indicator to verify whether its business model can "make money".
