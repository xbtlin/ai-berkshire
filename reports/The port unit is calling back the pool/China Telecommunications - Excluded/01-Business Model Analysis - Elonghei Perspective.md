# China Telecom (0728.HK) business model analysis

## 1. The essence of business

The essence of China Telecom is an **infrastructure operator** - laying out networks (fiber, base stations, data centers), and then charging users monthly usage fees. This is a business of "heavy investment in the early stage and continuous harvest in the later stage".

From Duan Yongping's perspective, communications operators are a "not too good, not too bad" business:
- **The good side**: Once users are connected, they are sticky (the cost of changing numbers is high), their income is predictable, and their cash flow is stable.
- **The bad side**: Huge investment in heavy assets, rapid technology iteration (3G → 4G → 5G → 6G each generation requires a large amount of new investment), frequent price wars

## 2. Moat assessment

**Rating: ★★★☆☆ (3.5/5)**

### Existing moat:
1. **License Barrier**: There are only 3 full-service operators in China (Telecom/Mobile/China Unicom), making it almost impossible for new entrants
2. **Network effect (weak)**: The more users there are, the lower the unit cost of network coverage and service investment
3. **Switching Cost**: Mobile phone numbers have become "digital identities", and the psychological and practical costs for users to change numbers are high.
4. **Infrastructure comes first**: Once fiber-to-the-home and base station deployment are completed, copying costs for latecomers will be extremely high

### Moat defects:
1. The products of the three operators are highly homogeneous and difficult to differentiate.
2. At the policy level, "speed increase and fee reduction" continue to drive down tariff levels.
3. The return period of 5G/6G investment is uncertain

## 3. Business structure evolution

China Telecom is undergoing a transformation from a "traditional pipeline operator" to a "technology service provider":

| Business Segments | 2025 Revenue | Strategic Positioning |
|----------|-----------|----------|
| Mobile communications | ~200 billion yuan | Stock base |
| Fixed broadband | ~110 billion yuan | Stable cash cow |
| Tianyi Cloud | 120.7 billion yuan | Core growth points |
| Industrial digitalization | 147.3 billion yuan | Second growth curve |
| AIDC | 34.5 billion yuan | Future directions |

Key changes: Tianyi Cloud + industrial digitalization + AIDC total approximately 300 billion yuan, accounting for more than 60% of service revenue. This shows that China Telecom is no longer just a "phone" company.

## 4. User value and differentiation

### Traditional business (C-side)
For individual users, there is almost no difference between the three operators. When users choose telecommunications or mobile, they mainly look at:
1. Signal coverage (China Mobile>Telecom>China Unicom)
2. Tariff plan
3. Historical path (too lazy to change numbers)

Duan Yongping said, "Users will tell their friends to use your product" - in the field of operators, no user will enthusiastically recommend "using China Telecom's package".

### Cloud/Digital Business (B-side)
The differentiation of Tianyi Cloud lies in:
1. **Security Compliance**: Government and enterprise customers trust clouds with central enterprise background more.
2. **Localized services**: Service advantages in third- and fourth-tier cities
3. **Network and cloud integration**: Can provide an integrated solution of "network + cloud"

This is the most promising direction for China Telecom.

## 5. Whether management is "doing the right thing"

### Correct direction:
1. Vigorously develop Tianyi Cloud and AIDC business to seize the explosive demand for computing power in the AI era
2. Reduce capital expenditure by 27% (in the first half of 2025) and shift to more efficient investment
3. Maintain a high dividend payout ratio of 75%
4. Lay out satellite communications (obtain satellite mobile communications license)

### Questions to be verified:
1. Can Tianyi Cloud maintain its share under the attack of Alibaba Cloud and Huawei Cloud?
2. What is the rate of return on AIDC investment? Will it become a new "5G investment trap"?
3. The value and sustainability of smart revenue (12.3 billion yuan)

## 6. Is it a good business?

**Rating: ★★★☆☆ (3/5)**

Communications operators are "general businesses" in Duan Yongping's framework:
- Not asset-light – requires substantial ongoing capital expenditure
- There is a certain stickiness - but not because of "like" but because of "too lazy to change"
- Stable cash flow - suitable for being a "money printing machine"
- Limited growth - the demographic dividend disappears and it is difficult to increase ARPU

But China Telecom has an interesting transformation story: If Tianyi Cloud and AIDC businesses are really successful, it will be upgraded from a "low ROE infrastructure provider" to a "medium ROE technology service provider." Whether this transformation is successful or not determines whether it is a 2-point business or a 3.5-point business.

## 7. Core Conclusions

China Telecom is a "public utility in transition". The current core question is: How much valuation premium does the market give to its "technical content"? If we still value it based on public utilities (PE 10-12 times), the current PE of 16 times is too expensive; if we value it based on the technology transformation story (PE 20-25 times), there is still room for it. The key lies in whether Tianyi Cloud and AIDC can continue to realize their growth.
