# CSPC Pharmaceutical Group: Risk and Management Assessment

> Analysis dimensions: management quality, governance structure, key risks, human factors of safety margin
> Core question: Is management trustworthy? What risks could result in permanent capital loss?

---

## 1. Management portrait: from founder to second generation successor

### Cai Dongchen (Founder/Chairman of the Board)

- **Background**: MBA from Nankai University, executive director since 1997
- **Contribution**: Building CSPC from a local pharmaceutical factory in Shijiazhuang to one of the top 15 pharmaceutical companies in the world in terms of pipeline scale
- **Style**: A practical entrepreneur, low-key and pragmatic, focusing on R&D investment

Cai Dongchen spent nearly 30 years to complete the "from 0 to 1" of CSPC. He accumulated manufacturing and sales capabilities during the turbulent period of China's pharmaceutical industry, and maintained high-intensity R&D investment during the innovation and transformation period. This strategic determination deserves recognition.

### Cai Lei (second generation/CEO)

- **Background**: Bachelor's degree in Biochemistry from Beijing Normal University, PhD from National University of Singapore
- **Resume**: Joined CSPC in 2014 and served successively as Vice President of the U.S. R&D Department and President of the Pharmaceutical Product Sales Department
- **Time to take office**: Vice Chairman, Executive Director and Chief Executive Officer from December 2025
- **Age**: 45 years old

**Positive review:**
- Have overseas R&D experience (U.S. R&D department) and understand internationalization
- Have sales management experience (President of Pharmaceutical Product Sales Department), not a purely technical background
- Have more than 10 years of internal company experience before officially taking over

**Concerns:**
- Less than half a year after taking over, CEO Hei Yongjiang resigned, hinting that there may be internal differences in line.
- The success rate of "rich second generation succession" among Chinese private enterprises is not high, especially in industries that require professional judgment.
- Can we gain the sincere following of core executives? Or just rely on the status of "Prince"?

## 2. Management turmoil: warning signs

### Hei Yongjiang’s resignation incident (May 2026)

**Event history:**
- Hei Yongjiang will join CSPC Pharmaceuticals in December 2024 as executive president and global chief medical officer.
- Registered Shanghai Huisibo Biomedical Technology Co., Ltd. in April 2026
- Officially resigned in May 2026, serving only about 1.5 years

**What does this mean? **

1. **Route divergence is likely**: Hei Yongjiang is a veteran in the industry. When he joined CSPC, he was regarded as a key talent in advancing the oncology pipeline and international clinical development. With such a short tenure, strategic differences or cultural clashes are almost certain.

2. **Talent attraction is damaged**: The "coming and leaving" of senior talents will send negative signals within the industry and increase the difficulty of subsequent recruitment of talents.

3. **Strategic continuity risk**: Will the projects and layout led by Hei Yongjiang be continued after his resignation?

### Wider personnel turmoil

Many financial media reported on the management changes of CSPC with the title "When will the personnel turmoil end?", indicating that this is not an isolated incident. In the period of declining performance, coupled with management replacement, internal "popularity" is the biggest intangible risk.

## 3. Analysis of governance structure

### Equity and Control

- Cai Dongchen’s family holds a controlling stake in listed companies through CSPC Pharmaceutical Holdings
- Cai Lei serves as CEO and Cai Dongchen serves as chairman of the board of directors, forming a "father and son co-governance" pattern
- It is essentially a family-controlled enterprise, and the key to governance depends on the quality of family decision-making

### Governance Risk

1. **Risk of interest transfer**: Family-controlled enterprises have inherent risks of related transactions and unequal interests. It is necessary to continue to pay attention to capital transactions, asset injection/divestiture and other actions.

2. **Risk of centralized decision-making**: Major strategic decisions may rely too much on the will of the Cai family and lack an effective check and balance mechanism.

3. **Inheritance Risk**: If Cai Lei’s ability is indeed insufficient, where is the error correction mechanism? The biggest risk in a family business is "not being able to replace people even if there are problems."

### Positive factors

- The identity of a listed company provides a certain level of information transparency
- There is a mechanism to link executive compensation to performance
- There are no major scandals in the 30-year business history, and the company is basically honest and trustworthy.

## 4. List of key risks

### Risk 1: The conversion rate of innovative drugs is lower than expected ★★★★☆

**Description**: CSPC claims to have more than 200 ongoing research projects and more than 50 new drugs planned to be submitted for marketing by 2028, but the success rate of drug research and development itself is low. If most pipelines fail or commercialization falls short of expectations, huge R&D investments will become "sunk costs."

**Probability Assessment**: Moderate. The pipelines are dispersed enough to reduce the risk of "annihilation", but there is a possibility of "large areas being lower than expected".

**Impact**: High. If no blockbuster products materialize from 2026 to 2028, market confidence will further collapse.

### Risk 2: Management continues to be turbulent ★★★★☆

**Description**: After Cai Lei took over, senior executives frequently left. If core technical and business talents continue to be lost, it will seriously affect the execution of R&D strategies and BD negotiation capabilities.

**Probability Assessment**: Medium to High. The running-in period for second-generation succession usually takes 2-3 years, during which personnel changes are unavoidable.

**Impact**: Medium to High. The core competitiveness of pharmaceutical companies lies in people, and the loss of key talents will directly affect the quality of the pipeline.

### Risk Three: Centralized Procurement Renewal Standards Worse Than Expected ★★★☆☆

**Description**: The renewal prices of the first eight batches of centralized purchasing varieties may further decline, and there is still uncertainty about the subsequent processing of core varieties such as Enbipu (whether to enter centralized purchasing).

**Probability Assessment**: Moderate. The overall trend of centralized procurement policies has been clear, and the probability of unexpected deterioration is decreasing.

**Impact**: Moderate. The profit contribution of the generics business has declined, with diminishing marginal impact.

### Risk 4: The BD licensing market cools down ★★★☆☆

**Description**: Changes in the global Biotech financing environment may affect the license-in willingness of multinational pharmaceutical companies. If BD transaction volume and amount decline, CSPC's "pipeline monetization" strategy will be frustrated.

**Probability Assessment**: Moderate. The driver of the MNC patent cliff remains, but the interest rate environment and geopolitics may affect transaction willingness.

**Impact**: Moderate. BD's revenue is incremental rather than existing, and will not affect the company's fundamentals.

### Risk Five: Geopolitical Risk ★★☆☆☆

**Description**: Tensions in Sino-US relations may affect overseas clinical development and commercial cooperation of Chinese pharmaceutical companies. The FDA's attitude toward clinical data in China tends to be cautious.

**Probability Assessment**: Medium-Low. At present, what is more affected is independent overseas expansion, and the BD licensing model is less affected.

**Impact**: Low to Moderate. CSPC’s current overseas strategy is mainly based on BD authorization and does not need to face the FDA itself.

### Risk 6: Risk of permanent capital loss ★★☆☆☆

**Description**: Under what circumstances will investing in CSPC lead to permanent capital loss?

- Corporate fraud or major financial fraud (very low probability, no major scandals in 30 years)
- The industry will be replaced by disruptive technology (the probability is extremely low, pharmaceuticals are just needed)
- Continued deterioration leading to insolvency (very low probability, debt level is controllable)

**Conclusion**: The risk of permanent capital loss is low. The more realistic risk is "not rising for a long time" - the performance is delayed to reach the turning point after buying, and funds are locked in a low valuation trap.

## 5. Positive signals

Although there are many risks, we should also look at the positive side:

1. **R&D investment increased against the trend**: R&D expenses were still increased by 11.9% to 5.8 billion yuan even when profits were declining, indicating that management did not choose the short-sighted behavior of "relying on reducing R&D to beautify short-term profits."

2. **BD Authorization Gains International Recognition**: The total contract value of US$28.2 billion was obtained not through relationships but through pipeline quality. The cooperation between AstraZeneca, Madrigal and other multinational pharmaceutical companies is a "vote of confidence".

3. **Prospective strategic layout**: We have plans in cutting-edge directions such as weight loss (semaglutide analogues, oral GLP-1), AI drug discovery, etc., and we do not rest on our laurels.

4. **Resilience to survive for 30 years**: The Chinese pharmaceutical industry has survived and developed after multiple rounds of policy reshuffles (GMP certification, new medical insurance, centralized procurement), which shows that the organization has a certain ability to adapt.

## 6. Li Lu’s summary

### Management trust score: ★★★☆☆ (3/5)

**Reason:**
- Founder Cai Dongchen’s historical achievements deserve recognition, but his influence is receding into the background
- Cai Lei’s performance after taking over remains to be seen, and Hei Yongjiang’s resignation is a negative signal
- The inherent defects in family governance cannot be fully compensated by external constraints.
- No major integrity issues were found, and it is basically credible

### Risk controllability score: ★★★☆☆ (3/5)

**Reason:**
- Low risk of permanent capital loss (★★★★★)
- But the risk of "value trap" is moderately high (★★★☆☆)
- Management risk is currently the biggest uncontrollable factor
- Industry risks are becoming passivated (the impact of centralized procurement is decreasing)

### Core conclusion

From Li Lu's perspective of "buying stocks means buying people", CSPC is currently in a delicate position:

**Positive**: The founder's "genes" are good - he has attached great importance to research and development and products in the past 30 years, and has never engaged in capital operation scams. The undertone of this corporate culture is positive.

**Negative**: The uncertainty of second-generation succession is a real risk. Cai Lei needs to prove himself in the next 1-2 years - whether he can stabilize the team, whether he can promote the realization of the pipeline, and whether he can show judgment in BD negotiations.

**Investment Suggestion Prerequisites**: If you invest in CSPC, you are essentially "betting" on the last mile that Cai Lei can successfully take over and promote the transformation of innovative drugs. This judgment requires more time and evidence to verify.

**Risk Management Advice:**
- Position control: Not suitable as a heavy position target, it is recommended to be within 3-5%
- Stop-loss conditions: If management turmoil intensifies further (core executives leave again), or if the 2026 mid-term results fall short of expectations, exit should be considered
- Conditions for adding a position: If performance clearly improves in 2026H1 and the management team becomes stable, you may consider adding a position
