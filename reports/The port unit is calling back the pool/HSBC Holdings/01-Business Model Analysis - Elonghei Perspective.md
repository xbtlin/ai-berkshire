#HSBC Holdings (0005.HK)—Business Model Analysis

## 1. The essence of business

HSBC Holdings is essentially a global deposit and loan intermediary and cross-border financial service provider with Asia as its profit core. The core logic of its business model is:

1. **Deposit absorption**: Use global network and brand trust to obtain deposits at lower costs (especially low-interest demand deposits in Hong Kong and Asia)
2. **Credit lending and interest spread**: Lending funds at a higher interest rate to earn a net interest spread
3. **Cross-border service premium**: Utilize the network advantages of 60+ markets around the world to provide trade financing, foreign exchange, wealth management and other services to multinational enterprises and high-net-worth customers, charging handling fees

**Key understanding**: 80% of HSBC’s profits come from Asia, but it retains a global network to serve cross-border needs. This is not a simple regional bank, but a hybrid of "Asia for the body and global for use".

## 2. Moat Assessment ★★★★☆ (4/5)

**Strengths:**
- **Network Effect**: The physical presence of 60+ markets around the world makes it almost irreplaceable in the field of cross-border trade finance. If enterprises want to do cross-border business, HSBC is one of the few "one-stop" options.
- **Brand and Trust**: With a history of 158 years, the positioning of "Global Finance, Local Wisdom" is deeply rooted in the hearts of the people, and the brand recognition is extremely high among high-net-worth groups in Asia.
- **Low-cost deposit base**: Hong Kong’s large number of demand deposits provides a natural capital cost advantage
- **Regulatory License Barriers**: The global banking license network itself is a very high barrier to entry.

**Weaknesses:**
- **Scale does not equal efficiency**: The complexity of global operations results in a high cost-to-income ratio (~40%), which is far less streamlined than a focused bank
- **The moat must be inerodible**: Fintech and digital banks are cannibalizing some retail and small and medium-sized enterprise customers
- **Political risk erodes the moat**: Under the competition between China and the United States, HSBC's positioning as a "bridge" may instead become a target for pressure from both sides.

## 3. "Simplicity" test of business model

Duan Yongping emphasized "doing the right thing" and the simple understandability of the business model. HSBC’s model has **structural complexity**:

- **Positive**: The core logic is simple - low-cost savings, high-yield lending, and service fee collection. Banking is a sustainable business.
- **Negative**: However, compliance, supervision, and geopolitics in the global 60+ markets make "simple business very complicated." Management needs to deal with multiple conflicts such as British regulation, US sanctions, and China relations at the same time.

**Conclusion**: This is not a "simple business" that Duan Yongping would like. Too many variables are outside management's control.

## 4. User value and "differentiation"

The core value HSBC creates for customers is the convenience and certainty of cross-border finance:
- If a company exports from China to Latin America, HSBC can provide services at both ends.
- A wealthy Asian man emigrates to the UK so HSBC can seamlessly transfer his wealth management relationships
- A multinational company needs global capital pool management, HSBC can provide a unified platform

The value is real, but **replacement costs are falling** – and fintech companies such as Wise and Airwallex are increasing penetration in cross-border payments.

## 5. Long-term sustainability ★★★★☆ (4/5)

**Pros:**
- Banking is a sustainable business and humans need financial intermediaries
- Asia’s wealth growth trend remains unchanged, and HSBC has deep roots in the region
- The 2026-2028 ROTE ≥ 17% target indicates that management is improving capital efficiency
- US$6 billion in buybacks + 50% dividend payout shows confidence in cash flow

**Disadvantages:**
- Uncertainty in the interest rate cycle: If the world enters a sustained interest rate cutting cycle, the net interest margin will be under pressure
- Geopolitics: Deteriorating Sino-US relations may force HSBC to "choose one of the two"
- Restructuring costs: US$1.8 billion in restructuring expenses in 2025-2026 drags down short-term earnings
