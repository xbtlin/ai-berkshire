#HSBC Holdings (0005.HK) - Investment Research Final Report

## Overall rating: ★★★★☆ (4.0/5.0)

## One sentence summary

HSBC is a good "bridge" business in Asia's cross-border finance. The valuation is reasonable under the ROTE target of 17%, and the 6% dividend rate + buyback constitute a source of steady returns. However, geopolitics is a core risk that cannot be hedged.

## Core investment logic

### Reasons to be bullish
1. **Direct beneficiaries of Asia’s wealth growth**: 80% of profits come from Asia, and Asia-Pacific high-net-worth individuals accumulate wealth at a growth rate of 8-10%
2. **High certainty of shareholder returns**: 50% dividend payout rate + buyback, annualized shareholder return rate of 10-13%
3. **ROTE improvement path is clear**: Exit low-return business + cost reduction + buyback to reduce tangible net assets
4. **Inexpensive valuation**: 11 times PE, 6% dividend rate, outstanding value for money among global systemically important banks

### Reasons to be bearish
1. **Geopolitical risks are uncontrollable**: The deterioration of Sino-US relations may fundamentally shake HSBC's "bridge" value
2. **Falling interest rates suppress NII**: Narrowing of net interest margin is inevitable during the interest rate cut cycle
3. **Reorganization Execution Risk**: US$1.8 billion in restructuring costs + large-scale layoffs, short-term efficiency may not be improved
4. **Concerns about China’s credit exposure**: Uncertainty about the quality of commercial real estate and mainland corporate loans

## Conclusion and suggestions

HSBC Holdings is the target with the highest quality but also the highest valuation in the Hong Kong financial sector. Its core value lies in the irreplaceability of "Asia's cross-border financial hub", and its core risk is that geopolitics may undermine this value.

**The key to investment decisions** is: Do you believe that the trend of globalization (at least regionalization within Asia) will not be reversed? If the answer is "most likely no", then HSBC has a reasonable risk-return ratio at 11 times PE and 6% dividend rate.
