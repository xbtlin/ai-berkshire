#HSBC Holdings (0005.HK) – Financial Valuation Analysis

## 1. Profit quality analysis

### Revenue structure (2025)
- Operating income: $68.3 billion (+4% YoY)
- Net interest income (NII): expected to be ≥ US$45 billion in 2026
- Non-interest income: handling fees, wealth management, transaction income, etc. constitute an important supplement

### Profit Trend
- Profit before tax: $29.9 billion (-7.4% YoY)
- Profit after tax: $23.1 billion
- The profit decline is mainly due to: (1) rising credit costs; (2) restructuring costs (USD 1.8 billion restructuring starting in 2025)

### Profit quality judgment ★★★★☆

**Front**:
- Diversified income sources, including stable fee income in addition to NII
- Asian business contributes 80% of profits, and economic growth in the region can be expected
- Management guides operating costs to increase by only 1% year-on-year in 2026, showing determination to control costs.

**Negative**:
- Credit costs remain at 40bp, which means there are still hidden concerns about asset quality
- Although the restructuring costs are one-time, the short-term drag is significant
- Profits are highly dependent on the interest rate environment, and fluctuations in net interest margin directly affect the bottom line

## 2. Capital efficiency and return

### Key Capital Indicators
| Indicators | 2025 | Targets |
|------|--------|------|
| ROTE | ~14-15% | ≥17%（2026-2028） |
| CET1 Ratio | 14.6% | 14-15% |
| Dividend payout ratio | — | 50% |
| Dividend per share | $0.75 | Year-over-year growth |

### Capital Return Assessment ★★★★☆

- If the ROTE 17% target is achieved, it will be an excellent level for the bank
- 50% dividend payout rate + repurchase constitutes a "dual-channel" shareholder return model
- USD 6 billion repurchase has been completed in 2025, but subsequent repurchases depend on CET1 returning to the target range
- Tangible net assets continue to shrink (shares can be canceled through repurchases) and per share value increases

## 3. Balance sheet health

### Capital Adequacy
- The CET1 ratio is 14.6%, which is higher than the Basel III requirements and higher than the management’s target range of 14-15%
- This means there is excess capital available for repurchases/dividends, but management has stated that new repurchases are on hold until CET1 returns to the target range

### Asset Quality
- Credit cost guidance is 40bp, which is within the normal level of the industry
- Global diversified geographical distribution helps diversify concentration risks
- China commercial real estate exposure is key focus

### Liquidity
- As a global systemically important bank (G-SIB), the liquidity coverage ratio meets regulatory requirements
- Hong Kong's large demand deposits provide a stable liquidity base

## 4. Valuation analysis

### Current valuation level
| Indicators | Values |
|------|------|
| PE (TTM) | ~11x |
| PB | ~1.2-1.3 times |
| Dividend Yield | ~6% |
| ROTE | Target 17% |

### Valuation rationality judgment ★★★☆☆

**The meaning of PE 11 times**:
- For banks with a ROTE target of 17%, PB should be ≥1.5 times to be reasonable (PB>1 when ROE>COE)
- Current PB ~1.2-1.3x means the market has discounted the 17% ROTE target
- 11 times PE corresponds to a profit yield of ~9%, plus a 6% dividend rate, the total return can be expected

**Comparison with peers**:
- JPMorgan Chase PE ~13-14 times, ROTE ~20%
- HSBC's lower PE reflects geopolitical discount and restructuring uncertainty
- Relative to 4-6 times PE of Chinese banks, HSBC’s valuation premium reflects its better governance and return quality

### Safety Margin Assessment

**Optimistic scenario** (ROTE reaches 17%, interest rates remain high):
- Reasonable PE of 12-13 times, corresponding to ~10-15% upside potential
- With the addition of 6% dividend, the total annualized return is ~12-15%

**Pessimistic scenario** (rate cutting cycle, worsening China exposure):
- PE may be compressed up to 9-10 times
- Downside risk ~10-15%, but dividends provide cushion

**Neutral judgment**: The current valuation is basically reasonable and the margin of safety is limited (~10%). It's not a "bargain" price, but it's not expensive either.

## 5. Cash flow and shareholder returns

### Capital allocation framework
HSBC management’s capital allocation priorities:
1. Investment in organic growth (expansion of wealth management in Asia)
2. Maintain 50% dividend payout ratio
3. Excess capital is returned through repurchases

###Report performance in 2025
- Full year dividend: $0.75 per share
- Buybacks: $6 billion
- Total return to shareholders is estimated to be approximately US$14-15 billion

### Return Sustainability ★★★★☆
- If ROTE reaches 17%, annual net profit will be approximately US$25-28 billion
- 50% dividend payment = US$12.5-14 billion, plus buyback, annualized return to shareholders of US$15-20 billion
- A total shareholder return corresponding to about 10-13% of the current market capitalization, which is very impressive
