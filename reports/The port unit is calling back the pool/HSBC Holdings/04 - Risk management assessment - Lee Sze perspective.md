# HSBC Holdings (0005.HK) – Risk and Management Assessment

## 1. Management evaluation ★★★★☆

### Current CEO: Georges Elhedery

**Background**: Taking over as CEO in September 2024, previously serving as CFO. A Lebanese-British man with a PhD in mathematics, he has served HSBC for more than 20 years and is well versed in the Asian market.

**Key Decision Assessment**:

1. **East-West Axis Reorganization**: Divide the business into "Eastern Market" (Asia) and "Western Market" (Europe and the United States), each operating independently.
   - Positive: Recognize Asia as a profit center and reduce cross-regional bureaucracy
   - Negative: global synergies may be weakened and efficiency lost during the transition period

2. **Cost Reduction Commitment**: Save $1.5 billion/year from 2025-2027
   - Positive: Shows execution and pursuit of efficiency
   - Negative: layoffs and restructuring may impact morale and service quality

3. **Capital Return Commitment**: ROTE≥17%, dividend payout rate 50%
   - Positive: clear goals, measurable, and timetable
   - Negative: May need to "do the math" by compressing tangible net assets in a rate cut environment

### Management quality judgment

| Dimensions | Assessment |
|------|------|
| Capabilities | ★★★★☆ Mathematics/Quantitative background, sensitive to capital efficiency |
| Integrity | ★★★★☆ The goal is open and transparent, and the compliance issues left by the predecessor have been greatly improved |
| Incentive alignment | ★★★★☆ Salary linked to ROTE/TSR |
| Capital allocation | ★★★★☆ Exit low-return business + repurchase + maintain dividend payment |

### Governance Structure
- The board of directors has a high degree of internationalization, with independent directors accounting for the majority
- Faced pressure from Asian shareholders for a spin-off, but management resisted (but at the cost of promising higher returns)
- CEO compensation will increase by 18% to approximately US$10 million in 2025, which is above the middle level among global banks.

## 2. Risk Assessment

### Risk 1: Geopolitical risk ★★★★★ (highest)

**Specific performance**:
- The deterioration of Sino-US relations may lead to: (1) the United States imposing secondary sanctions on China-related transactions; (2) China asking HSBC to "take sides"
- HSBC was previously involved in the case of Huawei CFO Meng Wanzhou, which caused damage to its reputation in the Chinese market
- If Hong Kong's "one country, two systems" framework is further tightened, HSBC's positioning as an international bank may face fundamental challenges

**Extent of impact**: In extreme scenarios, HSBC may be forced to spin off or exit a certain side of the market, which would destroy its core value proposition.

**Probability judgment**: The probability of extreme scenarios is <10%, but the probability of moderate friction is >50%.

### Risk 2: Interest rate cycle risk ★★★★☆

**Specific performance**:
- Major central banks around the world will enter the interest rate cut channel starting in 2025
- Management guides NII to be ≥45 billion US dollars in 2026, implicitly assuming that interest rates will not drop significantly
- If interest rates are cut more than expected, NII may be lower than guidance

**Scale of impact**: Every USD 1 billion change in NII has an impact on pre-tax profits of approximately 3-4%.

**Mitigating factors**: HSBC’s hedging strategy (structural hedging) can partially smooth the impact of downward interest rates.

### Risk 3: China Credit Risk ★★★★☆

**Specific performance**:
- China commercial real estate exposure is in focus
- Mainland corporate loan quality may worsen as economy slows
- Credit cost guidance is 40bp, which may rise sharply if China's economy has a "hard landing"

**Extent of impact**: Every US$1 billion increase in China-related credit losses will erode pre-tax profits by approximately 3%.

**Mitigating factors**: It has taken the initiative to reduce high-risk exposures in recent years; the provision coverage ratio is acceptable.

### Risk 4: Supervision and Compliance Risk ★★★☆☆

**Specific performance**:
- G-SIB surcharge capital requirements continue to increase
- Anti-money laundering compliance costs are high (previously $1.9 billion was fined for non-compliance)
- Cross-compliance with multi-national regulations is an eternal challenge

**Scale of impact**: Regulatory fines and compliance investments are ongoing costs, but the risk of large-scale fines has been reduced.

### Risk 5: Competition cannibalize risk ★★★☆☆

**Specific performance**:
- Fintech competition in retail payments and cross-border remittances
- Chinese banks (Bank of China, ICBC) increase their share in Asia’s trade finance sector
- Expansion of regional banks such as Singapore’s DBS in Southeast Asia

**Scale of impact**: Gradual erosion, the position will not be fundamentally shaken in the short term.

## 3. Institutional environment analysis (Li Lu framework)

### Rule of law and property rights protection
- Place of registration: United Kingdom (perfect rule of law)
- Main place of business: Hong Kong (common law system, but "one country, two systems" is evolving)
- **Assessment**: ★★★★☆ The overall legal environment is good, but Hong Kong’s institutional certainty has declined.

### Capital market openness
- Dual listed in Hong Kong and London, with sufficient liquidity
- No restrictions on foreign shareholding
- **Assessment**: ★★★★★ The capital market is fully open

### Currency Risk
- Revenue is mainly denominated in US dollars (about 40-50%), and Hong Kong dollars are pegged to the US dollar
- Supplementary exposure to GBP and RMB
- **Assessment**: ★★★★☆ USD anchored, currency risk controllable

### Policy predictability
- Faced with policy uncertainty in many countries
- The UK may raise taxes on the banking industry, and China may require "servicing the real economy"
- **Evaluation**: ★★★☆☆ It is difficult to predict the policy direction under the multi-country game

## 4. Investment risk and return assessment

### Worst case scenario (5% probability)
- Sino-US financial decoupling, HSBC was forced to break up
- Valuation may fall to 0.6-0.7 times PB
- Downside: -40-50%

### Pessimistic scenario (20% probability)
- Continued interest rate cuts + China’s economic weakness + geopolitical friction
- ROTE fell back to 12-13%, PE compressed to 8-9 times
- Downside: -15-25%

### Baseline scenario (50% probability)
- ROTE reaches the target range of 15-17%
- PE maintained at 10-12 times, dividend yield 5-6%
- Annualized total return: 10-14%

### Optimistic scenario (25% probability)
- Asia’s wealth management exceeds expectations growth + interest rates remain high
- ROTE exceeds 17%, PE expands to 13-14 times
- Annualized total return: 15-20%
