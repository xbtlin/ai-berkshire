#HSBC Holdings (0005.HK)—Industry Competition Analysis

## 1. Industry structure: Where is the "endgame" for the global banking industry?

The global banking industry is at the intersection of **triple structural changes**:

1. **Division after normalization of interest rates**: The interest rate hike cycle from 2022 to 2024 will significantly expand bank interest spreads, but starting from 2025, major central banks will enter the interest rate cut channel, and there will be differentiated competition among banks "who has the lowest deposit costs and the best asset duration management"
2. **Digital Disruption**: The penetration rate of financial technology in areas such as retail payments, cross-border remittances, and small and medium-sized enterprise loans continues to increase
3. **Geopolitics reshapes the financial landscape**: The US dollar system vs. multipolarity, and the exploration of SWIFT alternatives have put global banks under pressure to "choose sides"

## 2. HSBC’s competitive position ★★★★☆

### Core Competitive Advantages

**1. The "hub" status of Asia's cross-border finance**
- Approximately 30% market share in Hong Kong (deposits and loans)
- Leading Asia-Pacific trade finance market share
- The ability to provide "one bank, global services" to multinational enterprises, which cannot be replicated by regional banks

**2. Advantages of low-cost funds**
- Hong Kong has a large proportion of CASA (current/savings deposits)
- Funding costs are significantly lower than competitors that rely solely on interbank lending
- This is particularly important in a rate cut environment - high stickiness on the liability side

**3. "Time accumulation" of brand and trust**
- With a history of 158 years, it has established a "stable and reliable" perception in the minds of generations of people in Asia.
- Migration costs for high-net-worth clients and large corporate clients are extremely high

### Competitive disadvantages

**1. Efficiency lags behind focused opponents**
- Cost-to-income ratio ~40%, higher than JP Morgan (~33%)
- Huge compliance costs for global operations (anti-money laundering, sanctions screening, etc.)
- Reorganization (East-West axis split dragging) is being tried to be solved, but it will take time

**2. Technology investment is for followers rather than leaders**
- Compared with JPMorgan Chase’s annual technology investment of US$17 billion, HSBC is not leading in digitalization
- But "good enough" may be enough - corporate banking customers value stability

**3. "Sandwich Cookies" Situation**
- Between purely Asian banks (BOCHK, DBS, etc.) and purely European and American banks
- Potential political pressure on both sides

## 3. Analysis of multiple thinking models

### Game theory perspective
HSBC is in a typical **multi-party game**:
- Game with regulation: UK PRA vs Hong Kong HKMA vs China Banking and Insurance Regulatory Commission
- Game with shareholders: Asian shareholders (who called for a spin-off) vs. global institutional investors
- Game with geopolitics: the "tightrope" between China and the United States

The management chose the strategy of "no spin-off, focusing on Asia, and retaining the global network". This is a game equilibrium of "limited compromise".

### Economies of Scale and Anti-Scale Effects
- **Positive Scale Effects**: Global network makes serving a single customer cheaper than building from scratch
- **Reverse Scale Effect**: Excessive scale leads to bureaucracy, slow decision-making, and high compliance costs
- Net effect: scale advantage still exists, but at diminishing margins

### Mean reversion perspective
- The long-term average ROTE of the banking industry is about 10-12%
- HSBC’s target of 17% is higher than the average, mainly due to: (1) increasing profit contribution from Asia; (2) exiting low-return businesses; (3) buybacks to reduce tangible net assets
-Risk: If the Asian economy slows down or interest rates fall sharply, ROTE may return to the mean

## 4. Industry trends and HSBC’s adaptability

### Trend 1: The explosion of wealth management in Asia
- The wealth of Asia-Pacific high-net-worth individuals is growing at 8-10% annually
- HSBC’s IWPB (International Wealth and Banking Excellence) department is designed for exactly this purpose
- **Adaptability: ★★★★★** - Natural match

### Trend 2: Cross-border e-commerce and digital trade
- The risk of traditional trade finance being replaced by digitalization
- But large B2B trade still requires bank letters of credit and guarantees
- **Adaptability: ★★★★☆** - Large clients have obvious advantages, but there is a risk of losing small and medium-sized clients.

### Trend 3: ESG and sustainable finance
- HSBC is one of the world’s largest green bond underwriters
- Sustainable finance as a new growth point
- **Adaptability: ★★★★☆** - first mover advantage

### Trend 4: Central Bank Digital Currency (CBDC)
- If digital RMB/digital Hong Kong dollar becomes popular, it may change the payment and deposit pattern
- HSBC, as a licensed bank, remains a CBDC distribution node
- **Adaptability: ★★★☆☆** - high uncertainty

## 5. "Munchkin" test of moat sustainability

Munger's question: "Will this company still be around in 50 years?"

**Answer: Most likely. **Reason:
- The world needs cross-border financial intermediaries, and this demand will not disappear
- Brand and license network requires hundreds of years of accumulation and is almost impossible for new entrants to copy
- But "still" does not mean "still making so much" - competition may erode excess returns

"Who can kill HSBC?"
- Not fintech (they can’t beat corporate banks)
- Not a Chinese bank (lack of global network)
- The only risk: **Geopolitics leads to a break in globalization** - If the world splits into two financial systems, the value of HSBC's "bridge" will be reduced to zero.
