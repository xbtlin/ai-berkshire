# CRRC (1766.HK) industry competition analysis

## 1. Global Rail Transit Equipment Industry Pattern

The global rail transit equipment market is dominated by three giants:

| Enterprise | Annual Revenue | Global Share | Core Markets |
|------|--------|----------|----------|
| CRRC | ~270 billion yuan | ~50% | China + emerging markets |
| Siemens Mobility | ~100 billion yuan | ~15% | Europe |
| Alstom | ~120 billion yuan | ~20% | Europe + North America |

**CRRC’s global share is about 50%, which is 1.5 times that of the second and third places combined. ** This kind of monopoly is extremely rare in manufacturing.

### Industry Attraction
**Rating: ★★★★☆ (3.5/5)**

Front:
- Global urbanization drives demand for rail transit
- The carbon neutral policy is good for railways (railway carbon emissions are much lower than road/aviation)
- High-speed rail is still in the early penetration stage around the world
- Technical barriers are high and it is extremely difficult for new entrants

Negative:
- Long order cycle (3-5 years), revenue fluctuations
- Most of the customers are governments/state-owned enterprises with strong bargaining power
- Low net profit margin ceiling
- Geopolitics affects exports

## 2. Evaluation of CRRC’s competitiveness

### 1. Technical ability
**Rating: ★★★★★ (4.5/5)**

CRRC is a global leader in the following technical fields:
- High-speed EMU with a speed of 350 kilometers per hour - has been in commercial operation for 10 years+
- High-speed maglev with a speed of 600 kilometers per hour - the prototype has rolled off the assembly line
- New generation of urban rail vehicles (unmanned, lightweight)
- Hydrogen locomotive - the world's first hydrogen hybrid locomotive

### 2. Cost advantage
**Rating: ★★★★★ (4.5/5)**

CRRC product prices are usually 30-50% lower than their European counterparts:
- Labor cost advantage
- High degree of localization of supply chain
-Effects of scale (thousands of vehicles per year vs hundreds in Europe)
- Vertical integration of the entire industry chain

### 3. Overseas market expansion
**Rating: ★★★☆☆ (3/5)**

In 2025, overseas revenue will be 34.8 billion yuan (+22.88%), accounting for 12.75%. Main markets:
- Southeast Asia (Indonesia High Speed Rail, Malaysia Light Rail)
- Middle East (Saudi Arabia, UAE urban rail)
- Africa (Kenya, Nigeria Railway)
- Europe (limited breakout, North Macedonia, etc.)

The biggest obstacle to overseas expansion is not technology or price, but geopolitics - some countries restrict Chinese equipment out of "security" considerations.

## 3. Key issues of Munger format

### "How strong is CRRC's pricing power?"
In the domestic market: **weak**. The largest customer, China Railway Group, is a state-owned enterprise, and the purchase price is affected by the budget and policies of the Railway Corporation. CRRC is more like "undertaking national tasks" than "independent pricing".

In overseas markets: **Medium**. The price advantage gives CRRC the initiative in bidding, but political factors often interfere with normal business decisions.

### "Can new industries become the real 'second curve'?"
In 2025, new industry revenue will be 103.1 billion yuan, exceeding 100 billion yuan for the first time. Key observations:
- Wind power equipment: Benefiting from the dual-carbon policy, growth is certain
- IGBT semiconductor: There is a huge demand for domestic substitution, and the gross profit margin is higher than that of the equipment business
- New energy equipment: cutting-edge fields such as hydrogen locomotives and energy storage

If the new industry sector can maintain a growth rate of 15-20% and achieve an increase in profit margins (from 4% to 6%+), it will substantially improve CRRC's overall financial situation.

### "Who can challenge CRRC's status?"
In the foreseeable future (within 10 years), **nobody** will. Reason:
- The R&D and manufacturing of rail transit equipment requires more than 10 years of accumulation
- CRRC has eliminated domestic competition through mergers (CNR + CNR merged in 2015)
- Siemens and Alstom cannot compete with CRRC on cost
- Localization attempts in emerging countries (India, etc.) are progressing slowly

## 4. Core Conclusions

**Industry competitiveness rating: ★★★★☆ (4/5)**

CRRC's monopoly position in the global rail transit equipment industry is extremely rare - with a global share of 50%+, leading technology and significant cost advantages. What it faces is not "competition risk" but "market space risk" (whether domestic railway investment has peaked) and "political risk" (whether overseas expansion is blocked).

Munger's summary: CRRC is a "big fish in a big pond" - the global rail transit market has a trillion-level space, and CRRC accounts for half of it. If it can continue to increase its market share from 50% and expand the boundaries of the pond through new industries, long-term growth is guaranteed. PE 8-9 times provides a reasonable buying price for such a global leader.
