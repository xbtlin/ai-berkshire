## 1. Management map: the governance logic of the Lei Jun system

### 1.1 Core characters

**Lei Jun——Founder, soul figure, actual controller**

Lei Jun holds approximately 15.37% of Kingsoft's equity through controlled corporations, and together with persons acting in concert (including 7.32% held by Qiu Bojun), he controls approximately 22-23% of the voting rights in total. Lei Jun currently serves as a director and honorary chairman of the company.

Lei Jun's role is very special: he does not directly manage Kingsoft's daily operations, but his strategic influence is everywhere. Lei Jun is also the founder and CEO of Xiaomi Group, and his focus is mainly on Xiaomi and car manufacturing. This means:

- **Positive**: Lei Jun is one of the most successful entrepreneurs in China. His strategic vision and resource integration ability are the largest intangible assets of the Jinshan Group.
- **Negative**: Lei Jun has no skills at all times, Kingsoft software is not his first priority, and management may have the problem of "remote control" rather than "in person"

**Zou Tao——Senior executive and practical trader of Kingsoft Group**

Zou Tao joined Kingsoft in 1998 and has worked in the Kingsoft system for more than 28 years. In 2022, he succeeded Wang Yulin as acting CEO of Kingsoft Cloud, and later officially served as chairman of Kingsoft Cloud. Zou Tao is one of Lei Jun's most trusted "Jinshan veterans."

- **Positive**: Unparalleled understanding of Kingsoft’s business and extremely high loyalty
- **Negative**: Having grown up in the internal system for a long time, it may lack external vision and the courage to break the existing pattern.

**Zhang Qingyuan——CEO of Kingsoft Office**

Zhang Qingyuan is the direct operator of WPS business and has served as CEO of Kingsoft Office since 2020. Under his leadership, WPS completed its subscription transformation and AI strategic layout.

- **Positive**: He has a clear judgment on WPS's products and strategic direction. His candid statement that "he cannot enter the US market" shows that he will not blindly expand.
- **Negative**: Internationalization is progressing slowly, and the ability to develop overseas markets has not yet been verified.

## 2. Capital allocation capability: the core evaluation dimension

Li Lu values the management's ability to allocate capital the most - "Good management will spend money wisely."

### 2.1 "Three options" for using cash

Kingsoft has about 26 billion yuan in cash on its books, and management has three options:

**Option 1: Invest in business growth**
- R&D investment in office business (AI, internationalization) - reasonable
- New product development in the game business - the risk is higher, as "Unlocking Machine" has proven this
- Kingsoft Cloud’s expansion – requires continuous cash burn, but has strategic significance

**Option 2: Buy Back Stock**
- At the current holding company discount of 47-49%, buybacks are the capital allocation method with the highest ROI
- But management’s buyback efforts are far from strong enough, and there is no large-scale buyback plan like NetEase’s
- The final dividend is only HK$0.13 per share, with a dividend rate of less than 0.5%

**Option 3: Mergers and Acquisitions**
- No major mergers and acquisitions are currently seen
- The related-party transactions between Kingsoft Cloud and Xiaomi Ecosystem are implicit "mergers and acquisitions substitution"

### 2.2 Capital Allocation Score ★★☆☆☆

This is the biggest deduction for management. 26 billion in cash is lying on the account, and the company's stock price is at a sharp discount to SOTP. The management should vigorously buy back - this is the simplest math problem: use 1 yuan to buy 2 yuan of assets. But management's failure to do so either indicates a lack of awareness of their own value or that they have other considerations (such as stockpiling ammunition for future large investments).

Regardless of the reason, the result is the same: the interests of small shareholders are not adequately taken into account.

## 3. Governance structure risks

### 3.1 Inherent flaws of the holding company structure

Kingsoft’s holding company structure brings several governance issues:

**Related Transaction Complexity**

There are a large number of related transactions between Kingsoft, Kingsoft Office, Kingsoft Cloud and Xiaomi. Kingsoft Cloud’s related-party transaction amount with Xiaomi and Kingsoft is as high as 11.3 billion yuan from 2025 to 2027. Although these transactions have been audited and disclosed, it is difficult for external investors to fully grasp whether the pricing is fair and whether the benefits are reasonably distributed among the parties.

**Information Asymmetry**

Investors at the holding company level naturally have less understanding of the operational details of subsidiaries than investors at the subsidiary level. As an A-share listed company, Kingsoft Office has more detailed information disclosure; however, from the Kingsoft software level, when looking at the gaming business and the use of cash at the group level, the transparency is relatively insufficient.

**Potential for conflict of interest**

Lei Jun is also the actual controller of Xiaomi and Kingsoft. When there is a conflict between the interests of Xiaomi and Kingsoft, Lei Jun's position may be inclined to Xiaomi (the company to which he invests more energy and emotion). The deep binding between Kingsoft Cloud and Xiaomi is not only a resource advantage, but also a potential conflict of interest.

### 3.2 Protection of small and medium-sized shareholders in Hong Kong stocks ★★★☆☆

The protection of small and medium-sized shareholders in the Hong Kong stock market is weaker than that of A-shares and US stocks. Among the outstanding shares of Kingsoft Software, institutional investors do not account for a high proportion, and retail investors have limited influence on management. There is no compelling pressure on management to improve shareholder returns.

## 5. Key "test questions" for management

Here are the key questions management will need to answer over the next 12-24 months:

**Question 1: How do you plan to use the 26 billion cash? **
This is the most important question. If management announces a large-scale buyback plan or special dividends (such as a special dividend of HK$2-3 per share), it will directly change the market's pricing logic for holding companies. If we continue to keep cash on our books, it will be difficult for catalysts to narrow discounts to emerge.

**Question 2: Does the game business need to be strategically reduced? **
The resource input-output ratio of Xishanju is not ideal. Does the management have the courage to reduce the scale of the game business and focus more resources on office and AI? Or will you continue to chase the fantasy of "the next big hit"?

**Question 3: Will you consider simplifying the holding structure? **
For example, privatizing Kingsoft Cloud, increasing its holdings in Kingsoft Office, or promoting the independence of the gaming business - any action that simplifies the holding structure will help narrow the discount.

**Question 4: How to go about internationalization? **
The ceiling of WPS in the domestic market will eventually come. Internationalization is the key to long-term growth, but the current overseas market share is less than 5%. Does management have a clear path to internationalization?

For value investors, whether and when management "awakens" is a key variable that determines the time cost of investment return.
