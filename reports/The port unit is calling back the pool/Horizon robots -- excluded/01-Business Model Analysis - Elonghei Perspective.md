#Horizon Robot (9660.HK) Business Model Analysis

> Analysis dimensions: user value, business essence, differentiated positioning, long-term competitiveness
> Core question: Is this company doing "the right thing"? Do users really need it?

---

## 1. What business is the company doing?

Horizon Robotics is essentially an **intelligent driving computing platform company** that provides integrated smart driving solutions from chips to software to automotive OEMs. Its business model can be broken down into three layers:

1. **Bottom layer**: Self-developed AI chip (Journey series), optimized architecture for autonomous driving scenarios
2. **Middle layer**: Development tool chain and operating system (Horizon Tiangongkaiwu), lowering the customer development threshold
3. **Upper layer**: High-end smart driving solution (HSD/SuperDrive), providing end-to-end mass production solutions

This model is similar to Intel in the PC era - making "computing platforms" rather than terminal products. Yu Kai clearly proposed to become "Microsoft + Intel in the robot era."

## 2. User value analysis: Who is paying for it? Why?

### Direct customers: Automotive OEM (Original Vehicle Manufacturer)

The core reasons why car companies choose Horizon:
- **Cost-performance**: Compared with NVIDIA Orin chips, the Journey series has a significant cost advantage in mid- to low-end solutions.
- **Localized service**: fast response, engineering support team rooted in China
- **Open Ecosystem**: Unlike Huawei, which "makes chips, solutions and cars", Horizon is positioned as a pure Tier 2 supplier and does not steal customers' cakes.
- **Mass production verification**: 152 models have been mass-produced and launched, and we cooperate with 42 car companies

### End user: car owner

Car owners gain a safer and more relaxed driving experience through assisted driving functions. 25,000 units of HSD were delivered within eight weeks of its launch, and top-end models equipped with HSD accounted for 83% of the total sales of this model - this shows that end consumers are willing to pay for smart driving functions.

## 3. "Right" and "Wrong" business models

### Do the right thing ★★★★☆

**Right:**

1. **Choose the track correctly**: Intelligent driving is a major trend with high certainty, and the penetration rate from low to high is an inevitable process. The penetration rate of mid-to-high-end assisted driving will increase rapidly in 2025, and the company is stuck at this turning point.

2. **Positioning Restraint**: Yu Kai insists on being a Tier2 supplier and does not make complete vehicles or Tier1 integration. This positioning is similar to Qualcomm's positioning for mobile phone manufacturers - "empowering but not competing". Compared with Huawei's full-stack model, which makes many car companies worried, Horizon's open positioning has won more partners.

3. **Technical route integrating software and hardware**: Jointly optimize the autonomous driving scenario from the beginning of the chip architecture, which is something that neither pure software companies nor general chip companies can do. The vertical integration of algorithm-compiler-chip brings computing efficiency advantages.

4. **Both volume and price rise**: AD chip shipments accounted for 45% of the total, contributing more than 80% of revenue, and the average unit price increased by 75%. This shows that the product has successfully migrated to a high-value direction.

**Questionable aspects:**

1. **Continuous huge losses**: R&D expenses of 5.15 billion yuan far exceed revenue of 3.76 billion yuan, and it is "burning money in exchange for scale" every year. This is not a problem in itself, the question is when the scale effect will be demonstrated.

2. **The "winner takes all" rule in the chip industry**: In the chip industry, the top one or two companies often take most of the profits. Horizon currently leads the market share, but Nvidia is still the absolute king of the high-end market, and Huawei is also a strong rival in high-end solutions.

3. **Customer concentration risk**: Volkswagen Group was once an important customer. If a large customer loses, it will have a significant impact on revenue.

## 4. Differentiated positioning and moat assessment

### Moat analysis ★★★☆☆ (under construction, not yet stable)

| Moat Types | Assessment |
|-----------|------|
| Switching cost | ★★★★ Once a car company chooses a chip platform, the vehicle development cycle will be 2-3 years, and the switching cost is extremely high |
| Scale effect | ★★★☆ Increased shipments can dilute chip design costs, but the absolute scale is still not large enough |
| Network effect | ★★☆☆ The developer ecosystem has just started and is not as mature as the NVIDIA CUDA ecosystem |
| Brand effect | ★★★☆ Brand recognition has been established among Chinese OEMs, but global influence is limited |
| Technology leadership | ★★★★ The Journey 6 series is leading in performance, but may be caught up due to technology generation differences |

**Core differentiation**: Computing platform integrating software and hardware + open ecological positioning + local service capabilities. The combination of these three forms a unique competitive position in the Chinese market.

## 5. Questioning the nature of business

### Duan Yongping’s questioning:

**"Where will this business be in ten years?"**

If smart driving becomes standard (high probability), then Horizon has the opportunity to become a core supplier in China and even the world. But the cruelty of the chip industry is that technology iterates extremely quickly, and today’s lead does not guarantee tomorrow’s status. Intel was at its peak in the PC era, but was defeated by the ARM architecture in the mobile era.

**"Can the moat get wider and wider?"**

The key depends on two points: (1) whether the scale of mass production can continue to expand, forming a data flywheel and cost advantage; (2) whether the developer ecosystem can be truly established, making switching costs higher and higher. We are currently heading in the right direction, but it will take time to verify.

**"Is management doing what matters most?"**

Yu Kai's strategic focus deserves recognition - not to do complete vehicles, not to be Tier 1, and not to blindly diversify. Such restraint is a rare quality among technology companies. However, the fact that R&D investment exceeds revenue shows that the company is still in the "investment period" and the business model has not yet been fully operational.

**Overall Business Model Rating: ★★★★☆ (3.5/5)**

Horizon is doing the "right thing" - providing computing brains for smart cars. The business model logic is clear, differentiated positioning is reasonable, and user value is real. The main uncertainties are: (1) when losses will narrow to profits; (2) whether it can maintain its position in the long-term competition with Nvidia and Huawei; (3) the unpredictability of technological iterations in the chip industry.
