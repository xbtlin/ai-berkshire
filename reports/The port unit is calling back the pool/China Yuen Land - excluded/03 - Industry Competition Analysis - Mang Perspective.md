# China Resources Land (1109.HK) industry competition analysis

> Analysis dimensions: industry outcome, competitive landscape, ecological niche, multi-dimensional thinking model
> Core question: Where is this industry headed? Is China Resources Land’s niche in the industry stable?

---

## 1. Thoughts on the end of the industry: What is China’s real estate going through?

### 1.1 The irreversible transformation from the "incremental era" to the "stock era"

China's real estate industry is undergoing the most profound structural change since the housing commercialization reform in 1998. This is not a simple cyclical adjustment, but the end of one era and the beginning of another.

**Characteristics of the Incremental Era (1998-2021):**
- Rapid increase in urbanization rate (from 30% to 65%)
- Housing demand continues to increase
- Land finance drives supply
- "Three High Model" of high leverage, high turnover and high profit

**Characteristics of the stock era (2022 to present):**
- Urbanization rate growth slows down
- The population turning point has arrived (negative population growth in 2022)
- Housing changes from "shortage" to "structural excess"
- Industry clearing is accelerating, and private enterprises are experiencing widespread thunderstorms

**Picture of the end of the industry:** The total volume of residential development business will shrink in the long term, and may stabilize at the level of annual sales of 8-10 trillion yuan (halved from the peak of 18 trillion yuan). The surviving players will be dominated by state-owned enterprises, and industry concentration will increase significantly. Real growth opportunities are in asset operations, urban renewal, REITs and other fields.

### 1.2 Evolution of the policy environment

Main policy lines for 2025-2026:
- **City-specific policies to control growth, destocking, and optimizing supply** - Limiting new land supply and digesting existing stock
- **Encourage the acquisition of existing commercial housing for affordable housing** - Government support
- **Promote the construction of "good houses"** - Upgrading product quality
- **Commercial real estate REIT pilot expansion** - including office buildings and hotels into the scope of REIT

The core logic of the policy is: to acknowledge the shrinkage of "quantity" in the industry, but to promote the improvement of "quality"; to provide financial exit channels for high-quality existing assets. This policy direction is structurally positive for China Resources Land.

## 2. Competitive landscape: the “three giants” of state-owned enterprises stand together

### 2.1 Reshaping of the industry structure

The competitive landscape of the real estate industry has been profoundly rewritten. Former giants such as Evergrande, Country Garden, and Sunac have been eliminated one after another, and the share of private enterprises in the development field has shrunk sharply. What replaced it was the overall rise of central state-owned enterprises.

Companies with the top sales in 2025:
- Poly Development: 253.03 billion yuan
- China Overseas Real Estate: 251.23 billion yuan
- China Resources Land: 233.6 billion yuan
- China Merchants Shekou: 196 billion yuan
- Greentown China: 153.4 billion yuan

Among the top five, Poly, China Shipping, China Resources, and China Merchants are all state-owned enterprises, while Greentown is backed by China Communications Construction Company. **Central-owned enterprises have substantially dominated the industry. **

### 2.2 Comparison of core competitors

#### China Resources Land vs China Overseas Land

| Dimension | China Resources Land | China Overseas Land |
|------|---------|---------|
| 2025 sales | 233.6 billion yuan | 251.2 billion yuan |
| Core Strengths | Commercial Real Estate Operations | Ultimate Cost Control |
| Net debt ratio | 39.2% | About 35-40% |
| Recurring income | Shopping center as the core | Office building as the core |
| Financing cost | 2.72% | About 2.8-3.0% |
| REIT layout | Consumer REITs issued | Office REITs in progress |

China Overseas Land is the most direct competitor of China Resources Land. China Shipping's advantage lies in its ultimate cost control capabilities (known as the "King of Profit" in the industry), but it is not as good as China Resources in terms of commercial real estate operations. The differentiated competition situation between the two is relatively clear: China Resources is engaged in "urban comprehensive operations", and China Shipping is engaged in "fine residential + office buildings".

#### China Resources Land vs Vanke

Vanke was once an industry benchmark, but has encountered a severe liquidity crisis in recent years. Although the situation was temporarily stabilized with government support, the situation was severely damaged. Vanke's property management and long-term rental apartment businesses have bright spots, but its overall competitiveness has clearly lagged behind China Resources.

Vanke's predicament exactly reflects China Resources' advantages: the credit endorsement and financing convenience brought by its status as a central enterprise are the fundamental guarantee for survival during the industry liquidation period.

#### China Resources Land vs Longfor Group

Longfor is the only private enterprise that can compete head-on with China Resources in the field of commercial real estate. The positioning of Longfor Tianjie series is similar to Wanxianghui. However, as a private enterprise, Longfor has higher financing costs and lower credit rating, which limits its ability to expand. Longfor's business operation capabilities are respectable, but it is difficult to match China Resources in terms of scale and resource acquisition.

### 2.3 Summary of competitive landscape

In the dual track of "residential development + commercial operation", China Resources Land's comprehensive competitiveness ranks among the top three in the industry. In the field of commercial real estate, if you only look at the shopping mall track, China Resources Land can be said to be the number one in the industry.

## 3. Ecological niche of China Resources Land

### 3.1 Ecological niche definition: urban investment and development operators

China Resources Land positions itself as a "world-class urban investment, development and operator". The key words for this positioning are "city" and "operator", rather than simply "developer".

The uniqueness of this ecological niche lies in the fact that China Resources Land does not just sell houses or collect rents, but provides cities with a complete set of "investment, construction and operation" solutions - covering the entire chain from investment decision-making, development and construction to long-term operations.

### 3.2 "Three Growth Curves" Strategy

China Resources Land’s strategic framework:

**The first curve - development and sales business:** The target revenue by the end of the "15th Five-Year Plan" will be maintained at around 200 billion yuan. This is the "cash cow" that funds other curves.

**Second Curve - Commercial real estate rent-collecting business:** The target is to achieve rental income of 30 billion yuan by the end of the "15th Five-Year Plan", with profit contribution accounting for nearly 50%. This is the "core of value".

**The third curve - light asset management fee-based business:** The target revenue by the end of the "15th Five-Year Plan" is about 20 billion yuan, with an annual growth rate of more than 10%. This is the "future direction".

### 3.3 The strategic significance of the "big asset management" model

The "big asset management" model that China Resources Land is building is essentially to transform itself from an "asset holder" into an "asset manager." Through the REIT platform, mature assets are securitized and exited, and the operating rights and management fees are retained by the REIT platform to achieve separation of light and heavy assets.

If this model works, it means that China Resources Land can use less of its own capital to leverage a larger asset scale. For investors, this will bring about an increase in ROE and a re-evaluation of valuations.

## 4. Multi-dimensional thinking: negative argument

### 4.1 Industry-level risks

**The persistence of consumption downgrade. ** Will China's economic growth slow down, residents' income is expected to decline, and the consumption downgrade trend will affect the passenger flow and rent of high-end shopping malls? Is Vientiane City's 22.4% retail sales growth sustainable, or is it a one-time rebound after the epidemic?

**The substitution effect of online consumption. ** New business formats such as live streaming e-commerce and instant retail continue to capture market share. Although "experiential consumption" in high-end shopping malls is difficult to replace, mid-range Wanxianghui faces more intense competition.

**Uncertainty in the REIT market. ** China's public REIT market is still in its early stages of development, with limited liquidity and low pricing efficiency. Whether China Resources' "big asset management" strategy can be successfully promoted depends largely on the maturity of the REIT market.

### 4.2 Competition risks

**China Overseas Real Estate’s pursuit. ** China Shipping is accelerating its layout in the commercial real estate field and has similar financing advantages to central enterprises. If China Shipping's office REIT is successfully promoted, it may form strong competition on the "asset operation + REIT" track.

**Regional barriers for local state-owned enterprises. ** Local state-owned enterprises such as Yuexiu Real Estate in Guangzhou and Huafa Co., Ltd. in Zhuhai have deep political and business relationships and land resources in their respective regions. China Resources' national layout may encounter "local protection" in some cities.

**The threat of new entrants. ** Institutional investors such as insurance funds and sovereign funds are also investing in commercial real estate, which may push up prices in the asset acquisition market.

### 4.3 Company-level risks

**Drag on development business. ** If the real estate market continues to decline, the gross profit margin of the development business may fall below 15%, dragging down the overall performance. If the 398.1 billion inventory suffers large-scale impairment, it will seriously impact the income statement and balance sheet.

**Control of expansion rhythm. ** Equity land acquisition in the first half of 2025 increased by 76% year-on-year, indicating that China Resources is increasing leverage against the trend. If the market does not pick up as expected, these new land reserves may become a burden.

## 6. Core Conclusions

Look at this question with reverse thinking: **Should you invest in an industry that is shrinking significantly? **

The answer depends on whether you are investing in "volume" or "structural changes" in the industry.

If you are investing in "volume" (total residential sales), then the long-term outlook is bleak. China's residential sales peak has passed and will not come back.

But if the investment is "structural changes" (increased concentration of central enterprises, shift from development to operations, REIT system dividends), then China Resources Land happens to be standing at the intersection of these changes.

**The essential opportunity for China Resources Land is not "real estate recovery" but "winner-take-all in industry restructuring." ** It is growing market share in a shrinking industry while switching more of its profit sources to non-cyclical recurring businesses.

Investors need to be vigilant: do not regard "the industry's bottoming out" as a reason to invest in China Resources Land. The correct investment logic should be: Even if the industry does not rebound, China Resources Land's recurring business can provide stable growth and reasonable returns. If the industry rebounds, that's just a bonus.
