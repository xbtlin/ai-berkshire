# I, core risk profile

#1.1 Risk I: Development business Māori rate continues to deteriorate

** Risk description:** The Māori ratio for the development sales business continued to decline from 42.9% in 2018 to 15.5% in 2025. If it continues down to below 12%, the development business may be close to the profit/loss balance or even loss.

** Probability assessment: ** Medium high. Current inventory property account value is $3981 billion, and management acknowledges the pressure of decolonization. If house prices continue to fall in 2026-2027, high-priced projects will face greater pressure on profitability when they are settled.

** Impact assessment:** While recurrent operations have contributed more than 50 per cent of the core net profits, development operations remain the main source of revenue (84.6 per cent). If there is a loss in development operations, this will directly affect the overall statement and may raise market questions about the quality of the company.

** buffer:**
- China Yuchi has focused on the second-line core in recent years, and the project is of relatively good quality
- 94 per cent of first- and second-line urban investment in the first half of 2025, reducing risk exposure in three or four-line cities
- The identity of the company gives it a voice in pricing (brand premium)

##1.2 Risk II: inventory impairment risk <unk> (high risk)

** Risk description: **The inventory property of $39.81 billion is the largest source of uncertainty in the balance sheet. If house prices continue to fall, these stocks are exposed to a risk of impairment.

**Quantitative extension:** Assuming that the inventory industry needs to be prepared for 5% impairment, or approximately $19.9 billion, this will directly absorb nearly a year of core net profits (22.48 billion). While the actual impairment may not be so concentrated, the magnitude of the risk cannot be ignored.

** Historical reference:** Vanko, Bicqua Garden, etc., experienced large stock impairments during the industry’s downtime. China’s lube may be less than their peers, but not completely immune, due to the quality of the project.

##1.3 Risk III: Size of debt and interest rate environment <unk> (medium risk)

** Risk description:** Total lending of $281.5 billion, although the net liability rate of 39.2% is still good, is in absolute magnitude. If the interest rate environment changes or refinancing difficulties arise, financial costs will increase significantly.

** Probability assessment: ** Low. The Chinese-provincial identity makes it virtually non-refinancing risk. 2.72% of financing costs are continuously declining and six years apart, which indicates that financing is open.

** But it is a matter of concern:** Net debt ratio increased by 7.3 to 39.2% over the same period, and if the negative expansion (and the construction of the shopping malls) continues, the leverage rate may increase further.

#1.4 Risk IV: Investment property fair value fluctuations <unk> (medium risk)

** Risk description:** China Yuen holds large investment holdings, recorded at fair value. If macroeconomic deterioration leads to expected rental reductions or an increase in capitalization, the fair value of the investment property may be reduced and the profit statement hit.

** Impact path:** Changes in fair value of investment property are the main source of the difference between "core net profit" and "report net profit". In 2025, the difference (25.42 billion vs 22.480 billion) implies that fair value changes contribute to the positive impact of about $2.9 billion. If the direction is reversed, the decline in profits will accelerate.

##1.5 Risk five: Consumer environment and mall operating risk <unk> (medium risk)

** Risk description:** Vientiane shopping malls are the core assets of China’s luxuries. If consumption continues to weaken, brand tenants withdraw their rents, and new start-up projects climb further than expected, this will affect the growth of recurrent income.

** On both sides:**
- ** Positive: ** Retail sales grew by 22.4 per cent in 2025 and business profitability by 63.1 per cent was innovative, indicating strong operations
- ** Negative:** This increase is partly due to post-epidemic rehabilitation and new start-up projects, which may not be sustainable.

#1.6 Risk six: Policy and regulatory risk <unk> (lower risk)

** Risk description:** Real estate is highly influenced by policy. Policies such as restricted purchase, restricted lending, limited prices may affect sales and pricing.

** Current judgement:** Policy direction is generally favourable - inventory, push REIT, encourage city renewal. But there is uncertainty about the pace and pace of policy implementation. The progress of the RIT pilot expansion, and the specific rules of the commercial real estate REIT, may affect the strategic advancement of China.

# II. Management assessment

- ** Zhao Wei** succeeds Guo Shiqing as Director of Finance

It is important to note that "Hong Yue Land Holding" is a fully-owned subsidiary of the Mainland, with the function of issuing debts in the Mainland. Li Xin, as chairman of the board of directors of the listed company China Yue Land Limited, needs to be given a different look at the changes in his position.

#2.2 Lee Xin: Strategy Maker

Li Xin is the main driver of the three growth curves strategy in Hua Yuen.
- Strategic positioning of the Urban Investment Development Operator
- Promoting 50 per cent of profits from recurrent operations
- The ReIT platform was launched.
- Proposing business guidelines like "favourable revenue and cash flow profits."

** Evaluation:** Lee Xin's strategic direction is correct - to move proactively from development to operation in the industry at the bottom of the line. But there is also a controversy in implementation: for example, when industry is depressed, equity investment is still strong, and it is 76% higher than the previous, whether it is a "reverse cycle" or "radical expansion" that takes time to prove.

** Positive:** The Chinese endorsement brings financial advantages and credit support; the multidimensional configuration of the China-Lun Group in the areas of consumption, property, finance, etc. provides synergy for the Chinese-Linished.

# III. Corporate governance assessment

#3.1 Equity structure

The China-Lun Group holds about 60% of the shares in China-Lun land through its own company, and is an absolute shareholder. This means:
- The interests of large shareholders may not be fully consistent with those of small shareholders
- Related transactions (e.g. with other companies under the China-Lun Group) require attention
- Big decisions are largely determined by the Hua Yun Group.

#3.2 Information transparency

The company regularly conducts performance announcements and management is willing to interpret the strategy and performance in detail.

However, note:
- Greater subjectivity in the assessment of fair value of investment property
- Time points and ranges for impairment of inventory have management discretion
- "Core net profits" is a company-defined untarget indicator, although common in industry, the adjustment of calibres requires attention

#3.3 Shareholder Reward attitude

The rate of 37% of the distribution rate (based on core net profits) is five years of cumulative red money of HK$56 billion. This is the upper-middle level of the Ports’ Property Unit, which indicates management’s importance for shareholder returns.

# Four: Pressure test of investment argument

# 4.1 Cattle Market Situation

** Assumed:** Real estate market is warming, with Māori development going back to 18-20 per cent; recurrent business is growing at a rate of more than 15 per cent; and the RIT platform is expanding rapidly.

** Result:** Core net profit recovery to $25-28 billion, with recurrent business profits rising to over 60%. Valuations were increased from "developer" to "asset operator" from eight to 12-15 times. Stock price target is HK$50-60.

##4.2 Baseline scenario

** Assumed:** Real estate market across the board, development business Māori rate maintained at 15-16 per cent; recurrent business maintained at around 10 per cent growth; RIT platform steadily advanced.

** Result:** Core net profits maintained at $22-24 billion, with the share of recurrent business steadily rising to 55-60%. The valuation maintained 8-10 times PE. Stock prices fluctuated between HK$ 35-45.

# 4.3 Bear City scene

** Assumed:** The real estate market deteriorated further, with the Māori rate for development operations falling below 12 per cent; stock was significantly impaired; and consumption was weak to affect rental income.

** Result:** Core net profits have fallen to $15-18 billion, with inventory impairments likely to affect over 10 billion additional dollars. The valuation has been further reduced to 5-6 times PE. Stock prices may fall to HK$22-28.

# 4.4 Key signal of argument to perjury

The investment argument should be revisited in any of the following cases:
1. The rate of increase in profits from recurrent operations
2. Net liability rate out of 50 per cent
3. Large inventory impairment (over 10 billion)
4. Management turnover again
5. ReIT expansion is hampered or policy tightening
6. Continued decline in rental rates at shopping malls

# Six, core conclusion

The risk profile of the land can be summarized as "one tall and one low":

** High-risk areas:** Development business. The continued downside of the Māori rate, the risk of stock impairment, market uncertainty are real risks.

The market centre rents are growing steadily, operating efficiency is increasing, and the REIT exit route is gradually opening, which constitutes a relatively safe "basic plate".

** Management judgement: ** Overall positive. Li Xin has set the right strategic direction, and Seo-young's city updates the position of the company. But the change in management is normal, not excluding future adjustments.

** Final risk judgement:** HLN is not the subject of a "zero risk" investment. But if you agree with the value of a regular business and are willing to accept the volatility of a development business, the risk return ratio is reasonable at the current valuation level. The key is to distinguish between "permanent risk" (subversive business model) and "temporary risk" (down-of-cycle profit rate) - the main problem facing HLN is the latter.
