# IV. List of key risks

## Risk level I: Fatal risk (which may lead to the complete collapse of investment arguments)

**1. Continued deterioration of the Maori rate (probability: medium)**

If this is not seasonal fluctuations but structural decline (price war + cost rise) in 2026, the profit target of Q1 fell completely short and could even return to a sustained loss.

** Trigger**: Maori rate < 10% for two consecutive quarters
** Impact**: Investment argument is false and stock prices may return below HK$30

**2. Million vehicles with serious unmet targets (probability: medium-high)**

Q1.1 million vehicles, or 1 million vehicles for the year, means an average of approximately 74,000 vehicles per month for the next three quarters. The 71.4 million vehicles in April indicate that the pace is catching up, but completion is still very difficult throughout the year.

** Trigger **: cumulative sales in the first half of the year < 400,000
** Impact**: Downgraded expectations in the market, valuation pressure

**3. Stellanis cooperative change (probability: low-medium)**

The Stellentis strategy for running abroad will be seriously undermined if Stellantis reduces its investment in Stewardship International or adjusts its terms of cooperation.

** Trigger **: Stellantis has a small snuff-off and reduced joint venture resources
** Impact**: The logic of overseas growth is undermined, with 20-30 per cent below valuation

# Risk level II: significant risks (significant impact valuation and return)

**4. Continued escalation of the price war**

The Biadi DM5.0 series, the Gilly Galaxy series, is running at the core price of a runoff. If the price war forces the runoff to a further drop, the Maori rate will continue to be stressed.

**5. Deficiencies in driving lead to a decline in product power**

As consumers focus more on intellectual driving, the staircase for stunners may change from "no-lose" to "trapped-up." If urban piloting fails to land on schedule in the second half of 2026, it may affect the competitiveness of the D and C series.

**6. Increased cost of raw materials**

The price fluctuations of raw materials such as lithium, copper, rare earth, etc. directly affect costs.

**7. Capacity bottlenecks**

Between 600,000 and 1 million, capacity is doubled. If the new capacity is not built as expected, or if the yield climbs are not good enough, it will affect both sales and costs.
