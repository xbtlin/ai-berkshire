## 2. Profitability: the benchmark for global exchanges

### Profit rate analysis

| Indicators | Hong Kong Stock Exchange | Industry Level | Evaluation |
|------|--------|----------|------|
| EBITDA profit margin | 65%-70% | 50%-60% | Significantly higher than peers |
| Net profit margin | ~61% (2025) | 30%-50% | Global leader |
| ROE | ~27%-32% | 15%-25% | Excellent |

The profitability of HKEX ranks first among global exchanges. A net profit margin of 61% means that for every HK$100 of revenue, HK$61 is turned into net profit. This is the joint result of the asset-light model and monopoly status.

### In-depth analysis of ROE

The ROE of the Hong Kong Stock Exchange has been stable at 20%-30% in the past ten years, with an average value of approximately 24.6%. It is expected to reach 31.7% in 2025 due to an explosion in performance.

Breakdown of ROE (DuPont Analysis):
- **High net profit margin** (~61%): monopoly pricing power + light asset model
- **Moderate asset turnover rate**: The exchange model does not require high turnover
- **Lower financial leverage**: HKEx has little need for debt to operate

This is a **high quality ROE** - not achieved through leverage, but driven purely by profitability. It is this kind of ROE that Buffett values ​​most: high and stable, without relying on financial leverage.

## 3. Capital allocation: simple and disciplined

### Capital expenditures and free cash flow

The HKEx is a cash flow generating machine. As an asset-light company, capital expenditures are mainly used for IT system upgrades and office facility maintenance, accounting for a very low proportion of revenue. This means that the vast majority of profits can be converted into free cash flow.

### Shareholder Return Policy

HKEX’s shareholder return policy is extremely simple: **fixed 90% dividend payout ratio**.

The dividend per share in 2025 is HK$12.52. Based on the current stock price of approximately HK$330, the dividend rate is approximately 3.0%. The cumulative dividends in 2017 exceeded HK$100 billion.

Rating:
- **Positive**: The 90% dividend payout ratio shows that management is very restrained in capital allocation and will not spend money randomly for diversification.
- **Negative**: Almost no profits are retained for reinvestment, which also means that future growth mainly depends on the natural growth of the market rather than growth driven by capital investment.
- **Neutral**: For a monopolistic toll station business, a 90% dividend payout is reasonable - it does not require significant reinvestment to maintain a competitive position.

### Major capital allocations in history

- **Acquisition of LME in 2012 (HK$16.7 billion)**: This is the largest acquisition in the history of the Hong Kong Stock Exchange, with the purpose of expanding the commodities business. More than ten years later, the LME's revenue contribution is still limited (annual revenue is approximately US$130 million), but its strategic value lies in its acquisition of global nonferrous metal pricing power. The financial return from the acquisition was modest, but it wasn't a disaster either.

## 4. Long-term growth path

### Historical growth

From 2000 to 2022, the compound growth rate of Hong Kong Stock Exchange’s revenue and net profit reached **10.9% and 13.5%** respectively. This is a very solid long-term growth record, considering that it has experienced multiple shocks such as the 2008 financial crisis and the 2020 epidemic.

### Growth drivers

**1. Structural growth (high predictability)**
- Expansion of interconnection: expansion of target scope, inclusion of ETFs, and continuous improvement of bulk trading and other mechanisms
- The general trend of China’s capital market opening up: MSCI inclusion, global asset allocation of Chinese assets
- Derivatives market development: The derivatives penetration rate of the Hong Kong Stock Exchange is far lower than that of mature markets, and there is huge room for development.

**2. Cyclical growth (high volatility)**
- Hong Kong stock market activity: ADT will be HK$231.5 billion in 2025, but it was as low as HK$124.9 billion in 2022
- IPO market: 286.9 billion Hong Kong dollars will be raised in 2025, but it will shrink significantly in 2022-2023
-Interest rate environment: affecting margin investment returns

### Medium-term growth expectations

Brokerages unanimously expect revenue growth in 2026 to be about 5%-10%, assuming ADT remains at around HK$250 billion. If the market continues to be active, there is room for upside; if the market cools down, growth will slow down or even decline.

In the long term, a compound profit growth rate of 10%-15% is a reasonable central assumption, which is basically consistent with the historical growth rate.

## 5. Valuation analysis

### Current valuation level

| Valuation indicators | Values | Historical quantiles |
|----------|------|----------|
| PE (TTM) | ~30 times | Historical center is on the upper side |
| PE (2026E) | ~30 times | Based on consensus expectations of brokers |
| PB | ~8-9x | Matches ROE |
| Dividend yield | ~3.0% | Historical center level |
| Market capitalization | ~HK$415 billion | — |

### Reasonable range of PE valuation

The historical PE fluctuation range of the Hong Kong Stock Exchange is roughly between 25-40 times:
- **25-28 times**: When the market is extremely pessimistic or Hong Kong stock trading is sluggish
- **30-35 times**: normal valuation center
- **35-40 times**: The market is extremely optimistic, the peak of the bull market

The current PE of about 30 times is near the historical center, which is not expensive but not cheap either.

### The core contradiction of valuation

**Opposite opinion: 30 times PE is reasonable or even low**
- Legal monopoly status with extremely high certainty
- ROE above 27%, excellent profit quality
- Interconnection brings structural growth, not just cycles
- 90% dividend payout ratio, clear shareholder returns
- Similar global exchanges (CME, ICE, LSEG) are also trading at 25-35 times PE

**Contrary view: 30 times PE is too expensive**
- 2025 is a big year for performance (ADT +90% year-on-year), and the growth rate is likely to fall back in 2026
- If the valuation is based on normalized profits (ADT returns to the level of HK$150-180 billion), the PE may be more than 40 times
- The activity of the Hong Kong stock market is highly dependent on policy and sentiment, with large cyclical fluctuations.
- Geopolitical risks discount valuations

### Thoughts from DCF perspective

Assumptions:
- Long-term profit growth rate: 10% (conservative) to 13% (optimistic)
- Discount rate: 8%-10%
- Sustainable growth rate: 3%

Under these assumptions, the intrinsic value of HKEx is roughly between HK$300-420. The current price of about HKD 330 is in the middle and lower part of the reasonable range.

## 6. What would Buffett think?

Buffett will definitely appreciate the business model of the Hong Kong Stock Exchange: monopoly position, high ROE, light assets, strong cash flow, and simple and easy to understand. It's the same type of "economic franchise" he saw at See's Candies and Moody's.

But Buffett also looks at valuations. 30 times PE means that the buying yield is about 3.3%, plus 10% expected growth, the total return is about 13% - this is acceptable for a high-certainty monopoly asset, but it does not have a very safe margin.

**Buffett's possible conclusion**: Good business, reasonable price, but not an opportunity to "pick up bargains". If the PE falls back below 25 times, the attractiveness will increase significantly.
