# I. Risk profile: from core to edge

Lee-soo stresses that the most important thing in investment is "no loss." To do so, all risks that could lead to permanent capital losses need to be systematically identified and assessed.

# First layer: risk of survival (permanent destruction)

1. Fundamental wavering of the position of Hong Kong ' s international financial centre
** Risk description**: If Hong Kong loses its international financial centre function as a result of extreme geopolitical events such as the military conflict in the Taiwan Strait, large-scale international sanctions, the very foundations of the business model of the Hong Kong border crossing will be destroyed.

** Probability assessment**: 5-10% (for the next 10 years)

** Reason**:
- The Chinese Government has a strong incentive to preserve the financial center of Hong Kong - a central gateway to the opening of the Chinese economy to the outside world.
- Global capital flows in 2025 show that, despite geopolitical tensions, capital inflows to Hong Kong are accelerating
- The CEO Chen Jianting of Hong Kong Port Authority noted that geopolitical uncertainty is "driving capital to Hong Kong Market" - global investors need to be decentralized and Hong Kong provides the best regulatory access to investment in China.

** But cannot ignore**:
- It's a tail risk. If it's a big impact.
- A round of foreign withdrawals of the Ports ' shares occurred in 2022-2023, although it was reversed later
- There's no financial center in history.

##2. Connectivity mechanisms are abolished or significantly reduced
** Risk description**: If the Mainland closes or limits the connectivity for policy reasons, the port terminal loses its most important engine of growth.

** Probability assessment**: <3% (for the next 10 years)

** Reason**: Connectivity is the cornerstone of China's capital market opening strategy, and its abolition would close the door to China's capital market opening. The current trend is to expand its capacity rather than shrink – the scope of the targets is constantly expanding, the ETF is included, and large trading mechanisms are being studied.

## Second tier: significant risk (which could result in a significant decline in valuation)

##3. The market for the stock market is so low
** Risk description**: If China's economy continues to weaken and business profits decline, the Hong Kong stock of ADT may remain low for long.

** Probability assessment**: 20-30 per cent (a one-two-year period of depression in the next 3-5 years)

**Quantification of impact**:
- In 2022, ADT was about HK$ 12,4.9 billion, and the net profit from the counter-port was about HK$ 11.9 billion
- In 2025, ADT was about HK$ 23.15 billion, corresponding to a net profit of HK$ 17.754 billion
- If ADT returns to HK$150 billion, net profits could fall to about HK$13-14 billion
- This means that the low-pregnancy period may rise to 30-32 times based on current market value

** buffers**: About 35 per cent of the revenue from port offices is not directly linked to the volume of transactions (marketing fees, data fees, investment earnings, etc.), providing some downside protection.

## 4. IPO market is cold
** Risk description**: IPO financing in 2025 is an anomaly, significantly higher than the historical average.

** Probability assessment**: more than 50 per cent (possibly falling in 2026-2027)

** Impact**: The cost of listing is only about 6 per cent of total income, with limited direct impact. But the decrease in IPOs means fewer new listed companies, with long-term effects on the breadth and depth of the market.

5. Falling interest rates erode investment returns
** Risk description**: Ports hold deposits and clearing house funds of approximately HK$245.2 billion, which generate about 18% of total investment earnings. If the world enters a sustained interest-relief cycle, this share will be under pressure.

** Probability assessment**: 40-50 per cent

** Impact**: Investment returns will affect total income by about 2 per cent per 10 per cent decline.

#3rd floor: Minor risk (needs attention but does not pose a significant threat)

##6. Regulatory risk
There is an inherent conflict of interest in the dual identity of the port office (both as a listed company and as a market regulator). If the regulatory structure is adjusted in the future (e.g. by transferring the authority to register from the port to the CVM), it may affect its scope of operations.

##7. Technology system risk
Any major system failure could damage its reputation and market confidence. Ports and ports have invested more in technological upgrading in recent years, with a new generation of trading platforms launched by LME in 2025.

## 8. Rates downpressure
Although ports have all monopoly positions, regulators and market participants are pushing for lower transaction costs. The port offices have optimized the small transaction rates in 2025.

# Board and governance

The Hong Kong border post has a relatively special governance structure: the HKSAR Government appoints six directors (including the President) through the Director of Finance, and the remaining six are elected by shareholders.

Evaluation: <unk> The governance structure is more balanced, but the high level of government influence means that capital allocation may not be based solely on shareholder interests.

# IV. Assessment of investment suitability of Lee Sze perspective

Lee Su-hwan's investment philosophy emphasizes several dimensions:

# 1. Is it in the "competence circle"?
The exchange’s business model is simple and clear, and does not require an understanding of complex technologies or products. The main variables are market turnover, interconnectivity progress and interest rate environments.**

# 2. Do you understand the Chinese story?
The core narrative of the Hong Kong Exchange is "the biggest beneficiary of China's capital market opening out." To invest in the Hong Kong Exchange, it is essentially necessary to believe that: (a) China's economy will continue to grow; (b) China's capital markets will continue to be open; and (c) Hong Kong will continue to serve as a bridge to this process.

#3. Is management trustworthy?
Chen Jian Jian Jian has shown professionalism and pragmatism, but the term of office is short. But management is less important to Hong Kong business, where "management is no less poor than money".

## 4. Are prices safe margin?
This is the biggest problem today. 30 times the PE corresponds to "reasonable valuation," not "underestimation." Lee Sze stresses that the principle of "security margin" is not very satisfactory here.
