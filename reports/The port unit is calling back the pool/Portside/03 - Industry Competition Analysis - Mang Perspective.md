# Hong Kong Exchange (0388.HK) Industry and Competition Analysis

> Analysis dimensions: industry end-game thinking, competitive landscape, negative arguments, multidisciplinary models
> Data as of: May 2026

---

## 1. The essence of the exchange industry: a winner-takes-all network effect business

Munger emphasized the need to use a multidisciplinary thinking model to look at problems. To analyze the exchange industry, at least three models are needed:

### Model 1: Bilateral Network Effect

An exchange is a classic two-sided market - buyers and sellers meet on the same platform. Liquidity is the core competitive factor in this market: the more concentrated the liquidity of an exchange, the smaller the bid-ask spread and the lower the transaction cost, thus attracting more participants. This creates a positive feedback loop, making leading exchanges stronger and stronger, making it almost impossible for latecomers to catch up.

### Model 2: Economies of Scale

The marginal cost of an exchange is almost zero—the incremental cost of processing one more transaction is minimal. This means that revenue growth can be almost completely converted into profit growth. The Hong Kong Stock Exchange’s revenue will grow by 30% and profits by 36% in 2025, which is a reflection of economies of scale. Once fixed costs are covered, more than 70% of each incremental revenue in Hong Kong dollars becomes profit.

### Model 3: Institutional moat

Unlike most industries, where the moat comes from market competition, a large part of the exchange's moat comes from institutional arrangements. The monopoly status of the Hong Kong Stock Exchange is conferred by Hong Kong law, and the interconnection mechanism was jointly established by the regulatory agencies of the two places. This institutional moat is stronger than a purely commercial moat because it is less likely to be overturned by technological innovation or business competition.

| NASDAQ | ~450 | ~25x | Technology stock listing platform | IPO competition |
| Hong Kong Exchange (HKEX) | ~530 | ~30x | China portal monopoly | — |
| Singapore Exchange (SGX) | ~100 | ~22x | Southeast Asia Regional Center | Partial Competition |

Key observation: The PE of the world's major exchanges is generally 25-35 times, and the Hong Kong Stock Exchange's valuation of 30 times is in the middle. But HKEX's growth prospects - benefiting from the opening of China's capital markets - may be better than those in mature markets.

### Industry Trends

**1. Transformation from transactions to data and technology**
After LSEG acquired Refinitiv, data and analysis business revenue accounted for more than 60%. ICE's data business also contributed about 40% of revenue. The data business of the Hong Kong Stock Exchange only accounts for about 11%, which is both a gap and an opportunity.

**2. Derivatives business is becoming increasingly important**
Global derivatives trading volume continues to grow, and the derivatives business of CME and ICE contributes the main revenue. The penetration rate of derivatives on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange is relatively low, but it is improving—derivatives market trading volume will hit a record high in 2025.

**3. Platformization of exchanges**
Modern exchanges are no longer just trading venues, but financial infrastructure platforms covering the entire chain of trading, clearing, data, technology, and risk management.

## 3. Competitive landscape faced by the Hong Kong Stock Exchange

### Competitor 1: Shanghai/Shenzhen Exchange (A-share market)

**Relationship**: both competitive and complementary.

The size of the A-share market (total market value of approximately 80 trillion yuan) is much larger than that of Hong Kong stocks (approximately HK$42 trillion), and it is also a strong competitor in terms of IPO financing. However, the two markets have significant differences in investor structure (Hong Kong stocks are dominated by institutions, and A-shares are dominated by retail investors), listing systems, and valuation systems.

The interconnection mechanism enables the two to form a symbiotic relationship: the opening of A-shares requires the passage of the Hong Kong Stock Exchange, and the activity of the Hong Kong Stock Exchange also depends on the attractiveness of the A-share targets. Chen Yiting, CEO of the Hong Kong Stock Exchange, made it clear that the relationship with the Shanghai Stock Exchange is "dislocation development and complementary cooperation" rather than a zero-sum game.

**Threat Level**: ★★★☆☆—In the long term, if mainland China’s capital market is fully open, the value of the interconnection mechanism may decline. But this process will be very long.

### Competitor 2: Nasdaq/New York Stock Exchange (US market)

**Relationship**: Direct competition in IPOs and listings.

The listing of Chinese concept stocks in the United States was once a thorn in the side of the Hong Kong Stock Exchange. However, in recent years, geopolitical factors (controversies over the auditing and supervision of Chinese concept stocks, delisting risks) have instead prompted a large number of Chinese concept stocks to return to Hong Kong stocks, which has been a boon to the Hong Kong Stock Exchange. In 2025, the IPO financing amount of the Hong Kong Stock Exchange will return to the first place in the world.

**Threat Level**: ★★☆☆☆—Geopolitical trends are favorable to the Hong Kong Stock Exchange in the short term, and the resurgence of Chinese concept stocks may continue.

### Competitor 3: Singapore Exchange (SGX)

**Relationship**: Regional competition.

Singapore has been trying to compete with Hong Kong as Asia's financial center. SGX has advantages in the Southeast Asian market, and the political environment is considered more neutral by some investors. However, SGX's size (market value is only about 1/5 of that of the Hong Kong Stock Exchange) and depth of liquidity are far less than those of the Hong Kong Stock Exchange, and it is even less competitive in China-related businesses.

**Threat Level**: ★★☆☆☆—The magnitude gap is too large to shake the status of the Hong Kong Stock Exchange.

### Competitor 4: Virtual Asset Exchange

**Relationships**: Emerging competition, but limited impact so far.

Cryptocurrency and digital asset exchanges such as Coinbase and others are growing into new asset trading platforms. The Hong Kong Stock Exchange has actively deployed virtual assets and promoted digital asset transactions under Hong Kong’s regulatory framework.

**Threat Level**: ★☆☆☆☆—The short-term impact on traditional stock exchanges is limited.

## 4. Munger’s negative argument: Under what circumstances will the Hong Kong Stock Exchange become worse?

Munger said, "Think the other way around, always think the other way around." Investors in HKEx need to seriously consider the following negative scenarios:

### Scenario 1: Hong Kong’s status as a financial center is weakened

**Triggering factors**: Extreme geopolitical conflicts (such as the escalation of tensions in the Taiwan Strait leading to international sanctions), further restrictions on Hong Kong's rule of law and freedom, and a large-scale withdrawal of global investors.

**Impact**: If Hong Kong loses its positioning as an "international financial center", foreign capital inflows decrease, and the liquidity of Hong Kong stocks shrinks, the Hong Kong Stock Exchange will face a structural recession.

**Probability Assessment**: Low probability but high impact. At present, the Chinese government has a strong intention to maintain Hong Kong's status as a financial center, because this is indispensable for the opening up of China's capital market.

### Scenario 2: China’s capital market is fully open

**Triggering factors**: The RMB is fully convertible, and foreign investors can directly invest in A-shares without going through the interconnection.

**Impact**: The value of the interconnection mechanism has been reduced to zero, and the Hong Kong Stock Exchange has lost its unique positioning as the "Gateway to China".

**Probability Assessment**: The probability of happening in the next 10 years is extremely low. Capital account opening is an extremely gradual process, and complete opening does not meet China's risk management needs.

### Scenario 3: Hong Kong stock market remains depressed for a long time

**Triggers**: China's economy continues to slow down, corporate profits deteriorate, and valuations further compress.

**Impact**: ADT falls back to the level of HK$120-150 billion (similar to 2022-2023), and the revenue and profits of the Hong Kong Stock Exchange decline significantly.

**Probability Assessment**: Medium probability. The cyclical fluctuations of the Hong Kong stock market are certain. The question is how long the downturn will last. The prosperity of 2025 may not be sustainable.

### Scenario 4: Technological disruption

**Triggering factors**: Blockchain technology has made decentralized trading mainstream, and the intermediary role of traditional exchanges has been weakened.

**Impact**: The exchange's "toll booth" model is bypassed.

**Probability Assessment**: Extremely low probability. The regulatory nature of securities trading determines that it requires a regulated centralized platform. Decentralization is unlikely to replace traditional exchanges in the foreseeable future.

## 5. Industry Final Deduction

Munger likes to think about the end state of industries. The final outcome of the exchange industry is roughly:

**Dominated by a handful of super exchange groups in the world**, each group has a quasi-monopoly position in its core geography and product areas. The current situation is close to this ending:
- US: CME (derivatives) + NASDAQ/NYSE (equities)
- Europe: LSEG + Euronext + Deutsche Boerse
- Asia: Hong Kong Exchange (China Gateway) + Japan Exchange + SGX (Southeast Asia)

HKEX’s position in this endgame is very solid – **the international trading hub for China-related assets**. As long as China's share of the global economy continues to rise, the value of this hub will continue to grow.

## 7. Munger’s possible summary

"HKEX is a good business that I can understand. It has three moats - legal monopoly, interconnection, and liquidity network effects - any one of which is enough to deter competitors. The endgame of the global exchange industry is already clear, and HKEx occupies a very big part of it. A favorable position. But I also see a gray rhino: the value of this business is based on the premise of Hong Kong as an international financial center, and this premise is affected by geopolitics. The PE pricing of 30 times has reflected optimistic expectations, leaving little margin of safety for investors. "
