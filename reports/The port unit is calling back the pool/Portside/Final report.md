## 1. Core conclusion

**The Hong Kong Stock Exchange is a near-perfect business, but the current price is not a perfect one. **

30 times PE buys a monopoly asset with a long-term growth center of 10%-13%, and the expected annual return is about 13% (3% dividend + 10% growth). This is an opportunity for "reasonable return" rather than "excessive return". For long-term investors who pursue certainty, the Hong Kong Stock Exchange is worthy of being included in the observation pool; for value investors who pursue a margin of safety, they need to wait for a better buying price.

## 3. Pros and cons of investment arguments

### Bull Case

**1. One of the strongest moat combinations**
Legal monopoly (the only exchange in Hong Kong) + institutional moat (interconnection mechanism) + network effect (liquidity concentration). There are only a handful of companies in the world that have triple moats at the same time.

**2. The biggest beneficiary of China’s capital market opening**
China's GDP accounts for about 18% of the world's GDP, but the proportion of Chinese assets in global asset allocation is far lower than this. As global investors increase their allocation of Chinese assets, the Hong Kong Stock Exchange, as a core channel, will continue to benefit. The scope of interconnection targets is still expanding (there are more than 300 listing applications in hand at the beginning of 2026), which is a structural increase.

**3. 2025 performance proves growth elasticity**
ADT +89.5% year-on-year, IPO financing +227%, net profit +36%. When markets are active, HKEX's operating leverage is extremely strong.

**4. Global peer valuation benchmark**
CME 25 times PE, ICE 30 times PE, LSEG 28 times PE. HKEX’s PE of 30 times is within a reasonable range among global exchanges, and can even be said to be a slight discount considering its higher growth potential.

**5. High dividends provide downside protection**
With a payout ratio of 90% and a dividend yield of approximately 3%, it provides cash returns while waiting for growth.

### Reasons to be bearish (Bear Case)

**1. 2025 may be a cyclical high**
ADT will surge from HK$124.9 billion in 2022 to HK$231.5 billion in 2025. This growth rate is unsustainable. In 2026, brokerages expect ADT to grow by only about 5%. If market sentiment reverses, ADT may fall back to HK$150-180 billion.

**2. Measured by normalized profits, the valuation is expensive**
If you use "normalized" ADT (about 180 billion Hong Kong dollars) to calculate profits, the corresponding PE may be 33-35 times, which is no longer cheap.

**3. Geopolitics is the sword of Damocles that always hangs over your head**
Although the probability of extreme scenarios is low, the tail risk of "low probability and high impact" means that the Hong Kong Stock Exchange will never be able to obtain a valuation premium that matches the quality of its business model. This is a structural discount factor.

**4. Ceiling of room for growth**
The revenue of the Hong Kong Stock Exchange is essentially highly tied to "Hong Kong market trading volume". Under the existing market structure, ADT's long-term ceiling may be HK$300-400 billion. If growth mainly comes from linear growth in volume, long-term CAGR will be difficult to exceed 15%.

**5. Data business lags behind global peers**
The data business revenue of LSEG and ICE accounts for 40%-60%, while the Hong Kong Stock Exchange only accounts for about 11%. Data business is the "second growth curve" of exchanges, and Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing lags behind in this regard.

## 5. Investment strategy suggestions

### Strategy 1: Wait for a better buying opportunity (recommended)

**Operation**: Include the Hong Kong Stock Exchange in the core observation pool and set HKD 250-280 (corresponding to PE 25-28 times) as the ideal buying range.

**Reason**:
- The cyclical fluctuations in the Hong Kong stock market will almost certainly provide better buying opportunities in the next 1-3 years
- The high ADT in 2025 is likely to be unsustainable. If the market cools down, the stock price may fall back by 20%-30%.
- 25 times PE buy, expected annual return can reach 15%-17% (4% dividend + 11%-13% growth), more sufficient margin of safety

### Strategy 2: Build a bottom position with a small position

**Operation**: Establish an observation position of 2%-3% near the current price. If the price falls below 280 Hong Kong dollars, add another position to the target position.

**Reason**:
- HKEx is one of the very few companies that is "never ridiculously cheap" - its certainty premium means it rarely experiences deep discounts
- If the Internet continues to expand and the revaluation of Chinese assets continues, we may not wait until the ideal buying price
- 3% dividend provides cash return during holding period

### Strategy Three: Not Participating

**Reason**:
- Insufficient safety margin of 30 times PE
- The expected annualized return is about 13%, which is not attractive enough for taking on geopolitical tail risks
- Money has better places to go
