# Hong Kong Exchange (0388.HK) Business Model Analysis

> Analysis dimensions: business model essence, user value, moat depth, pricing power and economic franchise
> Data as of: May 2026

---

## 1. What is the essence of this business?

The essence of the Hong Kong Stock Exchange is a **financial infrastructure toll station**. It does not bear counterparty risks and does not have to play the game itself. It only provides a standardized trading, clearing, and settlement platform for all participants, and then charges channel fees based on transaction volume and transaction amount.

This business model can be summarized in one sentence: **You trade, and I collect the toll**.

Specifically broken down, the revenue sources of the Hong Kong Stock Exchange include four major parts:

**1. Transaction and clearing fees (accounting for about 65%)**
This is the core source of income. For every stock purchase, derivatives transaction, and commodity transaction completed on the HKEX platform, HKEx will charge transaction fees and clearing fees. In 2025, the total revenue from transaction fees and trading system usage fees, settlement and payment fees, custody and agent service fees will account for 64.7%, a year-on-year increase of 6.4 percentage points.

**2. Listing fee (approximately 6%)**
Companies that IPO or maintain their listing status on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange need to pay listing fees. The listing fee on the Stock Exchange in 2025 will be approximately HK$1.789 billion, benefiting from the listing of 119 new stocks and the increase in structured products.

**3. Investment income (accounting for about 18%)**
The Hong Kong Stock Exchange holds a large amount of margin and clearing house funds (approximately HK$245.2 billion in 2025), and the investment income from these funds constitutes an important source of income. This is essentially "making money with other people's money" - the margin deposited by trading participants generates interest income for the Hong Kong Stock Exchange.

**4. Market data and others (accounting for about 11%)**
Sell real-time market data, provide network connection services, etc. The data business will grow by approximately 5% year-on-year in 2025, with a slow but extremely stable growth rate.

### The subtleties of the business model

Duan Yongping said that it depends on whether a business "can be done well without being too smart." This is exactly what the Hong Kong Stock Exchange does:

- **Asset-light**: No factories, inventories, or large sales teams required. The main assets are technical systems and licenses.
- **High leverage operation**: The proportion of operating expenses is stable in the long term, the EBITDA profit margin is as high as 65%-70%, and the net profit margin is 50%-60% all year round. Revenue will grow by 30% and net profit by 36% in 2025, reflecting strong operating leverage.
- **Natural recurring income**: As long as the market is still functioning, there are transactions and there is income. Even if there is a bear market and trading volume drops, it will not return to zero.
- **Network Effect**: The more companies listed here, the better the liquidity; the better the liquidity, the more investors participate; the more investors participate, the more companies are willing to list. This is a self-reinforcing positive cycle.

## 2. How deep is the moat?

### Moat 1: Legal monopoly status ★★★★★

HKEX is the only recognized exchange group in Hong Kong. This is not obtained through market competition, but a legal monopoly. The Hong Kong SAR government is unlikely to approve a second exchange to compete with it. This means that any participant who wants to trade securities in the Hong Kong market must go through the Hong Kong Exchange's platform.

Unlike the U.S. market (where multiple exchanges such as NYSE, NASDAQ, and CBOE compete), Hong Kong adopts a single exchange model, which gives the Hong Kong Exchange strong pricing power.

### Moat 2: The irreplaceability of interconnection ★★★★★

The Shanghai-Shenzhen-Hong Kong Stock Connect mechanism makes the Hong Kong Stock Exchange one of the only compliant channels** for global investors to invest in China’s A-shares and for mainland investors to invest overseas. In 2025, the trading volume of Shanghai-Shenzhen-Hong Kong Stock Connect reached a record high, and the average daily transactions continued to be active.

This status is institutional - jointly constructed by the China Securities Regulatory Commission and the Hong Kong Securities Regulatory Commission and cannot be copied by any competitor. Neither the Singapore Exchange nor the Tokyo Exchange has this channel.

### Moat 3: Self-reinforcement of liquidity ★★★★☆

The exchange is a typical two-sided market where buyers and sellers meet on the same platform. Exchanges with more concentrated liquidity will have smaller bid-ask spreads and lower transaction costs, thus attracting more participants. This is a winner-takes-all situation.

The average daily trading volume of the Hong Kong stock spot market in 2025 will reach HK$231.5 billion, a year-on-year increase of nearly 90%. This depth of liquidity cannot be replicated by competitors in the short term.

### Moat Four: Switching Costs ★★★★☆

For listed companies, the costs of moving from one exchange to another are extremely high (legal, regulatory, investor base rebuilding, etc.). For trading participants and clearing members, the technical investment and compliance costs of accessing the HKEX system also create significant switching barriers.

### Moat Five: LME’s Global Pricing Power ★★★☆☆

The Hong Kong Exchange acquired the London Metal Exchange (LME) for HK$16.7 billion in 2012, gaining the global pricing center for non-ferrous metal futures. Most of the world's non-ferrous metal futures transactions are completed on the LME. In 2025, the average daily trading volume of the LME's fee-based metal contracts reached 717,000 lots. However, the LME's revenue contribution is still small and it faces competition from the Shanghai Futures Exchange.

## 3. Pricing power analysis

HKEX has **moderate pricing power**. As a monopoly, it can theoretically raise prices significantly, but in practice it is subject to two constraints:

1. **Regulatory constraints**: Although the Hong Kong Stock Exchange is a listed company, its regulatory functions prevent it from purely maximizing profits. The China Securities Regulatory Commission has the authority to approve fee adjustments.
2. **Competitive constraints**: If the fees charged by the Hong Kong Stock Exchange are too high, companies will choose to list in the United States, Singapore or A-shares. In 2025, the Hong Kong Stock Exchange also took the initiative to optimize transaction fee standards and reduce small transaction costs to improve liquidity.

But it is worth noting that the pricing power of the Hong Kong Stock Exchange is more fully reflected in the "volume" dimension - it does not need to increase prices, it only needs a more active market to naturally increase revenue. The 30% revenue growth in 2025 will mainly come from the explosion in transaction volume, not from fee increases.

## 4. Key questions from Duan Yongping’s perspective

**Q: Will this business still be there in ten years? **

It is almost certainly still there, and the probability is even greater. As long as Hong Kong's status as an international financial center is not overturned, the Hong Kong Stock Exchange will continue to exist. The deepening of the interconnection mechanism (more targets, more products, larger quotas) will continue to inject growth momentum into it.

**Q: How smart does management need to be? **

This is a business that "even a fool can run" - of course good management can add icing on the cake (such as promoting the expansion of interconnection and optimizing the market microstructure), but even if the management is mediocre, the Hong Kong Stock Exchange will not lose money. This is exactly the type of business Duan Yongping likes.

**Q: What could ruin this business? **

The biggest risk is not at the commercial level, but at the political level: if Hong Kong’s status as an international financial center is fundamentally shaken (an extreme geopolitical scenario), or if mainland China’s capital market is fully opened and the interconnection mechanism becomes meaningless, the moat of the Hong Kong Stock Exchange will be weakened. But both scenarios have a low probability of occurring in the foreseeable future.

## 6. Variables that require continuous attention

1. **Hong Kong Stock Average Daily Trading Volume (ADT) Trend**: This is the core driving variable of the Hong Kong Stock Exchange’s revenue. ADT will be HK$231.5 billion in 2025, and brokerages expect about HK$250 billion in 2026.
2. **Interconnection expansion rhythm**: Every target expansion and product expansion are incremental.
3. **IPO pipeline**: At the beginning of 2026, the Hong Kong Stock Exchange has over 300 listing applications, which is the potential listing fee income and subsequent transaction volume.
4. **Interest rate environment**: Affects the investment income of margin and clearing house funds.
5. **Policy certainty on Hong Kong’s status as an international financial center**.
