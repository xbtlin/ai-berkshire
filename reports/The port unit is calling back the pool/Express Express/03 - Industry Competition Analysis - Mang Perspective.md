## 1. Industry panorama: the "Warring States Era" of China's express delivery industry

### 1.1 Market size and growth

China's express delivery industry is already the world's largest express delivery market. In 2025, the industry-wide business volume will exceed 170 billion pieces, and the revenue will exceed 1.3 trillion yuan.

Growth, although slowing, remains resilient:
- Growth rate of approximately 19% in 2023
- Growth rate of approximately 21% in 2024
- Growth rate of approximately 14% in 2025
- The State Post Bureau expects the industry growth rate to be approximately 8% in 2026

The downward trend in growth rate is a definite trend, but 8% growth is still good in a mature industry. The key issue is not the total amount, but who obtains the increment and at what cost.

## 2. Analysis of competitive dynamics

### 2.1 The history and current situation of price wars

Using Munger's "think the other way around" method: Why does the express delivery industry repeatedly fall into price wars?

**Root cause analysis:**
1. **Products are highly homogeneous:** When sending a package from A to B, consumers can hardly perceive the difference between Zhongtong and Yuantong.
2. **Extremely low switching costs:** It only takes a few seconds for merchants to switch express delivery companies in the backends of Pinduoduo and Taobao
3. **Overcapacity:** Years of expansion have resulted in excess sorting and transportation capacity with low marginal costs
4. **Bargaining power of e-commerce platforms:** The platform controls traffic distribution and naturally tends to lower express delivery prices.
5. **Prisoner’s Dilemma:** No one dares to take the lead in raising prices for fear of losing share

**Price War Timeline:**
- 2019-2021: Jitu’s entry into China triggered the most intense price war, with the price of a single ticket in Yiwu falling below 1 yuan
- 2022-2023: Prices recover slightly, but competition remains fierce
- 2024: The industry single ticket price is 7.62 yuan, and the long-term downward trend has not changed
- July 2025: The State Post Bureau held a symposium on "anti-involution", marking a policy turning point.
- Q1 of 2026: The growth rate of single ticket revenue exceeds the growth rate of business volume for the first time, and the industry will see a substantial price recovery

### 2.2 The real impact of the "anti-involution" policy

The "anti-involution" policy of the State Post Bureau in July 2025 is an important variable. Judging from the data in the first quarter of 2026, the effect is showing:

**Positive Signs:**
- For the first time in two years, the national express delivery revenue growth rate (6.8%) exceeded the business volume growth rate (5.8%)
- Yunda's single ticket revenue +9.77% year-on-year, STO +11.4%, SF Express +4.2%
- Distribution fees have been raised in many places, and the price bottom line in Yiwu and other grain-producing areas has been stable.
- Extreme low-price competition has been curbed

**Uncertainty:**
- Sustainability of policy effects - Can administrative intervention change the laws of market economy?
- If companies such as Jitu choose not to abide by "industry self-discipline", the effectiveness of the policy may be greatly reduced
- After industry growth slows down, competition for share may intensify again

### 2.3 Competitors’ strategic intentions

**SF Express:** A differentiated route, focusing on time-sensitive parts and the high-end market, but it is also entering the economical parts market through SF Express. The revenue per ticket is much higher than that of the Tongda system, but the willingness to participate in price competition is increasing.

**YTO:** Following the strategy steadily, the market share is stable at around 15%. Global layout (YTO International) is the direction of differentiation.

**STO:** It has the strongest growth momentum in recent years, and Alibaba has gained resources after taking a stake. Q1 of 2025 will show strong growth potential.

**Yunda:** Relatively passive, with downward pressure on market share. In a weak position in the access system.

**Jitu:** The most aggressive challenger. Backed by Duan Yongping's resources and experience in Southeast Asia, it adopts the two-wheel drive of "price subsidy + service upgrade". First annual profit in 2025, proving sustainability.

## 3. Analysis of multiple thinking models

### 3.1 Biological model: ecological niche and competitive exclusion

The express delivery industry is similar to an "ecosystem with limited resources." According to the principle of competitive exclusion, two species in the same ecological niche cannot coexist for a long time.

But the reality of China's express delivery industry is that six or seven companies with "nearly the same ecological niche" have coexisted for more than ten years. Why?

The answer is: Although their products are similar, they each have subtle differences in specific regions and specific customer groups. ZTO has an advantage in cost, SF Express has an advantage in timeliness, and Jitu has an advantage in the sinking market. These small differences are enough to allow several companies to coexist, but not enough for any company to completely eliminate its rivals.

**Enlightenment:** The expectation that the industry will quickly consolidate into two or three companies may be too optimistic. It may be normal for six or seven companies to coexist for a long time and keep profit margins at a low level.

### 3.2 Game theory model: from prisoner’s dilemma to cooperative game

A price war is essentially a multi-person prisoner's dilemma: each company's optimal strategy is to lower prices to grab share, but if everyone lowers prices at the same time, everyone will lose money.

The role of the "anti-involution" policy is similar to the introduction of an "enforcer", changing the game from a "one-time game" to a "repeated game", making cooperation (maintaining prices) possible.

But the stability of this equilibrium depends on:
- Number of participants (the more, the harder it is to maintain cooperation)
- Penalty for breach of contract (whether the policy is strictly enforced)
- The degree of radicalization of individual participants (whether Jitu is willing to cooperate)

### 3.3 Industrial Economics Model: Economies of Scale and Minimum Effective Scale

There are significant economies of scale in the express delivery industry, but there is also a "minimum effective scale" (MES) - when the volume of pieces reaches a certain level, the marginal cost decrease caused by continuing to increase the volume of pieces decreases.

ZTO, YTO, STO and Jitu have all exceeded the minimum effective scale. This means that even though ZTO is the largest, its cost advantage over YTO and Jitu may not be as great as imagined. "Diminishing economies of scale" limits the depth of the leader's moat.

### 3.4 Psychological Model: Anchoring Effect and Herd Psychology

The market's valuation of express delivery stocks has long been anchored by the "price war" narrative. The thinking model of investors is: "The express delivery industry is all about price wars - profits are unsustainable - and it is not worthy of high valuations." This anchoring can lead to lagging market responses to industry changes.

If the "anti-involution" policy does change the logic of industry competition, there may be an "expectation difference" in the current valuation. But on the other hand, we must be wary: we cannot conclude that the industry has completely changed just because of one or two quarters of data.

## 4. Industry Final Deduction

### 4.1 Optimistic Scenario: The Era of Value War

- Anti-involution policies continue to be effective, and industry competition shifts from price to service and efficiency
- 2-3 weak companies are gradually liquidated or acquired
- Industry concentration increased to CR3, reaching over 60%
- Profit margins of leading companies have rebounded, and the valuation center has moved up to 15-18 times PE
- Probability assessment: 30%

### 4.2 Neutral Scenario: Mild Competition

- The policy maintains the bottom line and avoids extreme price wars
- Sixty-seven companies continue to coexist, and their share slowly adjusts
- Industry profit margins remain at current levels, neither deteriorating nor significantly improving
- Leading companies rely on cost advantages to maintain reasonable profits, with valuations maintained at 12-15 times PE
- Probability assessment: 50%

### 4.3 Pessimistic Scenario: Price War Restarts

- Industry growth slowed further to below 5%
- A certain company (such as Jitu) once again launched an aggressive battle for share
- Policies cannot be effectively constrained, and price wars have returned.
- ZTO’s profits fell by 20-30%, and its valuation was compressed to less than 10 times
- Probability assessment: 20%

## 5. Zhongtong’s competitive positioning

### 5.1 Core Competence ★★★★☆

ZTO is indeed the industry benchmark in terms of cost control. However, the durability of "cost leadership" in the express delivery industry is questionable - competitors are also continuing to reduce costs, and the gap is narrowing.

### 5.2 Strategic Flexibility ★★★☆☆

ZTO’s layout in the parts and components business (average daily volume of 9.8 million pieces, +38%) shows strategic vision. The profit margin of bulk parts is higher than that of e-commerce parts, and it does not rely on platform distribution. However, the scale of the spare parts market is limited, and whether it can become the second growth curve still needs to be verified.

### 5.3 Stability of industry position ★★★★☆

As the business volume champion for ten consecutive years, ZTO’s scale advantage is difficult to overturn in the short term. However, the trend of market share falling from 22% to 19.4% requires vigilance - if it continues to decline to 17-18%, the cost advantage may be eroded.

---

## 6. Overall evaluation

### Industry competitive landscape rating: ★★★☆☆ (3/5)

**This is an industry with fierce competition and unclear structure. ** Product homogeneity, numerous participants, and slow integration determine that express delivery is not an industry in which it is easy to earn "excess profits."

**Zhongtong's position is "the best student in the poor class". ** It is indeed the most profitable company in the industry, but this is more because competitors are less profitable than ZTO itself has insurmountable barriers.

**"Anti-involution" policy is the key variable. ** If the policy continues to be effective, the industry is expected to shift from a "zero-sum game" to a "positive-sum game", which is good for all participants, but the biggest benefit is for the leader Zhongtong. On the contrary, if the policy fails, the industry will return to the old path of disorderly competition.

**Core judgment:** Investing in ZTO is essentially betting on "the improvement of the competitive landscape of China's express delivery industry." The company itself is doing well, but its fate depends largely on the industry environment. This is a case where "bottom-up stock selection is not enough and top-down industry judgment is needed."
