# II. Comprehensive risk factors assessment

##2.1 Core risk: price war restart

** Risk description:** The structural conditions of the price war in the express delivery sector have not been eliminated (product homogeneity, low conversion costs, excess capacity). The policy against inward volume is limited in its binding effect and the price war may re-emerge once the industry has further declined or a firm has broken its promise.

**Quantified impact:** If single-ticket prices fall by 5% and costs cannot decline in synchronous time, net profits from China may fall by 15-20%, corresponding to a rise from 13 to 15-16 times for PE. If the intensity of the 2020-2021 return, profits may fall by more than 30%.

** probability of occurrence:** medium (approximately 30-40% probability over the next two years)

** Mitigation:**
- The National Post Office's policy of "reversed" is clear.
- The major players in the industry are experiencing "breathing money and fatigue."
- After the first annual profit, the willingness to keep burning money may decline.

# 4.2 Does it deserve to be held for long?

It depends on your answer to two questions:

** Question I: Will the competition patterns in the Chinese courier industry really improve?**

If you believe that anti-intra-brief policies, industry self-regulation and natural liquidations will significantly improve the industry landscape over the next three to five years (CR3 up to 60 per cent +), the main beneficiary will be China-China, which will be the most attractive current valuation.

If you think that the six or seven co-existing, low-profit competition, is a long-term norm, then it would be more appropriate to serve as a warning for the case of the "value trap" than as a subject of long-term investment.

** Question II: Is the cost advantage of middle-level convergence sustainable?**

If it can, even if the industry pattern remains unchanged, China-China can earn reasonable profits on a cost-consistency basis, adding up to the buy-back write-off, and each share is steadily increasing.

If not (accompanied by competitors, reduced scale effects), the excess profits of the middle-class will be wiped out and returned to the average industry.

# V. Overall assessment

## Management score: <unk> (4/5)

The culture of "sharing with one another," low-debt financial discipline, and strategic certainty for profit preservation are all positive qualities. The sub-elements are that the succession layout is not yet complete, and that individual capabilities are difficult to change the structural constraints of the industry.

# # Risk rating: <unk> (3/5, medium risk)

The core risks are concentrated in two dimensions: price wars and market shares. These two risks are not fully controlled at the firm level, and depend more on the industry environment and the behaviour of competitors.

# # Security margin rating: <unk> (3/5)

Thirteen times the PE provides a certain margin of valuation buffer, but it is not "false price". For a risk exposure where profits may fall by 20-30%, 20% of the discount is not safe. Ideally, the purchase zone may be 10-11 times the PE, or wait for the industry to become more clear.

** Core judgement:** The management of the express delivery is respectable, but the "fate" of the business is largely out of management's hands. The price war is a central risk that will determine whether it is "undervalued shares" or "reasonable price-setting mediocre companies". The current valuation is in an awkward zone of "not expensive but not cheap" and requires industry catalysts to break the deadlock.
