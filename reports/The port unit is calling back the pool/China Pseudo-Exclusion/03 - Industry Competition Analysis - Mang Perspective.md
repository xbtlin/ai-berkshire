# Sinopec (0386.HK) industry competition analysis

## 1. Industry ecology and competitive landscape

### Essential characteristics of the industry
The petroleum refining and chemical industry is a typical **bulk commodity processing industry** with the following core characteristics:
1. **Raw material cost ratio is extremely high**: The cost of crude oil accounts for 70-80% of the cost of refining and chemical companies
2. **Serious product homogeneity**: gasoline, diesel, and chemical products have unified specifications, and there is no brand premium.
3. **Extremely capital intensive**: Building a modern refinery requires tens of billions of investments
4. **High policy sensitivity**: Refined oil pricing, environmental standards, and carbon emissions are all strictly controlled by policies.
5. **Strongly cyclical**: Fluctuate violently with oil prices and demand

Use Munger's "reverse thinking" method to look at it: What kind of companies are most difficult to succeed in this industry? ** The answer is small refineries that lack scale, are far away from the market, and have backward technology. Sinopec happens to have advantages in these dimensions.

### Domestic competition landscape
China's refining and chemical industry adopts a "three barrels of oil + private" pattern:

| Enterprise | Refining Capacity | Core Strengths | Core Weaknesses |
|------|----------|----------|----------|
| Sinopec | ~300 million tons/year | Refining + terminal integration | Insufficient upstream resources |
| PetroChina | ~200 million tons/year | Abundant upstream resources | Low refining efficiency |
| CNOOC | ~50 million tons/year | Low cost of offshore oil and gas | Little downstream layout |
| Private refineries (Rongsheng, etc.) | ~150 million tons/year | Flexible and efficient | Weak scale and channels |

Sinopec still dominates the downstream (refining + sales) field, with a market share of approximately 30-35%.

## 2. Multi-dimensional competitiveness assessment

### 1. Cost structure analysis
**Rating: ★★★☆☆ (3/5)**

Sinopec's cost structure disadvantage lies in its low self-sufficiency rate in upstream oil and gas resources (about 20-30%) and its heavy reliance on imported crude oil. This means that when oil prices rise, the pressure on the cost side is greater than that of PetroChina; when oil prices fall, the risk of inventory price decline is high.

The cost advantage lies in: low unit processing costs brought by scale, high device utilization, and optimized logistics network.

### 2. Terminal network value
**Rating: ★★★★☆ (4/5)**

30,000+ gas stations are Sinopec’s core assets. This network has:
- First mover advantage due to geographical location (good location is already occupied)
- Reach tens of millions of car owners at high frequency every day
- Can carry non-oil businesses (convenience stores, charging, advertising, etc.)
- Almost impossible for latecomers to copy

But the risk is: the increase in the penetration rate of electric vehicles will lead to a decline in gas station passenger flow, and the economic model of charging stations is very different from that of gas stations.

### 3. Chemical business competitiveness
**Rating: ★★★☆☆ (3/5)**

Sinopec leads the world in production capacity of basic chemicals (ethylene, propylene, synthetic resins, etc.), but it faces:
- Fierce competition from large private refineries such as Hengli and Rongsheng
- Impact of imports of low-cost chemicals from the Middle East
- The proportion of high-end chemicals (special materials) is still low

## 3. Industry trends and structural changes

### Three major structural trends

**1. Demand for refined oil products peaks**
China's refined oil consumption has entered a plateau period. The penetration rate of new energy vehicles will exceed 50% in 2025, and gasoline demand is expected to drop by 20-30% from the peak in 2030. This poses a long-term threat to Sinopec's core revenue source.

**2. Transformation from refining to chemical industry**
The global trend is "reducing oil and increasing chemical products" - reducing the proportion of refined oil output and increasing the output of chemical products. Sinopec is promoting this transformation, but it requires large capital expenditures and a long payback period.

**3. Carbon Neutral Pressure**
The refining and chemical industry is a large carbon emitter. In the future, carbon tax/carbon trading costs will gradually rise, increasing operating costs.

### Key issues of Munger style

**"If I were a competitor, how could I beat Sinopec?"**
- In the chemical industry: Large private refineries (such as Hengli) are already encroaching on share through more flexible mechanisms and higher operating efficiency.
- In the terminal field: Tesla supercharging network and NIO battery swap station are establishing new energy replenishment networks
- At the overall level: cannot be defeated in the short term because scale and network barriers are too high, but incremental markets can be cut through differentiation strategies

## 4. Global benchmarking analysis

| Indicators | Sinopec | ExxonMobil | Shell |
|------|----------|------------|-------|
| ROE | 6-7% | 15-20% | 12-15% |
| Profit margin | ~11% | ~15% | ~10% |
| Upstream share | ~20% | ~60% | ~50% |
| Terminal network | 30,000+ sites | 20,000+ sites | 46,000+ sites |

The core gap between Sinopec and international giants is that insufficient upstream resources lead to low ROE. The high ROE of international oil giants mainly comes from the contribution of low-cost upstream assets.

## 5. Core Conclusions

**Industry competitiveness rating: ★★★☆☆ (3/5)**

Sinopec is a "not bad" company in a "not so good" industry. Its moat (terminal network, scale, licenses) is enough to ensure survival and continued profitability, but the structural changes in the industry itself (peaking of demand for refined oil, substitution of new energy sources) mean that this is a "defensive war" rather than an "offensive war."

From Munger's perspective of "fishing in a pond with plenty of fish," the oil refining industry may not be the best "pond" at the moment. But if you have to choose among this pond, Sinopec’s terminal network advantages make it relatively equipped with the basic conditions for transformation.
