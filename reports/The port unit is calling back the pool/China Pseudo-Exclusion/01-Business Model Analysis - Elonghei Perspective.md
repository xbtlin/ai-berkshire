# Sinopec (0386.HK) business model analysis

## 1. The essence of business

Sinopec is a "processing" company, not a "brand" company. Its core business is to purchase crude oil and convert it into refined oil and chemical products for sale through refining and processing. The key problem with this business model is: **The price of raw materials is not determined by oneself, and the price of products is not entirely determined by oneself**.

From the perspective of Duan Yongping's "doing the right thing" framework, Sinopec faces a fundamental contradiction: it is a central enterprise that is too big to fail and is responsible for the national energy security mission, but this also means that it cannot pursue profit maximization like a free market enterprise. When oil prices are high, they may be required to maintain supply and stabilize prices, but when oil prices are low, they may face inventory declines.

## 2. Moat assessment

**Rating: ★★★☆☆ (3/5)**

### Existing moat:
1. **Scale Moat**: Sinopec has the world’s largest refining capacity (approximately 300 million tons/year), and scale effects bring unit cost advantages.
2. **Network Moat**: There are more than 30,000 gas stations across the country, forming a terminal network that is difficult to replicate. This is the most valuable asset
3. **License Moat**: Strict control over crude oil import and refined oil wholesale and retail licenses
4. **Integrated Advantages**: The entire industry chain layout of upstream exploration-midstream refining-downstream sales

### Moat defects:
1. The product is undifferentiated - gasoline is gasoline, and consumers will not pay a premium for "Sinopec brand gasoline"
2. No pricing power - subject to the refined oil pricing mechanism of the National Development and Reform Commission
3. Limited technical barriers - refining and chemical technology is relatively mature and not a unique technology

## 3. User value and user experience

Sinopec's "users" are gas station owners and downstream companies of chemical products.

From a user perspective:
- The choice of gas station mainly depends on location and price, and brand loyalty is extremely low
- "Easy Convenience Store" is an attempt to differentiate, but its current contribution to user stickiness is limited
- Chemical product customers value price and supply stability

Duan Yongping emphasized "differentiation" and "user mentality". In these two dimensions, Sinopec's scores are not high. Consumers go to Sinopec gas stations not because they "like" Sinopec, but because it is right on the roadside.

## 4. Whether management is "doing the right thing"

### Worthy of recognition:
1. High dividend policy (81% dividend payout rate) is shareholder-friendly
2. Actively deploy charging piles and hydrogen filling stations, and use the gas station network to transform
3. Cost control has improved, with operating costs reduced to 223.6 billion yuan

### If you have any doubts:
1. Maintaining large-scale capital expenditures during the oil price downturn
2. The return on investment in new energy transformation is still unclear
3. As a central enterprise, the decision-making chain is long and the speed of responding to market changes is questionable.

## 5. Long-term competitiveness outlook

Sinopec faces a structural problem: **The penetration rate of new energy vehicles continues to rise, and the demand for refined oil is close to its peak**. In 2025, the penetration rate of new energy vehicles in China will exceed 50%, and the demand for refined oil will enter a downward trend in the next 5-10 years.

This means that Sinopec's most valuable asset - its gas station network - is facing a revaluation. If it is transformed into an "integrated energy supply station" (refueling + charging + hydrogenation), it will still have vitality; if the transformation fails, it will become a depreciating asset.

## 6. Is it a good business?

**Rating: ★★☆☆☆ (2/5)**

Judging from Duan Yongping's "good business" standards:
- **Not an asset-light operation**: requires a large amount of fixed asset investment
- **Does not have pricing power**: Subject to dual constraints of oil prices and policies
- **No differentiation**: serious product homogeneity
- **Highly dependent on cycles**: Earnings fluctuate wildly with oil and chemical prices

It is a "profitable but not good enough" business. It has certain appeal as a high-dividend target, but from the perspective of the company's intrinsic value growth, it lacks exciting long-term growth logic.

## 7. Core Conclusions

Sinopec is more like a "bond stock" than a "growth stock." The logic for investors to buy should be: buy stable cash flow and high dividends at a reasonable price, rather than expecting a substantial increase in corporate value. The current payout ratio of 81% means that the company itself does not believe that retained earnings can earn a higher rate of return than shareholders' own investment.
