## One sentence conclusion

Sinopec is a "quasi-fixed income" Hong Kong stock - with protection on the downside, ceiling on the upside, and returns through dividends - but the current buying price of 15-17 times PE is not cheap enough and lacks a sufficient margin of safety.

## Investment thesis

### Look at the long logic
1. A dividend payout ratio of 81% corresponds to a dividend yield of approximately 5%, which is attractive in a low interest rate environment
2. The potential of transforming gas station networks into integrated energy stations
3. The identity of a central enterprise provides credit endorsement and downside protection
4. The chemical business has room for growth amid the trend of domestic substitution.

### Bearish logic
1. The demand for refined oil products has peaked, and the core business is facing structural shrinkage.
2. ROE is only 6-7%, and return on capital is lower than the social average.
3. Profit fluctuates violently with oil prices, with profits falling by 37% in 2025
4. Valuation is not cheap - PE 15-17 times is too high for low ROE cyclical stocks
5. The replacement of new energy vehicles is accelerating, and gas station assets may depreciate.

## Buying conditions (if configuration is considered)

1. **PE drops to 10-12 times** (corresponding to a dividend rate of 7%+), providing a sufficient margin of safety
2. **Oil prices have bottomed out and the signal is clear**, and profits are expected to rebound.
3. **Substantial breakthroughs in new energy transformation** (such as non-oil revenue from gas stations accounting for more than 20%)

## Position suggestions

- **Current Price**: It is not recommended to create a new position
- **Reasonable buying price**: Hong Kong stock price range of 3.0-3.5 yuan (corresponding to PE 10-12 times)
- **Position limit**: Even if you buy, it is recommended not to exceed 3-5% of the portfolio (as a high dividend allocation)

## Keep track of key points

2. International oil price trends and the company’s refining profit margin
3. Progress of non-oil business and new energy business of gas stations
4. Changes in dividend policy
