** Specific performance**:
- 82% of the piped area (11.6 million square metres, 14.32 million square metres) is from Chinese luxuries
- Over 60% of the property management area comes from the Hua Yui system

1. If development in China ' s lunar lands slows down (indicators are shown: core net profits declined by 11.4 per cent in 2025), supply for new projects will decrease
2. Is the associated transaction price fair? Is the company undertaking related party projects at a lower market level? or is there a channel of interest?
3. Lack of independence means that when a parent company is in trouble, it is difficult for a subsidiary to be unique.

** Deductive factors**:
- The third-party project ratio has been raised to 27.4 per cent (37) and is expanding at an accelerated pace
- Relative regulation of corporate governance, with stricter audit oversight over private enterprises
- Hua Yunjing is still one of the most robust housing enterprises in China.

**Assessment**: This is a "chronic disease" - not fatal in the short term, but if it is not continuously improved (upgrade third party ratio) it will limit company value in the long term. The current direction of third party outreach is right, but 82% of related parties still account for too high a ratio.

#3.2 Governance risk

** A unique share**: 72.3% of the shareholdings means that small shareholders have no voice in major decisions.

** Possible conflict of interest scenario**:
- Hua Yuenji has entrusted Vientiane Life Management Project with lower than market fees (benefits transferred from parent to subjects other than subsidiaries)
- Hua Yuenji requires Vientiane to live on uneconomical projects (service to parent companies rather than to shareholders in listed companies)

** The two sides of governance:
- Positive: regulatory systems of State capital, auditing mechanisms, compliance requirements are usually stricter than those of private enterprises
- Negative: corporate governance tends to be conservative and may miss opportunities for radical expansion; personnel arrangements are subject to organizational rather than business needs

# Assessment: governance structure #

The background provides the basic governance bottom line, but a single niche and the dependence of the related parties is a structural governance deficit. Small shareholders need to accept the position of "chop-by" – your interests depend on the goodwill of the controlling shareholders.

# IV. Security margin assessment

# 4.1 Downline Protection

** Balance sheet protection**:
- 15.8 billion cash in hand + 5.5 billion fixed-term deposits = 20.9 billion liquid assets
- Almost zero interest on liabilities
- Cash is enough to keep it running for years, even if it's completely stagnant.

** Cash flow protection**:
- Property management contracts usually of 3-5 years, providing predictability of income
- The mall lease usually lasts one to three years, and the 97% rent means that the cliff will not fall in the short term.
- 100% rate means that the shareholders have a cash return every year.

** Valuation protection**:
- 4.3% of the dividends are in the middle of the shares.
- Even if the PE drops from 24 to 18 (-25%), the rate of dividends will rise to 5.7%, forming a certain valuation support

# 4.2 Is there enough security margins?

To be frank, the current valuation of HK$40/24 times PE** is insufficient for security margins**.

The "pessimistic valuation" is about HK$30, calculated in conservative assumptions (a profit rate down to 8 per cent, and a PE contraction to 18 times), about 25 per cent of the current downside space. This is not a safe margin for "sleeping safely".

The ideal point of entry may be 30-33 Hong Kong Dollar Zone (equivalent to 18-20 times PE), at which point the dividends will be 5-6 per cent, with satisfactory returns even if the acceleration slows down.
