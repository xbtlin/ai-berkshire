# China Unicom (0762.HK) industry competition analysis

## 1. Competitive position: Forever the "third child"?

### The cruel reality of scale disparity

In Munger's way of "thinking mathematically":

China Mobile's mobile users (990 million) are **2.9 times** that of China Unicom (340 million). In an industry like network operators, where fixed costs are high and marginal costs are low, the scale gap means:
- For the same base station, the number of mobile service users is three times that of China Unicom
- With the same R&D investment, China Mobile’s diluted cost per user is 1/3 of China Unicom’s
- The same brand advertising expenditure, mobile ROI is higher

This mathematical reality determines: **In the absence of disruptive variables, it will be difficult for China Unicom to narrow the gap with China Mobile. **

### Comparison of competition in various fields

| Field | First place | Second place | China Unicom ranking | Gap |
|------|--------|--------|----------|------|
| Mobile Subscribers | Mobile (990 million) | Telecom (410 million) | Third (340 million) | Significant |
| Fixed Broadband | Mobile (300 million) | Telecom (190 million) | Third (110 million) | Huge |
| Cloud Computing | Telecommunications (Tianyi Cloud) | Mobile | Third | Obvious |
| Internet of Things | China Unicom (700 million connections) | Mobile | **No. 1** | Leading |
| Internet of Vehicles | China Unicom (65% share) | - | **No. 1** | Absolutely leading |

Core findings: China Unicom’s only leading areas are **Internet of Things and Internet of Vehicles**.

## 2. Internet of Things/Internet of Vehicles: China Unicom’s "Normandy"

### Why is China Unicom leading in this field?

1. **First-mover advantage**: China Unicom was early in deploying M2M (machine-to-machine) connections in the 2G/3G era
2. **In-depth binding with car manufacturers**: especially cooperation with new energy car companies such as BYD and Weilai
3. **Technical Standard Advantages**: Earlier network deployment in the field of NB-IoT (narrowband Internet of Things)
4. **Organizational flexibility**: The openness to cooperation brought about by the mixed-ownership reform makes it easier to connect with the industry.

### The business value of the Internet of Things

Question: 700 million IoT connections = how much value?

Frankly speaking, the current ARPU of IoT connections is extremely low (1-3 yuan per connection per month), and the total revenue contribution is limited. But the future value lies in:
- Internet of Vehicles moves from connection to service (OTA, data services, insurance)
- Industrial Internet of Things moves toward high-value private network services
- The number of connections is the "entrance", and subsequent value-added services are the profits.

## 3. Key issues of Munger format

### "What is the significance of China Unicom's existence?"
A sharp question: If China Unicom did not exist, would users suffer?

The answer is: yes. Competition between three companies is more beneficial to consumers than competition between two companies. But this "existence value" is arranged by policy, not chosen by the market. From a purely commercial perspective, if it can be merged, the efficiency of the merger of China Unicom + Telecom will be higher than that of operating independently.

### "Is the mixed reform a success or a failure?"
Since the mixed mixed reform in 2017 (nearly 9 years), the evaluation:
- User growth: improved (Tencent King Card and others have brought a large number of new users)
- Profitability: limited improvement (ROE increased from 4% to 5.7%, slow progress)
- Institutional change: limited reform (core management is still appointed by state-owned assets)
- Conclusion: **Partially successful, but far from meeting market expectations**

### "Can 5G private network + IoT become the 'second curve'?"
5G private network revenue of 12.3 billion yuan (+51%) seems to be growing at a promising rate, but:
- Small base, accounting for only 3% of total revenue
- Even doubling is not enough to change the overall profit outlook
- Requires continuous high investment and limited short-term profit contribution

## 4. Impact of future industry trends on China Unicom

### Favorable trends:
1. **Internet of Everything Era**: The number of IoT connections will move from tens of billions to hundreds of billions, and China Unicom’s first-mover advantage will be amplified
2. **Smart car penetration**: The penetration rate of new energy vehicles is increasing, and the demand for Internet of Vehicles connections continues to grow.
3. **Explosive demand for computing power**: If China Unicom can establish computing power advantages in specific vertical industries, it may be differentiated

### Unfavorable trends:
1. **Intensified scale effect**: 5G/6G investment is increasing, and the smallest operators are under the greatest pressure
2. **Price war continues**: The price competition among the three companies on the C-side will not stop.
3. **AI’s requirements for centralization**: Large model training tends to use large-scale centralized computing power, and China Unicom’s distributed advantages may not be compatible.

## 5. Core Conclusions

**Industry competitiveness score: ★★☆☆☆ (2.5/5)**

China Unicom is a clear "weak party" in the communications industry. The leading position of the Internet of Things/Internet of Vehicles is its only differentiating advantage, but the current revenue and profit contribution of these businesses are too small to change the overall outlook.

Munger's summary: **To be the smallest in an industry where scale is king, you need some kind of "asymmetric advantage" to make up for it. China Unicom's IoT layout has this potential, but there is still a long way to go from potential to realization. ** The current investment in China Unicom is essentially a bet that the Internet of Things era can reshape the competitive landscape. The odds of this bet are not attractive enough.
