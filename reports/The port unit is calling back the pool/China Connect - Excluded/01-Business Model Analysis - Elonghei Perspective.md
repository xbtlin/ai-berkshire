# China Unicom (0762.HK) business model analysis

## 1. The essence of business

China Unicom, China Telecom and China Mobile are in the same business - basic communication services. However, China Unicom is in the most embarrassing situation among the three: it has the smallest scale, the fewest resources, and the fewest users**. In an industry with significant economies of scale, the "smallest" are naturally at a disadvantage.

From Duan Yongping's perspective, the core question is: What does China Unicom have that the other two companies don't? The answer is less obvious. This is its biggest problem.

## 2. Moat assessment

**Rating: ★★★☆☆ (2.5/5)**

### Existing moat:
1. **License Barrier**: Sharing oligopoly status with Telecom and China Mobile
2. **User Base**: 340 million mobile users + 110 million broadband users are still huge assets
3. **Mixed-ownership reform cooperation**: Equity partnership with Tencent, Alibaba, and Baidu
4. **First launch of the Internet of Things**: The number of Internet of Things connections exceeds 700 million, and the share of the Internet of Vehicles is 65%

### Moat defects:
1. Mobile network coverage and quality are not as good as China Mobile, and the southern market is weak
2. Fixed-line broadband is not as good as telecommunications and lacks the historical accumulation of "backbone network"
3. The smallest user scale leads to the highest unit cost
4. Considered the "third choice" in terms of brand recognition

## 3. Differentiation strategy

China Unicom attempts to establish differentiation through the following methods:

### 1. Mixed ownership reform bonus
In 2017, strategic investors such as Tencent, Alibaba, and Baidu were introduced. Actual effect:
- Launched Internet packages such as "Tencent King Card" and "Alibaba Card" to quickly acquire customers
- But these users have low ARPU values and average quality
- Limited changes in management mechanisms brought about by mixed-ownership reform

### 2. Internet of Things/Internet of Vehicles
- With 700 million connections, IoT is a real highlight
- The share of new energy vehicle networking exceeds 65%
- Over 95 million Internet of Vehicles connections
- This is the area where China Unicom is most differentiated compared to China Telecom and China Mobile.

### 3. 5G private network
- 5G private network revenue in 2025 is 12.3 billion yuan (+51%)
- More than 9,000 5G factories
- More than 23,000 virtual private network customers
- Fast growth but still small scale

## 4. User value analysis

From the perspective of "user experience" that Duan Yongping focuses on:
- C-end users: China Unicom’s network experience is generally weaker than that of China Mobile (coverage) and China Telecom (stability). They choose China Unicom mostly because of its cheap price.
- B-side users: In the Internet of Things and Internet of Vehicles scenarios, China Unicom’s connection solution has certain technical advantages
- Government and enterprise users: weaker than telecommunications, but have accumulated experience in specific industries (such as automobiles, manufacturing)

Key issue: China Unicom’s users have no reason to “have to choose” and most of them can be replaced.

## 5. Whether management is "doing the right thing"

### Correct direction:
1. Focus on the four major tracks of "connection, computing power, service, and security"
2. Vigorously develop the Internet of Things/Internet of Vehicles (use your own advantages)
3. Obtain a satellite mobile communications license
4. Capital expenditure will be reduced to 50 billion yuan in 2026, and the proportion of computing power will increase to 35%+

### Doubts exist:
1. Revenue growth is only 0.7%, and core indicators have almost stagnated.
2. Can it break through despite the disadvantage of user scale?
3. Can the rate of return on investment in computing power be realized?
4. After years of mixed-ownership reform, the actual effect of institutional innovation has been limited.

## 6. Is it a good business?

**Rating: ★★☆☆☆ (2.5/5)**

China Unicom faces a fundamental dilemma: being the "third player" in an industry with significant economies of scale. This means:
- Higher unit costs than competitors
- Trade-offs must be made when resources are limited (cannot catch up in all areas)
- Brand recognition is weak

But it's not a "bad business" - licensing barriers ensure it doesn't become obsolete. The problem is: it's hard to be great without being obsolete.

Duan Yongping may ask: "Why not buy the number one company in the industry (China Mobile)?" This is a difficult question to answer.

## 7. Core Conclusions

China Unicom is the one with the "least moat depth" among the three major operators. It has unique advantages in the Internet of Things/Internet of Vehicles field, but the quality of its overall business model is weaker than China Mobile (the largest) and China Telecom (leading the cloud business). Unless there is an extremely cheap price (less than 10 times PE), from the perspective of "doing the right thing", priority should be given to industry leaders in the same industry.
