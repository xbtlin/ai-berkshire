#Tencent Music (1698.HK) Industry and Competition Landscape Analysis

> Analysis dimensions: industry structure, competitive dynamics, technological changes, multidisciplinary perspectives
> Core question: What is the final outcome of competition in this industry? Can Tencent Music win?

---

## 1. Industry structure: from "one super and many strong" to "three pillars"

China's online music market is undergoing a profound reshaping of its landscape.

### Current competitive landscape

| Platform | Monthly active users (MAU) | Paying users | Core strategy | Capital behind |
|------|-----------|---------|---------|---------|
| Tencent Music (QQ Music + Kugou + Kuwo) | ~530 million (declining) | 127 million | Subscription + Copyright + Ecosystem | Tencent |
| NetEase Cloud Music | ~200 million | ~45 million (estimated) | Community + independent music | NetEase |
| Soda music | ~140 million (rapid growth) | Free model | Free + algorithm recommendation | ByteDance |

**Tencent Music is still the absolute leader**, but its advantage is being eroded. The most alarming changes are:

1. **The growth rate of soda music is far faster than expected**: MAU in November 2024 was 70.48 million, and in January 2026 it was nearly 140 million, doubling in one year. Daily activity even exceeds NetEase Cloud Music
2. **Tencent Music’s MAU has declined for 16 consecutive quarters**: from a peak of about 650 million to 530 million, and the losses are mainly young users and price-sensitive users
3. **The incremental market is almost monopolized by soda music**: In December 2025, soda music had 20.75 million new installed users in a single month, ranking first in the industry

### Three possibilities for the industry’s ending

**Scenario 1: Tencent Music maintains dominance (30% probability)**
It is difficult for soda music to achieve profitability. ByteDance eventually reduced its investment, and the free model is unsustainable. Tencent Music re-accelerates growth with price hikes and Ximalaya integration.

**Scenario 2: Long-term coexistence of the three pillars (50% probability)**
Tencent Music maintains its paying user pool, soda music occupies the free market, and NetEase Cloud Music maintains its niche community positioning. Industry profits are concentrated in Tencent Music, but the room for growth is limited.

**Scenario 3: Soda music subverts the pattern (20% probability)**
The free model continues to expand, and the paid model is fundamentally impacted. Tencent Music is forced to cut prices or launch a free version, and its profit margins are significantly compressed.

## 2. In-depth analysis of competitors

### Soda Music: The Biggest Threat

Soda music is not an ordinary competitor, it represents a completely different business logic.

**Core competitiveness:**
- **Douyin traffic portal**: 82.1% of users come from Douyin diversion, with an average of 500 million recommended exposures per day, and the customer acquisition cost is almost zero
- **Free Mode**: Users can listen to music for free all day long after watching an 80-second advertisement, directly impacting the 18 yuan/month subscription model
- **Algorithm Recommendation**: Replicate Douyin's "interest recommendation" logic, which is more in line with the habits of Generation Z than the traditional search + playlist model
- **Short video-music closed loop**: 86% of the TOP1000 hot songs originate from Douyin, forming a complete link of "short video song creation-soda listening to songs"

**Key Weaknesses:**
- The profit model is unclear, and it is difficult to rely on advertising to cover copyright costs in the long term.
- Users mainly consume passively, with insufficient active search and in-depth usage scenarios.
- Copyright procurement costs will rise with the growth of users, and the sustainability of the free model is questionable
- ByteDance may reduce investment due to strategic focus shift

### NetEase Cloud Music: Stable second echelon

NetEase Cloud Music has maintained its base of about 200 million MAU by relying on its community culture and independent music positioning, but its growth has basically stagnated. Its threat to Tencent Music is limited, and it is more of a competition among specific user groups (artistic youth, independent music lovers).

### Spotify: a global benchmark but not a direct competitor

Spotify does not operate in China, but it provides a valuation anchor. Spotify’s 290 million paid users, revenue of 17.1 billion euros, 40-50 times PE – the huge gap with Tencent Music’s 10 times PE reflects both the growth difference and the discount of Chinese concept stocks.

## 3. Technological changes: The impact of AI on the music industry

This is an issue that must be examined from a multidisciplinary perspective.

### AI Music Generation: Supply-Side Revolution

2026 is known as "the first year of strong supervision of AI music", but the output of AI music is already astonishing - **7 million songs per day**. This means:

1. **Unlimited supply of content**: The pricing power of traditional record companies' "scarce content" is weakened
2. **Devaluation of Copyright Barriers**: When AI can generate alternatives with similar styles, the value of exclusive copyrights will decrease.
3. **Platform role change**: From "content distribution" to "content screening", algorithm recommendation capabilities become more important

### Specific impact on Tencent Music

**Positives:**
- Tencent Music has launched AI music products "VEMUS" and "Venus AI Song Composition" tool, charging a mastering fee of 29.9 yuan for AI works
- As the largest platform, it has the ability to formulate distribution rules for AI music
- AI can reduce content procurement costs

**Negatives:**
- AI-generated music may impact the income of human creators, thereby affecting the platform’s copyright negotiation relationship
- If AI music replaces human works on a large scale, the willingness to pay for subscriptions may decrease (the willingness to pay for AI works will be even lower)
- Regulatory policies are still unclear and there are compliance risks

### Multidisciplinary Perspective: Why This Time Is Different

Look at it from an ecological perspective: the music industry is experiencing a "species invasion." AI music is like an invasive species. Its reproduction cost is extremely low (the generation speed is much faster than human creation), but its "nutritional value" (emotional depth, cultural value) may be insufficient. In the short term, it will cause a "deflationary effect" - the unit value of music will decrease. In the long run, the ones that will survive are those species that have unique value—that is, high-quality music created by humans.

Looking at it from an economic perspective: AI music essentially changes music from a "scarce good" to an "abundant good". Economics tells us that when supply approaches infinite, the value of a commodity approaches zero. But music consumption is not purely "commodity consumption", it has social attributes ("What am I listening to" represents identity) and emotional attributes. These added values ​​will not disappear with the addition of AI music.

## 4. Acquisition of Himalaya: Expanding the boundaries of competition

On May 12, 2026, Tencent Music was approved to acquire Himalaya for US$2.9 billion (approximately 20 billion yuan). This is a strategic decision that deserves in-depth analysis.

### Transaction logic

| Dimensions | Analysis |
|------|------|
| Strategic value | Expanded from "music" to "audio", covering podcasts, audiobooks, and paid knowledge |
| User complementation | Ximalaya’s user profile is different from Tencent Music’s, and there is room for cross-selling |
| Content barriers | Himalaya has 488 million pieces of audio content, which has advantages in the car audio market |
| Competitive Defense | Preventing ByteDance or other competitors from acquiring Himalaya |

### Risk

1. **Strict additional conditions for supervision**: no price increase, no exclusivity, no restrictions on multiple platforms for anchors - these conditions limit the release of synergy effects
2. **Difficult to integrate**: Music and audio are different content consumption scenarios, and user habits are significantly different.
3. **Himalaya itself is in decline**: The audio track as a whole is "at a trough", and the growth of the podcast market is less than expected.
4. **The consideration of 20 billion yuan is not cheap**: It is equivalent to two years of net profit of Tencent Music. If the integration fails, this is a huge sunk cost.

## 6. Key Competition Conclusions

1. **Soda music is a real threat, not a false alarm**. The combination of Douyin’s traffic portal, free business model, and algorithm-recommended product form poses a structural challenge to traditional paid music platforms. It’s no accident that Tencent Music’s MAU has declined for 16 consecutive quarters.

2. **The long-term impact of AI music is underestimated**. The daily production of 7 million AI songs means that music content is moving from scarcity to excess. This is a fundamental impact on business models that rely on copyright barriers.

3. **The acquisition of Himalaya is an "offensive and defensive" move**, but successful integration is not inevitable. Five regulatory constraints significantly weaken the collaborative space.

4. **The end of the industry may be the coexistence of "paid + free" dual tracks**. Just like Netflix (paid) and YouTube (free) in the video field, a similar pattern may form in the music field. Tencent Music's challenge is how to prove the value of a subscription when free alternatives exist.
