# Tencent Music (1698.HK) Business Model Analysis

> Analysis dimensions: essence of business model, user value, moat depth
> Core question: What is the nature of this business? Why can’t users live without it? Can it continue to make money?

---

## 1. The essence of business

The essence of Tencent Music's business is to be China's largest music content distribution platform, connecting copyright owners (record companies, independent musicians) and consumers (listeners), and monetizing it through subscription memberships, advertising, digital album sales, etc.

There are several core features of this business worth noting:

**1. From "copyright hoarding" to "platform ecology"**

In the early days, Tencent Music’s competitive strategy was to sign a large number of exclusive copyrights and use copyright barriers to lock in users. After the antitrust supervision in 2021, the exclusive copyright model was broken and the company was forced to transform. Judging from the results, the transformation is successful - after losing the exclusive copyright, Tencent Music did not collapse, but its revenue increased by 15.8% to 32.9 billion yuan in 2025, indicating that the source of user stickiness has shifted from "you have songs that I don't have" to "platform experience and habits".

**2. 2WD: Subscription + Non-Subscription**

In 2025, online music subscription revenue will be 17.66 billion yuan (+16% year-on-year), and non-subscription revenue will be 9.07 billion yuan (+39.2% year-on-year). Non-subscription businesses are growing faster, including digital albums, advertising, live broadcast reward sharing, etc. This structure is more flexible than a pure subscription model and can extract value from different user groups.

**3. Success of Super Member Strategy**

By the end of 2025, the number of super members will exceed 20 million, and the average monthly payment will be about 40 yuan, which is five times that of ordinary members (8 yuan/month). This shows that among the core user groups, the company has the ability to increase ARPU through product stratification. However, the overall ARPPU is only 11.9 yuan/month, which is far lower than Spotify’s 4.76 euros (about 37 yuan), indicating that most users’ willingness to pay is still low.

## 2. User value analysis

**User size: large but shrinking**

Tencent Music's three major platforms, QQ Music, Kugou Music, and Kuwo Music, have online music monthly activity of about 530 million (2025Q4), but they have declined year-on-year for 16 consecutive quarters. This is a sign that needs to be taken seriously.

The destination of user losses is clear – ByteDance’s soda music. Soda Music’s monthly active users in January 2026 reached nearly 140 million, a year-on-year increase of over 85%, and its daily active users even exceeded NetEase Cloud Music. Its trump card is "free + algorithm recommendation". Users only need to watch 80 seconds of ads to listen to songs for free all day long, which completely replicates Douyin's "interest recommendation" logic.

**Why do users still stay in Tencent Music? **

- **Completeness of music library**: Although the exclusive copyright has been broken, Tencent Music still has the most complete genuine music library, especially Chinese classics and leading artists
- **Usage Habits**: QQ Music and Kugou are old products for many users for more than ten years. Playlists, collections, and playback records form migration costs.
- **Member Rights Binding**: Joint membership with ecosystems such as Tencent Video, which increases exit costs
- **Social function**: The connection between QQ Music and WeChat/QQ ecosystem (sharing songs, status display, etc.)

**But why are users leaving? **

- The music consumption habits of young users are shifting from "active search" to "passive recommendation", and soda music is more in line with this trend.
- The free model is highly attractive to price-sensitive users
- Short video platforms have become the first channel for discovering new songs (86% of the top 1000 soda music songs are from Douyin)

## 3. Moat assessment

### Copyright Barrier ★★★☆☆ (3/5)

The era of exclusive rights is over. Although Tencent Music still has the largest copyright library, the threshold for competitors to obtain copyrights has been significantly lowered. The development of AI music (7 million songs per day) is further eroding the scarcity of traditional copyrights. The audio copyright (488 million pieces of audio content) brought by the acquisition of Himalaya is an increase, but it belongs to a different track.

### Network Effect ★★★☆☆ (3/5)

The network effect of music platforms is weaker than that of social platforms. The interaction between users (comments, playlist sharing) has a certain network effect, but it is far from being "irreplaceable". National karaoke has certain community attributes, but it is being impacted by new forms such as AI covers.

### Switching Cost ★★★★☆ (4/5)

This is Tencent Music’s current most substantial moat. The playlists, collections, playback records, and personalized recommendation models accumulated over the years make migration costs high for old users. The deep binding with Tencent ecosystem (WeChat, QQ, Tencent Video) further strengthens this stickiness. But for new users, this barrier does not exist.

### Economies of Scale ★★★★☆ (4/5)

Music platforms have typical economies of scale—the cost of copyright procurement is relatively fixed, and the more users there are, the lower the unit cost. Tencent Music’s scale of 127 million paying users gives it stronger bargaining power in copyright negotiations. But this advantage is being eroded by AI music and the free model.

### Brand awareness ★★★★☆ (4/5)

QQ Music and Kugou Music have extremely high brand recognition in China. QQ Music has a strong brand image especially among young people in first- and second-tier cities. But the brand itself does not constitute pricing power - users will not pay more because of the brand.

## 4. The core contradiction of the business model

Duan Yongping's thinking will focus on a key question: **Where is the "difference" of this business? **

Frankly, the differentiation of online music streaming is getting weaker:

1. **Content Homogenization**: After the exclusive copyright is broken, the differences in the music libraries of each platform will be reduced.
2. **Functional homogeneity**: The recommendation algorithm, sound quality, interface and other functions are similar.
3. **Price War Pressure**: Soda Music’s free model and NetEase Cloud’s low-price strategy limit the room for price increases.
4. **AI Impact**: AI-generated music may fundamentally change the content supply structure

However, we should also look at the positive side:

1. **There is still room for price increase**: ARPPU is 11.9 yuan/month, only one-third of Spotify’s. Chinese consumers’ willingness to pay is still on the rise.
2. **Multiple monetization**: Non-subscription business grew at a rate of 39.2%, indicating that the company is exploring monetization paths other than subscriptions.
3. **Acquisition of Himalaya**: Expanding from "music" to "audio", opening up new scenarios such as podcasts and audiobooks
4. **Live Performance**: Tencent Music is developing an offline performance ecosystem and converting online traffic into offline experience

## 5. Business model score

| **Comprehensive Rating** | **★★★☆☆** | **Good business, but the moat is being washed away, we need to pay close attention to changes in the competitive landscape** |

## Key Observations

Do the right thing: Tencent Music’s successful transformation after the exclusive copyright was broken shows that management has the ability to adapt. The acquisition of Himalaya is a reasonable attempt to expand into the "audio ecosystem".

But the question that needs to be faced honestly is: when soda music uses "free + algorithm" to steal incremental users, when AI music weakens the value of copyright barriers, and when ARPPU growth stagnates, how far can Tencent Music's "price increase story" go? It's not an easy question to answer, but it determines the value of the business over the next five years.
