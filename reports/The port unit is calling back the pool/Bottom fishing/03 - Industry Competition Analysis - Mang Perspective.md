# Haidilao (6862.HK) industry competition analysis

> Analysis dimensions: industry structure, competition landscape, multiple thinking models, reverse thinking
> Core question: What is the final outcome of competition in the hot pot industry? Can Haidilao’s leading position be sustained?

---

## 1. Nature of the industry: catering chain

### Use multiple thinking models to understand the hot pot/catering industry

**Biological analogy**: The catering industry is a **"low concentration ecosystem"** - species are extremely diverse, and no single species can dominate the entire ecology. This is in stark contrast to the tech industry (winner takes all). Reason: Consumers have a natural need for variety and cannot eat the same food every day.

**Psychological model (adaptive bias)**: Human beings have a natural tendency to "diversify" food - no matter how good a restaurant is, there is an upper limit on the frequency of consumption. This determines that the market share ceiling of a single brand in catering is very low.

**Economic Model**:
- Catering is a **labor-intensive** industry, and the scale effect has limits (unlike the technology industry where marginal costs tend to zero)
- Low entry barriers, anyone can open a hot pot restaurant
- However, the threshold for **chain management** is high, and maintaining a consistent level of thousands of stores requires extremely strong organizational capabilities.

### Industry scale and structure

- China’s catering market is about 5 trillion yuan
- The hot pot category is about 500-600 billion yuan (accounting for about 12% of catering)
- Industry concentration is extremely low: Haidilao's hot pot market share of about 7-8% is already the absolute leader
- The chain rate is increasing, but it is still far lower than mature markets such as Japan and the United States.

## 2. Competitive landscape: one super, many weak

### Core characteristics of the competitive landscape

1. **Haidilao is the only national leader**: revenue exceeds 40 billion, several times that of the second place
2. **Competitors generally have operating difficulties**: Xiabuxiabu continues to suffer losses, and Jiumaojiu (Tai Er)’s growth slows down
3. **Lots of regional brands**: Every city has local hot pot brands, with low entry barriers.
4. **New Category Diversion**: Barbecue, barbecue, Japanese food and other categories are competing for consumers' "stomach capacity"

### Why can Haidilao become the only super large hotpot chain?

This is a question worth pondering. The answer lies not in "the best hot pot" (hot pot has limited flavor differentiation), but in:

1. **Management System**: Master-apprentice system + profit sharing + transparent promotion channel, solving the management problem of "thousands of people and thousands of faces"
2. **Brand Trust**: In a market where food safety incidents occur frequently, "big brand = peace of mind" is a huge competitive advantage
3. **First-mover scale**: The brand exposure and supply chain advantages brought by 1,383 stores will be difficult for latecomers to catch up.
4. **Service Label**: Make "service" the core label of the brand, occupying a unique position in consumers' minds.

## 3. "Pomegranate Plan" and multi-brand strategy

### Strategic Intent

Haidilao launched the "Red Pomegranate Project" and incubated 14 new brands such as Yanqing Barbecue Shop, Xiaohai Aizha, and SHUA BAKERY, and operated a total of 207 stores under 20 brands.

### Multi-brand strategy analysis

**Advantages:**
- Utilize existing supply chain and management systems to reduce trial and error costs
- Cover categories other than hot pot (barbecue, fast food, baking) and broaden the boundaries of growth
- If a certain brand emerges, it can copy Haidilao’s expansion model

**Risk:**
- Too many brands distract management efforts
- Most brands are too small (207 ÷ 20 brands = an average of 10 brands/brand), making it difficult to form economies of scale.
- The failure rate of new catering brands is extremely high (>70%)
- Different from Anta's multi-brand strategy - Anta acquires mature brands, while Haidilao incubates them from scratch.

### Mang format judgment

"Doing 20 brands at the same time" is an almost impossible task in the catering industry. A more realistic path is to select 2-3 potential key ones from 20, and abandon the rest decisively.

## 4. Franchise system: growth or risk?

### The Economics of Franchising

| Dimension | Self-operated | Franchise |
|------|------|------|
| Capital investment | High (you need to invest your own money to open a store) | Low (invested by franchisees) |
| Quality Control | Strong | Weak (depends on supervision) |
| Profit sharing | 100% owned by the company | Only franchise fee + supply chain profit charged |
| Expansion speed | Slow | Fast |
| Brand risk | Low | High (franchisees make bad brands) |

### Special considerations for Haidilao franchise system

- Received more than 10,000 franchise applications, indicating that the brand has strong appeal
- Currently there are only 79 franchise stores, accounting for 6%, which is in the very early stage
- The key is quality control capabilities - if a franchise store has food safety issues, the entire brand will be damaged.

**Historical Lessons**: Many Chinese catering brands have failed due to franchising - the lessons of brands such as Zhengxin Chicken Steak and Braised Chicken are still vivid in our minds.

## 5. Reverse thinking: Under what circumstances will Haidilao decline?

### Scenario 1: Service is no longer competitive

If consumers are no longer willing to pay a premium for "extreme service" (pursuing cost-effectiveness or novelty), Haidilao's core selling point will be weakened.

**Probability Assessment: Medium Low**. Service is an eternal need in human nature, but the premium may be compressed.

### Scenario 2: Labor costs continue to rise

Haidilao’s service model relies on a large amount of manpower. If labor costs continue to rise by 15-20% per year, profit margins will be significantly compressed.

**Probability Assessment: Moderate**. This is a structural trend that Haidilao needs to hedge through price increases or automation.

### Scenario 3: Food safety incident

A serious food safety incident can cause a major blow to a brand. The larger the scale, the greater the probability of something going wrong.

**Probability assessment: low but not negligible**. This is a "black swan" type of risk.

### Scenario 4: Consumers are "aesthetically tired"

The growth of the hot pot category itself has stagnated, or consumers have switched to other categories.

**Probability Assessment: Medium Low**. Hot pot is one of China’s favorite categories and is unlikely to disappear.

## 6. Industry Final Deduction

### The most likely ending (50% probability)

Haidilao maintains its leading position in the hot pot industry, with a stable market share of 7-10%. The growth rate has slowed to low single digits, but with high ROE and stable dividends, it has become a "utility-like" stock. Similar to McDonald's position in the global fast food market.

### Optimistic ending (probability 20%)

The multi-brand/franchise strategy is successful, and Haidilao has transformed from a "hot pot company" into a "Chinese version of Yum Brands" (a multi-brand catering group), opening up new growth space.

### Mildly pessimistic ending (25% probability)

Growth has completely stagnated, new brands have failed, and franchise quality problems have occurred frequently. The company has become a pure "cash cow", with PE compressed to 12-15 times.

### Extremely pessimistic ending (5% probability)

Major food safety incidents + deterioration of the consumer environment have significantly damaged brand value.

## 7. Munger’s key judgment

### Core question: Are restaurant chains a good business?

Munger's answer might be: **"Restaurant chains are a good but not great business."**

- Good things: high cash flow, replicable model, brand value
- Not great aspects: labor intensive, limited economies of scale, limited consumer loyalty, low entry barriers

**Compare ideal business models**:
- Not as good as software (zero marginal cost)
- Not as good as branded consumer goods (inventory can be stored)
- But better than manufacturing (more asset-light)
- Better than retail (higher gross profit margin)
