# Haidilao (6862.HK) business model analysis

> Analysis dimensions: user value, business essence, differentiated positioning, long-term competitiveness
> Core question: Is this company doing "the right thing"? Do users really need it?

---

## 1. What business is the company doing?

Haidilao's core business is to operate hot pot restaurant chains and replicate hot pot stores across the country and even the world through standardized service experience and supply chain management. Current business structure:

1. **Core Business**: Haidilao brand hot pot restaurant (accounting for 90%+ of revenue)
2. **Takeaway business**: Revenue in 2025 is 2.658 billion yuan (+112%), rapid growth
3. **Multi-brand matrix**: Operates 20 catering brands (barbecue, pasta, fast food, etc.), with a total of 207 stores
4. **Franchise Business**: Franchise will be opened in 2024, and there are already 79 franchise stores.

In essence, Haidilao is in the business of "standardizing service experience and replicating it on a large scale."

## 2. User value analysis: Why do consumers go to Haidilao?

### The core reasons why consumers choose Haidilao

1. **Service experience**: Haidilao’s service is the benchmark in China’s catering industry. From manicures and shoe shines while queuing to attentive service during meals, it creates a dining experience that far exceeds the industry average.

2. **Quality Trust**: Big brands provide food safety guarantees. When consumers go to Haidilao, they won’t worry about the ingredients. This is a huge trust premium in the Chinese market where food safety incidents occur frequently.

3. **Social scene**: Hot pot is naturally suitable for gatherings/socializing, and Haidilao’s big store model can accommodate various combinations of people.

4. **Stable Expectation Management**: The quality and service of any Haidilao restaurant across the country are highly consistent. Consumers know what they are getting - this "certainty" is value in itself.

### The premium users are willing to pay

The unit price of Haidilaoke is about 100-120 yuan, which is higher than most local hotpot brands (60-80 yuan) but lower than high-end restaurants. Consumers pay a premium of about 30-50% for "service + quality + certainty".

### Key Questions

1. **Can the service premium be sustained? ** As Haidilao expands in scale and pressure to turn over increases, will service quality decline?
2. **Do young consumers still pay the bill? ** The new generation of consumers may pursue cost-effectiveness or novelty rather than "extreme service".
3. **What is the ceiling of the hot pot category itself? ** China’s hotpot market is huge but saturated.

## 3. "Right" and "Wrong" business models

### Do the right thing ★★★★☆

**Right:**

1. **Service as core competitiveness**: In an "involved" catering market, Haidilao has established a unique brand recognition through service. Most competitors cannot learn this kind of service-centered management system - because this is not only a training issue, but also an issue of organizational culture and incentive mechanisms.

2. **Supply chain advantages**: Yihai International provides standardized base ingredients, and Shuhai Supply Chain provides food distribution. This "backend standardization" capability makes it possible to serve thousands of stores.

3. **Organizational ability**: Haidilao's original "master-apprentice" management model solves the most difficult problem in the catering industry - people management. Systematic training of store managers enables quality control during rapid expansion.

4. **Brand extension (multi-brand + takeaway)**: Takeaway revenue will double to 2.658 billion in 2025, and multi-brand revenue will increase by 214%. This shows that Haidilao’s capabilities are overflowing into more scenarios.

**Questionable aspects:**

1. **Growth peaked**: Revenue only increased by 1.1%, and the turnover rate returned to 4.0, but there is limited room for further improvement. The single-store model is already very mature, but where does the growth come from?

2. **Double-edged sword of franchising**: Opening up franchising can accelerate expansion, but it may also dilute service quality. What hot pot brands are most afraid of is "the franchise will ruin the brand".

3. **Insufficient verification of multiple brands**: 20 brands and 207 stores look lively, but most brands are extremely small and it is doubtful whether they can independently grow into another "Haidilao".

4. **Labor cost pressure**: Haidilao’s service density means extremely high labor costs. As labor costs rise, margins may come under pressure.

## 4. Differentiated positioning and moat assessment

### Moat Analysis ★★★★☆

| Moat Types | Assessment |
|-----------|------|
| Brand value | ★★★★★ China’s No. 1 catering brand, recognized by the whole people |
| Organizational ability | ★★★★★ Master-apprenticeship + incentive system, extremely difficult to copy |
| Supply Chain | ★★★★☆ Yihai + Shuhai have built an efficient backend |
| Scale effect | ★★★★☆ 1,383 stores bring purchasing and brand advantages |
| Switching costs | ★★☆☆ Consumers can go to other hot pot restaurants at any time |

**Haidilao's biggest moat is not its product (hotpot itself is not difficult to copy), but its organizational capabilities - managing tens of thousands of employees into a team that can provide a consistent high level of service. This is a "systemic capability" that cannot be cracked by a single point of technology. **

### Duan Yongping’s questioning

**"Where will this business be in ten years?"**

- People will still eat hotpot ten years from now ✓
- The Haidilao brand will most likely still exist in ten years ✓
- But can growth continue? Not sure
- Where is the ceiling for table turnover rate and number of stores?

**"Do consumers really need Haidilao?"**

Necessary, but not "must have". Hotpot is an extremely fragmented market—consumers have plenty of alternatives to choose from. Haidilao's advantage is "good certainty" (it won't step on thunder), but if consumers are looking for freshness, cost-effectiveness or other tastes, they can always choose other brands.

**"Is management doing what matters most?"**

Zhang Yong/Gou Yiqun did two important things:
1. Open to franchising - recognize the limit of self-operated expansion speed and use franchising to gain incremental growth
2. Multi-brand exploration – trying to find the second growth curve

But the core issue has not changed: the ceiling of the hot pot track. Whether self-operated or franchised, there is a limit to how many hot pot restaurants the Chinese market can accommodate.

## 5. The ultimate torture of the business model

### "Good business" or "good company"?

**Characteristics of a good business**:
- ✓ High frequency consumption (1-2 times per month)
- ✓ Brand premium (price per customer is higher than the industry)
- ✓ Reproducible models
- ✓ High cash flow (advance collection + fast turnover)
- ✗ Low capital expenditure (opening a new store requires a lot of capital)
- ✗ High growth (peaked)

**Conclusion**: Haidilao is a "good but not perfect" business - high ROE, strong cash flow, and a moat, but its growth space is limited, and opening new stores requires continuous capital investment.

**Overall Business Model Rating: ★★★★☆ (4/5)**

Haidilao has the strongest moat and the highest ROE among the five companies. Its service experience and organizational capabilities do constitute a competitive barrier. The main concern is peaking growth - continuing to grow an already successful business is far more difficult than going from zero to one. But even without growth, this is still a high-quality "cash cow" business.
