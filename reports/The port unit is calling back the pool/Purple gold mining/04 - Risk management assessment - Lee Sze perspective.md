##1.2 Risk I: Large commodity price cycle (lethal level risk)

** This is the number one risk that mining equity investors cannot avoid.**

Each of the mining supercycles in history ended in a tragic way:
- 2008 financial crisis: copper prices fell from $8,000 to $3,000 (-63 per cent) and mineral stock prices generally fell by more than 70 per cent
- 2011-2016: copper prices dropped from $10,000 to $4,300 (-57 per cent) and mandatory and top shares fell by 55 per cent
- 2020 epidemic: copper price dropped briefly to US$ 4,600

Copper is now at a price of about $12,000 per ton, gold of about $5,000 per ounce, and is at an all-time high. No one can predict exactly the point of commodity prices, despite structural factors such as the new energy transition.

** Specific impact on purple gold**: Pressure test in reference financial analysis - if copper prices fall to $8,000/ton, gold prices fall to $3,000/ounce (all historical median levels), the net profit on purple may shrink from $51.8 billion to $25-35 billion. If extreme scenarios occur in 2016, the profit may fall to $10-15 billion.

** Depressive **: The low-cost advantage of purple gold means that even with a sharp fall in commodity prices, companies can remain profitable (copper costs approximately US$ 4,000-$5,000/tonne) and not face survival crises like high-cost mining companies. But the profit is fixed.

#1.3 Risk two: Geopolitics and Resource Nationalism (Major Risks)

The globalized configuration of the purple gold mining industry is both an advantage and a risk exposure:

** Congo** — Kamoa-Cakura copper mine (global fourth largest copper mine):
- Armed clashes continued in the eastern M23, although the mine was not affected, but the situation was unstable
- The Government, in the name of "resource autonomy", has requested that mining contracts be re-signed and that the tax rate and the share ratio be increased by force
- It's one of the most important overseas assets of Purple.

** West Africa (Mali, Côte d'Ivoire)**:
- The Sahelian extremist groups are active and the Malian Army policy is repeated
- Ghana has taken the lead in substantially raising the gold franchise and abolishing the long-term stabilization agreement

** Argentina** — 3Q Lithium salt lake project:
- Environmental regulations tightened, indigenous communities resisted and the pace of commissioning was lagging

** Big country game level**:
- The United States promotes the "re-engineering of key mineral supply chains" and the coalition has restricted access to resources by middle-income mining companies
- The deployment of Purple Gold in Africa has become the focus of a big resource game.

** Worst-case scenario** If the Democratic Republic of the Congo applies nationalization or substantial tax increases to the Kamoa copper mine, the impact will be directly related to the net profit of 15-20% of the purple gold. The impact will be even more severe if policies are tightened simultaneously in several countries.

# 1.5 Risk four: M&As are expanding too fast

Cumulative M & As expenditures since 2020 were approximately $11 billion, and in 2026 Q1 were added to the total by over $46.2 billion.

** Concern**:
- High historical failure rate of large-scale M&As (the industry statistics for mining M&As show that about 50 per cent of transactions fail to achieve the expected returns)
- Merger and purchase at a high gold and copper price equal to higher asset prices
- Merger and acquisition integration requires management resources that may divert attention from existing operations
- Partial M&As in high-risk countries, increasing geopolitical exposure

**Defence**:
- The history of the purple gold M & As record is generally excellent (cases such as Kamoa, Bor copper mine, etc., demonstrate vision and enforcement)
- The scarcity of mineral resources means "no more now, more expensive."
- Company ROIC (15.5%) is well above the WACC (8-9%) and capital allocation capacity has been tested

# III. Security margin assessment

#3.1 Downward protection mechanisms

** Cost-fenced river**: Even if copper prices fall to US$ 6,000/t (historically low), purple gold can operate without a survival crisis. This is not the case with high-cost mining.

2. ** Multi-mine hedge**: The price of gold and copper is not highly relevant, and gold tends to be more inverse during economic downturns, partly reflecting the impact of falling prices for copper.

3. ** Resource value bottom line**: Purple has 120 million tons of copper resources and nearly 4,000 tons of gold resources, the market value of which provides a "floor" for the market value of the company.

4. ** Adequate cash flow**: Operating cash flow of approximately 75 billion yuan/year, which would sustain operations and debt repayments without new investments.

#3.2 Security margin deficit

1. **PE is not less than the liquidation value**: The 5.7 times PB means that the market values the company well above the net assets. If the industry goes into deep depression, the PB may fall to one to two times.

** Implicit liabilities**: The political risk of overseas projects is essentially "exceeding liabilities" - which cannot be reflected on the balance sheet but may be cashed suddenly at one day.

3. **The security margin at the top of the cycle is not natural enough**: buying mining shares at high gold and copper prices is equivalent to investing in the assumption that "good days will last".

# Four, Lee Siu's perspective core judgment

Ree-Seong emphasized three core elements:** honest management, reasonable prices, and long-term competitive advantage**.

# 4.1 Management honest?

It is largely credible. Chen Jie has demonstrated itself with 30 years of performance. The new management has no negative records and the succession arrangements are reasonable. But any management evaluation needs to take time to verify, especially, the performance of the new management in the midst of adversity.

# 4.2 Is the price reasonable?

In the current commodity price cycle, 21 times the price is not expensive, but not cheap. If commodity prices fall back to a moderate cycle level, the current price corresponds to a significant rise in the price. This is a "slow-looking" valuation.

##4.3 Is competitive advantage sustainable in the long term?

The resource reserves are hard barriers, but the ultimate risk for mining is not competition, but price and political environment. Purple is best on "controllable factors," but "uncontrollable factors" are too heavy.

# 4.4 What kind of investor is it for?

The gold mine is suitable for:
- Investors who have their own judgment about the commodity cycle.
- Investors able to withstand 30-50% of the price of their shares without panic
- Use of purple gold as a resource-placed investor (and non-core-held)
- Investors willing to push back in the lower cycle of commodity prices

Not suitable for:
- Investors seeking to stabilize cash flow returns
- No distinction between "company quality" and "industry cycle" investors.
- Conservative investors who consider security margins as the first principle
