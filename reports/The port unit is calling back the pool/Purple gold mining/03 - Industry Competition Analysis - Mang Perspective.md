I. Sectoral structural analysis - basic economics of mining

# 1.1 What kind of industry is mining?

To understand the mining of purple gold, first of all the underlying logic of the mining industry.

** Speciality of supply:
- Natural scarcity and non-renewableity of mineral resources
- New mines usually take 7-15 years from exploration to commissioning.
- Global large-scale mining discovery rates have continued to decline, and average grade levels of mines have been found to decline year by year
- The supply elasticity is extremely low - copper is 50% higher, you can't dig 50% more copper in a year.

** Specificity of the demand side**:
- Copper: King of Industrial Metals, highly related to the economic cycle, while benefiting from the new energy transition (engine powered vehicles three to four times the amount of fuel)
- Gold: As industrial material, and also as monetary and risk-free assets, central bank purchases have been over 1,000 tons/year for years.
- Lithium: core material for new energy batteries, long-term needs identified but short-term supply and demand volatile

** Industry cycle**: Mining is the most typical cyclical industry. For example, copper prices fell to $4,300 per ton in 2011, to $4,300 per ton (-57%) in 2016, to $10,000 in 2021, and to $12,000 per ton in 2024-2025. Price fluctuations directly determine the profits and losses of enterprises.

# 1.2 Where are you in the cycle?

In 2025-2026, the gold and copper prices were in the highest range of history:

- ** Copper **: approximately $12,000/tonne, forecasted at Goldman Sachs for 2026 at $12,200/t, as it appears to have risen to $12,750/t
- ** Gold **: approximately US$ 4,800-5,000/ounce, with a forecast average of US$ 4,978/ounce in Goldman Sachs for 2026, as it appears to have risen to US$ 5,700/ounce

High prices are underpinned by new energy transformations that drive copper demand, global central banks move to dollarize to increase gold, geo-conflicts that raise demand for risk avoidance, and mining supply is growing slowly.

Risk factors: Global economic recession has led to a decline in copper demand, long-term high interest rates to suppress gold prices, and concentration of new copper production (part of the large copper mining project will be launched in 2026-2028).

** Evaluation: Currently at the mid- and late-stage of the upper-price cycle for copper. Positive factors are still in place, but the possibility of a return in mean value cannot be ignored.**

# Three, the competitive advantage of the purple gold mining - the reverse thinking

Manger's analysis emphasizes "backward thinking" -- rather than asking why purple gold is better, "whose loss may be."

# 3.1 Where purple gold wins

** Cost advantage**: Project construction costs are more than 20% lower than international counterparts, and construction cycles are 30-50% shorter. This is not a coincidence of one or two projects, but of a systemic capacity. The three stages of Kamoa copper mine are in production six months earlier, the two phases of dragon copper mine are in production as planned, and the Serbian Bor copper mine operations are improving — many cases are mutually supporting.

**M&A** The stock of violet gold has increased by 805% over the last decade, while the world's major counterparts have either levelled or declined. This suggests that the ability of violet gold to "see for mine plus buy" is rare in global mining companies.

**The Chinese engineer dividend**: China’s engineering efficiency and cost advantages in the areas of infrastructure and mining are the structural advantages of purple gold over Western mining companies. Purple gold can build offshore mines at a lower cost by using Chinese construction teams and equipment manufacturing capacity.

** Policy support**: The Chinese Government encourages mining companies to “go out” to obtain strategic mineral resources. The global distribution of purple gold is consistent with the country's strategic direction and is supported in areas such as financing, diplomacy and so forth.

# 3.2 Place where purple gold may lose

The US is pushing for the "re-engineering of key mineral supply chains" and allies are limiting access to resources by middle-income mining companies. Purple gold is in the middle of a big power resource game in the Democratic Republic of the Congo and in many African countries.

**Resource nationalism**: Many African countries are following the Ghana model to raise mining taxes and fees and demand higher localization shares. The Government of the Democratic Republic of the Congo has requested re-signing mining contracts in the name of "resource autonomy".

** Management radius**: Purple is operating in more than a dozen countries, each with a very different legal, cultural, and community environment. The complexity of management is growing exponentially. Western miners (e.g. BHP) have 100 years experience overseas, and the history of the globalization of purple gold is only 10 years.

**ESG pressure**: Western investors and regulators are increasingly demanding environmental and community responsibility for mining companies. The lithium mine in Argentina has been delayed by environmental issues.

# IV. INDUSTRIAL LEVEL ANALYSIS - THE PLACE OF THURRY LEVEL IN THE LEVEL

##4.1 Mining value chain

```
Exploration mining mining mineral extraction smelting processing end consumption
(Maximum profit) (Middle profit) (Minimum profit)
```

The gold mine covers the entire industrial chain from exploration to refining, but the core value is in the exploration and mining link upstream**. This is the most profitable link because:
- Resource scarcity leads to over-profits
- Once the mines are built, the marginal costs are low.
- Downstream smelting processes are competitive and poorly profitable

# 4.2 Up and down bargaining power

** Upstream (supplier)**: mining inputs such as equipment, explosives, diesel, numerous suppliers and strong bargaining power for purple gold.

** Downstream (client)**: Minerals are traded in bulk prices, both of which have no pricing rights, and prices are determined by global supply and demand. Purple is not a risk for large customers.

##4.3 Alternative threat

- Copper: There are no large-scale economically feasible alternatives. aluminium can be partially replaced, but performance varies significantly.
- Gold: Bitcoin is considered a competitor of "digit gold" as a hedge asset, but central bank purchases indicate that traditional gold demand remains strong.
- Lithium: Sodium ion batteries may partially replace lithium batteries, but lithium is still the dominant in the short term.

# V. Long-term trends - structural changes

#5.1 Long-term trends favouring purple gold

1. **The level of mineral deposits has been declining **: The global average of copper in mining ranges has fallen from 1.5% 30 years ago to about 0.6% today. Low-grade mining is the core technological advantage of purple gold.

2. ** New energy transformation pulls copper**: Electric cars, photovoltaics, wind power, grid upgrades each require a large amount of copper. IEA expects copper demand to grow by more than 40% in 2030 compared to 2020.

3. **Mine M&As**: Global MSMEs face financing difficulties, providing M&A opportunities for large, financially powerful mining companies.

4. ** Global governance disordered gold prices**: de-dollarization, geo-conflicting, widening fiscal deficits, and multiple factors underpinning the long-term strengthening of gold.

#5.2 Unfavourable long-term trends in purple gold

1. ** Global spread of resource nationalism**: This is the greatest systemic risk for Chinese mining companies abroad.

2. ** ESG standard tightening**: The Westernly-led ESG framework may increase the operating costs and compliance burden of mining enterprises.

3. **The Central American Games**: Competition among major powers in key mineral fields may limit the overseas expansion space of Chinese mining companies.

4. ** Possibilities for technological substitution**: While no alternatives to copper are available in the short term, long-term technological advances may change the rules of the game.

# VI. COMPETITION APPRECIATION SUMMIT

The competition in the industry of the Magenta mining industry is assessed by the Mange "multi-think model":

** List of enabling factors**:
- industry supply end limits (new mine discovery is becoming more difficult) in favour of stock holders
- Structural growth on demand (new energy transformation pulls copper demand)
- Purple gold is supported by a systemic competitive advantage in costs, mergers and acquisitions, and engineering efficiency
- Companies are in an increasing period of expansion of their capacity (targets for 2025 are 40-50 per cent higher than the 2025 level)
- Multi-mine layouts provide a certain amount of cyclical squid.

** List of disadvantages**:
- The inexorable cyclical nature of commodity prices
- Continued rise in political and resource-nationalistic risks from overseas operations
- Uncertainty associated with the change of management team
- Industry valuations are highly influenced by commodity prices. "Low" may be a cyclical hallucination.
