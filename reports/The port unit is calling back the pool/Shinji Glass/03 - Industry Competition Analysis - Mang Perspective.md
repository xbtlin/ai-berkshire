# I. Industry structure: two distinct battlegrounds

Shinji glass is involved in two industries: floating glass and car glass. The logic of competition between these two industries is quite different and has to be analysed separately.

# The floating glass industry

** Industry **: China ' s floating glass produces about 1 billion boxes of annual capacity and markets about 150-200 billion yuan.

** Industry characteristics**:
- High homogenization of products, with prices as a core element of competition
- Supply rigidity (production lines cannot be easily shut down, cold-repair cycle 8-10 years)
- High correlation between demand and real estate (approximately 65 per cent), high cyclicality
- Limited transport radius (300-500 km) and natural formation of regional markets

** Concentration**: Industry has a three-tier pattern:
- ** First Platoon**: Shinji Glass (of approximately 30,000 tons of solar melting), Flagside Group (of about 16,100 tons of solar melting per day)
- ** Second Platoon**: South Bob A, Crystal Technology, China Glass
- ** Third Plateau**: Large number of SMEs, usually 1-2 production lines

The combined capacity of the first two major enterprises, which accounts for about 25-30 per cent of the country’s total, is low in concentration. This means that it is difficult for the leading firms to influence prices by controlling supply, and that competition in the industry remains intense.

# The car glass industry

** Industry **: The global automotive glass market is estimated at $36.4 billion (2024) and is projected to reach $48 billion in 2030, with a compound growth rate of about 4.5 per cent.

** Industry characteristics**:
- High barriers to certification (induced certification by vehicle vendors for 2-3 years)
- High product differentiation (high functional glass premium)
- High customer sticky (high switching cost)
- Globalizing Competition (global layout of head firms)

** Concentration**: High global concentration:
- **Footglass**: Global share is over 40% and China is about 70%
- **AGC(XIXIX)**: Global share approximately 16%
- **San Goban**: Global share approximately 12%
- **NSG (Japan Plate Nitros): ** Global share is about 8%
- **Sinvest glass**: Global share about 3-5%, mainly in Asian markets

Auto-glass is an oligopoly industry, but the faith is not one of the oligopolies — at least not in the OEM market.

# II. THE COMPETITION OF BELIEVE

# Floating glass: tap but not monopoly

Faith is one of the largest floating glass producers in China and the world, but "largest" is not the "most powerful." In the vast commodity industry of floating glass, the main advantage is that:

1. ** Cost leads**: kilns, multibases, centralized purchases. 18% Māori rate for industry-wide losses in 2025
2. ** Cycle resistance**: Strong financial capacity to maintain operations during the industry ' s low-end period pending withdrawal of competitors
3. ** Supply impact**: To some extent, market supply and demand are affected by controlling the pace of production of their own.

But these advantages are "relative" and not "absolute." Flagside groups have similar sizes and cost advantages. A large number of second- and third-line firms are profitable during the industry ' s career, and the vanguard advantages are diluted.

** Key question**: The floating glass industry will never be as highly concentrated as the cement industry (cement CR10 exceeds 60%), because the exit of the glass line is extremely difficult - "Firing is easy to put off." This determines the long-term high level of competition in the floating glass industry.

# III. Industrial chain analysis

# Upstream: raw materials

The main raw materials for floating glass are pure alkali (about 30-40 per cent of the cost), lime sand and energy (natural gas/oil coke).

- **Pure **: China has a high concentration of production (e.g., energy exuberants, tri-friend chemical industry) and price fluctuations have a significant impact on floating glass costs
- **Energy**: Gas and oil coke prices are affected by international energy markets

As one of the largest downstream users, the letter has a bargaining advantage in the procurement of raw materials. But price volatility in upstream supply remains an uncontrollable factor.

# Downstream: demand end

** Downward glass:

# Key trends

** Trend I: Floating glass production is clear**

Industry expects that cold production lines will accelerate in 2026, and supply-side contraction will drive prices back up. But the pace of output and liquidity depends on the financial strength and willingness of the firms to withdraw, a process that may be slower than expected.

** Trends II: Automobile glass upgrade**

The size and value of the glass on the bicycles have been continuously increasing, and smart network cars need to integrate more functionality (HUD, antenna, heating, light-tighting, etc.). This trend has favoured the technologically leading glass industry.

** Trends III: Localization of overseas production capacity**

Trade barriers (tariffs, anti-dumping) drive glass firms to build plants overseas. The Sputnik production and production patterns in Malaysia and Indonesia are responding to this trend.

** Trends IV: Photovoltaic glass demand**

Although the glass itself does not produce photovoltaic glass (responsible to the affiliated company Shinto), excess capacity and price wars in the photovoltaic industry have seriously affected the profitability of the energy source, and hence the profits and losses of the conglomerates of the glass.
