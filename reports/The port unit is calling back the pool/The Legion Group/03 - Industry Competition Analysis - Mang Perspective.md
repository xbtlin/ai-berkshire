# Ctrip Group: Industry Competition Analysis

> Analysis dimensions: industry structure, competition landscape evolution, second-level thinking, negative arguments
> Core question: How does the industry ecology change? Will Ctrip's competitive position be better or worse?

---

## 1. Industry Panorama: Ecological Map of China’s Online Tourism

The transaction size of China's online travel market is expected to exceed 2 trillion yuan in 2025, with an online penetration rate of about 60%, and there is still room for improvement. This is a large and growing market.

### Competitive Landscape (Market Share 2024-2025)

| Platform | Market share | Core advantages | Main battlefields |
|------|----------|----------|----------|
| Ctrip (including Qunar) | ~56% | Supply chain depth, brand awareness, mid-to-high-end users | All categories, all markets |
| Tongcheng Travel | ~15% | Lower market, WeChat entrance | Low-tier cities, cost-effective users |
| Meituan | ~13% | High-frequency traffic in local life, "high frequency and low frequency" | Local hotels, short-distance travel |
| Fliggy | ~8% | Alibaba Ecosystem, Outbound Travel | Young Users, Outbound Travel |
| Douyin | ~3% | Content cultivation, short video traffic | Wine and travel group buying, brand marketing |

At first glance, Ctrip is far ahead with a market share of 56%, and its position seems to be very solid. But if you draw conclusions just by looking at this number, you will make the mistake of "static thinking". What's more important is to look at **trends and directions**.

---

## 2. Evolution of the competitive landscape

### Trend 1: Fundamental migration of traffic portals

This is the **deepest structural challenge** faced by Ctrip.

Ten years ago, when users wanted to book a hotel, they would open the Ctrip App or search for "Beijing Hotel" on the search engine. Ctrip is the starting point for travel decisions.

Today, more and more users get their travel inspiration from Douyin’s short videos and Xiaohongshu’s pictures and texts. Users may complete the reservation directly on Douyin after seeing a video of a Sanya hotel. In this scenario, **the user does not even have the action of "opening Ctrip"**.

This migration of traffic entrances is structural, not short-term. It means:
- The user's travel decision-making link is shortening, from "planting→search→price comparison→booking" to "planting→instant booking"
- Ctrip's core value in the "search-price comparison" link may be bypassed
- Content platforms are pushing OTA from the "front desk" to the "back desk"

**But we also need to see the other side**: Douyin’s write-off rate for its wine and travel business is much lower than Ctrip’s. Users who impulsively order a hotel package on Douyin may end up not going due to time conflicts, inconvenient location and other reasons. Users on Ctrip "book later when they have clear travel needs", and the conversion efficiency and write-off rate are higher. For suppliers, Ctrip brings "more certain orders", and this value will not disappear.

### Trend 2: Meituan’s “high frequency band and low frequency” continue to erode

Meituan is Ctrip’s most threatening competitor because:

1. **Traffic cost is almost zero**: Users are already ordering takeout and buying group purchases on Meituan, and they easily see hotel recommendations. Meituan does not need to acquire customers separately for its wine and travel business.
2. **Local hotels have obvious advantages**: For the need of "finding a temporary place to stay tonight", Meituan's user habits have been established.
3. **In Q3 2024, Meituan’s hotel and travel orders will increase by more than 50% year-on-year**, and the growth rate is much faster than Ctrip.

But the disadvantages of Meituan are:
- Focus on the mid- to low-end market, with low unit price per customer
- Weak ability to handle complex itineraries (cross-city, cross-border, multi-stage transportation)
- International business is almost zero
- The brand mentality of "takeaway platforms" limits the recognition of mid-to-high-end users

### Trend 3: Fliggy and Tongcheng each defend their corner

**Fliggy** relies on the Alibaba ecosystem (Taobao, Alipay) and has a certain base among outbound travelers and young users, but its 8% market share has not increased significantly over the years. Alibaba’s own strategic focus is not on tourism. Fliggy is not a major threat to Ctrip.

**Tongcheng Travel** has established advantages in the sinking market (third- and fourth-tier cities and below) with the help of the WeChat mini program. But Tongcheng is more like a complementor to Ctrip than a direct competitor - the overlap in user profiles between the two is limited.

### Trend 4: New battlefield in the international market

In the international market, Ctrip's competitors are Booking Holdings and Expedia Group. The two companies' revenue in 2025 will be approximately US$23 billion and US$13 billion respectively, far exceeding Ctrip.

There are two entry points for Ctrip’s internationalization:
1. **Chinese outbound travel users**: This is Ctrip’s natural advantage. The Trip.com brand has a high penetration rate among this group.
2. **Global users travel to China (inbound travel)**: The expansion of China’s visa-free policy has brought unique opportunities to Ctrip. Inbound passenger bookings will increase by more than 100% in 2025

But in the biggest cake of "foreigners booking foreign hotels", Ctrip is still a small player. To compete head-on with Booking requires huge investment and a long time.

## 3. Reverse thinking: Under what circumstances will Ctrip lose?

Counterthinking is the most important part of analysis. Let’s think carefully about the several ways Ctrip might fail:

### Path 1: Douyin improves contract fulfillment capabilities and completes the closed loop of travel consumption

If Douyin can not only "plant grass and buy in groups", but also achieve real-time inventory management, complex itinerary processing, and 7x24 customer service - then Ctrip's core value will be significantly weakened.

**Probability assessment: Medium-low. ** Travel services are much more complex than food delivery. The more likely path for Douyin is to cooperate with OTA platforms (to become a traffic channel) rather than building a complete fulfillment system by itself. However, we do not rule out the possibility of gradual penetration through acquisitions or cooperation in the long term.

### Path 2: Antitrust changes the rules of the game

If regulatory requirements require Ctrip to:
- Reduce the commission rate for hotels (from 15%-25% to less than 10%)
- Any form of exclusive cooperation is prohibited
- Open data interface to competitors

Then Ctrip's advantage on the supply side will be weakened, and the competitive landscape may change from "one superpower and many strong players" to "many strong players working side by side."

**Probability assessment: Moderate. ** Referring to the precedents of Alibaba and Meituan, a combination of fines + behavioral restrictions is the most likely outcome. However, these two companies still maintained their market leadership positions after the penalties, which shows that although antitrust penalties will weaken the leaders, it is difficult to fundamentally change the pattern.

### Path 3: Structural changes in the tourism industry

For example: AI technology allows users to completely bypass OTAs and directly connect with hotels and airlines through AI assistants; or Web3 technology enables disintermediation.

**Probability assessment: Low. **These scenarios are unlikely to occur in the short term. The fragmentation and complexity of the tourism supply chain determines that intermediate platforms still have value. But in the long term (5-10 years), if AI agent can really replace the role of OTA, it will subvert the entire industry.

## 4. Where is the "sweet spot" of the industry?

From an industry perspective, there are several ongoing trends that are beneficial to Ctrip:

### 1. Policy bonus period for China’s inbound tourism

China is expanding the scope of visa exemption on a large scale, and the 144-hour transit visa exemption has covered many cities. Inbound tourists will increase significantly in 2025. Ctrip will be the biggest beneficiary of inbound tourism with Trip.com’s international reputation and multilingual service capabilities. This is an advantage that is difficult for global competitors to copy - Meituan and Douyin have almost no reputation overseas.

### 2. The rise of high-end tourism consumption

As the middle class grows and consumption upgrades, the demand for high-end hotels, customized travel, and business travel is growing. This happens to be Ctrip's strongest area - its brand tone, service capabilities, and supplier relationships all point to the mid-to-high end. It may be a more rational strategy to leave the low-end market to Meituan and Tongcheng, and Ctrip to deepen its efforts in the high-end market.

### 3. The impact of AI on OTA is a "double-edged sword with a positive side"

AI may subvert OTAs (users directly use AI to plan travel), or it may strengthen OTAs (whoever has the most data and supply chain will have the best AI). In the short term, Ctrip’s 26 years of data accumulation and R&D investment of 15.1 billion put it in a favorable position in the AI ​​competition. The 200% increase in TripGenie conversation volume is a positive sign.

However, mid- to long-term uncertainties still exist: if general-purpose AI assistants (such as ChatGPT, Doubao, etc.) can directly help users complete travel planning and booking, the value of OTAs may be compressed.

## 6. Summary

| Dimensions | Scores | Core Judgments |
|------|------|----------|
| Industry Attraction | ★★★★☆ | Big market, still growing, there is still room for penetration |
| Competitive landscape | ★★★☆☆ | One superpower and multiple powers are loosening up, and the migration of traffic entrances is a structural challenge |
| Ctrip’s competitive position | ★★★★☆ | Supply chain and mid- to high-end are still core strengths, but they cannot rest easy |
| The biggest source of threats | Short term: antitrust | Medium term: continued penetration of Meituan and Douyin | Long term: AI subverts the OTA model |

**Overall Rating: ★★★☆☆ (3.5/5)**

Ctrip occupies a leading position in a good industry. This is a fact. However, the competitive landscape of the industry is undergoing slow but definite changes - the traffic entrance is shifting from search to content, the cross-penetration of local life platforms, and regulatory constraints on leading companies. Ctrip will not "suddenly collapse", but its market share and profit margins may face moderate and sustained pressure in the next 3-5 years.

The question investors need to ask themselves is: Do current valuations already reflect these pressures? If so, that might be an opportunity; if not, a larger safety margin is needed.
