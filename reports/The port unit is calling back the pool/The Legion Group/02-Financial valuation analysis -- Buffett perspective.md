# Ctrip Group: Financial Valuation Analysis

> Analysis dimensions: financial health, earnings quality, capital allocation, intrinsic value estimation
> Core question: Is the current price below intrinsic value? Is the margin of safety sufficient?

---

## 1. Financial Overview: An Excellent Report Card

Let’s first look at the core financial data for the whole year of 2025:

| Indicators | 2024 | 2025 | Year-on-year change |
|------|--------|--------|----------|
| Net operating income | 53.3 billion yuan | 62.4 billion yuan | +17% |
| Accommodation booking revenue | 21.6 billion yuan | 26.1 billion yuan | +21% |
| Transportation ticketing revenue | 20.3 billion yuan | 22.5 billion yuan | +11% |
| Tourism and vacation revenue | 4.4 billion yuan | 4.7 billion yuan | +8% |
| Business travel management revenue | 2.5 billion yuan | 2.8 billion yuan | +13% |
| Comprehensive gross profit margin | ~81% | ~81% | Flat |
| R&D expenses | - | 15.1 billion yuan | Accounting for 24% of revenue |
| Net profit (including investment income) | 17.2 billion yuan | 33.4 billion yuan | +94% |
| Operating net profit (excluding investment income) | ~16.1 billion yuan | ~13.4 billion yuan | Verification required |

### Key Observations

**1. Revenue growth is steady but not explosive**
A revenue growth rate of 17% is considered healthy growth for an Internet company with a scale of 100 billion. Accommodation booking is still the largest source of revenue (42%) and has the fastest growth (21%); transportation ticketing accounts for 36% and has a slower growth rate (11%), reflecting that air tickets are standardized products and have limited room for price increases.

**2. Extraordinarily stable gross profit margin**
77.5% in 2022, 81.8% in 2023, 81.2% in 2024, and about 81% in 2025 - the gross profit margin is extremely stable in the range of 80%-82%. This shows that Ctrip has very strong pricing power and cost control capabilities. But it should also be noted that a gross profit margin of 81% means that profits on the supplier side have been compressed, which may be one of the triggers for antitrust investigations.

**3. Net profit needs to be split carefully**
The net profit in the 2025 report is 33.4 billion yuan, a surge of 94% year-on-year - but this number is seriously misleading. This includes investment gains of 19.9 billion yuan (only 1.1 billion yuan in 2024). After excluding investment income, operating profits were approximately 13.4 billion yuan, corresponding to an operating net profit rate of approximately 21%. This is the number that reflects the true operating capabilities.

## 2. Profit quality: Is the profit "real money"?

### Cash Flow Analysis

Ctrip's operating cash flow is better than net profit in the long term, which is a sign of high-quality profitability. Net cash flow from operating activities in 2025 is expected to be in the range of 25-35 billion yuan (including cash recovery of part of investment income), far exceeding operating net profit.

The reason is simple:
- Users pay first, suppliers settle later (forward working capital cycle)
- Non-cash expenses such as depreciation and amortization need to be added back
- The balance of advances received continues to grow

### Balance Sheet Health

| Indicators | Values | Judgment |
|------|------|------|
| Asset-liability ratio | ~44% | Reasonable, not radical |
| Net debt ratio | About -31% | Net cash position, very healthy |
| Current ratio | >1.5x | Sufficient short-term debt solvency |

Ctrip is a net cash company, meaning it still has plenty of cash and short-term investments on its books minus all interest-bearing liabilities. This gives the company a sufficient cushion in the face of antitrust fines. Even if the fine is 6.5 billion yuan, which is the most severe standard, it is completely within the scope of its affordability.

### Profit Quality Rating: ★★★★☆ (4/5)

Operating profits are real, cash flow is abundant, and the balance sheet is clean. The deductible item is that investment income is highly volatile, which can easily distort single-year profit performance.

## 3. Capital allocation: How does management spend money?

The quality of capital allocation often determines long-term shareholder returns.

### 1. R&D investment: generous but reasonable (★★★★☆)

R&D expenses of 15.1 billion yuan, accounting for 24% of revenue. This proportion is relatively high among Internet companies, but considering that Ctrip is advancing its AI strategy (TripGenie) and international expansion (Trip.com globalization), this investment has a clear strategic purpose. The appointment of a chief product officer to lead the AI ​​strategy in early 2025 shows that the company is serious about its investment in AI.

The key question is: What is the payback period for these R&D investments? If the AI ​​strategy can improve conversion rates and user stickiness, then the current high R&D investment will translate into higher profitability in the future.

### 2. Share buyback: active and sizable (★★★★☆)

The repurchase program approved in February 2025 was subsequently upgraded in August to a repurchase authorization totaling no more than $5 billion. Approximately US$400 million in repurchases have been completed by the end of 2025. This repurchase effort is considerable, indicating that management believes the company is undervalued.

But please note: the effect of buyback depends on the purchase price. If you buy back when you are overvalued, you are actually destroying shareholder value. Considering that Ctrip's PE is in the range of 14-17 times, the current repurchase pricing is relatively reasonable.

### 3. International investment: long-term bet (★★★☆☆)

Ctrip announced that it will invest 1 billion yuan in inbound tourism every year, increasing Trip.com’s investment in the global market. In 2025, international OTA bookings will increase by 60%, and inbound tourism will double. The data is impressive. But internationalization is a marathon - Booking.com and Expedia have been deeply involved in the global market for decades. To compete with them overseas, Ctrip needs continuous and huge investment, and the results are uncertain.

### Capital Allocation Rating: ★★★★☆ (3.8/5)

R&D investment has a strategic direction, buybacks are active and reasonably priced, and international investment is in the right direction but has a long return cycle. Overall, management's capital allocation is rational.

## 4. Valuation analysis: How much is Ctrip worth?

### Current valuation level

Calculated based on the Hong Kong stock price on May 16, 2026 (approximately 417-517 Hong Kong dollars range, taking the median value of approximately 470 Hong Kong dollars):

| Valuation indicators | Numerical values | Judgment |
|------|------|------|
| Rolling PE (including investment income) | ~7x | False underestimation, unsustainable investment income |
| Operating PE (excluding investment income) | ~17x | A more realistic valuation level |
| Forward PE (2026E) | ~14x | Market consensus |
| PEG | <1x | Assuming 15% growth, PEG is about 0.9 |

### Intrinsic value estimation (DCF framework)

Do a simplified discounted free cash flow:

**Key assumptions:**
- Revenue growth rate from 2026 to 2028: 15% → 12% → 10%
- Long-term growth rate: 5% (tourism industry GDP + growth)
- Operating profit margin: 22% (slightly higher than current, assuming economies of scale are released)
- Discount rate: 10% (taking into account the risk premium of Chinese Internet companies)
- Terminal value multiple: 15x free cash flow

**Rough estimate:**
- 2026E operating profit: ~16 billion yuan
- 2026E free cash flow: ~14 billion yuan
- 10-year DCF + terminal value → intrinsic value of approximately HK$350-450 billion
- Corresponding to approximately HK$540-690 per share

### Scenario analysis

| Scenario | Core assumptions | Corresponding valuation (HKD/share) |
|------|----------|---------------------|
| Optimistic | Light antitrust penalties, successful internationalization, significant AI efficiency improvements | 650-750 |
| Benchmark | Antitrust fines of 3 billion to 4 billion, normal business growth | 500-600 |
| Pessimistic | Heavy antitrust fines + behavioral restrictions, growth slows to below 10% | 350-420 |

### Safety margin judgment

The current stock price (approximately HK$470) is at the lower edge of the base scenario valuation range. If you believe in the baseline scenario, the margin of safety is about 15-25%; if you prefer the pessimistic scenario, the margin of safety is insufficient.

**Valuation Rating: ★★★☆☆ (3.5/5)**
The valuation is not expensive but it is not extremely cheap either. Uncertainty caused by antitrust investigations has suppressed valuations, but there is room for valuation repair if the results of the investigations are better than expected.

## 5. Financial impact of antitrust fines

This is the largest variable in current valuations. Reference precedent:

| Company | Amount of fine | Proportion of revenue | Subsequent performance of stock price |
|------|----------|------------|-------------|
| Alibaba (2021) | 18.2 billion yuan | 4% (2019 revenue) | Short-term rebound after all the bad news |
| Meituan (2021) | 3.4 billion yuan | 3% (2020 revenue) | Stabilize after all bad news |
| Ctrip (estimated) | 6-6.5 billion yuan | 1%-10% (2025 revenue) | To be determined |

**Core Judgment**:

1. **The fine itself is not the biggest problem**. Even if calculated at 5%, which is about 3.1 billion yuan, Ctrip has plenty of cash on its books, and the impact on operations after a one-time provision will be limited.
2. **Behavioral restrictions are key**. If we are required to abolish the "price adjustment assistant", stop the disguised "choose-one", and reduce the commission rate, the impact on long-term profitability may be far greater than the fine itself.
3. **Uncertainty itself suppresses valuation**. Judging from the precedents of Alibaba and Meituan, after antitrust penalties are implemented, the elimination of uncertainty may actually be a catalyst.

## 6. Summary

| Dimensions | Scores | Core Judgments |
|------|------|----------|
| Financial health | ★★★★☆ | Net cash, high gross profit, abundant cash flow |
| Profit quality | ★★★★☆ | Real operating profits, large fluctuations in investment returns |
| Capital allocation | ★★★★☆ | R&D has direction, buyback is active, and internationalization has bets |
| Reasonable valuation | ★★★☆☆ | Not expensive but not extremely cheap, antitrust is the biggest variable |

**Overall Rating: ★★★★☆ (3.8/5)**

From a financial perspective, Ctrip is a very healthy company - high gross profit, net cash, and positive cash flow. The core issue is not financial quality, but rather: (1) the amount of antitrust penalties and the degree of behavioral restrictions; and (2) how much the upper limit of long-term profitability will be depressed if it is required to reduce commission rates or open up supply chains.

The current PE of 14-17 times is undoubtedly underestimated for a company with annual growth of 15%+, gross profit margin of 80%+, and net cash without the hanging sword of antitrust investigation. The question is when the sword falls and how deep it cuts.
