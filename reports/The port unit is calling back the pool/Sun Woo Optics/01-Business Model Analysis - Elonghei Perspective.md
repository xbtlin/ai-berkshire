# Sunny Optical Technology (2382.HK): Business Model Analysis

> Analysis Dimension: Business Model and Moat Depth
> Core question: What is the nature of this business? How deep is the moat? Why do users insist on it?

---

## 1. The essence of business

What kind of business does Sunny Optical do? In the simplest terms: **Create "eyes" for various smart devices**.

Mobile phones need to take pictures and require lenses; cars need assisted driving and cameras; AR glasses need displays and optical modules. Sunny manufactures these core optical components. From the design of a lens, mold manufacturing, coating processing, to lens assembly and module integration, Sunny covers the middle and upper reaches of the optical industry chain.

Essential features of this business:

**1. Precision manufacturing + scale effect**
Optical lenses are a precision manufacturing industry that has extremely high requirements for process accuracy, yield control, and production capacity scale. A mobile phone lens contains 5-8 lenses, and the surface precision of each lens is required to reach the nanometer level. This is not something you can do with just throwing money at it, it requires long-term accumulation of craftsmanship. Sunny ships more than 100 million mobile phone lenses every month. This scale itself is a barrier - the larger the scale, the lower the unit cost, the more yield data is accumulated, and the faster the improvement.

**2. Driven by technology iteration, but not a pure technology company**
The optical industry has a technological iteration every 2-3 years - from plastic lenses to glass-plastic hybrids, from fixed focus to optical zoom, from single camera to multi-camera, and from 2D to 3D perception. Sunny must keep up with every wave, but the real competitiveness is not a single technological breakthrough, but the ability to quickly engineer and mass-produce new technologies**. This is consistent with Duan Yongping's emphasis on "doing the right thing": not pursuing the most cutting-edge paper technology, but optimizing proven technology for mass production.

**3. Customer stickiness comes from certification barriers**
The cost for mobile phone brands to change lens suppliers is extremely high: they need to redo optical design matching, production line debugging, and quality certification, which takes 6-12 months. Vehicle-mounted optics is even worse, and the vehicle-level certification cycle is as long as 2-3 years. Once it enters the customer's supply chain, it is difficult to replace it in the short term.

## 2. Moat analysis

### Moat 1: Vertically integrated full industry chain capabilities ★★★★☆

Sunny is one of the few companies in the world that has the full chain capabilities of "optical design → lens manufacturing → lens assembly → module integration". Largan only makes lenses and lenses, not modules; Qiutai Technology mainly makes module packaging, not lenses. Sunny’s full-chain capabilities mean:

- The overall solution can be optimized from the design source, not just processing of supplied materials.
- The collaboration efficiency between various links is higher and the yield rate is improved faster
- Stronger customer service capabilities and can provide "turnkey" solutions

This is a real moat. However, it should be noted that the entire industry chain also means heavy assets and high capital expenditures, which will amplify risks during the industry downturn.

### Moat 2: Scale advantage and cost leadership ★★★★☆

In 2025, Sunny will rank first in global shipments of mobile phone lenses, with a global market share of over 30% of automotive lenses, and cumulative shipments of more than 100 million sets. The cost advantages of this scale are significant:

- Strong bargaining power in raw material procurement
- Higher production line utilization and more fully diluted fixed costs
- The return on investment in automation is higher (the greater the output, the faster the return on investment in automation)

The advantage of scale is one of the most reliable moats in manufacturing. But it should also be noted that scale itself cannot prevent the subversion of technical routes.

### Moat Three: Customer Authentication and Switching Cost ★★★★☆

Sunny's core customers include Huawei, Xiaomi, OPPO, vivo and other leading mobile phone brands, as well as major global automobile manufacturers. In the automotive field, the cycle from certification to mass production usually takes 2-3 years, which constitutes a high level of customer stickiness.

In the field of mobile phones, Sunny is already a leading supplier of periscope lens modules, with its major customers accounting for more than 90% of the periscope lens smartphone market. This means that in the segmented track of high-end telephoto modules, Sunny is almost irreplaceable.

### Moat 4: R&D capabilities and technical reserves ★★★☆☆

In 2024, R&D expenses will exceed 2.9 billion yuan, accounting for approximately 7.5% of revenue, a year-on-year increase of 13.9%. Sunny has a presence in cutting-edge fields such as glass-plastic hybrid lenses, ultra-thin periscope lenses, automotive 8M/17M high-definition cameras, and AR optical waveguides.

The reason for giving Samsung and a half is: the technical barriers in the optical industry are real, but there is no absolute generational gap like the chip. Largan's technology is still leading in the field of high-end lenses. Sunny's advantages lie more in system integration and mass production capabilities than in its absolute leadership in a single technology.

## 3. User value: Why do customers choose Sunny?

From the perspective of downstream customers (mobile phone brands, car companies), the reasons for choosing Sunny are:

1. **Full solution capability**: No need to find lens factory, lens factory, module factory separately, Sunny can do it all in one place
2. **Mass production reliability**: Production capacity of hundreds of millions of units per month is guaranteed. What major customers fear most is supply chain interruption.
3. **Cost-effectiveness**: Compared with Taiwanese manufacturers such as Largan, Sunny has obvious price advantages in mid-to-high-end products.
4. **Response speed**: The engineering culture and overtime culture of mainland manufacturers make the project response speed significantly faster than that of Taiwanese and Japanese competitors.
5. **In-vehicle certification accumulation**: A complete vehicle-level certification system has been established in the automotive field, and the introduction period for new customers is short.

## 4. Weaknesses of the business model

### 1. Over-reliance on the mobile phone industry
In 2025, the mobile phone business will still account for 63% of total revenue. If the global mobile phone market declines by 15% in 2026 as Morgan Stanley predicts, Sunny's fundamentals will be substantially impacted. The mobile phone industry itself is a market whose growth has peaked. There is limited room to rely solely on "upgrading" (increasing the value of single-machine lenses) to offset the decline in shipments.

### 2. Manufacturing profit margin ceiling
Sunny's gross profit margin is about 20%, and its net profit margin is about 11%. There is a huge gap compared with Largan's 70% gross profit margin. This reflects that Sunny's business model is still manufacturing in nature - making money based on scale and efficiency, rather than relying on irreplaceable technology premiums. When the industry is good, profit elasticity is high, but when the industry is down, there is little protection.

### 3. Uncertainty of new business
Automotive optics and AR optics are definite directions, but they are still far from becoming the mainstay of profits. The revenue of the automotive business in 2025 is 7.33 billion, accounting for 17%. Although the growth is fast (+21.3%), the absolute volume is not enough to compensate for the potential decline of the mobile phone business. The AR/XR business is in its early stages and will still be in the investment period in 2025.

### 4. Customer concentration risk
Sunny's major customers are mainly several leading mobile phone brands, and any strategic adjustment (such as self-developed optics, switching suppliers) may have a significant impact. Apple has not yet included Sunny in its core lens supply chain (Apple mainly uses Largan Light). This is a fortress that has not been breached for a long time.

## 5. Soul torture from Duan Yongping’s perspective

**"Can you understand what this business will look like in ten years?"**

Frankly, not entirely. Will humans still use mobile phones in ten years? If used, what will the optical system of the mobile phone be like? Can AR glasses truly replace mobile phones and become the mainstream terminal? The answers to these questions are uncertain. But one thing is relatively certain: **As long as humans need to "see", they will need optical systems, and the precision manufacturing capabilities of optical systems will not suddenly be subverted**. The precision manufacturing capabilities accumulated by Sunny have long-term value. The question is whether this value can be converted into sustained profit growth.

**"Do the right thing vs. do things right"**

Sunny has performed well in "doing the right things": deploying automotive optics in advance, investing in Goertek Optics to enter the AR supply chain, and promoting the spin-off and listing of the automotive business. We also have a solid foundation in "doing things right": integration of the entire industry chain, large-scale mass production, and continuous investment in R&D.

But there’s a fundamental problem with this business: Optical component manufacturers have limited pricing power. Sunny does not directly face end consumers. Its products are hidden in mobile phones and cars, and brand owners have the final pricing power. This means that it is difficult for Sunny to continue to increase product unit prices like Apple, and it is more about squeezing profits on the cost side.

| Pricing power | ★★★☆☆ | The attributes of manufacturing determine that pricing power is limited and rely on scale to reduce costs |

| Business model quality | ★★★★☆ | A high-quality target in the manufacturing industry, but not a "lay and make money" type of business |

**Overall Rating: ★★★★☆ (3.6/5)**

Sunny Optical is an excellent precision manufacturing company with solid competitive barriers. But it is not the kind of "perfect business" that can be held with eyes closed for ten years - it requires continuous investment in research and development, keeping up with technology iterations, and managing the switch between new and old businesses. For investors who are willing to track and understand the optical industry, this is a target worthy of attention; for investors who pursue ultimate certainty, the volatility of the manufacturing industry and dependence on mobile phones are hidden concerns that need to be faced squarely.
