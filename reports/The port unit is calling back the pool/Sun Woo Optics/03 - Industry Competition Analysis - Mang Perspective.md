# Sunny Optical Technology (2382.HK): Industry Competition Analysis

> Analysis Dimensions: Industry Ecology, Competition Pattern and Final Deduction
> Core question: What is the endgame for this industry? Who will survive to the end? What is Sunny's place in the ending?

---

## 1. The nature of the optical industry: the undervalued "water seller"

Let’s take a step back and think about a fundamental question: Why is the optical industry worth paying attention to?

Because **almost all smart devices need to "see"**. Mobile phones need cameras, cars need perception systems, AR/VR needs optical displays, industrial robots need machine vision, and drones need aerial photography lenses. Optical components are the infrastructure of the intelligent era - not the sexiest track, but the lowest track.

The industry has several structural characteristics:

**1. Downstream applications continue to expand. ** Ten years ago, optics mainly served cameras and mobile phones, but now it has expanded to automobiles, AR/VR, industrial vision, medical imaging, security monitoring, etc. Each new application scenario is expanding the total addressable market (TAM) of optics.

**2. The value of a single machine continues to increase. ** Mobile phones have changed from single cameras to three cameras and four cameras, and the number of cameras per car has increased from 2-3 to 8-12. The value of the optical modules of AR glasses may be several times higher than that of mobile phones. Even if terminal equipment shipments do not grow, the value of optics is still growing.

**3. Technical barriers are increasing. ** From simple plastic lenses to glass-plastic hybrids, free-form surfaces, and diffraction light waveguides, the complexity of optical technology is rising rapidly. This is good for existing leaders - companies with deeper technological accumulation are more likely to keep up with the new generation of technology.

## 2. Competitive landscape: three-layer pyramid

### The first floor: high-end lenses - Largan's "independent kingdom"

Largan Guang (Taiwan) is the most special entity in this industry. It only makes mobile phone lenses (not modules), but its gross profit margin is around 70% year-round, and its net profit margin exceeds 40%. The lenses for flagship phones of top customers such as Apple and Samsung are basically provided by Largan.

Largan's moat lies in:
- Ultimate lens processing precision (micron-level process control)
- Decades of accumulated mold and process experience
- Long-term binding relationships in Apple’s supply chain

Largan's weaknesses are:
- Only focus on mobile phone lenses, single business
- It does not make modules or vehicles, and its business is not diversified enough.
- The willingness and speed of capacity expansion are not as good as those of mainland manufacturers

**Sunny vs Largan:** In the high-end lens segment, Sunny still lags behind Largan. However, Sunny's strategy is not to engage head-on - it is to occupy a dominant position in the mid-to-high-end market through full-chain capabilities (lenses + lenses + modules) and stronger cost performance. The two are more differentiated competitions than direct substitutes.

### The second level: lens + module - Sunny’s main battlefield

At this level, Sunny is the global leader:
- Ranked first in the world in mobile phone lens shipments
- Ranked first in the world in mobile phone camera module shipments
- The global market share of automotive lenses exceeds 30%

Main competitors:

**Qiu Titanium Technology (1478.HK):** The third place in the module field, mainly producing mid-to-low-end camera modules and fingerprint modules. The gap with Sunny is narrowing but still obvious - Qiu Titanium's gross profit margin is only 8-10%, indicating that the added value of the product is low. Qiu Titanium does not have lens manufacturing capabilities and is at a disadvantage in the high-end trend.

**OFILM (002456.SZ):** It was once the boss of camera modules, but its vitality was severely damaged after being kicked out of the supply chain by Apple in 2021. At present, it still has a certain share in the mid-to-low-end module market, but its brand power and technical strength have declined significantly.

**Yujingguang (Taiwan):** The second lens supplier in Apple's supply chain, its size is much smaller than Largan, but it has a unique position in Apple's ecosystem.

**Crystal Optoelectronics (002273.SZ):** Mainly produces optical filters, optical windows and other components. It has business overlap with Sunny but does not completely compete with it.

### The third level: Vehicle-mounted optics - a new battlefield, the pattern is undecided

Automotive optics is the fastest growing track, but the competitive landscape is far from stable:

| Competitors | Strengths | Weaknesses |
|--------|------|------|
| Sunny Optical | No. 1 in global market share (30%+), complete vehicle certification | Automotive business profit margin is still climbing |
| Lianchuang Electronics | Has a first-mover advantage in the ADAS field | Much smaller than Sunny |
| Mobileye supporting manufacturer | Bound with Mobileye, with ADAS chip collaboration | Ups and downs with Mobileye |
| Traditional automotive optics manufacturers | Long-term cooperative relationships with car companies | Slow technology iteration |

Sunny has obvious competitive advantages in the automotive field: the largest scale, the most complete vehicle certifications, and the most complete product line (from standard definition to 17MP high definition, from cameras to lidar optical components). But this track is still changing rapidly, and the battle over the technical route of intelligent driving (pure vision vs lidar vs fusion) will affect the choice of optical solutions.

## 3. Industry Final Deduction

### Mobile phone optics: stock game, the strong will always be strong

Global smartphone shipments have peaked, and future growth will come from "upscaling" rather than "increasing volume" - each phone uses better lenses, more cameras, and higher-end optical solutions.

In this scenario:
- Largan continues to dominate the high-end lens market
- Sunny's position in the mid-to-high-end full-chain market is further consolidated
- Qiu Titanium, OFILM, etc. face greater profit pressure in the mid- to low-end market
- Industry concentration continues to increase

Prediction: After 5 years, the mobile phone optics market will basically be divided by Sunny + Largan, with more than 70% of the profit pool.

### Automotive optics: rapid growth, Sunny leads the way

The penetration of autonomous driving drives the continued increase in the number of in-vehicle cameras:
- L2 level requires 5-8 cameras
- L3 level requires 8-12 cameras
- L4 level may require 12-16 cameras + lidar

Coupled with pixel upgrades (from 2MP to 8MP to 17MP) and new applications such as automotive AR-HUD, the TAM of automotive optics is rapidly expanding.

Prediction: Sunny is expected to maintain a market share of 30%+ in the field of automotive optics. If the spin-off and listing is successful, the automotive business may reach a revenue scale of 15-20 billion within 5 years.

### AR/XR optics: Stars and sea, but the road is long and difficult

AR glasses are the biggest "variable" in the optical industry. If AR glasses really become a substitute (or an important supplement) for mobile phones, optical demand will explode - the value of the optical modules of AR glasses may be 3-5 times that of mobile phones.

Sunny’s layout:
- Invested in Goertek Optics and entered the supply chain of brands such as Meta
- Self-developed core technologies such as diffraction optical waveguide and light engine
- CEO Wang Wenjie predicts that AI glasses shipments will reach 50 million units within 5 years

However, the risks that must be acknowledged are: the technical route of AR glasses is still uncertain (optical waveguide, Birdbath, and free-form surfaces each have their own advantages and disadvantages), and the time when the industry will break out is difficult to predict. Sunny himself also publicly pointed out the "fatal flaw" of current smart glasses at the 2025 Shenzhen Exhibition - this honesty is worthy of recognition, but it also shows that there is still a distance from large-scale mass production.

Prediction: The explosion of AR optics may be from 2028 to 2030. Sunny is expected to become one of the core suppliers, but its contribution will be limited in the short term (2026-2027).

## 4. Industrial chain position and bargaining power

Sunny's position in the industrial chain determines its bargaining power - it is in the middle layer, with optical glass/plastic raw materials upstream and brand owners downstream.

**Bargaining power against upstream: ★★★★☆**
Sunny is one of the world's largest purchasers of optical lenses and has strong bargaining power over raw material suppliers.

**Bargaining power for downstream: ★★★☆☆**
Brand owners (Huawei, Xiaomi, car companies) have stronger bargaining power than Sunny. Although Sunny has advantages in technology and scale, brand owners have multiple suppliers to choose from (at least at the module level), and brand owners themselves are also facing cost pressures, which will be transmitted upstream.

**Net conclusion:** Sunny is not a company with strong pricing power, but it can partially break through the limitations of pricing power through technology upgrades (from low-end to high-end) and business expansion (from mobile phones to vehicles, AR).

## 5. Disruptive Risks

### 1. Computational photography replaces optical upgrades?

Advances in AI algorithms may reduce the need for physical optics upgrades. If software algorithms can achieve high-end effects on low-end lenses, then the space for optical hardware upgrades will be squeezed.

**Assessment:** Short-term risks are limited. Computational photography is a "supplement" to optics rather than a "replacement" - no matter how good the algorithm is, it requires a good optical foundation. But we do need to pay attention to the impact of AI on the pace of optical upgrades in the long term.

### 2. Will smartphones be replaced by new terminals?

If AR glasses really replace mobile phones, the mobile phone optics market will shrink. But this replacement process may take more than 10 years, and the AR glasses themselves require more and better optical components - for Sunny, it may be "lost in the east and gained in the old".

### 3. Brand owners develop their own optics?

Brand owners such as Apple and Huawei have been strengthening their self-research capabilities. However, the threshold for optical manufacturing is very high. Brand owners are more likely to develop their own design solutions and commission OEM manufacturing, rather than fully building their own optical production lines.

**Overall Rating: ★★★★☆ (3.8/5)**

From an industry perspective, optics is a "good industry" - downstream demand continues to expand, technical barriers are rising, and industry concentration is increasing. Sunny is in one of the best positions in this good industry. The main uncertainties come from the short-term fluctuations in the mobile phone industry and the ramp-up time of AR/XR. If we use Munger's thinking framework - "fishing where there are many fish" - the optical industry is a pond with more fish, and Sunny is the largest fishing boat in this pond.
