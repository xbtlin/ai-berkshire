# MINISO (9896.HK) business model analysis

> Analysis dimensions: business model essence, user value, product philosophy, long-term competition barriers
> Core question: What is the nature of this business? Why do users pay? Is the moat sustainable?

---

## 1. The essence of business: what exactly is being done

The business nature of Miniso has undergone a fundamental change.

**The first phase (2013-2019): "Ultimate cost-effective" channel business. ** The core logic is to use large-scale procurement + efficient supply chain to sell "good-looking" lifestyle department stores to consumers at extremely low prices. It is essentially a channel efficiency business - making money from supply chain integration. Miniso at this stage is more like an upgraded version of a "two-yuan store", using Japanese aesthetic packaging + shopping mall channels to sell low-priced department stores with a sense of brand.

**The second phase (2020 to present): "IP design retail" brand business. ** Miniso is shifting from "selling cheap goods" to "selling IP experience". By the end of 2025, the company has established cooperation with more than 150 world-renowned IPs such as Disney, Sanrio, and Pokémon. The global IP product sales have exceeded 10 billion yuan, and more than 10,000 new IP products are launched every year. In the first quarter of 2024, IP product sales accounted for 26%, and the proportion of overseas IP increased significantly to more than 40% year-on-year.

The management's goal is even more radical: increasing the proportion of IP sales to 50%, and even reaching more than 80% in the future.

**What does this shift mean? ** The problem with cost-effective channel business is that the barriers are not deep enough - Pinduoduo, 1688, and Temu can do it. As for IP design retail, if it succeeds, the barriers will be much deeper: IP licensing relationships, design capabilities, and offline experience scenarios, these cannot be copied by spending money.

### Rating: ★★★★☆

The transformation of the business model from "channel efficiency" to "IP brand" is in the right direction, but the transformation is still in progress, and whether it can truly establish a brand premium needs to be verified.

---

## 2. User value: why consumers continue to pay

To understand the user value of Miniso, we need to look at the domestic and overseas markets.

**Domestic market:** Miniso’s core users are young women aged 18-35. What they buy is not just a water cup or a doll, but "obtaining the emotional value brought by IP at an acceptable price." A Sanrio co-branded cup sells for 29.9 yuan. What consumers get is not only the use value of the cup itself, but also the emotional satisfaction of "owning a lovely IP product". This kind of consumption is essentially "light luxury substitution" - spending very little money to satisfy similar emotional needs.

**Overseas markets:** Overseas, the user value of Miniso is even more prominent. Taking the US market as an example, similar IP co-branded products may sell for US$20-30 in Disney stores, while Miniso products sell for US$5-10. This value proposition of "IP equality" is far more attractive overseas than at home. In 2025, U.S. GMV will grow by more than 50% year-on-year, and from January to February 2026, same-store sales growth in the North American market will reach mid-to-high double digits, verifying this logic.

**Hidden concerns about user stickiness:** The consumption of Miniso products is "low decision-making cost + randomness". Consumers rarely make a special trip to Miniso to shop, but more often "pass by + browse + buy casually". This means that store location (traffic in core business districts) is extremely important, but users’ loyalty to the brand is relatively limited.

### Rating: ★★★★☆

The emotional value brought by IP does exist, and the value proposition in overseas markets is particularly clear. However, user stickiness is weak and consumption is highly random, which are structural characteristics that need attention.

## 3. "711" product philosophy and supply chain moat

Miniso's product development follows the "711 concept": screening from a creative library of more than 10,000 product solutions, launching about 100 new SKUs every seven days, and maintaining a frequency of about 550 new SKUs every month.

**Core advantages of supply chain:**

1. **Large-scale direct procurement:** Cooperate with suppliers in the form of OEM/ODM, large-scale direct procurement eliminates price increases in intermediate links, and has terminal pricing power.
2. **Digital management:** Digital tools such as product life cycle management systems, intelligent inventory replenishment modules, and online quality control systems organically combine designers, product managers, and suppliers to minimize the product development cycle.
3. **Geographical concentration advantage:** More than 80% of the supply chain is located in China, and more than 50% of the supply chain customers are in the Pearl River Delta, which facilitates management and response.
4. **IP Authorization Network:** Long-term cooperative relationships with 150+ world-renowned IPs cannot be established overnight.

**Is this moat deep enough? **

Frankly speaking, the supply chain advantage is the most solid competitive barrier for Miniso. Globally, there are very few companies that can achieve the three points of "IP licensing breadth + product introduction speed + price competitiveness" at the same time. However, it should be noted that this barrier is more of an "operational barrier" than a "brand barrier" - if one day consumers no longer pay for IP, the value of the supply chain advantage will also be reduced.

### Rating: ★★★★☆

The supply chain system is a real competitive advantage and is difficult to replicate in the short term. But whether it can ultimately be transformed into brand barriers depends on the continued success of the IP strategy.

## 4. Franchise model: the double-edged sword of light assets

MINISO adopts the "MINISO partner model" - franchisees pay to open the store, and the headquarters is responsible for site selection, decoration, supply, and operation management. The benefits of this model are obvious:

**Advantages:**
- Asset-light expansion, high capital efficiency, does not occupy a large amount of own funds
- Franchisees bear rent and labor costs, and the headquarters focuses on products and brands
- Strong ability to expand rapidly, reaching 8,485 stores globally by 2025

**Hidden dangers:**
- Franchisees have limited profit margins, and some franchisees report that gross profit is not enough to support operations.
- Overcrowding of stores leads to diversion of customers - a net net closure of 111 stores in China in Q1 of 2025
- Quality control risk - it is difficult to fully guarantee the consistency of service and product quality under the franchise system
- There have been cases in history where overseas agent management was out of control (such as the 2019 South Africa incident)

**A key judgment:** The sustainability of the franchise model depends on whether the franchisees can continue to make money. If Miniso’s IP upgrade and store upgrade (from 200 square meters to 400-600 square meters) can significantly increase the output of a single store, and franchisees can make money, this model can continue. On the contrary, if the investment caused by the upgrade increases but the output fails to increase simultaneously, problems may arise in the franchise system.

### Rating: ★★★☆☆

The asset-light model has high capital efficiency, but the protection of franchisees’ interests and the consistency of quality control are long-term hidden dangers. The signal of net store closures in China deserves vigilance.

## 5. Globalization: The biggest growth story

The globalization of Miniso is currently the most attractive growth logic:

**Data speaks:**
- There will be 3,213 overseas stores in 2025, with a net increase of 617, accounting for the vast majority of new stores.
- US stores will nearly double from 238 in 2024 to 503
- Total overseas revenue increased by 42% year-on-year (2024 data), and the growth rate far exceeded that of domestic revenue
- Same-store sales growth in the North American market reaches mid-to-high double digits (Q1 2026)

**Structural advantages of globalization:**
- There is huge room for brand premium in overseas markets, and the selling price and gross profit margin of the same product overseas are significantly higher than domestically.
- The "Chinese supply chain + global IP + localized operations" model has been verified in multiple markets
- Construct a seven-layer store matrix (MINISO LAND, potential store, theme store, flagship store, regular store, MINISO GO, pop-up store) with obvious differentiation

**Risks of Globalization:**
- Differences in regulations, culture, and consumption habits in different markets bring operational complexity
- Geopolitical risks, especially the potential impact of Sino-US relations on the US market
- Management risks during the transition period from agency model to direct operation model in overseas markets

### Rating: ★★★★★

Globalization is the clearest growth engine for Miniso, and the brand premium and growth rate in overseas markets are impressive. This is the core support of investment logic.

## 6. General evaluation of business model

| Dimensions | Scores | Core Judgments |
|------|------|----------|
| The nature of business | ★★★★☆ | The transformation from channel business to IP brand business is in the right direction, but has not yet been fully verified |
| User value | ★★★★☆ | The emotional value of IP is real, especially overseas, but user stickiness is weak |
| Supply chain moat | ★★★★☆ | The most solid barrier to competition, difficult to replicate in the short term |
| Franchise model | ★★★☆☆ | High capital efficiency, but the protection of franchisees’ interests is a long-term hidden danger |
| Globalization potential | ★★★★★ | The biggest highlight, outstanding overseas growth rate and premium space |
| **Comprehensive Rating** | **★★★★☆** | **The prototype of a good business, but it still takes time to verify whether the transformation can be truly successful** |

**Summary in one sentence:** Miniso is trying to transform from a channel provider that "earns price differences by relying on the supply chain" to a retail brand that "earns brand premiums by relying on IP." Globalization is the biggest engine of growth, and supply chain is the deepest moat. The key suspense is whether the IP strategy can truly build brand loyalty, not just a short-lived traffic dividend.
