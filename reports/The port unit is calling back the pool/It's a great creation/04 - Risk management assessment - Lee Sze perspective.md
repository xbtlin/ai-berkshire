# I. I. Ye Kufui: Picture by Founder

# Background and character

Ye was born in 1977 in Hubei, a family-poor industry. The "Ooh-O" ring was created in 2004 in Guangzhou, with experience in low-cost retailing and union management.

** Several key features:**

1. **The strong executive power and expansion desire.** From its creation in 2013 to 2025, 8,485 global stores were created, with an average annual net increase of 700 plus stores, a rate of expansion that is extremely rare in the global retail industry.

2. ** Good at catching consumption trends.** From "Oh, my" jewelry business to "Big Life" to "Turning the Best" to "Purpose the Best" strategy, Ye has shown sensitivity to consumption trends.

3. ** ambition and adventure coexists.** The 62.70 billion dollar buy-in of 29.4% of the Infinity Supermarket is a typical "big bet" decision. In the case of the high growth of the leading outstandings, a large share of capital is being allocated to a lost traditional supermarket, which suggests that the ambition of the wealthy nation is not limited to the creation of the good itself, but rather to the building of a larger retail empire.

# # Management capacity assessment

** Do the right thing:**
- The IP strategic shift captures the general trend of rising consumption
- The overall pace of globalization expansion is being properly controlled and the ratio of camps abroad is increasing gradually
- Continuous increase in supply chain digitization inputs and lead the business efficiency industry
- A greater sense of shareholder return, with 65.8 per cent of adjusted net profits returned in 2025

**Suspicious decision-making:**
- The purchase of Yonghui Supermarket – the biggest controversy. The $6.27 billion, a highly leveraged traditional supermarket that buys a loss, has little synergy with the core of the best-known talent (IP design retail, globalizing light assets). The explanation for Ye is "Build Underline Retail Ecology," but this logic is not yet proven.
- TOP TOY is listed - it's been pushing for fragmentation in the immediate period of rapid growth in TOP TOY, probably to supplement the cash flow that was consumed by the acquisition of Yonghui by the best. Fragmentation is not necessarily bad in itself, but the motive is to be vigilant if it is "money short" rather than "strategic."
- The door shop "Cocking for Birds" — Management announced that it would close and reopen 80% of the global door, from small to 400-600 square meters of thematic insulation space. This is an ambitious strategy, with high risks for implementation.

# Rating: # (weak)

Ye was a very powerful founder of IP strategy and globalization, and was proactive. But the acquisition of Yonghui exposed his risk-taking tendencies in capital allocation and the risk of investing in "exceeding the circle of competence."

# III. List of core risks

# Risk one: Never-fail Supermarket - Time bomb on balance sheet # (high risk)

**Quantitative impact:**
- A deficit of about 813 million yuan in 2025 was recognized
- Loans jumped from >600 million to $7.51 billion.
- Asset and liability ratio increased from 42.85 per cent to 62.23 per cent
- Interest spending has increased significantly, eroding profits

** Worst-case scenario:** Infinity continues to lose, and the good will requires additional investment or security for the Infinity debt. The balance sheet has deteriorated further, affecting the source of finance and shareholder returns for major expansion.

** Mitigation factor: ** ** Q1 Etreunes net profit of 287 million yuan in 2026 (+94.4% year-on-year), although the base is low but in a positive direction. If Yonghui achieves a profit by 2026-2027, the delay in the creation of the good will be phased out.

# Risk two: Geopolitics and overseas operating risks # (high risk)

The US is the most important overseas market, with more than 57% of the outstanding profits in 2025. The uncertainty of US-China relations poses a systemic risk:
- Potential tariff escalation could push costs up.
- Brand reputational risk.
- Differences in legislation and consumer protection in different countries

** Mitigation factors:** low political sensitivity of product groups (households) of excellence and a higher degree of fragmentation of the global landscape (no overdependence on a single market).

# Risk three: Slower growth in the domestic market # (medium risk)

In 2025, Q1 domestic shops were closed, with the domestic market growing at a significantly lower rate (16.8%) than abroad (29.3%). If China continues to experience low consumption, the best is likely to be faced with:
- The Allieds are out of the way.
- The sales with the store are down.
- The doorstep is not getting the same payoff as expected.

** Mitigating factors:** China’s market GMV grew by more than 25% in January-February 2026, and sales with the same shop have returned to positive growth. If the upgrading strategy is successful, the increase in single shop output could offset the decline in the number of shops.

# Risk IV: IP Strategy Implementation Risk <unk> (Medium Risk)

The management targets raise the IP ratio to 50% or more, while upgrading 80% of the stores to 400-600 m2 of thematic space. These targets are ambitious, but the implementation risks are equally high:
- IP authorized costs may increase with IP ratio
- The rent and operating costs of large shops are much higher than those of small shops
- The output ratio of inputs may deteriorate if the upgraded premises are not functioning as expected

# Risk five: Valuation trap risk # (lower risk)

The famously good looks cheap (adjusted PE ~ 14x), but "favour" may be a false image if future increases are lower than market expectations or if Eunhui continues to drag profits. However, given the company's free cash flow and shareholder return policies, the mere probability of valuation traps is low.
