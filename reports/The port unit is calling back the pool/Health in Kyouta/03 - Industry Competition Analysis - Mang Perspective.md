# JD Health (6618.HK) - Industry Competition Analysis

> Analysis dimensions: industry structure, competition landscape, moat evolution, long-term industry trends

## 1. Industry panorama

### 1.1 China’s Internet Medical + Pharmaceutical E-commerce Market

JD Health spans two overlapping markets:

| Market | Size in 2025 | Growth rate |
|------|-----------|------|
| Pharmaceutical e-commerce | ~300 billion | 15-20% |
| Online diagnosis and treatment | ~186.4 billion | 25-35% |
| Digital Health Management | ~100 billion | 20%+ |

Taken together, the "Internet + Health" track where JD Health is located is a 500 billion+ market and maintains a growth rate of 20%+. This is a huge and rapidly expanding field.

### 1.2 The stage of the industry

The pharmaceutical e-commerce industry is transforming from "quantitative expansion" to "qualitative improvement":

- **2015-2020**: The industry start-up period, solving the problem of "can I buy medicine online?"
- **2020-2023**: Rapid growth period, catalyzed by the epidemic + policy liberalization
- **2023-2026**: A period of deepening competition, shifting from price competition to service competition
- **2026+**: Ecological integration period, "medicine + medicine + insurance + management" integration

JD Health is in a favorable position in the "deepening competition period" - leading in scale and profits are beginning to be released.

### 1.3 Industrial chain positioning

```
Pharmaceutical companies → Distributors → [JD Health] → Consumers/Patients
                         ↕
               Online doctor ← AI assistance
                         ↕
               Health management/chronic disease services
```

The uniqueness of JD Health is that it plays the dual role of "retail terminal" and "medical service entrance" at the same time.

## 2. Competitive landscape

### 2.1 The first echelon: three major Internet health platforms

| Company | 2025 Revenue | Core Model | Core Barriers |
|------|-----------|----------|----------|
| JD Health | 73.4 billion | Self-operated + platform + medical care | Logistics + quality trust |
| Alibaba Health | 30.6 billion | Self-operation + platform + digitalization | Traffic ecology + data |
| Meituan buys medicine | ~20 billion+ | Instant delivery + platform | 30-minute delivery + rider network |

### 2.2 Evolution of the competitive landscape

**Current Pattern**: JD Health leads Alibaba Health with more than 2 times revenue, and the industry pattern is relatively clear.

**But the landscape is changing**:
- Meituan's rapid rise in buying drugs has created unique competitiveness in the "immediate" scenario
- Douyin e-commerce begins to expand into pharmaceutical categories
- Traditional chain pharmacies (Dashenlin, Laobaixing, etc.) accelerate their online transformation

### 2.3 Comparison of three competition models

| Mode | Representative | Advantage Scenario | Disadvantage |
|------|------|----------|------|
| Warehousing and distribution model | JD Health | Planned drug purchase, chronic disease medication | Insufficient immediacy |
| Traffic platform | Ali Health | Rich varieties, price comparison | Poor experience consistency |
| Instant delivery | Meituan buys medicine | Acute disease medication, temporary demand | Limited SKU, weak cold chain |

No one model fits all scenarios. The competition between the three is more likely to be a "scenario division of labor" rather than a "winner takes all" one.

### 2.4 JD Health’s differentiated competitiveness

**Advantages over Alibaba Health:**
1. Logistics experience crushing (self-built vs third-party)
2. Stronger trust in quality (JD.com’s self-operated mind)
3. Revenue scale is 2.4 times
4. Higher profit margin (8.9% vs 6.4%)

**Advantages over Meituan:**
1. More complete SKUs (10 million+ vs Meituan partner pharmacies limited)
2. Cold chain capabilities (biological drugs/vaccines, etc.)
3. Deeper medical services (an average of 500,000 consultations per day)
4. Data accumulation lasts longer

**Disadvantages relative to Meituan:**
1. Insufficient immediacy (next day delivery vs 30 minutes)
2. Meituan’s local life traffic is more frequent
3. Meituan rider costs have been shared by the food delivery business

## 3. In-depth analysis of moat

### 3.1 Moat composition

| Moat Type | Strength | Description |
|-----------|------|------|
| Logistics infrastructure | ★★★★★ | The strongest barrier, difficult to replicate after ten years of investment |
| Quality Trust | ★★★★☆ | "Jingdong = Authentic" Mental Strength |
| Economies of scale | ★★★★☆ | 73.4 billion revenue brings cost advantages |
| Data/AI | ★★★☆☆ | Under construction, flywheel not yet formed |
| User stickiness | ★★★★☆ | PLUS membership + health profile + habits |
| Doctor Network | ★★★☆☆ | Certain accumulation but not exclusive |

### 3.2 The strength of the moat

**The strongest barrier - logistics:**
JD Logistics’ pharmaceutical warehousing and distribution system is the result of more than ten years and tens of billions of investments. To copy this system, competitors need to:
- Hundreds of distributed drug warehouses
- National cold chain network
- GSP certification for pharmaceutical warehousing
- Tens of thousands of trained drug delivery personnel

This is not a problem that can be solved in the short term by burning money.

**The most likely barrier to be breached—immediacy:**
Meituan’s 30-minute delivery model creates a scenario that JD’s logistics system cannot cover. In the field of medicines for acute diseases (such as cold medicines, antipyretics, and analgesics), "delivery in 30 minutes" has an absolute user experience advantage over "delivery the next day."

### 3.3 Future trends of moats

**The deepening moat:**
- Scale effects continue to increase with revenue growth
- Data accumulation makes AI recommendations more and more accurate
- PLUS membership system enhances user locking

**Moats facing erosion:**
- Meituan’s instant delivery diversion in acute medication scenarios
- Alibaba may establish differentiation in B-side industrial services
- O2O counterattack of traditional pharmacy chains

## 4. Long-term trends in the industry

### 4.1 Deterministic trend

1. **Pharmaceutical e-commerce penetration rate continues to increase**: from 15% to over 30%
2. **The outflow of prescription drugs is accelerating**: from hospital to online + retail
3. **AI is deeply integrated into the entire diagnosis and treatment process**: pre-diagnosis screening → assistance during diagnosis → post-diagnosis management
4. **Payment method changes**: Online payment of medical insurance is gradually opened up
5. **Integration of "medicine + medicine + storage + management"**: Evolution from selling medicine to health management

### 4.2 Uncertainty factors

1. **The final competitive landscape**: Is it a duopoly or a tripartite?
2. **How big can Meituan’s pharmaceutical business be? **
3. Will **AI fundamentally change the online diagnosis and treatment model? **
4. **Timetable for full access to online medical insurance payment**
5. **Whether JD Group’s strategy continues to support the health business**

### 4.3 Industry Final Deduction

**Possible ending:**
- JD Health: occupying the track of "quality + planned drug purchase + in-depth medical services"
- Meituan buys medicine: occupying the track of "immediacy + emergency medication + local life"
- Alibaba Health: Occupying the track of "Platform Connection + Industrial Digitalization + Pharmaceutical Enterprise Services"

If this endgame comes true, each of the three companies will have room for survival, but JD Health may have the largest revenue scale and the highest profit margins.

## 5. JD Health’s long-term positioning

### 5.1 The evolutionary path from "selling medicine" to "managing health"

```
Current: Selling medicine (product GMV driven)
 ↓
Mid-term: integration of medicine + medicine (inquiry + prescription + medication + follow-up)
 ↓
Long term: health management platform (data-driven full life cycle health management)
```

If JD Health can evolve along this path:
- Profit margin will increase from 9% to 15%+
- User stickiness will be greatly enhanced (from "only come when buying medicine" to "daily health management")
- Valuation will be revalued from "e-commerce" (15-25x) to "health technology" (30-40x)

But the uncertainty of this path is also high - it requires a breakthrough in AI technology, integration of doctor resources, and an open payment system.
