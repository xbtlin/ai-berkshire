#JD Health (6618.HK)—Business Model Analysis

> Analysis dimensions: nature of business model, user value, competition barriers, long-term economic characteristics

## 1. What is the essence of business?

The core business of JD Health is "selling medicine + providing Internet medical services using JD's method".

Business composition:
- **Commodity revenue** (82.9%, 60.885 billion): Sales of self-operated drugs and health products by JD Pharmacy
- **Service revenue** (17.1%, 12.557 billion): platform commission, advertising, online diagnosis and treatment, health management, etc.

This is a business model that "takes self-operated e-commerce as the base and medical services as an extension". The underlying logic is: first use "selling medicine" to obtain users and data, and then use "medical services" to improve stickiness and monetization efficiency.

### Relationship with JD Retail

JD Health is similar to a high-growth business that extends from core competencies to JD Group.

Inheritance of core competencies:
- **Logistics Network**: JD Logistics’ warehouse and distribution system is the core infrastructure of JD Health
- **Quality Trust**: "JD.com self-operated = quality assurance" mentality extends to medicines
- **Technology Platform**: Reuse of e-commerce technology + AI capabilities
- **User Base**: Natural conversion of JD.com’s hundreds of millions of active users

## 2. User value analysis

### Value to consumers/patients

1. **Quality certainty**: JD.com’s self-operated quality endorsement is extremely valuable in the pharmaceutical field (consumers are most afraid of buying fake medicines)
2. **Logistics certainty**: JD Logistics’ delivery speed and cold chain capabilities, medicines can be delivered the next day (some are delivered on the same day)
3. **Rich Variety**: Over 10 million SKUs, covering prescription drugs, OTC, health products, medical devices, etc.
4. **Convenience of medical services**: The average daily consultation volume exceeds 500,000, achieving one-stop "consultation + prescription + drug purchase + delivery"

**User stickiness evaluation: ★★★★☆**

JD Health’s user stickiness is the strongest among pharmaceutical e-commerce companies for the following reasons:
1. **Quality trust inertia**: Once consumers form the mindset of "JD.com = genuine", they will not easily switch to a platform they do not trust.
2. **Logistics experience binding**: It is difficult for users who are accustomed to next-day delivery to accept the 2-3 day delivery of other platforms.
3. **Health data accumulation**: consultation records + medication history form a personal health file, switching means data loss
4. **PLUS membership system**: The health rights of JD.com PLUS members increase switching costs

But be warned: this stickiness is not unbreakable. Meituan's "30-minute delivery" provides stronger immediate value in acute medication scenarios, which is a shortcoming of JD.com.

### Value to upstream (pharmaceutical companies)

1. **Omni-channel distribution**: Directly reaching end consumers online, supplementing traditional distribution channels
2. **Data Insight**: Accurate user portraits and consumption behavior data
3. **Marketing Services**: Platform advertising and precise recommendations
4. **Full-link service**: Full-link support from brand marketing to patient management

## 3. Barriers to competition (moat)

### 3.1 Logistics supply chain barriers ★★★★★

This is the core moat of JD Health and the biggest difference from its competitors:

- **Self-built warehousing**: a nationwide dedicated pharmaceutical warehousing network
- **Cold chain capabilities**: For pharmaceuticals such as biological products and vaccines that require cold chain distribution, JD.com has unique advantages
- **Delivery time**: Self-operated medicines are delivered the next day (same day delivery in some areas)
- **Quality Control**: Full temperature control and quality traceability from warehousing to distribution
- **GSP Compliance**: Full set of pharmaceutical quality management practices certification

This set of logistics infrastructure is the result of more than ten years and tens of billions of investments. Alibaba Health relies on third-party logistics, and Meituan relies on its rider network—neither can compete with JD.com in the field of professional pharmaceutical warehousing and cold chain.

### 3.2 Quality trust barrier ★★★★☆

"Jingdong Self-operated" represents "guaranteed authenticity" in the minds of consumers. In the field of pharmaceuticals, this trust is particularly important:
- Medicines are special commodities, and consumers have zero tolerance for counterfeiting.
- Compared with third-party platforms/small pharmacies, JD.com’s self-operated ones have higher trust
- This kind of quality mentality requires years of consistent experience to build up, and it is difficult to be taken away by competitors in the short term.

### 3.3 Barriers to Economies of Scale ★★★★☆

Advantages brought by the revenue scale of 73.4 billion:
- Stronger bargaining power with upstream pharmaceutical companies (lower procurement costs)
- Lower unit logistics costs (scale dilutes fixed costs)
- Lower marginal cost of technology and system investment
- More complete SKUs (10 million+) to meet long-tail needs and increase users’ transaction rate within the platform

### 3.4 Data and AI Barriers ★★★☆☆

- Health data accumulation of 200 million+ active users
- Training data with daily average of 500,000+ consultation records
- AI-assisted diagnosis and treatment, intelligent medication recommendation and other capabilities are under construction

However, this barrier is still in the construction stage and has not yet formed a true "data flywheel" - that is, the positive cycle of more data → better products → more users → more data.

### 3.5 Doctors and Service Barriers ★★★☆☆

- Over 50,000 own/external partner physicians
- Internet hospital license
- "Pharmaceutical + medical + examination" integrated service

However, the doctor resources for online consultation are not exclusive—doctors can practice on multiple platforms at the same time.

## 4. Economic characteristics of business model

### Revenue Predictability ★★★★☆

- The increasing penetration rate of pharmaceutical e-commerce is a definite trend
- JD Health’s market share steadily increases
- In the self-operated model, income is directly linked to GMV, making it more predictable.
- Pharmaceutical consumption is less affected by the economic cycle (rigid demand attribute)

### Profit rate characteristics

| Indicators | 2024 | 2025 | Trends |
|------|--------|--------|------|
| Gross profit margin | ~23% | ~24.8% | Continuous improvement |
| Operating Margin | ~2.5% | ~5.2% | Significant improvement |
| Non-IFRS net profit margin | ~8.2% | ~8.9% | Steady improvement |

Core drivers of margin improvement:
1. Product structure optimization (increased proportion of high-gross profit categories)
2. Scale effect brings down expense ratio
3. The proportion of service revenue increases (the profit margin is much higher than that of self-operated goods)

### Profit ceiling analysis

Referring to JD Group's overall net profit margin (~4%), JD Health's net profit margin of 8.9% has surpassed the parent company. The core reason is that the health business is supplemented by high-margin service revenue.

If the proportion of service revenue increases from 17% to 25-30%, the overall net profit rate is expected to reach 10-12%.

### Capital efficiency ★★★☆☆

ROE is about 6%, which is low. Reason:
1. The company is still in a period of scale expansion and profit margins are still climbing.
2. There is too much cash on hand after the IPO financing, which reduces the efficiency of using net assets.
3. The self-operated model requires more working capital (inventory + receivables)

But with profit margins improving and potential buyback/dividend plans starting, ROE has a clear path toward 10-15%.

### Free Cash Flow ★★★★☆

Operating profit in 2025 is 3.8 billion (a year-on-year increase of 158%), with strong cash flow generation capabilities. Although the self-operated model requires inventory investment, the accounts payable cycle can provide a working capital buffer. Free cash flow will continue to improve as we scale.

## 5. Core questioning from Duan Yongping’s perspective

**"Is the moat of this business deep enough?"**

JD Health’s moat is much deeper than Alibaba Health’s, and it is also deeper than Meituan’s barriers in the pharmaceutical field:
- Logistics barriers are real "heavy investment, difficult to copy" barriers - they cannot be bypassed by relying on subsidies and operational skills.
- Quality trust requires years of consistent experience and accumulation and cannot be built overnight.
- But in the instant delivery scenario, JD.com's "next-day delivery" is not as good as Meituan's "30-minute delivery"

Overall, the moat depth is the strongest in China's Internet medical field.

**"What will happen in ten years?"**

Two paths:

1. **Evolution path (60% probability)**: Evolve from "selling medicine" to "managing health" - integrating consultation + medication + testing + insurance to form a closed loop of health management. If realized, the profit margin will increase to 12%+, user stickiness will be greatly enhanced, and the valuation will be revalued from "e-commerce" to "health technology".

2. **Maintain path (probability 40%)**: Maintain the positioning of "online pharmacy + online consultation", grow at a rate of 15-20%, and stabilize the net profit rate at 8-10%. It's a good but not amazing business.

**"Can management create value for shareholders?"**

In the Jin Enlin era (2021-2025), revenue will increase from 30.7 billion to 73.4 billion, and the execution ability has been verified. The new CEO Cao Dong comes from the financial system and may focus more on efficiency and profits - which may be a good thing for the current stage (from "chasing growth" to "chasing profits").

**"Is this price a good deal?"**

23 times PE corresponds to 26% revenue growth and 36% profit growth, with a PEG of 0.64. This pricing provides a reasonable margin of safety in the current market environment. Even if growth slows to 15-18%, valuations are supported.

JD Health is the company with the most solid business model in the pharmaceutical e-commerce track. It has a substantial moat (not a false barrier maintained by subsidies), strong and accelerating growth, and a reasonable valuation. The core focus is the profit margin ceiling and the possibility of evolving into a "health management platform". Among the four Hong Kong-listed medical and health companies, its business model has the best quality.
