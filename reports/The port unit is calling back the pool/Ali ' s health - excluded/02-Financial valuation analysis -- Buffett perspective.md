> Analysis dimensions: financial quality, profitability, capital allocation, valuation rationality

| Sector | Proportion | Growth rate | Gross profit margin |
|------|------|------|--------|

Core contradiction: Revenue mainly comes from low-margin self-operated business, while the proportion of high-margin platform business and service business is still low.

## 2. In-depth analysis of profitability

### 2.1 Drivers of profit margin improvement

FY2025 net profit increased by 62% (adjusted growth of 36%), and the profit improvement came from:

1. **Gross profit margin increase** (22% → 24%): Product structure is optimized, and the proportion of high-gross profit categories increases
2. **Expense Ratio Optimization**: Improve operational efficiency and reduce marketing expense ratio
3. **Scale effect**: fixed cost dilution caused by 13% revenue growth

### 2.2 Analysis of profit margin ceiling

How high can Alibaba Health's profit margins reach? Reference benchmark:

| Company | Net Profit Margin | Model |
|------|--------|------|
| JD Health (2025) | ~8.9% | Self-operated + platform |
| Ali Health (FY2025) | ~6.4% | Self-operated + platform |
| Meituan (in-store + takeaway) | ~8-10% | Platform-based |
| JD.com (overall) | ~4-5% | Mainly self-operated |

If Alibaba Health's model structure tilts towards "more platforms + services", the net profit margin is expected to increase from 6.4% to 8-10%. But if we continue to be self-operated, the ceiling may be around 7-8%.

### 2.3 ROE decomposition

| Factor | Value | Evaluation |
|------|------|------|
| Net profit margin | ~6.4% | Medium to low |
| Asset Turnover | ~1.5x | Medium |
| Equity Multiplier | ~1.3x | Low Leverage |
| ROE | ~8% | Low |

Reasons for low ROE:
1. Net profit margin is in the climbing stage
2. Low-leverage operations (Internet companies usually do not use leverage)
3. There is a lot of cash in the account, which reduces the efficiency of asset use.

### 2.4 Profit Quality

**Positive Signs:**
- The difference between adjusted profit and reported profit is narrowing (more reflecting true profitability)
- Operating cash flow continues to be positive and improving
- No large one-time gains or impairments

**Issues needing attention:**
- There is still a certain amount of share-based payments (the main source of adjustments)
- Efficiency and price drop risk of self-operated inventory management
- Whether the pricing of related-party transactions with Alibaba Group is fair

## 3. Capital allocation analysis

### 3.1 R&D and technology investment

As an Internet health company, Alibaba Health’s technology investment is mainly used for:
- Development of AI-assisted diagnosis and treatment system
- Supply chain management system optimization
- User experience and personalized recommendations
- Digital marketing tools

Technology investment accounts for about 3-4% of revenue, which is not high among Internet companies.

### 3.2 Mergers, Acquisitions and Investments

Important investments/cooperations in recent years:
- Reached digital patient support cooperation with international pharmaceutical companies such as Eli Lilly and AstraZeneca
- Layout in the field of chronic disease management
- Small investment in medical services

The overall strategy is asset-light and does not pursue large-scale mergers and acquisitions.

### 3.3 Shareholder Return

The company currently does not pay dividends, and profits are mainly used for reinvestment and business expansion. This is understandable for a company in the growth stage, but no dividends are paid at 39 times PE, which means that investment returns completely rely on capital appreciation.

## 4. Valuation analysis

### 4.1 Relative valuation

| Company | PE (TTM) | PS | Growth rate | Net profit margin |
|------|-----------|-----|------|--------|
| Ali Health | ~39x | ~2.5x | +13% | ~6.4% |
| JD Health | ~23x | ~2.0x | +26% | ~8.9% |
| Ping An Good Doctor | Loss | ~3x | — | Negative |

**Key findings: Alibaba Health’s PE (39x) is higher than JD Health (23x), but its growth rate and profit margin are lower than JD Health. **

This means:
1. The market may have overestimated Alibaba Health’s growth potential
2. Or the market has given an extra premium to "Alibaba Ecosystem"
3. Or Alibaba Health’s valuation bubble is larger than JD Health’s

### 4.2 Historical valuation range

Alibaba Health's historical PE has fluctuated greatly (from 50x to 100x+ to the current 39x), reflecting the market's changing judgment on its profit pace. The current 39x is in a historically low range.

### 4.3 Thoughts on DCF valuation

Assumptions:
- Income CAGR in the next five years: 12-15%
- Net interest rate gradually increases to 8-10%
- Terminal growth rate: 5%
- Discount rate: 10%

Under neutral assumptions, DCF valuation is equivalent to current market capitalization. This means that the current pricing has basically reflected the expectation of "continuous improvement in profit margins", and the margin of safety is limited.

### 4.4 Key Valuation Drivers

1. **Can profit margins continue to increase**: From 6.4% to 8-10%
2. **Can revenue growth be maintained**: Is 13% sustainable?
3. **Can the proportion of platform business be increased**: This is a key variable for profit margins
4. **Valuation convergence/divergence with JD Health**

## 5. Financial risks

### 5.1 Risk of less than expected growth ★★★☆☆
39 times PE has high requirements for growth. If the revenue growth rate drops to single digits, the valuation will face significant pressure.

### 5.2 Intensified competition erodes profit margins ★★★★☆
The competition between JD Health and Meituan in buying drugs may force Alibaba Health to increase subsidies and marketing investment, suppressing the improvement of profit margins.

### 5.3 Alibaba’s ecological dependence risks ★★★☆☆
If Alibaba’s overall e-commerce share continues to decline, Alibaba Health’s traffic base will also be affected.

### 5.4 Policy Risk ★★☆☆☆
Pharmaceutical e-commerce policies are generally liberalizing (online sales of prescription drugs are expanding), and policy risks are relatively controllable.
