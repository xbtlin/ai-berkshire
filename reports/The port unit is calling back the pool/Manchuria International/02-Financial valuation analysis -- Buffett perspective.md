## 2. Profitability analysis

### 2.1 Evolution of profit margins

| Indicators | 2025 | 2024 | 2023 |
|------|--------|--------|--------|
| Gross profit margin | About 19.0% (estimated) | 20.0% | 19.5% |
| Operating profit margin | Approximately 9.3% | 9.3% | 5.6% |
| EBITDA margin | 10%-12% (Fitch estimate) | — | — |

The jump in operating profit margin from 5.6% to 9.3% in 2024 is a qualitative change - mainly due to the North American pork business turning a profit. It will remain at 9.3% in 2025, indicating that the improvement is structural and not one-off.

### 2.2 ROE analysis

WH Group's annual report does not directly disclose ROE, but it can be calculated:
- Profit attributable to shareholders US$1.567 billion
- Shareholders' equity of approximately US$11 billion (estimated based on net leverage of 0.3-0.5 times and total asset size)
- **ROE about 14-15%**

For a traditional food company, an ROE of 15% is a good level. However, it should be noted that the 142% increase in adjusted net profit to US$1.464 billion in 2024 has a low base effect and may return to normal in the future.

> Buffett's standard is "Sustained high ROE is much more important than high ROE in a single year." WH Group's ROE has fluctuated greatly in the past few years (due to losses in North America in 2023) and lacks stability.

**Key findings**: WH Group's Hong Kong stock market value has a discount of approximately 12% to NAV. Before Smithfield went public, the discount was as high as more than 40%. The spinoff does unlock a lot of value, but the holding company discount remains.

## 7. Summary of positive and negative arguments

**Reasons to be bullish (cheap valuation)**:
1. PE is about 10 times, significantly lower than the average 15-20 times of global meat peers.
2. The dividend yield is 6.5%, which is attractive among high-dividend stocks in Hong Kong stocks.
3. Balance sheet is extremely healthy with limited downside risks
4. Smithfield’s business transparency has been greatly improved after the spin-off
5. Beta is only 0.23, stock price fluctuation is extremely low, suitable for defensive allocation

**Reasons to be bearish (value trap)**:
1. China’s meat product sales continue to decline, and the growth engine stalls
2. Pig prices will enter a deep decline in 2026, which may drag down the annual performance.
3. Pork is a cyclical commodity, and low PE may reflect a cycle high rather than a true undervaluation.
4. The holding company’s discount may exist for a long time
5. The founder is 86 years old, management handover risks

> Buffett's "cigarette butt investment" criteria: If a stock is cheap enough to be "a puff of cigarette butts on the ground," with a healthy balance sheet and limited downside risk, then it's worth considering even if it's not a great business. WH Group's valuation level and asset quality do meet this standard. But we need to be wary that the downturn in the pig cycle may make "cheap" even cheaper.
