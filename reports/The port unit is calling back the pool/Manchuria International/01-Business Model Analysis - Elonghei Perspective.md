# 01 Business model analysis: The nature of WH Group’s business

> Core question: **Does the global pork empire of "Shuanghui + Smithfield" have the ability to continue to create value? **
>
> Study date: May 16, 2026 | Currency unit: US dollars (unless otherwise noted)

---

## 1. Business essence: vertical integrator of the global pork industry chain

WH Group's business model can be summarized in one sentence: **The world's largest pork food company, through vertical integration and cross-regional arbitrage, obtains excess profits in the pig cycle**.

Specific disassembly:

**Industry chain coverage**: From pig breeding → slaughtering → pork processing → branded meat products → channel distribution, WH Group is the only pork company in the world that has completed the entire industry chain layout in the three major markets of China, North America and Europe. The group's total revenue in 2025 is US$28.026 billion (+8.0%), of which meat product revenue is US$14.178 billion, accounting for 50.6%, and pork revenue is US$11.383 billion, accounting for 40.6%.

**Three core assets**:
- **Shuanghui Development** (000895.SZ, 70.33% stake held): Asia’s largest meat processing company, with revenue of 59.46 billion yuan and net profit of 5.105 billion yuan in 2025
- **Smithfield Foods** (SFD, Nasdaq, holding about 80% of shares): The largest pork food company in the United States, an independent IPO in January 2025, and record profits in fiscal year 2025
- **European business**: including Italian Argal Group, Poland, Romania and other multinational meat processing assets

**Revenue Geography** (2025):

## 2. Characteristics test of good business

### 2.1 Pricing power: Yes, but limited by the pig cycle

**The meat products business has certain pricing power**. Shuanghui ham sausage is synonymous with "ham sausage = Shuanghui" in the minds of Chinese consumers, and the brand has a strong price premium. The average profit per ton of Shuanghui meat products in 2025 was RMB 4,736, a record high, indicating that despite a 3.8% sales decline, profits can still be maintained through structural upgrades and price increases. Smithfield has also achieved segment profits of over US$1 billion in the U.S. packaged meats field for four consecutive years.

**But the fresh pork business lacks pricing power**. Pork is a bulk agricultural product, and its price is entirely determined by supply and demand. In the first half of 2026, domestic pig prices once fell below 9 yuan/kg, entering the industry-wide loss range. Although WH Group's pork division accounts for 40.6% of the group's revenue, its operating profit contribution is only 22.1%, and its profit margin is significantly lower than that of meat products.

> Duan Yongping said, "Don't use gross profit margin to judge the quality of a business, but whether it has pricing power." WH Group has pricing power for its meat products, but not for fresh pork. Its overall pricing power is medium.

### 2.2 Repeat consumption attribute: extremely strong

Pork and meat products are rigid consumer goods. China is the world's largest pork consumer, with annual consumption exceeding 55 million tons. Per capita pork consumption in the United States has stabilized at approximately 23 kg/year. This is a business that "happens every day" and there is no risk of technological disruption - people will not suddenly stop eating pork.

### 2.3 Asset-light or asset-heavy?

**Focus on assets**. WH Group's net cash flow from operating activities in 2024 is US$3.28 billion, and free cash flow is approximately US$1.9 billion (only US$800 million the previous year), a significant improvement. However, the breeding and slaughtering processes naturally require a large amount of fixed asset investment. Smithfield is reducing its own breeding scale (internal slaughter volume will drop from 14.6 million to 11.1 million in 2025) and shifting to a lighter model, which is the right direction.

The meat product processing developed by Shuanghui is relatively asset-light - relying on brands and channels, with little investment in fixed assets. This is the core reason why the profit margin of the meat products segment is much higher than that of the pork segment.

### 2.4 Free cash flow: ample

| Indicators | 2024 | 2023 | Changes |
|------|--------|--------|------|
| Operating cash flow | $3.28 billion | Approximately $2.5 billion | +31% |
| Free Cash Flow | Approximately $1.9 billion | Approximately $800 million | +138% |
| Operating cash flow (2025) | $2.526 billion | — | — |

The surge in free cash flow in 2024 is mainly due to the turnaround of the pork business in North America (operating profit increased from -620 million to +170 million US dollars) and improvements in operating efficiency. Although operating cash flow will decline slightly in 2025, it will still remain at a high level.

> Duan Yongping said that "operating cash flow is the touchstone for testing business quality." WH Group passed the test on this point.

## 3. Cross-regional arbitrage model: unique but weakening

The most unique business model of WH Group is to use the difference in pork prices between China and the United States to conduct **cross-border arbitrage**:

- The cost of raising pigs in the United States is much lower than that in China (about 8-10 yuan/kg in the United States vs 12-16 yuan/kg in China)
- Shuanghui imports low-priced pork from Smithfield to reduce domestic production costs
- When China’s pig price peaked in 2020 (53 yuan/kg), Shuanghui imported about 12 billion yuan of pork from Smithfield that year.

**But this model is weakening**. Data shows that Shuanghui’s related purchases from Smithfield have dropped from a peak of 12 billion yuan to approximately 5.8 billion yuan in 2024. Reason:
1. China’s pig prices fall and price differences narrow
2. U.S. pork costs rise due to inflation
3. Uncertainty in Sino-US trade relations increases tariff risks

This means that WH Group needs to rely more on the independent profitability of each regional market rather than cross-border arbitrage.

## 4. Smithfield spin-off: pros or cons?

In January 2025, Smithfield held an independent IPO on Nasdaq with an issue price of US$20. The current (May 2026) stock price is approximately US$25.3, with a market value of approximately US$9.95 billion. WH Group holds approximately 80% of the shares (reduced to approximately 88% after the secondary offering in September 2025, and may further reduce its holdings thereafter).

**Front**:
- Improved business transparency - Smithfield, as a U.S. listed company, is required to disclose quarterly, and investors can finally see clearly the true operations of Wanzhou's "American half"
- Release valuation discount - before the spin-off, the combined market value of Shuanghui + Smithfield exceeded HK$120 billion, but the market value of Wanzhou Hong Kong Stock Exchange was only about HK$71.4 billion, and there was a significant holding discount.
- Management efficiency may improve after Smithfield operates independently

**Backside**:
- After the spin-off, group synergies weakened and cross-border procurement flexibility decreased.
- WH Group may be further "shelled" as a holding company
- Smithfield IPO pricing was lower than expected (initial range 23-27 US dollars, actual US$20), indicating that the market’s valuation of pork companies is not high.

## 6. Key issues and unanswered questions

1. **How low will Smithfield’s shareholding ratio be reduced? ** If WH Group continues to reduce its holdings below 50%, WH Group will lose consolidated control over its largest source of income and its business model will fundamentally change.

2. **Has China’s meat consumption peaked? ** Shuanghui meat product sales in 2025 will decrease by 3.8% year-on-year. Although profits have been maintained through structural upgrades, the continued decline in sales is worthy of vigilance. Chinese consumers are shifting towards a healthier diet, with low-temperature fresh meat and prepared dishes replacing traditional ham sausages.

3. **Does globalization really create synergy? ** The cross-border pork arbitrage model is weakening, and businesses in China, the United States and Europe are tending to operate independently. What is the added value of WH Group as a holding platform?

> Duan Yongping's test standard is "If you can't explain a company's business clearly in one sentence, it means you don't understand it well enough." One sentence summary of WH Group: **The world’s largest pork company makes money by relying on branded meat products and growing the volume of fresh pork, but the pig cycle is the lifeblood**.
