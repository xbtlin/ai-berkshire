# 03 Industry competition analysis: Is the moat widening or diminishing?

> Core question: **Are the competitive barriers of the world's largest pork company sufficient to withstand long-term erosion? **
>
> Study date: May 16, 2026 | Currency unit: US dollars (unless otherwise noted)

---

## 1. Industry Panorama: Different Pictures of the Three Major Markets

WH Group spans the three major pork markets of China, North America and Europe, and each market has a completely different competitive landscape.

### 1.1 Chinese market: highly fragmented and consolidating

China is the world's largest pork consumer market, with more than 700 million pigs produced annually, but the concentration rate is still very low.

**Industry Concentration Ratio (2025)**:
| Ranking | Enterprise | Sales volume (10,000 heads) | Market share |
|------|------|--------------|---------|
| 1 | Muyuan Shares | 7,798 | About 11% |
| 2 | Wen's Shares | 4,048 | About 5.7% |
| 3 | New Hope | 1,755 | About 2.5% |
| Total TOP30 | — | About 27,700 | About 39% |

The proportion of pigs produced by the top 30 pig companies in 2025 will increase from 33% in 2024 to 39%, and industry concentration will accelerate.

**But Shuanghui’s main battlefield is not breeding, but slaughtering and processing**. Shuanghui will slaughter approximately 12.69 million heads in 2025 (2023 data), and its market share in the slaughtering segment is much higher than that in the breeding segment. More importantly, Shuanghui has almost no direct competitors of the same size in the field of branded meat products - Shuanghui is the largest player in the ham sausage category.

**Trends to Watch**:
- Breeding leaders such as Muyuan and Wenshi are extending their reach to downstream slaughtering and processing, and may form more direct competition with Shuanghui in the future
- Low-temperature fresh meat and pre-prepared dishes form a category replacement for traditional high-temperature ham sausages
- E-commerce and community group buying have changed the traditional channel structure, and Shuanghui’s channel advantage may be diluted

### 1.2 North American market: oligopoly structure

The U.S. pork processing industry is highly concentrated, with four giants dominating the market:

| Enterprise | Position | Characteristics |
|------|------|------|
| Smithfield Foods (Wanzhou) | The largest pork processor in the United States | Vertical integration, farming to brand |
| Tyson Foods | The largest meat company in the United States | Multiple categories of chicken, beef, and pigs |
| JBS USA | A subsidiary of JBS Brazil | The world's largest meat company |
| Hormel Foods | Branded meat leader | Strong brands such as SPAM |

Smithfield's competitive position in the U.S. pork market is solid. Record profits were achieved in fiscal 2025, with packaged meat products segment profits exceeding US$1 billion for the fourth consecutive year. The company is building a new processing facility in Sioux Falls, South Dakota, showing confidence in future growth.

**The competitive advantage in the North American market is more sustainable**: Once the oligopoly is formed, it is difficult to break, the barriers to entry are extremely high (large-scale breeding, slaughtering, and processing facilities are required), and the brand recognition is strong.

### 1.3 European market: Steady expansion

Wanzhou's European business includes Italy's Argal Group (50.1% stake) and assets in Poland, Romania, Slovakia, Hungary and other countries. European revenue in 2025 is US$5.039 billion (+2.3%), and EBITDA margin will reach 8.2% in 2024 (up from 6.8% in 2023).

European market characteristics: slow but stable growth, continued improvement in profit margins, and strong regional brand barriers.

## 2. Moat assessment

### 2.1 Scale advantage ★★★★☆

The core moat of WH Group is **scale**. As the world’s largest pork company:
- In 2025, the sales volume of meat products will be 3.054 million metric tons, and the sales volume of pork will be 4.089 million metric tons.
- Approximately 106,000 employees worldwide
- Procurement, processing and sales network across three continents

Benefits of scale: purchasing bargaining power, sharing of fixed costs, and resistance to cyclical fluctuations. When pig prices fall, small companies are eliminated, and large companies rely on their financial strength and cost advantages to survive and gain market share.

**But scale does not equal monopoly**. Pork is a standardized commodity and even the biggest players cannot influence the price. The advantage of scale is more reflected in the cost side rather than the pricing side.

### 2.2 Brand Barriers ★★★★☆

**Chinese Market**:
- The "Shuanghui" brand has extremely high consumer awareness in the field of Chinese meat products
- "Shuanghui ham sausage" has become synonymous with the category
- The profit per ton of meat products will hit a new high in 2025 (RMB 4,736), indicating that the brand’s premium capability is still strong
- Shuanghui continues to expand terminal coverage through the "outlet doubling" strategy, with 30 production bases in 18 provinces across the country forming a dense channel network

**North American Market**:
- Smithfield owns multiple well-known brands
- The profit margin of the packaged meat products business continues to increase, and the brand power is verified
- Smithfield drives growth through product innovation, expanded retail distribution and increased foodservice volumes

### 2.3 Vertical integration ★★★☆☆

The control of the entire industry chain from breeding to terminal sales is theoretically a strong barrier to competition. But in practice, vertical integration is a double-edged sword in the pork industry:

**Advantages**:
- Smooth raw material price fluctuations
- Ensure supply chain security
- Quality controllable

**Disadvantages**:
- Breeding is capital-intensive and has low returns
- When the pig cycle goes down, the profits of the entire industry chain are compressed
- Smithfield is reducing its own breeding scale (internal slaughter will drop from 14.6 million to 11.1 million in 2025), indicating that management also realizes that vertical integration is not necessarily a good thing.

> Munger's standard: "The moat depends on whether competitors can copy your advantages within 5 years." WH Group's global layout and brand accumulation cannot be replicated in five years, but pure scale and vertical integration can.

### 2.4 Switching Cost ★★☆☆☆

Consumer switching costs for pork and meat products are low. Consumers can easily switch from Shuanghui ham to other brands, or from pork to chicken or beef. Brand loyalty exists, but not nearly as much as in "addictive" categories like technology or consumer goods.

### 2.5 Regulatory Barriers ★★★☆☆

Food safety regulations pose certain barriers to entry. Strict health permits are required for slaughtering and meat processing, and environmental approvals are required for new production capacity. But this is more of an industry barrier than Wanzhou’s unique advantage.

## 3. Competitive Threat Assessment

### 3.1 Downstream extension of upstream farming giants

Muyuan Co., Ltd. will produce 77.98 million heads in 2025, making it the largest breeding enterprise in China. Muyuan is actively deploying its slaughtering business, and its slaughtering volume in 2023 has reached a considerable scale. If Muyuan unifies the entire "breeding + slaughtering + processing" chain, it may become Shuanghui's direct competitor in the Chinese market.

**But**: Meat product processing requires long-term accumulation of brands, channels, and R&D. There is a huge gap between Muyuan and Shuanghui in this regard. It does not pose a real threat in the short term.

### 3.2 Changes in consumption trends

| Trend | Impact on Wanzhou | Severity |
|------|------------|---------|
| Healthy diet, low fat and low salt | Traditional high-temperature ham consumption shrinks | ★★★☆☆ |
| The rise of prepared dishes | Both a threat (substitutes) and an opportunity (new categories) | ★★★☆☆ |
| Artificial meat/plant protein | The current penetration rate is extremely low and the short-term impact is limited | ★☆☆☆☆ |
| Cold chain logistics development | Low-temperature fresh meat replaces high-temperature products, Shuanghui needs to accelerate its transformation | ★★★☆☆ |

Shuanghui’s meat product sales will drop by 3.8% in 2025. This is no accident—Chinese consumers are shifting from traditional high-temperature meat products to low-temperature fresh meat and prepared dishes. Shuanghui has proposed a "four modernizations" strategy (industrialization, diversification, internationalization, and digitalization), but it will take time to verify the effectiveness of the transformation.

### 3.3 Technological changes

Leading breeding companies (Muyuan, Wenshi) are investing heavily in "intelligent pig farming" systems: automated feeding, AI health monitoring, gene editing breeding, etc. These technological investments may reduce breeding costs and narrow the cost gap with WH International.

**But on the processing side**, there is limited room for technological change - meat product processing relies more on brands and channels than on technology.

Wanzhou is unique in that it is the only meat company with a significant presence in both Chinese and Western markets. This is both an advantage (diversifying geographical risks) and a disadvantage (both markets face strong local competitors).

## 6. Key conclusions

**Moat in "widened" areas**:
1. North American market - Smithfield’s operational efficiency has improved and its competitive position has been consolidated after its independent listing.
2. European market - EBITDA profit margin continues to increase, and regional brand power strengthens
3. Global layout - China, the United States and Europe are increasingly capable of hedging risks in a single market

**The moat is in the field of "digestion"**:
1. Chinese meat products - the category of traditional high-temperature products is shrinking, and the substitution of low-temperature and prepared dishes is accelerating
2. Cross-border arbitrage - the price difference between China and the United States for pork has narrowed, and related-party transactions have dropped from 12 billion yuan to 5.8 billion yuan.
3. Channel advantages - e-commerce and new retail are changing the traditional distribution pattern

> Munger said "Investors should look for companies whose moats are constantly widening." WH Group's moat is not widening across the board, but is showing a differentiated pattern of "widening in North America and Europe, and erosion at the Chinese end." Investors need to judge: Can Smithfield's upside compensate for Shuanghui's downside?
