# II. Risk I: Pig cycle - the greatest immediate risk of the present

#2.1 Status of the pig cycle

The year 2026 is the next period of the pig cycle.

- The average national price of a lean-born pig in November 2025 was only $14.04 per kilogram, a decline of 16.7 per cent compared to the same period in 2024
- The pig price is estimated at $10-11.4 per kg in the first half of 2026,** which is running continuously below the cost line**
- April 2026, pig prices fell by $9.00 per kilo, refreshing history.
- Industry is in a total loss zone.

#2.2 The differential impact of pig cycles on the continent

** Impacts on different business plates are different**:

|------|--------------|------|

This is what makes the international business model unique in all continents –** the whole industry chain layout plays a certain role in the pig cycle**. When prices fall, farm-farming losses fall, but slaughtering and processing ends benefit from low-cost raw materials.

** Butterflies are not perfect. ** If the pig price continues to drop down:
- Lack of consumer confidence and shrinking demand for end-of-life pork
- A farm end loss may affect the group ' s overall profits
- Industry losses lead to market depression and stock prices

# 2.3 Pre-view judgement of the pig cycle

Industry forecasts suggest that prices will rebound after August 2026:
- Deep loss acceleration.
- Mid-Autumn, National Day, Southern Pickle seasons to boost demand
- If the disease breaks out in winter, the drop in the survival rate of the pig will reduce the supply

** But on the other hand** If the price of feed raw materials remains high, the cost of industrial farming may exceed $12/kg, further compressing the profit margin.

"Investors should not attempt to predict cycles, but should ensure that companies have sufficient resources to survive the worst." The net leverage of the continent is 0.3 times that of the world, $2.3 billion in cash available, $5.4 billion unincorporated -- enough money to survive the pigs cycle. This is its central advantage over highly leveraged farming enterprises.

# VI. Risk five: Disease and food security

The African Pig Pistol (ASF) is a black swan in the pork industry. The African Porcelain caused a 40% drop in Chinese pig stocks in 2018-2019, and the price of pigs rose to $53 per kilogram.

** Current status**: The African swine plague is largely under control, but the virus is still present in the environment and there is a risk of localized outbreaks during winter. If a further major outbreak occurs:
- Short-term damage to farm businesses.
- But the effect on the meat processing end of double sinks is limited (substitute raw materials may be imported or purchased)
- The price hikes could push costs, but they could also stimulate consumers to turn to processed meat products

Food safety incidents are another risk point. The double-skinned "skinner" incident in 2011 had a brand-new impact, and although quality control has since been strengthened, food security has been an area of "zero tolerance".
