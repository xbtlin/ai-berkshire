# Xiaomi Group: Business Model Analysis

> Analysis dimensions: business essence, user value, corporate culture and long-term competitiveness
> Core question: Does Xiaomi's "Full Ecosystem for People, Cars and Homes" model create real user value and sustainable competitive advantages?

---

## 1. The nature of business: What exactly is Xiaomi doing?

Xiaomi's business model can be summarized in one sentence: **Hardware is the entrance, ecology is the stickiness, and Internet services are the source of profit**. This model is not the first in the consumer electronics industry - Apple is the ultimate embodiment of this model - but Xiaomi is taking a completely different path: building an ecosystem with "extreme cost-effectiveness" rather than "extreme premium".

The composition of full-year revenue of 457.3 billion yuan in 2025 clearly demonstrates this model:

- **Mobile phone business**: As a traffic portal and brand carrier, it contributes about 190 billion in revenue, but the gross profit margin has dropped to 8.3%, almost "selling at cost";
- **IoT and consumer products**: 123.2 billion revenue, which is the core carrier of ecological stickiness. Once a user buys a Xiaomi mobile phone, the probability of buying a Xiaomi air conditioner, purifier, or bracelet is extremely high;
- **Internet Services**: 37.4 billion revenue, gross profit margin 76.5%, these are the real profit cows - advertising, game distribution, financial services;
- **Automotive and Innovation Business**: 106.1 billion revenue, which is the engine of growth and the source of valuation imagination.

**Key judgment:** The premise for the establishment of this model is that "the user scale is large enough and the stickiness is strong enough." 754 million monthly active users and 1.079 billion IoT device connections prove the existence of scale. But the problem is: the drop in mobile phone gross profit margin to 8.3% means that the "cost-effectiveness" strategy is being eroded by industry involution - when everyone is playing the cost-effectiveness card, cost-effectiveness itself is no longer a differentiation.

### Rating: ★★★★☆ (4/5)

The business model is logically self-consistent, and the ecological synergy is truly visible. However, profits on the mobile phone are being severely squeezed, and the value of the "entry" is being diluted.

---

## 2. User value: Why do consumers choose Xiaomi?

The core reason why Xiaomi can gain market share in multiple categories at the same time is that it provides a unique value proposition: "get an experience of more than 80 points at a reasonable price"**.

The performance of this value proposition in different business lines:

**Mobile phone:** Xiaomi 15 series will hit the high-end market and achieve phased results in 2025, but it is still in the "half-step high-end" stage. In the Chinese mobile phone market in 2025, Xiaomi will be in the top three with more than 40 million activated units, but the high-end market (above 4,000 yuan) is still firmly controlled by Huawei and Apple. The core reason why users choose Xiaomi phones is still "cheaper with the same configuration" rather than "brand recognition".

**Auto:** The success of SU7 is the best verification case of Xiaomi’s user value proposition. Pricing starts at 219,900, and the benchmark is a 300,000-level driving experience and smart cockpit. In less than two years since its launch, the SU7 has sold more than 380,000 units, which is 1.6 times that of the BMW 3 Series and twice that of the Mercedes-Benz C-Class. YU7 even locked 240,000 orders within 18 hours of the pre-sale period. Consumers voted with their feet.

**IoT:** Xiaomi ecological chain is the world's largest consumer-grade IoT platform. 22.7 million users have connected more than 5 Xiaomi devices, which means extremely high switching costs - once your air conditioner, refrigerator, sweeping robot, and door locks are all from Xiaomi, it will be difficult for you to switch to other brands' ecosystems.

**But pay attention to the flip side:** Xiaomi's user loyalty is based more on "rational calculation" rather than "emotional identification". Once competing products beat Xiaomi in terms of cost performance (such as Huawei's return in the mid-range market), the psychological cost for users to switch is not high. This is in sharp contrast to the brand loyalty of Apple users.

### Rating: ★★★★☆ (4/5)

User value is clear and quantifiable, especially in the automotive and IoT fields. However, the brand moat in the mobile phone field is narrow.

## 3. Corporate culture and values: Are you doing the right thing?

Xiaomi’s corporate culture has several distinctive features:

**The concept of "making friends with users". ** Lei Jun has emphasized this point from the first day he started his business. The Xiaomi community, early users of MIUI participated in the development, and Lei Jun personally responded to user feedback on Weibo - these are not marketing techniques, but true beliefs that have penetrated into the organizational DNA. This culture has continued in the automotive business: when quality problems arose after the release of SU7, Lei Jun immediately responded publicly on social media and proposed solutions. This kind of transparency is extremely rare among traditional car companies.

**The product philosophy of "touching people's hearts and being priced kindly". ** Xiaomi has publicly promised that the comprehensive net profit margin of hardware will not exceed 5%. This promise is not only a guarantee for users, but also a constraint on itself. Judging from the financial data, the mobile phone's gross profit margin of 8.3% basically fulfilled this promise.

**Flat management. ** Xiaomi only had a three-tier management structure in its early days: co-founders-department heads-employees. There are no KPIs, and everything is resolved in the group. This kind of culture was a tool for efficiency when the company was small, but with a revenue of 457.3 billion, the management complexity has far exceeded the scope of flatness. The establishment of the Economic and Management Committee and the Human Resources Committee in 2023 is a correction to this problem.

**Poor concerns:** Xiaomi’s corporate culture is highly dependent on Lei Jun’s personal words and deeds. Co-founders Li Wanqiang, Huang Jiangji, and Zhou Guangping have left successively, and Hong Feng and Wang Chuan have also stepped down from their senior positions. The continuous loss of the core team shows that Xiaomi's cultural cohesion may not be as strong as the outside world imagines - or in other words, this culture is more "Lei Jun culture" than "Xiaomi culture".

### Rating: ★★★☆☆ (3.5/5)

The corporate culture has a sincere side, and its promises to users are basically fulfilled. But culture is overly reliant on founders, and as organizations grow, flat cultures are being diluted.

## 4. "Full Ecology for People, Cars and Homes": Moat or concept?

This is the most critical issue in evaluating Xiaomi's business model.

**Positive evidence of ecological synergy:**

1. **Data closed loop**: The data between mobile phones, watches, home appliances and cars are connected, allowing Xiaomi to build full-scenario user portraits. For a user who uses a Xiaomi mobile phone, wears a Xiaomi watch, has a Xiaomi smart home in his home, and drives an SU7, the value of his behavioral data far exceeds that of a single touch point;
2. **Cross-selling**: Part of the reason for the popularity of YU7 pre-sales is the natural conversion of Xiaomi mobile phone users - they already trust the Xiaomi brand and can seamlessly experience the linkage between mobile phones and cars;
3. **R&D reuse**: Self-developed chips (Paper series) and self-developed OS (HyperOS) serve mobile phones, IoT and automobiles at the same time. The marginal benefit of R&D investment increases with the expansion of the ecosystem;
4. **Competition Barriers**: Xiaomi may be the only company in the world that has competitive products in the three high-barrier fields of mobile phones, chips, and automobiles.

**Contrary evidence of ecological synergy:**

1. **The profit margins of each business line vary greatly**: 8.3% for mobile phones, 24.3% for automobiles, and 76.5% for the Internet - This shows that the competitive environment of each business line is completely different, and "ecology" cannot smooth out industry differences;
2. **Unknown user conversion rate**: How many of the 1.079 billion IoT devices are low-priced products that are "bought and thrown in the corner"? How many of the 754 million monthly active users are actually active in multiple Xiaomi ecosystems? Officials have not disclosed these key figures;
3. **Fungibility**: Huawei’s Hongmeng ecosystem is catching up quickly and has stronger brand power in the high-end market. Although Apple’s HomeKit is closed, its penetration rate among high-net-worth users continues to increase.

### Rating: ★★★★☆ (4/5)

"Full ecology for people, cars, and families" is not an empty concept, it has real synergy effects and data support. But the depth of the moat remains to be verified - especially in the face of head-on competition from Huawei and Hongmeng.

---

## 5. Long-term economic characteristics: Where is the ceiling of this business?

**Room for growth:**
- The automobile business targets 550,000 vehicles in 2026. Huatai/Citi predicts 650,000-700,000 vehicles. The long-term goal is to enter the top five in the world. If realized, the automotive business alone can contribute more than 200 billion in revenue;
- AI investment has exceeded 60 billion in three years, deploying embodied intelligence and large-scale client-side models to open a new growth curve;
- There is still huge room for IoT penetration in overseas markets, and overseas revenue from Internet services increased by 15.2%.

**Ceiling and Restraints:**
- Global mobile phone shipments are close to the ceiling, and competition in the stock market will only become more and more brutal;
- The automobile industry is an extremely capital-intensive industry, with a net investment cash outflow of 71.68 billion in 2025. Continued capital expenditures will suppress free cash flow;
- The halving of purchase tax subsidies (starting in 2026) may compress automobile gross profit margins;
- Xiaomi's business model is destined to not enjoy high gross profits like Apple. The promise of "kind prices" itself is the ceiling of profit margins.

### Rating: ★★★★☆ (4/5)

The growth space is clear and quantifiable, but the profit margin ceiling is determined by the business model and is difficult to break through.

## 6. Summary

| Dimensions | Scores | Key Judgments |
|------|------|----------|
| The nature of the business | ★★★★☆ | Hardware entrance + ecological stickiness + Internet monetization, the logic is self-consistent but the value of the mobile entrance is being diluted |
| User value | ★★★★☆ | The user value of cars and IoT is outstanding, but brand loyalty is based on rational calculation rather than emotional identification |
| Corporate Culture | ★★★☆☆ | Sincere to users, but over-reliance on Lei Jun, and the continued loss of the core team is a hidden concern |
| Ecological moat | ★★★★☆ | "People and cars" have real synergy, but the depth and defensibility still need to be verified |
| Long-term economic characteristics | ★★★★☆ | There is clear room for growth, but the business model determines the profit margin ceiling |
| **Comprehensive Rating** | **★★★★☆ (3.9/5)** | **Good business, but not a "great" business - profit margin ceiling and founder dependence are two structural constraints** |

**Core Conclusion:** Xiaomi’s business model is one of the most ambitious experiments in consumer technology in China. If the "Full Ecosystem of People, Cars and Homes" is fully realized, it will create an unprecedented consumer technology platform. However, the establishment of this model requires fighting and winning on multiple fronts at the same time, and history tells us that companies that can do this are very rare. Investing in Xiaomi is essentially betting on whether Lei Jun can become China's "Musk" - a super founder who can control multiple highly complex businesses at the same time.
