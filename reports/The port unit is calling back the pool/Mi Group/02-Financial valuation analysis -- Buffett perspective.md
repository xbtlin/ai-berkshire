** The profit rate (43.8%) significantly exceeds the income rate (25.0%), which is a good sign - the scale effect is being released.** But the profit quality needs to be judged by dismantling the lines of business:

- ** Internet services**: 37.4 billion, Māori 76.5%. This is the best source of profit for millet, similar to apple service. High Māori, low capital expenditure, strong cash flow. If valued separately, this is worth at least HK$ 200-300 billion;
- ** Cellular business**: The trend is worrying: the Māori rate has fallen from 12.1% to 8.3%. The strategic value of mobile phones as access points to traffic has remained unchanged, but their role as profit contributors is rapidly weakening. Q4 Māori has fallen to one of the lowest levels in the history of small rice phones;
- **IOT Business**: 123.2 billion in income, with a Māori rate of about 18-20%.
- ** Automobile business**: 24.3% of the year's Māori, operating at a profit of 900 million. This is a critical turning point – from burning money to profiting six quarters, at a speed greater than all new forces. But 900 million operating profits are still at an early stage, compared to 100 billion dollars in income, with a profit margin of only 0.85%.

** Key judgements:** The quality of the rice’s profits shows a "dipolarization". Internet services are high-quality profits, mobile phones are micro, and cars are just a flat profit. Investors need to judge which weights will increase in the future.

## Rating:  (3.5/5)

The growth of profits is strong but the structural quality is questionable, and the downward trend in the rate of cellular Maori is particularly alarming.

# Two, ROE and Capital Efficiency: How's the shareholder's money?

#2.1 ROE analysis

ROPE 2025 is about 11.5-15% (different calibres).

- ** Net profit **: about 8.6% (392/4573) at reasonable level but well below apple (25%+) and tether (30%+);
- ** Asset turnover**: approximately 1.13 times (4573/4032), medium for a hardware + Internet company;
- ** Equity multiplier**: approximately 1.5-1.8 times, financial leverage is moderate, no overuse of debt.

** ROE drivers:** Mi's ROE is driven mainly by "flip-trading" rather than "high-mori". This model is characterized by limited up-front space (which is difficult to raise prices) but downside risks are also relatively manageable (because it is not dependent on a single high-profit product).

##2.2 R & D capital allocation

In 2025, R&D invested $33.1 billion, or 7.2 per cent of income, in close to full annual profits. The plan was over $40 billion in 2026, of which AI was over 16 billion.

This is a data that needs to be seen in a dialectic way:

- **positive**: For a company that is going to make both mobile phones, cars, AI, chips, and a continuous high level of research and development inputs. The self-researching of micro-milles (pills), self-researching of OS (hyperos), auto-pilot, etc., are all rapidly improving;
- ** Negative**: How much of the $33.1 billion in R & D inputs is "maintenance" (must be spent, otherwise the product lags behind) and how much is "growth" (a drop would have yielded a higher return)?
- **Back **: China invested about 180 billion dollars in 2024, 5.4 times the size of millet. In core technical areas such as chip, communications, rice is still not strong enough to create real technical barriers.

## Rating:  (3.5/5)

Capital efficiency is moderate, and R&D inputs are in the right direction but in absolute measure are not sufficient to create technical barriers. The ROE upgrade requires a sustained improvement in the profitability of automobile operations.

** This is an important warning signal.** The business cash flow of the millet in 2025 (34.1 billion) could not cover the cash flow of the investment (71.7 billion), which means that free cash flow is negative.

1. ** Capital expenditure on automobile operations is about 12 billion**: plant construction, equipment purchase, power charging networks - these are necessary upfront inputs, but also means that the automobile business will remain a "black hole" in cash flow for the next 2-3 years;
2. ** Significant increase in inventory**: In response to delivery of 410,000 vehicles and hundreds of millions of mobile phones/IOT equipment, stock stock reserve absorbed significant operating funds.

** Key question:** How does the intrinsic value of a firm with negative free cash flows be measured?

If the car business of Mi is going to be a steady profit period in 2027-2028 (a 10 billion annual profit +), the current capital expenditure is "sown" and will yield future gains. If the profit margin is not raised because of increased competition in the automobile industry, these inputs may become sunk costs.

#3.2 Balance sheet health

At the end of 2024, the total assets of the rice were approximately $403.2 billion, with a moderate asset-liability ratio. Cash and short-term investments were abundant (estimated to exceed $120 billion) and there was no liquidity risk in the short term. Companies did not have significant interest-bearing liabilities and had a higher financial security cushion.

# # Rating:  (3/5)

Free cash flows are negative and hard, and while there is a reasonable strategic explanation, for investors seeking "determinate" this is a threshold of conviction that needs to be crossed.

##4.3 Security margin assessment

** Neutral scenario (50% probability): ** 550,000 vehicles delivered in 2026, with profits increasing to around 45 billion, with shares held between HK$ 50-60. 10-20% return.

# V. Shareholder returns: splits and buybacks

The rice was repurchased in 2025 (with 3.42 million shares at the end of April at HK$29), but its size is minimal in relation to market value. Companies are now reinvested in all profits, regardless of their size.

This is in line with the reasonable choice of a high-growth stage – that it is reasonable to make red-rate when the return on internal investment is higher than the return on equity, which also means that shareholders rely entirely on rising equity prices rather than cash dividends.

# # Rating:  (3/5)

The growth phase is understandable without distinction, but it is not an ideal option for investors seeking cash returns.

It is not a "good company" in the traditional Buffett sense: ROE is not high enough (less than 15%), free cash flows are negative, and profit margins for mobile phone operations are worsening. The value of 18-22 times PE corresponds to 25-30%, which is reasonable, but it is higher if the speed of growth (e.g., increased competition for cars). The investment in rice is essentially a "profit curve" for automobile operations — if the car profit rate is to be stabilized at 10% + in 2027, the value of the rice is 40-50 times the current price of Internet services; if not, it is a ROE 12%, PE 20 times the price of ordinary hardware company.
