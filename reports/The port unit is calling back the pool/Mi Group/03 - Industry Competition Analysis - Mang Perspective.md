** Are these three death-rate scenarios happening?** The decline in the Māori rate of mobile phones is happening (early signs of Death One), the price battles of cars have been fought (prelude to Death Two), and the multi-line effort has been spread (the fear of Death Three). # V. The cost of pluralism: the risk of several wars at the same time

Mi's strategic ambition is admirable, but the complexity of diversity cannot be ignored:

** Management bandwidth is scarce:** The army is also handling four main lines: mobile phones, Iot, cars, and AI, each of which is 100 billion-grade. Even the best managers have limited attention. Some quality and delivery problems in the early days of SU7 listing may be partly due to inadequate bandwidth management.

** Resource allocation dilemma: ** 33.1 billion R&D investments seem to be quite large, but the amount of input per line is not very high when allocated to cell phones, IOT, cars, AI. By the way, Biadey Light has invested tens of billion in R&D, and hundreds of millions in chips alone. Mems are at risk of "saper noodles."

**Synergy of organisations:** "Care of people" is beautiful on PPT, but in practice, synergy between mobile phone teams, IOT teams, and car teams does not happen automatically. Strong organizational structures and incentives are needed to break the sector walls.

** Reflections on the mark:** Historically successful diversified enterprises (GE, Samsung, Birkhshire) have either a very strong management system (GE management training student system), a clear investment discipline (Buffett's capital allocation capacity) or a deep technological accumulation (three star semiconductors).

# # Rating: <unk> (3/5)

Diversity is the right direction, but implementation is extremely difficult. There is a huge gap between "do anything" and "do anything good."
