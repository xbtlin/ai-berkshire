##2.2 Critical risk profile

** Risk I: Founder's dependence (high risk)**

This is an unquantifiable but highly significant risk. The Lui Army is 56 years old, energetic, but it manages a huge enterprise across the four-track of cell phone/Iot/car/AI, with a 456.3 billion battalion. He once mocked himself as "too hard-working" -- which, in turn, means "the organization is too dependent on him."

A healthy business should be "whoever turns away," but it's not what it is right now. If the Lui army decides to "retire" like Ma Yun, though there is no indication, the value of the rice could be cut by eight.

** Mitigating factors:** The establishment of the Administrative and Human Resources Commission in 2023 was an attempt to institutionalize the organization. Lu Weiice's performance in mobile operations proved that he was a qualified executor. But there is still a long way to go between "institutionalization" and "demilitarization".

**Risk II: Deterioration of Māori rates for mobile phones (high risk)**

The decline in the Māori rate for mobile phones from 12.1% to 8.3% is not a quarterly fluctuation, but a continuing trend. The driving force behind this is:
- The high-end market squeezes brought about by China ' s return;
- Price wars resulting from competitive industrial stock;
- The high end of the millet requires higher R & D and marketing inputs.

If the Māori rate continues to decline below 5 per cent, mobile phone operations will shift from a "micro-entry" to a "deficit-entry" that directly erodes the profits of Internet services.

**Small response:** Rolling out super-high-end products like millet 15 Ultra tried to raise the ASP and Māori ratio. Q1 Māori is a key observation point in 2026.

** Risk three: geopolitics (medium risk)**

Mi's experience in the Indian market is a microcosm of the risk of China's technology enterprises going to sea:
- Rs. 55.5 billion (approximately RMB 4.8 billion) have been frozen and about Rs. 4.7 billion remain unfrozen to date;
- US$ 72 million in customs disputes are in the litigation stage;

India, once the largest overseas market for Mi, has now become a drag from "growth engine".

More broadly, the uncertainty of US-China relations may also affect the supply of chips and overseas market access for millet. However, millet is not currently listed as a real-estate, and production bases have begun to be established in Europe and South-East Asia to spread risk.

**Risk 4: Increased competition in automobile operations (high risk)**

2026 was the year of the "disruption race" in the new energy automobile industry in China:

# IV. Governance structures and shareholder protection

# 4.1 Equity structure

Mi uses a WVR structure, with the RLA holding a B-unit with a vote of 10 votes per unit. This means that the RLA has absolute control over the company, with limited voice for external shareholders.

** Positive reading:** Founder control ensures long-term strategic coherence and avoids short-term shareholder interference. The RLA car-making decisions may not pass if they are made in a more "democratic" company.

** Negative: ** External shareholders are not able to effectively check management. If the army makes the wrong decision (e.g. over-exploitation), there is little mechanism for shareholders to correct.

##4.2 Disclosure of information

Mi's information is disclosed as being of the highest middle standard in Hong Kong stock listing companies:
- Quarterly financial disclosure is timely and detailed;
- Management teleconferences are held regularly;
- The personal activity of the RNA in social media provides additional information windows.

But there is still room for improvement:
- The detailed profit splits among the lines of operations are not sufficiently transparent;
- Key ecological indicators such as IOT equipment activity, user ARPU, etc., are not regularly disclosed;
- Lack of detailed splitting of the cost structure of the automobile operations.

## Rating: <unk> (3.5/5)

The rules in the governance structure are clear, and the founder has control over a double-edged sword.

# V. Civilization evolution and institutional environment

#5.1 Institutional environment for technology enterprises in China

The institutional environment in which Mi is living has several structural features:

** Favourable:**
- China is the largest consumer of electronics and new energy vehicles worldwide, and indigenous enterprises enjoy a natural market advantage;
- Government has clear policy support in the areas of new energy vehicles, AI, etc.;
- The integrity and efficiency of China ' s supply chain is unique globally;
- In April 2026, the dignitaries visited Mi "I must see" Mi, reflecting her status as a Chinese technology card.

** Adverse:**
- The risk of supply chain break-ups (chips, operating systems) that may result from Chinese-American technology competition;
- Nationalistic policies in emerging markets such as India are detrimental to Chinese brands;
- Uncertainty in the domestic regulatory environment for the Internet (data security, antimonopoly);
- The impact of the down-cycle real estate on consumer confidence.

#5.2 Long-term institutional risk

Mi, as a company listed in Hong Kong, operating in China and selling globally, faces a triple system risk:
1. Liquidity and valuation discounts in the Hong Kong stock market;
2. Uncertainty in China ' s domestic policies;
3. Geopolitical risks in overseas markets.

These risks are not unique to Mi, but common to all Chinese technology firms. But Mi is more affected by policy because she is a mobile phone, IOT, and car in many areas.

# # Rating: <unk> (3/5)

The institutional environment is supportive and constrained, and generally falls within the "investable but subject to discount" level.
