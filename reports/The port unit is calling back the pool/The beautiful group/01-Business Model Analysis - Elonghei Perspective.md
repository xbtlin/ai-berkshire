# Midea Group: Business Model Analysis

> Analysis dimensions: business model quality, user value, corporate culture, long-term sustainability
> Data cutoff: 2025 annual report

---

## 1. The nature of business: What business is Midea doing?

Midea Group essentially does two businesses:

**Door 1: Consumer appliances (C-side). ** Smart home business revenue from air conditioners, refrigerators, washing machines, kitchen appliances, small household appliances, etc. in 2025 will be 299.93 billion yuan, accounting for approximately 65.7% of total revenue. This is a business of economies of scale + brand premium. The core competitive factors are cost control, channel efficiency and product iteration speed.

**Gate 2: Commercial and Industrial Solutions (B-side). ** Building technology, robotics and automation (KUKA), new energy and industrial technology, revenue in 2025 will be 122.75 billion yuan, accounting for approximately 26.9% of total revenue, a year-on-year increase of 17.5%. This is a business with technical barriers + customer stickiness, higher profit margins, and different cyclicality than home appliances.

This "two-wheel drive" structure makes Midea no longer a simple home appliance company, but more like a technology-driven diversified industrial group.

## 2. User value: Why do consumers buy Midea?

From a user's perspective, Midea's core value proposition can be summarized as "good enough, economical enough, complete enough"**:

**Good enough. ** Midea does not take the extremely high-end route (that is COLMO's positioning). The main brand is positioned as "high quality" that mass consumers can afford. Main categories such as air conditioners, washing machines, refrigerators, etc., the product strength is in the first echelon of the industry, with reliable quality and complete functions.

**Saving enough. ** Midea's cost control capabilities are industry benchmarks. 41 manufacturing bases around the world and a vertically integrated supply chain enable it to maintain competitive prices while ensuring quality. This is not simply low price, but efficiency-driven "cost-effectiveness".

** Complete enough. ** Multi-category coverage is Midea’s unique advantage. From air conditioners to sweeping robots, from small kitchen appliances to central air conditioning systems, consumers can complete most of their home appliance needs under one brand. This reduces consumers’ choice costs and enhances channels’ willingness to cooperate.

**Multi-brand matrix. ** Midea, COLMO (high-end), Toshiba (mid-to-high-end/Japanese), Little Swan (washing machine expert), Hualing (younger), Comfee (overseas cost-effectiveness), different brands cover different groups of people and price ranges, reducing internal brand consumption.

**Noteable concerns:** Midea’s brand mentality is not outstanding in many categories. Consumers may first think of Gree when buying air conditioners, Haier when buying refrigerators, and Xiaomi Ecological Chain when buying small home appliances. Midea is "doing everything and doing nothing bad", but its brand loyalty in a single category is not as good as that of its specialized competitors.

## 3. Economic characteristics of business model

### 3.1 Economies of scale and cost advantages

Midea's manufacturing scale ranks among the top in the global home appliance industry. The annual production capacity exceeds 80 million units of air conditioners and tens of millions of units of refrigerators and washing machines. The procurement bargaining power, production efficiency and logistics cost dilution brought about by this scale constitute a sustained cost advantage.

### 3.2 Channel Network

Midea has one of the most complete home appliance distribution networks in China, covering tens of thousands of retail terminals offline, and occupies a leading position in major e-commerce platforms online. In 2025, online retail sales market share will rank first in multiple categories. The proportion of e-commerce continues to remain above 45%, and the degree of online and offline integration leads the industry.

### 3.3 Cash flow characteristics

A good feature of the home appliance industry is that downstream dealers usually pay in advance or receive payment quickly, while upstream raw material procurement can take advantage of the account period. Midea's net operating cash flow in 2025 will increase significantly by 74.9%, far exceeding the growth rate of net profit, indicating that the company not only "made money" but also "received money." This is a sign of a healthy business model.

### 3.4 Capital Return

ROE remains at around 20%, which is an excellent level in the manufacturing industry. Considering that a large amount of Midea's asset-liability ratio of about 61% is operating liabilities (advance receipts, accounts payable), the real return on operating capital is even higher.

## 4. Corporate culture and business philosophy

### 4.1 The courage to "do subtraction"

The core concept that Fang Hongbo has repeatedly emphasized in 2025-2026 is **"simplification"**: simplifying categories, streamlining SKUs, and shutting down and transferring non-core businesses. This is rare in a Chinese corporate environment that habitually pursues "bigger and more complete". The business philosophy proposed in the 2025 annual report is to "promote growth through simplification and face challenges head-on through self-subversion."

This ability to "do subtraction" deserves attention. Many companies fail not because they do not do enough, but because they do too much and are too complex. Midea's management can recognize this and take action, indicating that the quality of decision-making is high.

### 4.2 Professional manager culture

Midea is one of the few large-scale enterprises in China that has successfully transitioned from its founder (He Xiangjian) to professional manager (Fang Hongbo). Although He Xiangjian's family is still a major shareholder, they are not involved in daily operations (He Xiangjian's only son He Jianfeng no longer serves as a director). This kind of culture of "the boss lets go and the manager takes responsibility" is extremely rare in Chinese private enterprises.

### 4.3 Long-termism or opportunism?

On the positive side, Midea's layout in ToB business (acquisition of KUKA in 2016 and continued investment in building technology) does reflect its long-term strategic vision. The B-side business has grown from scratch, accounting for nearly 27% of revenue in 2025, and its growth rate is significantly faster than that of the C-side.

But we must also look at the other side: Midea's diversification history also has many cases of "paying tuition", such as its early involvement in automobiles, real estate and other fields without success. As a nurturing business, the current medical sector is uncertain whether it can really take off.

## 5. Sustainability Assessment of Business Model

### Positive factors

1. **Home appliances are a necessity for consumption**. Even if the economy goes down, the demand for replacement of air conditioners, refrigerators, and washing machines is still there. Midea’s market share in these categories is solid and unlikely to be disrupted.
2. **In-depth globalization layout**, overseas revenue accounts for more than 40%, and the direction is to go overseas with independent brands (OBM), reducing dependence on a single market.
3. **ToB business opens the second growth curve**. The market space for building technology and industrial automation is much larger than that of home appliances, and customer stickiness is stronger.
4. **Abundant operating cash flow** provides a solid foundation for continued investment and shareholder returns.

### Concern factors

1. **The overall growth rate of the home appliance industry is slowing down**, the domestic market is close to saturation, and the dividends of the "old for new" policy are unsustainable.
2. **Brand premium ability is limited**. Midea’s share in the high-end market is not as good as Gree (air conditioners) and Haier (refrigerators), and the room for profit margin improvement is limited.
3. **ToB business is still in the climbing phase**. KUKA’s global competitiveness still lags behind FANUC, ABB, etc., and profit contribution will take time to be verified.
4. **Cross-border competition** from Internet companies such as Xiaomi may erode Midea’s market share in the fields of smart homes and small home appliances.
