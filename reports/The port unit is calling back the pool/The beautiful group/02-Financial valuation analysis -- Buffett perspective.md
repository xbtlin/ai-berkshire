# Midea Group: Financial and Valuation Analysis

> Analysis dimensions: earnings quality, capital allocation efficiency, intrinsic value, margin of safety
> Data cutoff: 2025 annual report

---

## 1. Profitability: Is it “real money” earned?

### 1.1 Revenue and profit growth

| Indicators | 2025 | YoY | 2024 | YoY |
|------|--------|------|--------|------|
| Operating income | 456.45 billion | +12.1% | 409.1 billion | +10%+ |
| Net profit attributable to parent company | 43.95 billion | +14.0% | 38.55 billion | +14%+ |
| Excluding net profit attributable to parent companies | 41.27 billion | +15.5% | - | - |

It has maintained double-digit growth for many consecutive years, and the growth rate after non-net profit (+15.5%) is higher than the revenue growth rate (+12.1%), indicating that profit margins are improving and scale effects are still playing a role.

### 1.2 Profit quality: the ability to convert profits into cash

Net operating cash flow will increase significantly by 74.9% in 2025, which means that the ratio of operating cash flow/net profit continues to improve. The "pay first, then deliver" business model in the home appliance industry determines that the cash flow of high-quality companies is usually better than book profits.

However, there is also a signal that needs attention: some analysts pointed out the warning that "the ratio of net cash flow from operating activities/net profit continues to decline." This may be related to the expansion of the scale of accounts receivable (the collection cycle of ToB business is usually longer than that of ToC). This metric needs to be tracked continuously.

### 1.3 Profit rate trend

Midea's net profit margin is about 9.5%-10%, which is relatively high in the manufacturing industry. Gross profit margin has increased steadily in the past few years, mainly due to:
- Product structure tilts towards high-margin categories
- Increase in the proportion of ToB business (usually gross profit margin is higher than ToC)
- Cost optimization brought about by global operations

## 2. Balance sheet: Is the financial security safe?

### 2.1 Asset-liability ratio

The asset-liability ratio at the end of 2025 was 61.17%, a year-on-year decrease of 1.16 percentage points. On the surface, this number may seem high, but one needs to understand the structure of household appliance companies’ liabilities:

**Operating liabilities account for the bulk. ** A large number of Midea's liabilities are accounts payable (accounts to upstream suppliers), accounts received in advance (prepayments from dealers) and contract liabilities. These are not financial liabilities, but a reflection of operating efficiency - using upstream and downstream money to operate, reducing the occupation of own capital.

**The interest-bearing debt ratio is controllable. ** After deducting operating liabilities, Midea's interest-bearing debt ratio is much lower than the superficial figure of 61%. The company has plenty of cash on its books and a positive net cash position.

### 2.2 Asset Quality

Two points to note:
1. **Goodwill. ** The scale of goodwill formed by the acquisition of KUKA, Toshiba Home Appliances, etc. is relatively large. If the related business does not meet expectations, there is an impairment risk.
2. **Accounts receivable. ** As ToB business expands, the volume of accounts receivable is growing, and attention needs to be paid to bad debt risks and recovery cycles.

## 3. Capital allocation: How does management allocate the money it earns?

### 3.1 Shareholder Return

The capital allocation in 2025 is textbook level:

| Project | Amount | Proportion of net profit |
|------|------|-------------|
| Cash dividend | 32.4 billion yuan | 73.7% |
| Share buyback | 11.6 billion yuan | 26.4% |
| **Total** | **44 billion yuan** | **~100%** |

Returning 100% of the full-year net profit to shareholders is extremely rare among Chinese listed companies. It also plans to repurchase 6.5-13 billion yuan of A shares in 2026.

Based on the current market value, the dividend yield is about 4%-5%. Coupled with the increase in per-share value brought about by repurchases, the total shareholder return is about 6%-7%. Even if the company doesn't grow at all, it can generate substantial annualized gains from shareholder returns alone.

### 3.2 R&D investment

R&D investment in 2025 will be approximately 16 billion yuan, accounting for approximately 3.5% of revenue. The company plans to invest 60 billion yuan in research and development in the future. In the home appliance industry, this level of investment is at the forefront, reflecting the management's emphasis on technology-driven growth.

### 3.3 Capital expenditure and expansion

Overseas production capacity continues to expand (new smart factories are built in Mexico and Saudi Arabia), and the layout of 38 R&D centers and 63 manufacturing bases around the world is being strengthened. These investments will affect free cash flow in the short term, but are necessary costs to build global competitiveness in the long term.

### 3.4 Capital Allocation Evaluation

Midea has achieved a good balance between shareholder returns and business investment:

- **Dividends and buybacks** ensure immediate returns to shareholders
- **Investment in R&D** maintains technological competitiveness
- **Overseas expansion** reserves production capacity for future growth
- No large-scale inefficient diversification or "empire building" tendencies

This capital allocation method essentially embodies the management's philosophy of "prioritizing returns to shareholders after making money while prudently investing in the future."

## 4. Valuation analysis

### 4.1 Current Valuation Level

| Valuation indicators | Numerical values | Industry comparison |
|----------|------|----------|
| PE (TTM) | ~13-14 times | Industry average ~14 times |
| PB | ~3x | Matches ROE 20% |
| Dividend yield | ~4-5% | Much higher than industry average |
| EV/EBITDA | ~8-9 times | Reasonably low |

### 4.2 Historical valuation comparison

Midea's historical PE center is about 15-20 times. The current valuation of 13-14 times is in the historically low area, mainly reflecting market concerns about the following factors:
- Domestic home appliance market growth slows down
- The real estate downturn drags down the demand for home appliances
- Sino-US trade friction and tariff risks
- RMB exchange rate fluctuations

### 4.3 Intrinsic value estimation

**Method 1: Sustainable Growth Model**

Assumptions:
- Free cash flow in 2025 is about 35-40 billion yuan
- Sustainable growth rate: 4% (considering overseas and ToB growth)
- Discount rate: 10%

Intrinsic value = FCF / (Discount rate - Growth rate) = 375 / (10% - 4%) = 625 billion yuan

The current total market value of A+H is approximately 550-600 billion yuan, which is roughly equivalent to the estimated intrinsic value.

**Method 2: Profit rate method**

Calculated based on 13 times PE, the profit yield is approximately 7.7% (1/13). Coupled with annual earnings growth of 5%-8%, the expected annualized return for long-term holding is approximately 12%-16%.

This rate of return is not extremely attractive, but considering Midea's business quality and certainty, it is a reasonable and even preferred level in the current market environment.

**Method Three: Shareholder Return Anchoring Method**

If estimated based on "6%-7% shareholder return + 5%-8% earnings growth", the long-term annualized return is approximately between 11% and 15%. Provided that valuations are not compressed further.

### 4.4 Safety Margin Assessment

**Pros:**
- PE is at a historical low, with limited downside space
- 100% of net profits are returned to shareholders, providing "quasi-fixed income" bottom protection
-ToB business and overseas business provide incremental growth and are not completely dependent on the domestic home appliance cycle

**Disadvantages:**
- 13-14 times PE is not a "break-the-bone" cheap price, it is just reasonably low.
- If earnings growth falls from double digits to single digits, valuations may come under further pressure
- Deterioration of the global trade environment may lead to damage to overseas business

Safety margin evaluation: **above average**. Not an extremely undervalued "butt picking" opportunity, but current prices provide a reasonable entry point for a company of this quality.

## 5. Key financial risks

1. **Growth in accounts receivable. ** The expansion of ToB business may lead to an increase in the turnover days of accounts receivable, so attention should be paid to the risk of bad debts.
2. **Goodwill impairment. ** If the goodwill formed by overseas mergers and acquisitions such as KUKA is impaired, it may impact current profits.
3. **Exchange rate fluctuations. ** Overseas revenue accounts for more than 40%, and RMB appreciation will directly affect profits.
4. **Raw material prices. ** Fluctuations in commodity prices such as copper and steel affect gross profit margin.
