# Midea Group: Industry Competition Analysis

> Analysis dimensions: industry structure, competition landscape, moat depth, long-term trends
> Data cutoff: 2025 annual report

---

## 1. Industry panorama: What stage is China’s home appliance industry at?

China's home appliance industry is at an important turning point: **shifting from incremental competition to stock game**.

### 1.1 Domestic market: mature but not fully saturated

The average household ownership of white appliances such as air conditioners, refrigerators, and washing machines has approached the level of developed countries, and new demand has slowed. However, replacement demand continues. There are more than 400 million households in China, and the average replacement cycle of white appliances is 8-12 years, which means that there is a replacement demand of tens of millions of units every year.

The "old for new" policy will bring a wave of additional demand in 2024-2025, but this essentially releases future demand in advance, and demand may fall after the policy is withdrawn.

### 1.2 Overseas markets: core source of growth

The overseas expansion of Chinese home appliance companies is accelerating. Midea's overseas revenue in 2025 will be 195.95 billion yuan, a year-on-year increase of 15.9%, and the growth rate is significantly faster than domestic (+9.4%). There is still room for significant improvement in the penetration rate of overseas markets, especially emerging markets such as Southeast Asia, the Middle East, Africa and Latin America.

### 1.3 ToB field: a new competition dimension

ToB businesses such as building technology, industrial automation, and new energy technology have opened up new growth space for the industry. This is no longer the competition logic of traditional home appliances, but the competition of industrial products and solutions. Customer decisions are more rational, technical barriers are higher, but the market space is also larger.

## 2. Competitive landscape: "Three giants + new forces"

### 2.1 Midea vs Gree vs Haier

The data for the first half of 2025 clearly shows the differentiation of the Big Three:

| Indicators | Midea Group | Haier Smart Home | Gree Electric Appliances |
|------|---------|---------|---------|
| First half revenue | 252.3 billion | 156.5 billion | 97.6 billion |
| Revenue growth | +15.7% | +10.2% | -2.7% |
| Net profit growth rate | +26% | +15.6% | Under pressure |
| Proportion of overseas revenue | >40% | ~50% | <15% |
| ToB business | Proportion ~27% | Layout | Very few |

**Midea's advantages:** It has the strongest comprehensive strength, balanced development of multiple categories, leading ToB business, complete overseas layout, and the most stable growth.

**Haier's advantages:** It has the deepest overseas expansion (its own brands have a certain reputation in Europe and the United States) and the best high-end products (Casarte), but its scale and efficiency are not as good as Midea's.

**Gree’s Dilemma:** Over-reliance on the single category of air conditioners (accounting for more than 75% of revenue), slow progress in overseas expansion, repeated failures in diversification, negative revenue growth, and facing urgent pressure for strategic transformation.

### 2.2 Xiaomi: a new variable that cannot be ignored

Xiaomi's expansion in the field of home appliances deserves great attention. Its IoT and consumer products business grew by 50.7% year-on-year. The first phase of the smart home appliance factory is scheduled to be put into production in November 2025, with large-scale mass production in 2026.

Xiaomi’s competitive logic is different from traditional home appliance companies:
- **Ecological Advantages**: Mijia App connects more than 1 billion devices, and the smart home ecological closed loop is difficult for traditional home appliance companies to copy.
- **Channel efficiency**: direct sales + e-commerce model, few intermediate links, strong price competitiveness
- **Younger users**: High brand awareness among consumers aged 25-35

At present, Xiaomi's market share in the field of large white appliances (air conditioners, refrigerators, washing machines) is still not high, but it is growing rapidly. For Midea, Xiaomi is more of a competitive threat in the mid-to-low-end and small home appliance fields, and has limited impact in the large white goods and ToB fields.

### 2.3 Overseas Competitors

In the global market, Midea’s main competitors include:
- **Whirlpool/Bosch**: The brand recognition in the European and American markets is much higher than that of Midea
- **Samsung/LG**: Advantages in home appliance technology innovation and brand premiumization
- **Daikin**: A solid global position in the field of commercial air conditioning

Midea's strategy is "multi-brand + localization": conquer the Japanese market through the Toshiba brand, enter various regions through its own brands and OEMs, and avoid tariff barriers by building local factories.

## 3. Moat analysis

### 3.1 Economies of scale moat ★★★★★

Midea's scale advantage is its core moat. With annual revenue exceeding 450 billion yuan and 63 manufacturing bases around the world, the advantages in procurement costs, manufacturing costs and logistics costs are almost impossible to catch up with.

**Quantitative indicators:** Midea's operating efficiency (inventory turnover, receivable turnover, capital return) continues to lead the industry. This is not accidental, but the result of the superposition of scale and management capabilities.

### 3.2 Channel Network Moat ★★★★☆

Tens of thousands of offline retail terminals + the leading position of the online full platform constitute a strong distribution capability. Especially in third- and fourth-tier cities and rural markets, the density and depth of the channel network are difficult for latecomers to replicate in the short term.

**But please note:** Channel advantages have been weakened in the e-commerce era. Online channels are relatively flat, and new brands such as Xiaomi can bypass traditional channels to reach consumers directly.

### 3.3 Brand moat ★★★☆☆

The Midea brand is well-known, but its brand loyalty is not as good as that of Gree (for air conditioners) and Haier (for refrigerators). Midea's brand relies more on "cost-effectiveness" and "multi-category coverage" rather than the ultimate reputation of a certain category.

**Positive changes:** The layout of COLMO and Toshiba brands in the high-end market is strengthening the depth of the brand moat.

### 3.4 Technical Barrier Moat ★★★★☆

38 global R&D centers with an annual R&D investment of 16 billion yuan. It has self-research capabilities in core components such as compressors, frequency converters, and motors. ToB business (building technology, KUKA robots) has higher technical barriers than consumer home appliances.

**But let’s be honest:** The technical barriers in the home appliance industry are generally not as good as those in industries such as semiconductors and biomedicine. The core technologies of air conditioners and refrigerators are relatively mature, and technological innovation is more reflected in functional overlay and user experience optimization rather than disruptive breakthroughs.

### 3.5 Conversion Cost Moat ★★★☆☆

The switching cost of C-end home appliances is low - there is almost no obstacle for consumers to switch brands. However, B-side businesses (central air-conditioning systems, building automation, industrial robots) have high switching costs, and once they enter the customer's supply chain, they are not easily replaced. This is an important strategic value of ToB business.

## 4. Industry trends: key variables in the next five years

### 4.1 Domestic "old for new" policy cycle

The subsidy policy from 2024 to 2025 has already stimulated a wave of demand, but after the policy intensity is reduced, a drop in demand is a high probability event. The industry needs to return to endogenous growth from policy drive.

### 4.2 Dividends and risks of globalization coexist

The globalization of Chinese home appliance companies is in a period of acceleration, but the rise of trade protectionism is a significant risk. Midea's response strategy - building factories overseas to achieve localized production - is the right direction, but it will take time and capital investment to realize it.

### 4.3 AI and intelligence

The application of AI technology in home appliances is still in its early stages. It's more of a marketing concept than a substantive product change at the moment. But in the long term, the integration of smart home ecology may change the industry competition landscape. Xiaomi has ecological advantages in this regard, and traditional home appliance companies need to catch up quickly.

### 4.4 The long-term space of ToB business

The growth space in data center refrigeration, new energy equipment, industrial automation, smart buildings and other fields is much greater than that of traditional home appliances. Midea's early layout in these areas, if executed properly, may become the company's main growth engine in the next 5-10 years.

## 5. Final deduction of competitive landscape

If the horizon is extended to 5-10 years, the final outcome of competition in China's home appliance industry may be:

1. **Midea and Haier** will become a global integrated home appliance + industrial group, similar to Siemens Home Appliances or General Electric in the past.
2. If **Gree** cannot successfully transform, it may become a regional enterprise focusing on air conditioning.
3. **Xiaomi** occupies a place in the smart home ecosystem, but it is difficult to shake the traditional giants in the asset-heavy white goods field.
4. Industry concentration has further increased, and small and medium-sized brands have accelerated their clearance.

In this final analysis, Midea is in the most advantageous position - it has scale, globalization capabilities, and strategic depth in its ToB business. However, "advantageous position" does not mean "inevitable winner". Execution ability and strategic judgment are equally critical.
