# Midea Group: Risk and Management Assessment

> Analysis dimensions: management quality, governance structure, key risks, safety margin
> Data cutoff: 2025 annual report

---

## 1. Management Assessment: Buying stocks means buying people

### 1.1 Fang Hongbo: One of the most successful professional managers in China

**Basic information:** Fang Hongbo, born in 1967, joined Midea in 1992 and worked his way from internal magazine editor to general manager of the air-conditioning division and president of the refrigeration group. In 2012, he officially took over as chairman and president. He has been at the helm of Midea for more than 13 years.

**Core Competency Assessment:**

**Strategic Judgment ★★★★★**
Fang Hongbo made several key strategic decisions during his tenure:
1. **2012-2014 "capacity reduction" reform**: Cut off a large number of inefficient production capacities and categories, and reduce SKUs from more than 20,000 to less than 2,000. This requires great courage under the pressure of short-term revenue decline.
2. **Acquisition of KUKA and Toshiba Home Appliances in 2016**: Advanced layout of robots/automation and globalization, and now KUKA and Toshiba have become important parts of Midea's strategic layout.
3. **Continue to promote the two-wheel drive of "ToB + globalization"**: The strategic direction of transforming Midea from a home appliance company into a technology group has always been consistent.
4. **Proposed to "promote growth through simplification" in 2025**: When the company has reached a revenue volume of 450 billion, it still has the consciousness of "subtracting". This kind of anti-human decision-making ability is very scarce.

**Execution ★★★★★**
Midea’s strategy is not to stop at PPT. From 2020 to 2025, ToB business will grow from tens of billions to over 120 billion, and overseas revenue will grow from 110 billion to nearly 200 billion, with annual growth rates above 15%. Strategic ideas are truly translated into financial figures.

**Capital allocation capability ★★★★★**
In 2025, 100% of net profits will be returned to shareholders (32.4 billion in dividends + 11.6 billion in buybacks), while maintaining R&D investment of 16 billion yuan and continuing to build overseas factories. Excellent balance between "spending money" and "spending money".

**Risk Awareness ★★★★☆**
Fang Hongbo has publicly expressed the concept that "only the paranoid can survive" on many occasions, emphasizing that he should not listen to good words, but only solve problems. This kind of crisis awareness is especially valuable when the company's performance is rising. However, as a professional manager, it remains to be seen whether his interests are completely consistent with the long-term interests of the company (the shareholding ratio is relatively low).

### 1.2 Successor issue: the biggest uncertainty

In August 2025, Midea established the position of "executive president" for the first time, which was held by 48-year-old Wang Jianguo. This was widely interpreted by the industry as a signal of succession arrangements.

**Introduction to Wang Jianguo:**
- Joined Midea in 1999 and worked in the company for more than 25 years
- Served in many key positions including air-conditioning supply chain management, human resources, general manager of refrigerator business unit, president of Midea International, and president of smart home business group
- Experienced a complete rotation from supply chain to sales, from domestic to overseas

**Positive review:**
- Internal training, with a deep understanding of Midea's business and culture
- Experience in multiple job rotations shows that his abilities have been fully verified
- Fang Hongbo is still in office and has enough time for a smooth transition of power.

**Concerns:**
- Can Fang Hongbo’s strategic vision and decisiveness be replicated? History tells us that there are many examples of great companies performing mediocrely after a coaching change.
- Wang Jianguo's actual performance in the position of CEO remains to be seen, and the market has not yet formed an independent evaluation of him.
- Although the He Xiangjian family has withdrawn from daily management, it is still the largest shareholder. Will it affect the independent decision-making of the management in the future?

**Conclusion:** The succession issue is one of the biggest uncertainties for Midea in the next 3-5 years. Not because there are any obvious negative signals, but because a management transition of this level is inherently full of variables.

### 1.3 Overall evaluation of the management team

In addition to Fang Hongbo and Wang Jianguo, Midea’s management team also includes:
- Financial managers such as CFO: The precise execution of capital allocation shows that the financial team is strong
- Heads of each business unit: Young managers such as Guan Jinwei (44 years old, already on the board of directors), President of Building Technology, are on the rise
- Midea Holdings CEO Zhao Jun (48 years old) also joined the board of directors

The management team shows the characteristics of "echelonization, youthfulness, and internal training", which is a manifestation of organizational health.

## 2. Analysis of governance structure

### 2.1 Equity Structure

The He Xiangjian family holds about 30% of Midea Group's shares through Midea Holdings and is the largest shareholder. Management such as Fang Hongbo hold a relatively low shareholding ratio (less than 1%) and mainly participate in the company's growth through equity incentives.

**Positive factors:**
- The interests of major shareholders and small shareholders are aligned (the He family relies on dividends and stock prices to obtain returns)
- Management has equity incentives tied to it
- The He family has let go of daily operations and achieved the separation of ownership and management rights

**Factors to pay attention to:**
- The shareholding ratio of professional managers is low. Is the incentive intensity sufficient?
- If He family increases or decreases its holdings in the future, it may cause market fluctuations
- Is the design of equity incentives reasonable? Will it dilute existing shareholders' equity?

### 2.2 Quality of information disclosure

Midea's information disclosure is at a relatively high level among Chinese listed companies. The annual report is informative, the management discussion section contains substantive content, and the management can face sensitive issues directly at the performance briefing meeting. The 2025 annual report fully discloses the business structure, strategic direction, and risk factors.

### 2.3 Historical Integrity Record

No major financial fraud or information disclosure violations were found. Fang Hongbo's public statements are generally consistent with the company's actual operating conditions, and there is no systematic tendency to "report good news but not bad news."

## 3. Key risk assessment

### 3.1 Macro and industry risks

**Domestic real estate downturn ★★★☆☆ (medium risk)**

Real estate has a strong correlation with demand for home appliances, but this risk should not be overestimated:
- The demand for supporting facilities for new houses is indeed decreasing, but the demand for updating existing houses is still huge
- Midea's building technology business has taken the initiative to diversify into non-real estate fields such as data centers, medical and health care, and rail transit.
- Domestic revenue will still grow by 9.4% in 2025, indicating that the impact of the real estate downturn is controllable

**Weak domestic consumption ★★★☆☆ (medium risk)**

The lack of consumer confidence among residents may affect the demand for replacement of home appliances. However, as home appliances are strictly necessary consumption, they will be less affected than optional consumer goods. Midea's "cost-effective" positioning may actually benefit from the consumption downgrade environment.

### 3.2 Globalization Risks

**Tariffs and Trade Barriers ★★★★☆ (higher risk)**

This is the risk that currently requires the most attention. Trade protectionism in the United States and Europe continues to increase:
- The additional tariffs imposed by the United States may directly affect the profit margin of Midea's exports to the United States.
- However, Midea's revenue share in the US market is "very low" (exact words from the management performance meeting), and its main impact is limited.
- The bigger risk is if the trade war expands to more countries and regions

**Countermeasures:** Midea is realizing "production localization" by building factories overseas (Mexico, Saudi Arabia, Southeast Asia, etc.) to fundamentally avoid tariff risks. The layout of 63 manufacturing bases around the world is prepared for this.

**Exchange rate fluctuation ★★★☆☆ (medium risk)**

Overseas revenue accounts for more than 40%, and fluctuations in the RMB exchange rate directly affect financial statements. The Company uses financial instruments to hedge, but cannot completely eliminate exchange rate effects.

### 3.3 Business risks

**ToB business is not as good as expected ★★★☆☆ (medium risk)**

ToB businesses such as building technology and KUKA Robotics are important reasons why the market gives Midea a higher valuation. If the growth of these businesses is less than expected, or profitability cannot be improved, the market may "return" Midea to the valuation system of a pure home appliance company.

**Kuka’s global competitiveness:** In the field of industrial robots, KUKA faces strong competition from Fanuc (Japan) and ABB (Sweden/Switzerland). KUKA China's orders increased by 35% in the first quarter, but it will still take time to improve its competitiveness on a global scale.

**Technological disruption risk ★★☆☆☆ (lower risk)**

The home appliance industry is less likely to be disrupted by technology. The core technologies of air conditioners, refrigerators and other products have matured, and future innovations will be more incremental. AI and intelligence may change product forms, but they are unlikely to change the basic logic of manufacturing and channels.

### 3.4 Financial Risk

**Goodwill Impairment ★★★☆☆ (medium risk)**

The goodwill formed by the acquisition of KUKA (approximately 30 billion yuan) and Toshiba Home Appliances is relatively large. If the relevant business continues to suffer losses or does not meet expectations, impairment pressure may appear.

**Accounts receivable growth ★★★☆☆ (medium risk)**

The collection cycle of ToB business is usually longer than that of ToC, and the volume of accounts receivable is growing. Accounts receivable turnover days and bad debt ratio need to be continuously tracked.

## 4. Worst case scenario analysis

If the following negative factors occur simultaneously:
- Domestic demand for home appliances fell sharply (growth rate fell below 0%)
- The escalation of the global trade war has caused damage to overseas business
-ToB business growth is less than expected
- Problems with management transition

In the worst-case scenario, Midea's earnings may experience single-digit negative growth, and the PE may be compressed to less than 10 times. But consider:
1. Midea’s fundamentals (domestic leading position in home appliances) will not disappear
2. The policy of returning 100% of profits to shareholders provides a "safety cushion"
3. The company’s balance sheet is healthy and has sufficient financial flexibility

The risk of permanent capital loss is low in the worst-case scenario. This is not a company that will "disappear".

## 5. Positive scenario analysis

If the following positive factors materialize:
- Overseas business continues to grow by more than 15%
- ToB business has successfully become a global leader
- Smooth management transition
- Valuation returns from 13 times to 17-18 times

Under an optimistic scenario, earnings growth of 50% + valuation expansion of 30% within 3-5 years = total return of approximately 100%, and annualized return of approximately 15%-20%.
