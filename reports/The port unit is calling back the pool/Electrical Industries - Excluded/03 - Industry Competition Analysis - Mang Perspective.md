# Power Industry (0006.HK) industry competition analysis

## 1. Industry positioning

Strictly speaking, the electric energy industry is not in a "competitive industry". It is an investment holding company that invests in utility assets that are natural monopolies. Therefore, the focus of the analysis in this chapter is not "who to compete with", but "the quality and prospects of the assets invested".

### Core view: Grid assets are the "winners of energy transition"
Whether future energy comes from solar, wind or nuclear power, electricity will need to be delivered through the grid. Therefore, grid assets are the inevitable beneficiary of the energy transition—more renewable energy access means more investment in grid upgrades, which means a larger asset base and higher regulatory allowed profits.

## 3. Key issues of Munger format

### "What is the independent value of the electric energy industry?"
Frankly – limited. Cheung Kong Infrastructure holds 75% of it, and their investment portfolios are highly overlapping. The independent existence of the electric energy industry is more for historical reasons than commercial necessity.

If merged:
- Eliminate the complexity of cross-shareholdings
- Improve capital efficiency
- Simplify investor decision-making

If not merged:
- Electric Power Industry continues to exist as a "pure dividend carrier"
- Small shareholders enjoy an extremely high dividend payout ratio of >90%

### "Why not just buy Yangtze River Infrastructure?"
Here are the questions every investor considering the power industry must answer:
- If you are looking for the highest dividend yield: choose Electric Energy (4.6% vs CK Infrastructure 4.0%)
- If you pursue growth + dividend balance: choose Cheung Kong Infrastructure
- If you pursue merger arbitrage: choose electric energy (minority shareholders have the opportunity to negotiate a premium)

### "What are the long-term returns for utility investment holding companies?"
The long-term annualized return of global utility holding companies is approximately 5-8% (including dividends). Electric Power Industries' expected return of 6-7% is in the middle of this range. This means: long-term holding will not lose money, but it will not significantly outperform the index.

## 4. Impact of industry trends

### The double-sided impact of the energy transition
1. **Positive**: Increased demand for power grid upgrades → Expanded asset base → Improved profits
2. **Negative**: The gas distribution network may face the risk of decarbonization → asset impairment

### The core impact of the interest rate environment
Utility stock valuations are highly inversely correlated with interest rates. If the world enters an interest rate cut cycle, the electric energy industry will benefit (valuation increases); if interest rates remain high, current valuations will be under pressure.

## 5. Core Conclusions

**Industry competitiveness rating: ★★★☆☆ (3/5)**

The public utility assets held by Electric Power Industry are of excellent quality (global, regulated, and highly monopolistic), but as a "pure investment holding" they lack the ability to create independent value. Its investment value depends entirely on two variables: 1) whether the return on assets is stable; 2) whether the purchase price is reasonable.

Munger's summary: This is not a "business" but a "utility ETF." If the valuation is right (PE < 18 times), it is a high-quality interest-earning tool; if the valuation is on the high side (current PE 22 times), you are paying too high a price for certainty.
