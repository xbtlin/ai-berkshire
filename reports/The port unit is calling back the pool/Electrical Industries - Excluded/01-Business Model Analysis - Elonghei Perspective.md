# Analysis of Business Model of Electric Power Industry (0006.HK)

## 1. The essence of business

Electric Power Industrial is a **pure utility investment holding company**. It does not directly operate any power plants or grids, but instead earns returns on its investments through stakes in utility companies around the world. Essentially, it is a "utility fund."

Its relationship with Cheung Kong Infrastructure: Cheung Kong Infrastructure holds about 75% of the shares of Power Industry, and the investment portfolios of the two are highly overlapping. Buying electric energy industry is largely a subset of "buying Yangtze River Infrastructure".

From Duan Yongping's perspective: This is a "purer interest-earning business" - almost no operations, no innovation, no competition, just holding assets and collecting dividends.

## 2. Moat assessment

**Rating: ★★★★☆ (3.5/5)**

The moat of the electric energy industry is basically the same as that of Yangtze River Infrastructure:
1. The public utility assets invested in have natural monopoly properties
2. Global diversification reduces single market risk
3. Yangtze River Group’s operating experience and brand reputation

However, as an "investment holding" rather than an "operating company", Electric Power Industry itself does not create independent value - its value depends entirely on the rate of return on the assets held.

## 3. Particularity of business model

### The "minimalist model" of electric energy industry
- Very few employees at headquarters
- No direct operations
- Hold utility assets through associates/joint ventures
- Distribute almost all profits as dividends

### Why is the revenue only 771 million but the profit 6.2 billion?
Because the revenue of 771 million is a small amount of direct business revenue, while the profit of 6.2 billion mainly comes from "shared profits of associates/joint ventures". The real value is in equity investments.

## 4. Relationship with Yangtze River Infrastructure—Core Issues

The core question facing investors: **Since CK Infrastructure holds 75% of Electric Power Industry, why not buy CK Infrastructure directly? **

Reasons to buy Power Industries instead of Cheung Kong Infrastructure:
1. Higher dividend payout ratio (>90% vs. CK Infrastructure’s ~65%)
2. More "pure" utility exposure
3. If the two companies merge, small shareholders of Electric Power Industry may receive a premium

Reasons to buy CK Infrastructure instead of Power Industries:
1. A more diversified asset portfolio (Changjiang Infrastructure, transportation, environmental protection, etc.)
2. Valuation is more reasonable (PE 20x vs 22x)
3. 28 consecutive years of distribution record

## 5. Whether management is "doing the right thing"

### Front:
1. Ultra-high dividend payout ratio (>90%)—returning almost all profits to shareholders
2. Global investment diversifies risks
3. Have good investment discipline - do not blindly pursue scale expansion

### Negative/question:
1. How valuable is its independent existence as a holding company?
2. Revenue will fall sharply by 16% in 2025 (although profits will be stable)—are some businesses shrinking?
3. Growth is almost zero—are we "resting on our laurels"?

## 6. Is it a good business?

**Rating: ★★★☆☆ (3/5)**

Electric Energy Industry is essentially not a "business enterprise", but a "dividend tool":
- It holds good assets (global utilities) ✓
- It distributes profits to you (payout rate >90%) ✓
- But it does not grow (profit growth is about 2%/year) △
- And the valuation is expensive (PE 22 times) ✗

From Duan Yongping's perspective: This is not a "business you want to own" - because it does not create value, has no users, and has no differentiation. It is just an "interest-collecting carrier".

## 7. Core Conclusions

Electric Power Industry is a "high-interest long-term bond" - 4.6% dividend rate + 2% growth = annualized return of approximately 6-7%. Attractive in a low interest rate environment, uncompetitive in a high interest rate environment.

Core question: Since CK Infrastructure has covered most of the assets of the power industry, buying CK Infrastructure directly may be a better choice (lower PE, more diversified, and better growth record). The independent investment value of Electric Power Industry depends on: 1) whether you purely pursue the highest dividend payout ratio; 2) whether you gamble on the possibility of a merger between the two companies.
