# Financial Valuation Analysis of Electric Power Industry (0006.HK)

## 1. Financial quality assessment

### Profitability
| Indicators | 2025 | 2024 | Evaluation |
|------|--------|--------|------|
| Revenue | HK$771 million | HK$920 million | -16.1% |
| Profit attributable to shareholders | HK$6.236 billion | HK$6.11 billion | +2% |
| Earnings per share | ~HK$2.92 | ~HK$2.87 | +1.7% |
| ROE | 5-6% | 5-6% | Stable but low |
| Dividend payout ratio | >90% | >90% | Extremely high |

**Profitability Rating: ★★☆☆☆ (2/5)**

ROE of 5-6% is considered "failing" under Buffett's framework. Profit growth was only 2%, almost stagnant. The only bright spot is the ultra-high payout ratio - management acknowledges limited internal reinvestment opportunities and chooses to return money to shareholders.

### Profit source analysis
Almost all of Power Industry's profits come from "share of profits from associates/joint ventures":
- UK electricity/gas distribution network
- Australian Electricity Distribution/Gas Distribution
- Hong Kong Electric
- Canadian/Dutch Utilities

These assets are regulated and have stable but limited returns.

## 2. Valuation analysis

### Current valuation level
- PE (TTM): ~22x
- PB: ~1.2x
- Dividend yield: ~4.6% (based on HK$2.82 per share)
- Implied growth rate: ~2%

### Is PE 22 times reasonable?
A company with a PE of 22 times a ROE of 5-6% is **more expensive**.

Reasons why the market gives a premium:
1. Extremely high and stable dividend payout (>90% dividend payout ratio)
2. Defensive nature of utility assets
3. Yangtze River brand premium
4. The scarce "certain high dividend" target in Hong Kong stocks

But rational assessment:
- The 4.6% dividend yield lacks a comparative advantage in the current high interest rate environment
- Almost zero growth means long-term returns are capped at about 5-6%/year
- As a "pure dividend instrument", its pricing should be as interest rate sensitive as a bond

### Compare with similar assets
| Assets | Rate of Return | Growth Rate | Certainty |
|------|--------|--------|--------|
| Electric Power Industry | 4.6% | ~2% | High |
| Cheung Kong Infrastructure | 4.0% | ~3% | High |
| Hong Kong REITs average | 5-7% | 1-2% | Medium to high |
| 10-Year U.S. Bond | 4.0% | 0% | Extremely High |

Electric Energy Industries' returns are between Treasury bonds and REITs, but its valuation multiples are higher than most REITs.

## 3. Buffett-style investment test

### 1. Can you understand the next 10 years?
**Yes. ** Utility asset returns are highly predictable over the next 10 years.

### 2. Does it have a lasting competitive advantage?
**Indirectly yes. ** The assets invested have natural monopoly barriers, but the electric energy industry itself is only the holder.

### 3. Is the management honest and competent?
**Yes. ** The ultra-high dividend payout ratio shows that management is honest about the reality of "limited growth" and will not waste shareholders' money.

### 4. Does the price have a margin of safety?
**Insufficient. ** PE 22 times is expensive compared to ROE 5-6%. The 4.6% dividend yield has a limited margin of safety in a high interest rate environment.

## 4. Core Conclusions

**Overall Rating: ★★☆☆☆ (2.5/5)**

Electric Power Industry is an "expensive bond":
- Annualized return is about 6-7% (4.6% dividend + 2% growth)
- High certainty
- But PE 22 times overdraft is too much certainty premium
- In an environment with interest rates of 4%+, annual returns of 6-7% are not attractive enough

If you are pursuing similar returns, Cheung Kong Infrastructure (PE 20 times, growth 3%, 28 consecutive years of growth) is a better choice. Electric Energy Industry only has a better risk-return ratio when its PE drops below 18 times (corresponding to a dividend rate of 5.5%+).
