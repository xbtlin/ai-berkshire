## 1. Industry Panorama: The Cruel Arena of China’s New Energy Vehicles

### 1.1 Market structure

China's new energy vehicle market has formed clear echelon stratification:

**First Echelon: One Million Vehicle Level**
- BYD: The absolute king, with annual sales exceeding 4 million vehicles, covering everything from low-end to high-end

**Second echelon: 500,000-800,000 vehicles**
- New energy sector of traditional car companies such as Geely/Changan

**The third echelon: 300,000-500,000 vehicles**
- Li Auto: leader in the family extended-range SUV segment
- Leapmotor: The sales champion of new forces in Q1 2026
-Xpeng Motors: Delivery of 430,000 vehicles in 2025

**The fourth echelon: 200,000-400,000 vehicles**
- NIO: high-end services + battery swap ecosystem
- Xiaomi Auto: Cross-border ecological chain

Among the new car-making forces, Leapmotor, Xpeng, Ideal, and Weilai are the only four remaining companies. However, the pattern of Q1 in 2026 has undergone subtle changes: Leapmotor is at the top of the list, and Ideal and NIO overtake Xpeng. Xpeng delivered 27,415 vehicles in March, a decline in both the single month and the quarter.

### 1.2 Competition Dimension

The current competition in new energy vehicles has evolved from a single dimension of "three electric technologies" to a multi-dimensional competition:

| Competitive dimensions | Leaders | Xpeng’s position |

## 2. Xiaopeng’s competitive advantage: How deep is the moat of intelligent driving?

### 2.1 Technology Leadership

Xpeng’s leadership in the field of intelligent driving is reflected in many aspects:

**Algorithm layer**
- The first batch of "end-to-end" large models in the industry to be put on the bus
- The second generation VLA (Visual-Language-Action) model is about to be mass-produced
- XNGP covers 2,595 cities across the country and has been tested for a total of 7.56 million kilometers.

**Chip layer**
- Self-developed Turing AI smart driving chip, which has been designated by the public
- From "using other people's chips to run your own algorithms" to "using your own chips to run your own algorithms"

**Data Layer**
- 430,000 vehicles on the road generate massive driving data every day
- Data flywheel effect: more cars -> more data -> better models -> better experience -> more users

**Engineering capabilities**
- 5 full releases within 70 days, 35 version iterations
- The AI Tianji system has become one of the fastest iterating smart driving systems in the industry

### 2.2 But how deep is the moat? ——Reverse thinking

**Counter Argument 1: The technology gap is shrinking**

From 2024 to 2025, the intelligent driving capabilities of many car companies will quickly catch up:
- Huawei ADS 3.0 is on par with Xpeng in some scenarios
- BYD's "Eye of the God" high-end smart driving becomes standard equipment in all series
- NIO and Lili both launched end-to-end solutions
- Tesla FSD has accumulated more data in overseas markets

Smart driving does not have a network effect like social networks - users will not choose Xpeng just because "others also use Xpeng". Technological leadership is temporary, not permanent.

**Counter argument 2: Intelligent driving is not the only decision-making factor in buying a car**

Factors that consumers consider when buying a car include: price, appearance, space, brand, service, battery life, etc. Intelligent driving usually ranks behind the top three in decision-making weights. The success of MONA M03 relies more on the ultimate cost-effectiveness of "119,800 high-end smart driving" rather than purely technological leadership.

**Counter argument three: Technology convergence in the era of large models**

The underlying architecture of end-to-end large models is convergent (all are Transformers), and differentiation increasingly relies on data volume and computing power. Computing power can be bought with money, and data can grow with sales - this means that once BYD (annual sales of 4 million vehicles) seriously engages in smart driving, its data volume will far exceed that of Xpeng.

### 2.3 Moat depth assessment

| Moat Type | Suitability | Assessment |
|------------|--------|------|
| Brand moat | Weak | The mentality of "Zhijia = Xiaopeng" has not yet been firmly established |
| Network effects | Medium | Data flywheels exist but are not exclusive |
| Switching costs | Weak | Users can change brands at any time |
| Cost advantage | Medium | Platform-based cost reduction, but not as good as BYD's vertical integration |
| Technology patents | Zhongqiang | Self-developed chips + algorithms, but barriers are lowered in the era of large models |
| Economies of scale | Medium | 430,000 vehicles is a certain scale, but far less than BYD |

**Comprehensive judgment: Xiaopeng has a moat, but it is not a wide moat. To be more precise, it is a moat that is "under development" - only if technology licensing can continue to expand customers can the technical advantages be transformed into real structural barriers. **

## 3. Technology licensing: the possibility of changing the rules of the game

### 3.1 From "selling cars" to "selling shovels"

If intelligent driving is regarded as a "gold rush", then:
- Selling cars = go digging for gold yourself (high risk, high capital, cyclical)
- Technology licensing = selling shovels (low risk, high profit, replicable)

The cooperation between Xpeng and Volkswagen is essentially an attempt to transform from a gold digger to a shovel seller:

- Volkswagen is the world's largest automobile group, with annual sales of nearly 9 million vehicles
- If the cooperation is successful, millions of Volkswagen vehicles will be equipped with Xpeng technology
- This is much larger than the data and revenue generated by Xpeng itself by selling 430,000 vehicles

### 3.2 Similar paths of competitors

| Company | Technology Licensing Model | Progress |
|------|-------------|------|
| Huawei (Intelligent Car Selection Model) | Provides HI solutions to Wenjie/Zhijie/Xiangjie | The most successful, but Huawei does not build complete vehicles |
| Mobileye | Provides ADAS solutions to multiple OEMs | Acquired by Intel, but growth slows down |
| DJI Vehicle Mount | Provides mid- to low-end solutions to many car companies | Obvious cost advantage |
| Xpeng | Providing EEA/VLA/chips to the public | Deep binding, but single customer |

The problem with Xpeng is that it currently only has one technology authorized customer, Volkswagen. If we can expand to 3-5 OEMs within 2-3 years, the moat effect of technology licensing will be exponentially enhanced.

## 4. Thoughts on the final outcome of the industry

### 4.1 Will the new energy automobile industry be like the mobile phone industry?

An analogy worth thinking about:

| Dimensions | The end of the mobile phone industry | The possible end of the automobile industry |
|------|-------------|-------------------|
| Market concentration | High (Apple + Samsung + Huami OV) | Probably medium to high (BYD + 3-5 companies) |
| Profit distribution | Extremely concentrated (Apple takes 80% of profits) | May be similar (BYD/Tesla takes the lion's share) |
| Sources of differentiation | Ecosystem > Hardware | Intelligence > Three Electrics > Mechanical quality |
| The end of the price war | No profit in the mid- and low-end | Now happening |

If the automotive industry moves toward mobile phones, then:
- BYD may be Samsung (full industry chain + scale advantage)
-Tesla may be Apple (brand + ecology + premium)
- Xpeng could be... Qualcomm? (Technical licensing + private brand)

It's an interesting but uncertain analogy. If Xiaopeng can become the "Qualcomm of smart driving", its value will far exceed that of a car company with annual sales of 400,000 vehicles.

### 4.2 When will the price war end?

The current price war in China's new energy vehicle industry has lasted for more than 2 years. The core reasons are:
- Overcapacity - the industry-wide capacity utilization rate is less than 60%
- Too many players - dozens of brands still operating
- Consumption downgrade - the economic environment suppresses purchasing power

Price wars usually end in a knockout round: when enough weak brands drop out, the survivors can regain reasonable profits.

Xiaopeng’s position: in the safe zone but not the comfort zone. 47.6 billion in cash and positive cash flow means that it will not be eliminated in this round of eliminations, but the continued price war will suppress the speed of profit improvement.

### Core judgment

Use reverse thinking to ask: **Under what circumstances will Xiaopeng lose? **

1. The gap in smart driving technology has been smoothed out, and Xpeng has lost its core differentiation - **medium possibility**
2. The price war lasted for more than 3 years, and Xpeng’s cash reserves were exhausted - **low possibility** (47.6 billion cash + positive cash flow)
3. Public cooperation is terminated or no longer expanded - **medium to low possibility** (has entered the deep binding stage)
4. He Xiaopeng opened multiple fronts at the same time, leading to a breakdown in execution - **medium possibility**
5. Policy changes (such as restrictions on L4, legislation on smart driving accidents, etc.) - **Low possibility but high impact**

**Think about it the other way around**: If none of the above five risks occur, what will Xpeng be like in three years? There is a high probability that it is a technology-based automobile company that delivers 600,000-800,000 vehicles annually, has technology licensing coverage for 3-5 OEMs, and physical AI is starting to generate revenue. How much is a company like this worth? Far exceeding the current market value of HK$120 billion.

The key is whether you are willing to take those "medium probability" risks for this "high probability".
