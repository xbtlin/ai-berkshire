# Master Kong Holdings (0322.HK) Risk and Management Assessment

> Analysis dimensions: management quality, family governance, long-term value creation capabilities, factors with Chinese characteristics

## 1. Management evaluation

### Business inheritance of the Wei family

#### Founder Wei Yingzhou

Wei Yingzhou was born in Changhua, Taiwan, in 1958. He started with instant noodles in 1992 and spent 30 years building Master Kong into a Chinese food and beverage giant. He retired in 2018 and handed over the company to the second generation.

Founder’s legacy:
- Established the most powerful FMCG channel network in China
- Developed a mature manufacturing and distribution management system
- Established a "two-wheel drive" (instant noodles + beverages) business structure
- Family holding but introducing the governance tradition of professional managers

#### Wei Hongming (Chairman of the Board of Directors, eldest son)

- Taking over as Chairman of the Board of Directors in 2018
- Salary in 2024 is 9.72 million yuan (+26% year-on-year)
- The role is more strategic and capital-oriented

#### Wei Hongcheng (CEO, third son)

- Officially taking over as CEO in January 2026
- Salary in 2024 is 9.37 million yuan (+26% year-on-year)
- Previously served as executive director, responsible for the instant noodles business group

**Succession Assessment**: The succession of Wei's second generation has been basically completed. Judging from the company's performance in 2025 (profit +20.5%), the succession transition period has performed well. However, it remains to be seen whether the new CEO Wei Hongcheng can maintain or even improve the company's competitiveness during his tenure.

### Professional manager system

It is worth noting that the former CEO Chen Yingrang is a professional manager who is not a family member and has performed steadily for many years in office. The arrangement for Wei Hongcheng to succeed him marked the family's "resumption of power."

**This has two sides**:
- **Positive**: Family interests are highly consistent with company interests (shareholding concentration), and decision-making efficiency may be higher
- **Negative**: Family management may lack outside perspective and professional checks and balances

## 2. Corporate governance analysis

### Equity Structure

The Wei family holds approximately 33% of the shares of Master Kong Holdings through Ting Hsin Group and is the actual controller. Other important shareholders include Itochu Corporation.

### Dividend Policy: Generous or Overdraft?

The dividend will be 3.74 billion yuan in 2024 (dividend payout rate is about 100%), which has triggered market controversy:

**The "generous" view**:
- The company's growth is limited and it is reasonable to return all profits to shareholders
- 30% ROE shows that the company does not need to retain funds to maintain high returns
- Shareholders can make better investment decisions themselves

**The "overdraft" view**:
- The asset-liability ratio is about 65-70%, and the company may be "borrowing money to distribute dividends"
- Cash dividends even slightly exceeded net profits, consuming the balance sheet
- The company may lack ammunition if an opportunity arises that requires investment (e.g. premiumization, new categories)
- High dividends may be a means for family controllers to "cash out"

**Objective Assessment**: Both views are valid. The key is to see whether the company needs large capital investment in the future. If the answer is "no" (maintenance capital expenditure is enough), then high dividends are reasonable; if it is necessary (such as large-scale product innovation or channel digitization), then high dividends are short-sighted.

### Mismatch between salary growth rate and company growth rate

In 2024, Wei Hongming/Wei Hongcheng's salary will increase by 26% and 25% respectively, while the company's revenue will decrease by 1.1%. This mismatch is noteworthy but not serious - the absolute amount is not large (about 10 million per person), and the company's profits did increase by 16.5%.

## 4. Long-term value creation ability

### Master Kong’s “sustainable management” capabilities

**Strong sustainable operation guarantee**:
1. Instant noodles and tea drinks will not disappear – humans need cheap and fast food and drinks
2. Once a channel network is established, it is extremely difficult to subvert—unless there are fundamental changes in the retail ecosystem.
3. Scale effects provide sustainability of cost advantages
4. Although the brand will age, it will not disappear - "Master Kong" is the common memory of generations

**Limitations of value creation**:
1. Unable to create "excess" value through organic growth - the industry is not growing
2. Difficulties in brand extension - consumers are unwilling to pay high-end prices for "Master Kong"
3. Limited space for internationalization – brand recognition is limited to the Chinese cultural circle

### Value judgment in the 10-year dimension

- **Best Scenario** (20% probability): Success in sugar-free tea/high-end noodles + overseas expansion + continued improvement in profit margins, doubling market value
- **Baseline Scenario** (55% probability): Maintain the status quo, earn 5% annual dividend + 3% moderate growth, and the total return in 10 years is about 100%
- **Worst scenario** (25% probability): Accelerated brand aging + substantial shrinkage of instant noodles + falling profit margins, and a 30% drop in market value

Expected annualized return: about 8-10% (including dividends)

## 5. Factors with Chinese characteristics

### The positioning of "Taiwan-funded enterprises" in mainland China

Master Kong is a typical "Taiwan-invested in mainland China" enterprise, which has a unique meaning:

**Front**:
- Combines the management sophistication of Taiwanese capital and the market scale of mainland China
- Not negatively affected by the "national trend" sentiment (already regarded as a "Chinese brand")
- Efficiency issues without state-owned enterprises
- Years of intensive cultivation and complete localization

**Potential Risks**:
- Possible political risks when cross-strait relations become tense (but have never had a real impact in history)
- The center of family interests is in Taiwan, which may affect long-term commitments to mainland business

### The value of "Chinese characteristics" of channel network

In a market as vast as China with fragmented channels, the value of a deep distribution network is magnified:
- There are millions of small retail terminals in China
- The digital penetration rate in lower-tier markets (counties/townships) is still lower than that in first-tier cities
- The delivery efficiency of the "last mile" determines the competitiveness of fast moving consumer goods

The channel network established by Master Kong over decades has a "quasi-monopoly" nature in China's special retail environment. This is a good example of what Li Lu said: "To invest in China, you must understand Chinese characteristics."

## 7. Core Conclusions

Based on Li Lu’s investment philosophy, Master Kong Holdings has the following characteristics:

**Aspects that meet Li Lu’s standards**:
1. **High certainty**: There is a high probability that Master Kong will still be the number one brand of instant noodles/tea beverages in China in ten years.
2. **The moat is real and durable**: The channel network will not be disrupted by technology
3. **Extremely high capital efficiency**: 30% ROE shows that every dollar of capital is working efficiently
4. **Risk is gradual rather than sudden**: There is no risk of "collapse overnight"

**Aspects that are not fully consistent**:
1. **Lack of "long slope"**: No room for exponential growth
2. **Not a "great" company**: It is "good" but does not meet the standard of "excellence"
3. **Opacity of family governance**: Although it is currently performing well, it lacks institutional guarantees

**Final judgment**: Master Kong Holdings is the most suitable long-term holding option among the four Hong Kong stock targets. Reason:
- The highest degree of certainty (no policy risks or technological subversion risks)
- Best capital efficiency (30% ROE)
- Minimal governance risk (compared to the parent company of Budweiser Asia Pacific)
- Reasonable valuation (16 times PE corresponds to 30% ROE)

Recommendation: It can be allocated as a medium position (8-12%), and wait for 12-13 times PE to increase the position to a heavier position. The annualized return is expected to be 8-10%, mainly coming from dividends + moderate appreciation. It is not a target of "ten times in ten years", but a target of "no loss and steady appreciation in ten years".
