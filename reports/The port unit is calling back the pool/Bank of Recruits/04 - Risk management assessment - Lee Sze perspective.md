# I. The core question: Can recruitment cross the current cycle?

Lee Siu’s investment philosophy emphasizes three cores: **(1) understanding a country’s economic development stage; (2) finding good firms in a good industry; and (3) management must be honest, competent, and in the interest of shareholders.** He emphasizes in particular “the honesty of knowledge” – investors must acknowledge that they do not know what, and management must be honest with investors.

The assessment of the commercial banks needs to be placed in the broader context of China’s economic transition. The Chinese banking sector is undergoing a profound transformation: interest rate marketization, real estate deleveraging, local debt resolution, and shift in consumer patterns – macrovariant effects on recruitment may be more important than any micro-factor.

** Conclusions: The risks to recruitment are real but manageable, and management has always been prudent and effective, but the transition to a new position has increased uncertainty. In the case of China, the average rate of recruitment passes through the cycle, under the soft landing scenario of the economy.

# II. Macro-risk assessment

#2.1 Three mountains in China's economy

Li Shu has repeatedly stressed the need to invest in China to understand its unique path to economic development. The Chinese economy is facing three structural problems, each of which is highly relevant to the banking sector.

**(1) Real estate deleveraging**

Real estate is one of the biggest exposures to Chinese bank assets.

- ** Retail housing loans**: LTV (loan value compared to loan) is relatively conservative, with a 0.5% increase in housing loans in 2025, indicating that management has significantly tightened new investment
- ** For public real estate**: The recruitment has been prudent in terms of public lending and the exposure is relatively manageable
- ** Positive signal**: 99% NBS delivery rate and real estate risk phase-out

** Risk rating: <unk> (moderately low)** - Recruitment is more prudent than most banks in real estate open management, but systemic risks cannot be avoided altogether.

**(2) Local debt resolution**

The debt of local government financing platforms is another major risk point for the Chinese financial system.

- According to the International Monetary Fund ' s 2026 assessment, significant vulnerabilities related to the debt backlog of local financing platforms remain
- Recruit as a share-stock bank, usually less open to local city drop platforms than State-owned banks
- Central government is moving forward with debt reduction, and one of the central bank 's priorities for 2026 is "to promote debt risk resolution in the financial support financing platform".

** Risk rating: <unk> (moderately low)** — systemic risk exists but direct exposure to recruitment is relatively limited.

**(3) Insufficient consumer confidence**

This has had the most direct impact on retail sales:

- Credit card loan balance of 2026Q1 decreased by $38.6 billion, resulting in an increase in the negative rate to 1.9 per cent
- Consumer loans, micro-credits, credit risk is also on the rise.
- Management has clearly stated that "market-wide retail credit risk is still on the rise."

** Risk rating: <unk> (moderately high)** - Retail credit risk is the greatest immediate risk to be met.

##2.2 Long-term impact of interest rate environment

The trend of lower Chinese interest rates is unlikely to be reversed in the short term. LPRs have been revised downwards several times, and deposit rates have been reduced even though they are not as large as the loan end.

** Worst-case scenario**: What would the banking sector face if China moved to "Janjaization" (long-term zero interest rate)?

- Japan ' s banking net interest differential dropped from 2 per cent to less than 1 per cent after the 1990s, while ROE has been under 5 per cent for a long time
- The value of the bank unit is consistently low, with the major banks, such as Mitsubishi Day Federation, having a PB of 0.3-0.6 times long.
- Banking has gone from growing to shrinking.

** The probability of full Japaneseization in China is low** (different population structure, different stages of development, greater policy space), but the probability of "modern Japaneseization" — i.e., the continued downward movement of interest rate hubs and the fall of the bank's ROE to 8-10 per cent — is not negligible.

** Risk rating: <unk> (moderately high)** — a chronic disease that does not cause sudden death but continues to erode profitability.

# III. Management assessment

#3.1 Historical management review

The success of the BP bank was largely due to the strategic vision and implementation of successive management positions:

- **Maguila era (1999-2013):** Sets strategic direction for retail banks and lays the foundation for "the king of retailing"
- **The Teewoo era (2013-2022)**: the transition to "big wealth management" was driven by a retail AUM that grew from about 5 trillion to over 12 trillion
- **The Wang Qiang era **: Proposing a "value banking" strategy and "divided" transformation that preserves asset quality and profitability in a reverse wind environment

** Coherence in management heritage is one of the most important soft powers to recruit.** Four of the presidents never experienced major deviations or break-ups in strategic direction. This is rare in Chinese banking – many of the banks have changed frequently as their strategic directors have changed.

# 4.2 SME loan risk

KPMG’s 2026 Risk Radar Report noted that bank lending to SMEs has increased significantly in recent years, resulting in credit risks that are of concern. Small loans, which are being recruited, have grown under the "inclusive finance" policy, and the risk characteristics of these assets are different from those of traditional retail loans, and require close attention.

# 4.3 "Natured" risk in wealth management

With the full net value of bank accounts, clients are required to take the risk of investment losses. If capital markets fluctuate significantly, a significant reversal of net property prices may lead to loss of customers and reputational risk.

## 4.4 Regulatory and policy risks

- ** Interest rate policy**: if LPR continues to move downwards and deposit rates fall insufficient, the difference will be further stressed
- ** Capital adequacy requirement**: Systemically important bank additional capital requirement may limit the allocation of dividends
- **Anti-money-laundering and data security**: increased regulatory capacity may increase compliance costs

# V. Scenario analysis

##5.1 Baseline scenario (50% probability): Moderate recovery

- China ' s economy maintained 4-5 per cent growth and consumption gradually recovered
- Net interest spreads stabilized at 1.7 - 1.8 per cent in the second half of 2026
- ROE is stable at 12-13%.
- Recruitment of shares to PB 1.0-1.2 times

#5.2 Optimization scenario (at 20 per cent probability): strong recovery

- Stimulation policies are over-expected and economic growth is accelerating
- Interest rate cycle reversed, interest difference recovered
- ROE up to 14-15%
- Recruitment of shares to PB 1.3-1.5 times

#5.3 Pessimism scenario (at 25% probability): Continued lowness

- Sustained economic slowdown and low consumer confidence
- The net difference has fallen below 1.5 per cent
- Retail credit failure has increased significantly
- ROE down to 10%.
- Recruiting shares to PB 0.6-0.8 times

## 5.4 Extreme scenario (at 5% probability): systemic risk

- Systematic default on real estate or local debt
- The banking sector as a whole faces a capital adequacy crisis
- It's hard to be alone, even if you're better than your partner.
- PB may drop to 0.4-0.5 times.
