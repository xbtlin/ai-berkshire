More importantly,** structural sticky**: When a customer is hiring mortgages, wage cards, credit cards, money management, insurance, he has a very high cost of moving. This whole-product cover customer relationship is hard to get broken once it is established.

# Three: Threats to the moat

#3.1 from the "Down-Vision Strike" of the State-owned majors

** Threat level: <unk>

This is the biggest competitive threat to recruitment. Business and industry banks, construction banks, etc., invest heavily in retail transformation, pushing recruitment in retail markets with broader network coverage, lower capital costs and national credit endorsements.

Examples include:
- The share of state-owned companies in the mortgage market is being seized by price advantage
- In the area of personal consumption loans, interest rates are generally lower than those of the public sector.
- The State-owned majors are also developing their private banking.

** But the differential advantage of recruitment still remains**: the experience of State-owned large-scale businesses, product innovation and client-level operations are still clearly less sophisticated than recruitment. Retail banks are ultimately "soft" than just prices.

#3.2 Side-wing attack from Internet finance

** Threat level: <unk>

Internet platforms such as ants, tweak finance, and so forth have become powerful users of stickyness in the areas of payments, micro-finance, and consumer lending. Although the expansion of Internet finance has slowed down following tighter regulation, its penetration among young users remains high.

** Response to recruitment**: Increase the digital input, upgrade the App experience, and try to compete positively with Internet platforms online. 123 million monthly events show that this strategy works, but among young users under 25 years of age, the branding influence of recruitment may be weaker than payment of treasures and micro-letters.

# 3.3 Rate marketed "undifferentiated attack"

** Threat level: <unk>

This is not a threat from competitors, but a change in the industry environment. Interest rates continue to drop down and all banks are cut off. The "different spread" is being narrowed, although absolute levels remain ahead.

Manger would say that if the whole pond water level were to fall, even if you were the biggest fish, it would not be possible to avoid the effects. The margin margin margin was reduced from 2.15 to 1.87 per cent, and the excess margin advantage that was recruited was reduced from about 45 bp to about 20-30 bp. If it were to fall further to 1.5 per cent, the differential advantage could be further reduced.

#3.4 Uncertainty in the regulatory environment

** Threat level: <unk>

China’s banking sector is strongly regulated, and policy changes may affect the Bank’s business strategy.
- Adjustment of the self-regulation mechanism for deposit rates
- Policy lending requirements for inclusive finance
- Changes in capital adequacy and allocation policies

Recruitment as a systemically important bank requires more social responsibility and regulatory requirements, which may constrain its ability to operate in a fully marketed logic.

# IV. Evolving trends in competition patterns

# 4.1 from "one big family" to "first-tier team"

Five years ago, the recruitment was almost "dry-dry" in retail banking. But today's competition has changed:

- The retail AUM ** grew from 337 trillion to 479 billion, with a 42% increase, with a clear momentum to catch up
- ** Bank of Peace** is uniquely competitive in insurance marketing and customer cross-marketing, based on the combined financial advantages of the Peace Group

The advantage of recruitment has been to move from "no competitor" to "one competitor but still leads." The moat is still in place, but it's not uncrowded.

# 4.2 The Fountain Management Race

Wealth management is at the heart of the "second growth curve." On this track:

- **Success**: Conveyment systems, product screening capabilities and customer trust leading the stock-stocking line
- **Challenge**: Public Funds Direct Sales, Internet Finance Platforms, Bonds Wealth Management are all diverting customers
- **Variations**: if the individual pension system is extended on a large scale, the role of banks as the main channel may be further strengthened and recruitment is expected to benefit

# 4.3 Where's the king of retail?

Retailing AUM has reached 17 trillion, and growth has inevitably slowed (from 20 per cent per annum to single digits). When size reaches a certain level, marginal growth becomes more difficult.

Manger’s "reverse thinking" reminds us that retail track itself may become crowded when markets are chasing retail transformation. Recruitment needs to be accompanied by new growth engines – a core issue facing the new Governor Wang Xiaoqing.

# # Consolidated rating: <unk> (4/5)

** Summary in Man format**:

The moat of the BP bank comes from two decades of customer trust, low-cost debt structures, and sustained scientific and technological inputs. These advantages are not created overnight, and will not be dismantled in the short term.

But we cannot ignore two structural changes: the interest rate down-side environment is narrowing the spread spread of differentials, and the competitors (especially the state-owned banks) are catching up. The absolute width of the moat is still ahead, but comparative advantage is shrinking.

In Manger’s words:** The recruitment is a company that is "good but not great enough."** In a fully competitive industry, no one can build a moat like Coca-Cola or GEOCO that is almost insurmountable. The moat is enough to keep it in the lead for a long time, but not enough to keep investors completely uncompetitioned.

** The most important point to note**: If the Chinese economy moves towards a long-term low growth and low interest rate "Janjaization" path, all banks' moats will be weakened and recruited. In this scenario, "best bank" may mean only "lowest-down bank." This is the last risk that investors must take into account when valuing their offers.
