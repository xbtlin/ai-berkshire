## 2. Valuation analysis

### Why isn’t PE 5-6 times cheaper?

On the surface, PE 5-6 times seems extremely underestimated, but it needs to be understood:
1. Real estate developers’ profits fluctuate greatly—this year’s profits do not represent “normalized” levels
2. ROE 3-4% means that even if the PE is very low, the actual return you get is only 3-4%/year (if there is no growth)
3. Property revaluation profits may not be sustainable
4. The market is systematically "devaluing" the real estate industry

### PB 0.3-0.4 times - a real safety cushion

PB 0.3-0.4 times means: you bought Changshi’s net assets at a 30-40% discount. These net assets include:
- Properties in core areas of Hong Kong
- Mainland property projects
-Infrastructure investment
- Aircraft leasing assets
- A lot of cash

Even if there is a 30% impairment of assets, PB is still less than 0.6 times. This is a real "hard bottom".

## 3. Buffett-style judgment

### Why doesn’t Buffett usually buy real estate stocks?
1. Profits are unpredictable - sales progress and delivery pace are difficult to accurately predict
2. Heavy assets - a large amount of funds are occupied by land and construction projects
3. The industry is highly cyclical - there is no guarantee that it will still be profitable 10 years from now
4. Management has limited control over outcomes – more depends on the macro environment

### But what is the difference between Changshi?
1. **Extremely low debt** – will not be killed by the cycle
2. **Diversification** – not entirely dependent on property sales
3. **Management Level**——The Li family’s asset allocation capabilities are recognized by the market
4. **Extremely low valuation** – 0.3-0.4x PB provides a huge margin of safety

## 4. Core Conclusions

**Overall Rating: ★★☆☆☆ (2.5/5)**

Changshi is a "deep value + extremely low risk" target: PE 5-6 times, PB 0.3 times, debt ratio 5%, almost impossible to go bankrupt. But an ROE of 3-4% shows that these "cheap" assets are not effectively creating value.

The logic of long-term investment is "value return" or "cycle reversal" rather than "continuous compound interest". It is suitable for patient contrarian investors, but not for long-term holders pursuing annual compound returns of 15%.
