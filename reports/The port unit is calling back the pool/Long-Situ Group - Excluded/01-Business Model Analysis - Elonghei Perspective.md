# Analysis of the business model of Cheung Kong Group (1113.HK)

## 1. The essence of business

The core of Changshi Group is a real estate development + investment holding company**. It buys land, develops and sells properties, while holding investment properties (rent collection) and diversified investments (infrastructure, hotels, aircraft leasing, etc.).

From Duan Yongping's perspective, real estate developers have a fundamental problem: **It is not a "repeatable" good business**. Each development is like a one-time project - you can't rely on "brand loyalty" to get customers to buy another property the next year.

## 2. Moat assessment

**Rating: ★★★☆☆ (3/5)**

### Advantages of existence:
1. **Low-cost land bank**: The cost of land acquired in the early stage is extremely low, and there is a natural profit safety cushion.
2. **Extremely low debt**: The 5% debt ratio is unique among global real estate developers and has strong counter-cyclical capabilities.
3. **Brand Reputation**: Properties developed by the Yangtze River System have market recognition of quality assurance
4. **Diversified Investment**: Not entirely dependent on property sales

### Moat defects:
1. Real estate has no brand loyalty – buyers only look at location and price
2. Project-based operation - every new building starts competition from scratch
3. Highly dependent on the macro environment - interest rates, policies, economic cycles
4. ROE of only 3-4% indicates low capital allocation efficiency

## 3. Special business model - "Asset Reaper"

Cheung Kong is not so much a "real estate developer" as an "asset harvester":
- Buy land at low prices when the market is down
- Sell your home at a high price when the market is booming
- Holding a large number of investment properties in the middle to collect rent
- Simultaneously invest in non-real estate assets such as infrastructure and aircraft leasing

The core competitiveness of this model is **patience and capital strength** - low debt means that you can wait until the best time to act.

## 4. Whether management is "doing the right thing"

### Front:
1. Extremely conservative financial management - 5% debt ratio is the lowest in the global real estate industry
2. Diversification avoids the risk of "full bet on real estate"
3. Accurate timing of asset sales (such as concentrated sales of mainland properties before 2017)

### Negative:
1. Over-conservatism leads to extremely low ROE (3-4%)
2. Large amounts of cash and low-debt assets are not effectively utilized
3. Profit continues to decline - lack of countermeasures when the property sales market is downturn
4. Decline in revaluation of property investment erodes profits

## 5. Is it a good business?

**Rating: ★★☆☆☆ (2/5)**

Real estate development is not Duan Yongping’s favorite business:
- Emphasis on assets and capital ✗
- No user stickiness ✗
- Strong cyclicality ✗
- No pricing power (determined by the market) ✗
- Non-repeatable (each item is a one-time use) ✗

The only advantage of Cheung Kong is that it is "safer than other real estate developers" (low debt), but "safe mediocrity" does not mean "good business."

## 6. Core Conclusions

Cheung Kong Group is a "deep value stock" - PE 5-6 times, extremely low debt, and a large amount of hidden asset value. But an ROE of 3-4% shows that these assets are not being used effectively. Investing in long-term investment is essentially a bet on "the reversal of the real estate cycle" or "the release of value from asset disposal" - not a bet on "the company's continued creation of value."
