# Industrial and Commercial Bank of China (1398.HK) - Industry Competition Analysis

## 1. China’s banking industry landscape

China’s banking industry is a highly regulated, oligarch-dominated, and policy-driven market:

### Market Structure
- The six major banks (Workers, Peasants, China Construction Communications and Postal Savings Bank): account for approximately 45% of the total assets of the banking industry
- Joint stock banks (China Merchants, Industrial Bank, Pudong Development Bank, etc.): about 20%
- City commercial banks + rural commercial banks: about 30%
- Foreign + private banks: <5%

### Internal comparison of the six major banks (2025)
| Bank | Net profit (billion yuan) | ROE | NPL ratio | Net interest margin | Total assets (trillion yuan) |
|------|-------------|-----|--------|--------|-------------|
| ICBC | 3,707 | ~9.5% | 1.31% | 1.28% | 53.48 |
| China Construction Bank | 3,389 | 10.04% | 1.33% | ~1.3% | 45.63 |
| Agricultural Bank of China | ~2,800 | ~9% | ~1.3% | ~1.3% | ~43 |
| Bank of China | ~2,500 | ~8.5% | ~1.3% | ~1.3% | ~36 |

### Industry characteristics
1. **Price Control**: Loan pricing space is limited under the LPR mechanism
2. **Scale Competition**: Major banks use credit growth as KPI and compete to expand
3. **Attributes of policy tools**: Banks are the transmission channel of macro-control
4. **Extremely homogeneous**: products, pricing, and strategies are almost identical

## 2. ICBC’s competitive position ★★★★☆

### Core Competitive Advantages

**1. Absolute scale leadership**
- Total assets are 53.48 trillion yuan, ranking first in the world
- The title of "Universal Bank" is not unfounded - ICBC outlets can be found in any county in China.
- Advantages brought by scale: largest single loan capacity, widest customer coverage, lowest unit operating costs

**2. "Default Selection" for Corporate Customers**
- The preferred cooperative bank for large central enterprises and state-owned enterprises
- The main channel for government funds and fiscal allocations
- Customer stickiness comes from "trust within the system" rather than product differences

**3. Leading in absolute amount of financial technology investment**
- Annual investment in science and technology exceeds 25 billion yuan
- Hundreds of millions of users of digital channels such as mobile banking and ICBC e-Life
- But the input-output ratio may not be optimal

**4. Global layout**
- Overseas institutions are located in 40+ countries
- Provide follow-up services for "Belt and Road" enterprises going overseas
- But the profit contribution from international business is still low

### Competitive disadvantages

**1. Rigid system**
- Long decision-making chain and slow market response
- Product innovation is subject to layers of approval
- Talent incentives are not as good as market-oriented banks (such as China Merchants Bank)

**2. Retail banks are not as good as China Merchants Bank**
- China Merchants Bank's retail brand and wealth management capabilities are significantly ahead
- ICBC's retail sales rely more on "more outlets" rather than "good service"
- There is a gap between APP experience and customer operation sophistication

**3. There is a lot of room for improvement in efficiency**
- The cost-to-income ratio is about 25-28%, which seems low but takes into account the natural distribution of the large number of customers.
- There is a gap between per capita profit creation and China Merchants Bank
- "Big but not good" is a long-term label

## 3. Analysis of multiple thinking models

### "Gravity" model
ICBC is at the "gravity center" of China's banking industry:
- The largest scale attracts the most customers → the most customers reduce unit costs → low cost supports scale expansion
- This cycle makes ICBC’s position almost impossible to surpass
- But it also makes it impossible to escape the fate of "big but mediocre"

### Institutional Economics Perspective
ICBC's competitive advantage largely comes from the ** granted by the ** system rather than from market competition:
- State-owned bank licenses themselves are scarce resources
- Government business naturally flows to major state-owned banks
- The risk of this advantage is that if the system changes (such as allowing more private banks, deregulating interest rates), the advantage may weaken

### "Warren buffer" thinking
What does Buffett look for when buying banks? Low-cost deposits + conservative credit culture + high ROE.
- ICBC has low-cost deposits - Yes
- Conservative credit culture - partly yes (low NPL ratio), but policy lending can distort
- High ROE - No (9.5% is not high enough)

**Conclusion**: ICBC meets the first two items of Buffett's framework, but does not meet the third item.

## 4. Industry trends and ICBC’s adaptability

### Trend 1: Net interest margin continues to narrow
- LPR decline + deposit competition = NIM pressure
- The six major banks are the first to bear the brunt (loans are mainly to large enterprises and have weak bargaining power)
- **Adaptability: ★★☆☆☆**——Passive acceptance, unable to resist the trend

### Trend 2: Financial technology competition
- Ant Group and Tencent’s penetration in payment and microfinance fields
- Although ICBC has invested heavily in science and technology, the pace of innovation within the system is still slow.
- **Adaptability: ★★★☆☆** - catching up but not leading

### Trend 3: Internationalization
- Financial needs along the "Belt and Road"
- Opportunities brought by RMB internationalization
- ICBC has the broadest global presence
- **Adaptability: ★★★★☆**——has natural advantages

### Trend 4: "China Special Valuation" and the reshaping of the value of central enterprises
- Policies promote state-owned enterprises to improve ROE and increase dividends
- ICBC, as one of the largest central enterprises, directly benefits from
- **Adaptability: ★★★★★**——directly promoted by policies

## 5. Sustainability of the moat

"Will ICBC still be around in 50 years?"

**Answer: 100% still there. ** This is the backbone of the nation’s financial system and is unlikely to disappear.

But "still there" and "worth investing in" are two different questions:
- Will ICBC make more money in 50 years? Depends on China’s economic growth and interest rate policy
- Will ROE increase in 50 years? Depends on institutional reform and competitive landscape
- The real source of returns for investors: stable dividends + occasional valuation fixes
