# JD.com — Business model analysis

> Analysis dimensions: business essence, user value, moat, business model sustainability
> Study date: May 16, 2026

---

## 1. What is the essence of business?

JD.com’s core business can be summarized in one sentence: **Use the deterministic experience of self-operated e-commerce to gain user trust, use logistics infrastructure to build fulfillment barriers, and then achieve profit amplification through platformization and service-oriented**.

This is a business model that is "heavy on assets for start-up and light on assets for realization". JD.com initially entered the market with self-operated 3C home appliances, built its own logistics system, and built users' minds with "authenticity guarantee + next-day delivery". When the scale reaches the trillion level, it will begin to transform to third-party platforms (POP merchants), advertising revenue, and logistics externalization, and gradually release profits.

From the perspective of income structure:
- In 2025, goods revenue will be 1,023.8 billion yuan (accounting for 78%), and service revenue will be 285.3 billion yuan (accounting for 22%)
- Service revenue increased by 23.6% year-on-year, much faster than goods revenue of 10.3%
- This trend shows that JD.com is transforming from "selling goods to earn the price difference" to "charging platform fees + advertising fees + logistics fees"

## 2. User value proposition

The core value JD.com provides to users is **certainty**:

1. **Quality Certainty**: The self-operated model naturally filters out fakes, and 3C home appliance category users hardly consider other platforms.
2. **Timeliness certainty**: 211 limited time delivery, next day delivery, supported by more than 1,600 warehouses across the country
3. **Service certainty**: door-to-door installation, trade-in, worry-free after-sales service

This kind of certainty is especially valuable in high-priced categories (home appliances, digital products, beauty products). Users are willing to pay a premium for "less worry".

But the problem is: **Low customer unit price, high-frequency consumption categories, users’ willingness to pay for certainty is limited**. This is the fundamental reason why Pinduoduo can erode JD.com in the field of daily necessities - users feel that buying paper towels and snacks does not require such a high level of certainty.

## 3. Moat analysis

### Strong moat: logistics infrastructure

JD Logistics is China's largest integrated supply chain service provider, with revenue of 217.1 billion yuan and net profit of 7.7 billion yuan in 2025. Key data:

- Over 1,600 warehouses across the country, with a total management area of over 50 million square meters
- Over 90,000 external integrated supply chain customers
- Overseas warehouses cover 25 countries and regions
- More than 900,000 employees (including front-line delivery personnel)

This is an asset that requires more than ten years of continuous investment to build, posing a very high barrier to entry. Even if Douyin and Pinduoduo have traffic advantages, they still cannot match JD.com in terms of fulfillment experience.

### Medium moat: user mind

"Buy 3C on JD.com" has become a conditioned reflex among consumers. But this mind is being eroded from both ends:
- High-end: "What you see is what you get" in Douyin's live broadcast room is competing for users of some brand home appliances
- Low-end: Pinduoduo’s “tens of billions of subsidies” make young users feel that JD.com is not cost-effective enough

### Weak moat: platform ecology

JD.com’s third-party merchant ecosystem still lags behind Taotian. Merchants regard JD.com more as a "supplementary channel" rather than as the main position. This means that JD.com is naturally weaker than Taotian in terms of SKU richness and long-tail category coverage.

## 4. The core contradiction of the business model

JD.com faces a structural contradiction: **The self-operated model brings experience certainty, but it also brings a profit margin ceiling**.

The gross profit margin of self-operated e-commerce is naturally lower than that of the platform model. JD.com's overall gross profit margin in 2025 will be about 15%, while Alibaba's platform model gross profit margin will be more than 35%. This means that JD.com must rely on scale effects and expense rate optimization to achieve profit growth, and its space is limited.

There are three solution paths:
1. **Increase the proportion of third parties**: Let POP merchants contribute more GMV and collect commissions and advertising fees (ongoing)
2. **Logistics externalization**: JD Logistics will serve external customers after its independent listing and dilute fixed costs (realized)
3. **New business expansion**: takeout, instant retail, internationalization (currently trying, but burning money)

## 5. Business logic of takeaway business

JD.com’s takeaway is the biggest strategic bet in 2025. The logical chain is as follows:

- Takeout is a high-frequency consumption scenario, which can significantly increase the frequency of users opening it.
- High frequency drives low frequency, and takeaway users may buy other products on JD.com.
- JD Logistics’ distribution network can be reused, and the marginal cost is lower than building it from scratch.

But the problem is also obvious:
- The food delivery market has been deeply educated by Meituan and Ele.me, and user migration costs are high
- Nearly 30 billion yuan will be invested in 2025, causing net profit to drop from 41.4 billion to 19.6 billion
- Target market share of 30% in 2026 (currently 15%), requiring continued large-scale subsidies
- Antitrust regulators are tightening their stance on the subsidy war

## 6. Doing the right thing and doing things right

From the perspective of "doing the right thing":
- Building self-built logistics is the most correct strategic decision in JD.com’s history, establishing its long-term competitiveness
- Adhere to quality positioning and do not fall into the quagmire of price wars
- Logistics externalization unlocks huge asset value

From the perspective of "doing things right":
- The timing of the food delivery business is questionable - burning cash just when profit margins are just starting to improve
- The third-party platform construction started late and missed the best expansion period of e-commerce.
- The internationalization strategy has been repeated many times, but it has not yet formed a scale.

## 8. Key conclusions

The essence of JD.com's business model is to use heavy asset investment to gain user trust, and then monetize it through scale and platformization. Logistics barriers are its deepest moat and assets that competitors cannot copy in the short term.

The core risk lies in the tension between the strategic rationality and execution rhythm of the food delivery business. If food delivery can achieve 30% market share in 2026 and gradually reduce losses, it will prove that JD.com’s platform transformation is successful; if it continues to burn money but cannot change the competitive landscape, the lessons of community group buying may be repeated.

For long-term investors, the core question that needs to be judged is: Is the barrier value of JD Logistics enough to offset the negative impact of cost-burning food delivery and slowing growth? **
