- Management incentives are time-limited (tenure appraisal system)
- There is a tendency to "play it safe" and will not make extremely risky decisions
- But it also means there won't be "the crazy investment that only founders have"

## 2. Analysis of governance structure

### 2.1 Equity Structure

| Shareholders | Shareholding ratio (approximately) | Role |
|------|---------------|------|
| COFCO (through indirect shareholding) | ~28% | Actual controller |
| Danone Group | ~9.8% | Strategic shareholder (long-term holding) |
| Public Shareholders | ~62% | Outstanding Shares |

**COFCO’s role**:
- COFCO, as a central enterprise, provides credit endorsement and resource support to Mengniu
- However, the management and control of central enterprises also brings some disadvantages: long decision-making chains, bureaucratic tendencies, and "not making mistakes" taking precedence over "doing the right thing"
- COFCO positions Mengniu as the "flagship of COFCO's large consumer sector" and has long-term willingness to hold it

**Danone’s role**:
- As a global dairy giant, Danone has held shares for nearly ten years and provided technical and management experience support.
- Long-term stable shareholding increases governance stability

### 2.2 Governance risk

1. **Frequent management changes**: Mengniu has had six leaders in its 25-year history, with an average change of less than five years. Strategic continuity is the biggest governance risk - if Gao Fei is replaced in 3-5 years, the current reforms may fall by the wayside.

2. **State-owned enterprise assessment orientation**: COFCO’s assessment system may focus more on revenue scale than profit quality, which has a potential tension with Gao Fei’s “profit first” strategy.

3. **Related transactions**: Mengniu has related transactions with other companies in the COFCO system (such as Magicland and COFCO Coca-Cola), and attention needs to be paid to whether they are fair.

## 4. Worst case scenario analysis

### 4.1 Worst case scenario (probability <10%)

Assumption: industry price war worsens + management is replaced + new round of wrong mergers and acquisitions

- Revenue fell further to below 70 billion
- Net profit margin fell back to 3-4%
- Valuation fell to 8-10 times PE
- Market value may drop to HK$35-40 billion (down 35-40%)

### 4.2 Stress Test

**Q: What will happen to Mengniu if its revenue drops by another 10%? **
Answer: Even if the revenue drops to 74 billion, based on a gross profit margin of 39%, the gross profit will still be 28.9 billion. After deducting sales expenses (assuming that the rate remains at 26% = 19.2 billion) and administrative expenses (about 3 billion), the operating profit is still 6 billion + billion, and the cash flow is still healthy. Mengniu will not face an existential crisis.

**Q: If raw milk prices rebound quickly by 20%, how much will Mengniu’s gross profit margin drop? **
Answer: The cost of raw milk accounts for about 60% of operating costs. The cost increased by 12% (=60%x20%), and the gross profit margin dropped from 39.9% to about 35%. Historically, leading companies usually absorb rising costs by raising prices within half a year.

### 4.3 Black Swan Scenario

- **Food Safety Incident**: The melamine incident in 2008 almost caused Mengniu to collapse. If another major food safety incident occurs, the brand could suffer a devastating blow. However, industry supervision has been significantly upgraded, and the probability is extremely low.
- **COFCO decides to sell Mengniu**: The probability is extremely low, but if it happens, it may trigger severe short-term stock price fluctuations.

## 7. Core Conclusions

Li Lu’s summary:

**Buying stocks means buying people. ** Mengniu's management evaluation is the dimension with the lowest score in this study. The reason is not that Gao Fei himself is incompetent, but that Mengniu's governance structure has systemic flaws:

1. **Management changes are too frequent**, and six leaders have led to a lack of strategic continuity.
2. **Professional manager system of central enterprises**, the management is not "people who regard the company as their own home"
3. **Past capital allocation proves** management has made large-scale mistakes (Bellamy’s 7.1 billion wasted)

**But there are positive signs**:
1. Gao Fei is a 26-year-old Mengniu man who has an emotional bond with the company.
2. After taking office, choose to "do the hard and right thing" (clearing impairments, controlling costs and improving efficiency)
3. Launch a three-year shareholder return plan to unleash goodwill
4. The record high cash flow proves that the operating level is improving

**Risk Management Conclusion**: Mengniu's risks are "mediocre risks" rather than "destruction risks". The worst-case scenario is not that the company collapses or suffers huge losses, but that the management is replaced, the strategy swings again, and the gap with Ilili continues to widen. This risk will not cause you to lose more than 50%, but it may prevent you from making money in 3-5 years.

**Core recommendation**: If you want to invest in Mengniu, you need to be mentally prepared for the risk of management change. It is recommended to use "whether Gao Fei is still in office" as a core indicator for continuous tracking - if the management is changed again, it will be a signal for re-evaluation.
