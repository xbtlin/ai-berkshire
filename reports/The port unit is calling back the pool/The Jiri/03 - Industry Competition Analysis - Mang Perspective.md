## 1. Industry Panorama: A battlefield undergoing drastic changes

### 1.1 Fundamentals of China’s automobile market

China's total automobile sales in 2025 will be approximately 34.4 million vehicles, and the China Automobile Association predicts that it will be approximately 34.75 million vehicles in 2026 (+1% year-on-year). The industry growth rate has slowed to single digits. But structural changes are far more important than aggregate amounts:

- **New energy penetration rate accelerates**: New energy sales accounted for approximately 47.9% in 2025, and is expected to exceed 61% in 2026
- **Rise of independent brands**: The market share of independent brands continues to increase, while the share of joint venture brands continues to be squeezed
- **Exports become a new increase**: China has become the world’s largest automobile exporter

This is a market where the total volume has peaked and the structure has differentiated. In such a market, the essence of competition is no longer "making the cake bigger" but "grabbing other people's cake".

### 1.2 Competitive Landscape: A Brutal Race with Increased Concentration

The combined share of the top ten sales groups in 2025 will reach 83.9%, with a significant head effect:

| Ranking | Car Companies | Sales Volume in 2025 | Core Features |
|------|------|-----------|---------|
| 1 | BYD | ~4.6 million vehicles | Vertical integration, cost crushing |
| 2 | SAIC Motor | ~3.5 million vehicles | Mainly joint ventures, slow transformation |
| 3 | Geely Holding | ~4.12 million vehicles | Multiple brands, accelerated integration |
| 4 | FAW Group | — | Hongqi Autonomous + Joint Venture |
| 5 | Changan Automobile | — | Dark Blue + Avita + Qiyuan |
| 6 | Chery Group | — | Export Champion |
| 7 | Great Wall Motors | 1.32 million units | Tank/Haval, strong exporter |

Note: Geely Holding’s sales volume (4.12 million units) includes Volvo China, etc. Geely Automobile’s listed company’s sales volume is 3.02 million units.

This pattern reveals several important facts:

1. **BYD is the absolute leader**, with a scale of 4.6 million vehicles far exceeding other players
2. **Geely is the second largest independent brand**, but there is a clear gap with BYD
3. **Traditional joint venture manufacturers (SAIC, FAW, Dongfeng, GAC) are generally in decline**, and their share is being eroded by independent brands
4. **The share of new forces (Wei Xiaoli, Leipao, etc.) is still small**, with annual sales ranging from 150,000 to 500,000 vehicles.

## 2. Geely’s competitive advantages (moat analysis)

### 2.1 Multi-brand matrix - breadth is both an advantage and a risk

Geely's core strategy is "multiple brands covering multiple price ranges". Different from BYD's "Dynasty/Ocean" dual series, Geely has chosen a model of independent operation of differentiated brands:

**Advantages:**
- Covers the complete price range of RMB 70,000 to RMB 500,000, leaving almost no blank space
- Different brands can conduct differentiated marketing for different user groups
- Diversify the risk of single brand failure

**Risk:**
- Internal friction and resource competition between brands (this is exactly the problem that the Taizhou Declaration aims to solve)
- Management complexity is much higher than BYD's simple structure
-Consumers’ perception of multiple brands may be confusing

The key judgment here is: whether the "One Geely" integration can truly transform the "breadth advantage" of multiple brands into "synergy advantage." If it can, it is a moat; if it cannot, it is a burden.

### 2.2 Scale effect - the value of second place

The scale of 3.02 million units (listed company caliber) is second only to BYD among independent brands, which means:

- **Supply chain bargaining power**: Large-scale procurement reduces parts costs
- **R&D investment allocation**: 17.6 billion yuan in R&D expenses spread over more than 3 million vehicles
- **Channel Coverage**: 4S stores and distribution network cover the whole country
- **Data accumulation**: A large amount of user data supports intelligent iteration

But be soberly aware: In the automotive industry, the gap between second place and first place can be non-linear. BYD's 4.6 million vehicles not only means stronger economies of scale, but more importantly, BYD has established a cost structure advantage that Geely does not have through vertical integration (self-developed batteries, chips, and motors).

### 2.3 Technology platform-an underestimated advantage

Geely’s accumulation in platformization is easily overlooked:

| Platform | Support Brand | Core Competencies |
|------|---------|---------|
| CMA | Lynk & Co, part of Geely | Jointly developed with Volvo, fuel/hybrid |
| SEA is vast | Extreme Krypton, part of the Galaxy | Pure electric platform, 800V architecture |
| GEA | Next generation | New architecture to support future models |
| Thor Hybrid | Galaxy, China Star | Plug-in hybrid/extended range power system |

The moat of platformization is that even if new entrants have funds, it is difficult to build a similar platform matrix in the short term. This is the technological asset accumulated by Geely over the past 20 years of car manufacturing.

### 2.4 Volvo’s “Halo Effect”

Geely's acquisition of Volvo Cars in 2010 was a landmark event in the history of China's automobile industry. Although Volvo Cars (separately listed in Stockholm) is not included in Geely Automobile's consolidated statements, the technological spillover effects brought by Volvo are real:

- CMA platform comes from technical cooperation with Volvo
- Improvement of safety and quality standards
- The Lynk & Co brand relies on Volvo’s brand endorsement
- Experience and channels for global operations

This kind of technology and brand spillover effect is difficult to be copied by competitors.

## 3. Geely’s competitive disadvantages

### 3.1 vs BYD: Gap in cost structure

BYD's core competitiveness lies in vertical integration - it makes its own batteries (Fudi Battery), chips (Fudi Semiconductor), motors and even seats. This "make everything by yourself" model will form a huge cost advantage when the scale is large enough.

Geely is far less vertically integrated than BYD. Although Geely also has its own power battery layout (such as Vair Battery), its scale and technological maturity cannot be compared with Fudi Battery. This means that under the same pricing, BYD has a larger profit margin; under the same profit margin, BYD can set a lower price.

This is the biggest structural disadvantage Geely faces.

### 3.2 vs Huawei: the gap in intelligence

Brands such as Wenjie and Zhijie empowered by Huawei have established strong awareness in intelligent driving and cockpit experience. Although Geely has invested in intelligence (cooperating with Mobileye, developing self-developed smart driving, etc.), it has not yet established the awareness of "intelligent leadership" in the minds of consumers.

Management's statement that "intelligent driving will surpass Tesla FSD within the year" was questioned by the market, which shows that Geely's brand credibility in the field of intelligence still has room for improvement.

### 3.3 vs Chery/BYD: Export gap

Overseas exports are currently the largest incremental market, and overseas bicycle profits are 5-10 percentage points higher than domestic ones. However, Geely will export only 420,000 vehicles in 2025, +0.4% year-on-year, far behind:

- Chery: 1.29 million units
- BYD: 1.04 million vehicles
- Great Wall: over 500,000 vehicles

Possible reasons for weak exports:

- Insufficient brand awareness in overseas markets
- Overseas channel construction lags behind
- Products are not adapted enough to overseas markets
- The overseas positioning of Jikrypton and Lynk & Co brands is not clear enough

The export target for 2026 is 640,000 vehicles (the internal challenge target is 750,000 vehicles). If achieved, it will be an important turning point.

## 4. Future trends of the industry and Geely’s position

### 4.1 Three deterministic trends

1. **The penetration rate of new energy will continue to increase**: It is expected to exceed 60% in 2026, and the withdrawal of fuel vehicles will accelerate
2. **Industry concentration will continue to increase**: Weak brands will be eliminated at an accelerated pace, while the strong ones will remain strong.
3. **Going overseas will become the main engine of growth**: Domestic growth is slowing down, and overseas markets have become a battleground.

### 4.2 Geely’s position in these three trends

| Trends | Geely's Position | Assessment |
|------|-----------|------|
| New energy transformation | New energy accounts for 55%, Galaxy + Ji Krypton is making great efforts | ★★★★☆ At the forefront of the industry |
| Industry integration | Active integration to improve efficiency | ★★★★☆ Integration in the right direction |
| Going overseas | 420,000 vehicles, the lowest growth rate | ★★☆☆☆ Obviously lagging behind |

### 4.3 When will the price war end?

This is the biggest concern for all car investors. Objectively speaking:

- **Short term (2026)**: The price war is likely to continue or even intensify, and the substitution of new energy and fuel will accelerate.
- **Mid-term (2027-2028)**: Competition may become more rational as weaker brands exit
- **Long-term**: The industry will form a pattern dominated by 3-5 large groups

Geely has a high probability of surviving until the day when the "price war ends" - its scale of 3.02 million vehicles and 68.2 billion yuan in cash give it enough buffer. But in the process, margins are likely to remain under pressure.

## 5. Comprehensive assessment of moat

### 5.1 Rating

| Moat Type | Rating | Description |
|-----------|------|------|
| Brand barriers | ★★★☆☆ | Multiple brands have breadth but lack depth, and a single brand is not as influential as BYD |
| Economies of scale | ★★★★☆ | The cost advantage brought by the scale of 3.02 million vehicles is real, but not as good as BYD's vertical integration |
| Technical barriers | ★★★★☆ | Deep platform accumulation, but shortcomings in intelligence |
| Switching costs | ★★☆☆☆ | The automobile industry has low switching costs, and consumers can switch brands at any time |
| Network effect | ★★☆☆☆ | The automotive industry has weak network effects, but intelligence may bring a certain degree of ecological stickiness |

### 5.2 Core Conclusions

**Auspicious moat can be described as "wide but not deep". **

It has a brand matrix covering all price ranges, a scale advantage of more than 3 million vehicles, and profound platform technology accumulation. But it lacks "killer" competitive barriers - it doesn't have BYD's vertical integration cost advantage, it doesn't have Huawei's leading intelligence cognition, and it doesn't have Chery's export scale.

In the ultimate pattern of China's automobile industry, Geely is likely to be "one of the 3-5 companies that survive." But whether it can evolve from "survive" to "live well" depends on:

1. Can integration transform multiple brands from "internal friction" to "synergy"
2. Can going overseas make up for the biggest shortcomings?
3. Can intelligence create discernible differentiation?

The answers to these three questions will determine whether Geely's moat is "wide and shallow" or "wide and deep."
