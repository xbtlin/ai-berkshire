# China Biopharma (1177.HK) – Risk Management Assessment

> Analysis dimensions: management capabilities and integrity, governance structure, key risks, safety margin

## 1. Management evaluation

### 1.1 Core Management Team

| Position | Name | Age | Background |
|------|------|------|------|
| Chairman of the Board of Directors | Xie Qirun | ~30 years old | Graduated from the University of Cambridge, UK, appointed Chairman in 2019 |
| CEO | Xie Chengrun | ~28 years old | Graduated from Wharton School of Business in the United States, appointed CEO in 2022 |
| Senior Vice Chairman | Xie Bing | ~70 years old | Founder, 30 years + experience in pharmaceutical investment management |

**Family inheritance characteristics:**
This is a typical Chinese family business that has been passed down to the third/fourth generation. Xie Bing (the third generation) started his business and established core assets such as Zhengda Tianqing, and now hands over the management to his children (the fourth generation).

### 1.2 Management capability assessment ★★★☆☆

**Advantages:**
- Clear strategic direction: four strategies of "organizational integration, comprehensive innovation, internationalization, and digitalization"
- Firm determination to transform: clearly put forward the goal of innovative drugs accounting for 50% of revenue and basically achieved it
- International perspective: Xie Qirun and Xie Chengrun both have overseas education backgrounds and promote AI+ pharmaceuticals and internationalization
- Resource integration capabilities: able to coordinate the R&D and commercialization of multiple subsidiaries

**Disadvantages:**
- Limited management experience: the two core managers are both born in the 1990s/1995s and have not been in charge of a company with a market value of 100 billion for a short time.
- Actual management and control is questionable: Are a large number of decisions actually led by Xie Binghe’s team of professional managers?
-Historical execution issues: Some mergers and acquisitions integration results are not satisfactory

### 1.3 Management Integrity Assessment ★★★☆☆

**Positive Signs:**
- Performance guidance is relatively conservative, and actual performance has exceeded expectations many times.
- Information disclosure is timely and transparent
- No serious bad history of integrity

**Need to pay attention to:**
- There are many related transactions (transactions within the family system)
- A high proportion of minority shareholders’ interests and a complex group structure
- The difference between adjusted profit and reported profit requires continued attention

### 1.4 Incentive mechanism

The management has a high shareholding ratio (the Xie family controls about 55%), and the interests are highly consistent with those of shareholders. This is the core advantage of family businesses - management is the largest shareholder and there is no agency problem.

But the flip side is: Family holdings also mean that small shareholders have limited say in major decisions.

## 2. Corporate governance structure

### 2.1 Equity Structure

- Tse family: ~55% (held through multiple entities)
- Public shareholding: ~45%
- Institutional investors: including many internationally renowned institutions

### 2.2 Governance Assessment ★★★☆☆

| Governance dimensions | Evaluation |
|----------|------|
| Equity concentration | High concentration among families, small shareholder protection needs attention |
| Board independence | There are independent non-executive directors, but family influence dominates |
| Information transparency | Above average, regular disclosure of operating data |
| Capital allocation discipline | Good, R&D investment is restrained, dividends are reasonable |
| Related transactions | Exist, but the scale is controllable |

### 2.3 The dual nature of family businesses

**Advantages (long-termism):**
- Will not sacrifice long-term strategy for short-term performance (e.g. R&D investment will not be cut due to profit pressure)
- Family reputation is bound to the fate of the company, making major decisions more prudent
- The perspective of intergenerational inheritance is naturally long-term

**Risk Surface (Governance Deficiencies):**
- "Competence Trap": Are family heirs really the most suitable managers?
- The decision-making chain is opaque: it is difficult for external investors to determine the real decision-makers
- Succession risk: Can young management demonstrate sufficient resilience in times of crisis?

## 3. Key risk assessment

### 3.1 Pipeline redemption risk ★★★★☆ (high)

This is the core risk. A considerable part of the company's valuation is based on the expectation that "nearly 20 new products will be approved from 2025 to 2027."

-Clinical failure probability: Even at the NDA stage, there is still a 10-15% failure rate
- Commercialization is less than expected: approval does not mean best-selling, and market competition may limit peak sales
- Time rhythm: Delay in approval will directly affect revenue expectations

**Historical reference**: The company has achieved great success with anlotinib in the past (annual sales of one variety exceed 5 billion), but there is uncertainty as to whether this "super single product" can be replicated.

### 3.2 Policy Risk ★★★☆☆ (medium)

- Centralized procurement continues to expand, but the impact on the company is "close to being cleared"
- The price reduction rate in medical insurance negotiations tends to be moderate (60% reduction in the first year → 30-40% reduction in recent years)
- DRG/DIP may impose restrictions on the use of innovative drugs in hospitals
- The overall policy orientation of the country’s support for innovative drugs is positive

### 3.3 Competitive risk ★★★★☆ (higher)

- Old rivals such as Hengrui compete directly in multiple fields
- Biotech companies may make better products for specific targets
- Multinational pharmaceutical companies (MNCs) accelerate their deployment in China
- "Involution" of drugs with the same target leads to price competition

### 3.4 Macro/Geographic Risk ★★☆☆☆ (lower)

- Mainly domestic business, limited geopolitical impact
- However, if we promote internationalization, we may face the impact of Sino-US relations.
- Macro risks to the long-term payment ability of medical insurance funds

### 3.5 Governance/succession risk ★★★☆☆ (medium)

- Whether young management can handle complex situations
- Will there be any disagreements within the family?
- Stability of key professional manager team

## 4. Safety Margin Analysis

### 4.1 Bottom line of asset value

- The company holds high-quality assets such as Zhengda Tianqing, which has a high asset value even based on conservative valuations.
- Cash on book + short-term investment provides a certain safety cushion
- Although the profit margin of the generic drug business has declined, it still contributes stable cash flow

### 4.2 Valuation safety margin

| Valuation method | Valuation range | Current location |
|----------|----------|----------|
| PE (current earnings) | 24x | Reasonably high |
| PE (2027E profit) | 15-18x | There is a certain margin of safety |
| PEG | 0.8 | Reasonably low |
| PS | ~3x | Medium |

The margin of safety at current valuations is not adequate. If the market gives a 20% valuation discount (PE drops to 20x), the margin of safety is more reasonable.

### 4.3 Scenario analysis

| Scenario | Probability | Target PE | Expected Return |
|------|------|--------|----------|
| Pipeline fully realized | 25% | 30-35x | +50~80% |
| Pipeline partial redemption | 50% | 22-26x | +0~20% |
| The pipeline seriously fell short of expectations | 20% | 12-15x | -30~-40% |
| Black Swan Event | 5% | <10x | -50%+ |

**Expected Return**: Approximately +10-15% (risk adjusted)

## 5. Investor suitability

### 5.1 What type of investors is it suitable for?

- Investors with in-depth understanding of the pharmaceutical industry
- Investors who can track pipeline progress and clinical data
- Medium and long-term investors with an investment cycle of more than 3 years
- Risk tolerance to withstand 20-30% drawdown

### 5.2 What type of investors is it not suitable for?

- Conservative investors who pursue stable and high dividends
- Ordinary investors who are unable to track professional information in the pharmaceutical industry
- Short-term investors with an investment cycle of less than 1 year
