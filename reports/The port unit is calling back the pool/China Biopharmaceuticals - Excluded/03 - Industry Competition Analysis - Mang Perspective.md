# China Biopharma (1177.HK) – Industry Competition Analysis

> Analysis dimensions: industry structure, competition landscape, moat evolution, long-term industry trends

## 1. Industry panorama

### 1.1 Structural changes in China’s pharmaceutical industry

China's pharmaceutical industry is undergoing three major structural changes:

1. **Centralized procurement reshapes the pattern of generic drugs**: Since the "4+7" centralized procurement in 2018, ten rounds of national centralized procurement have been completed, and the average price of generic drugs has dropped by more than 50%, marking the end of the era of huge profits.
2. **The Rise of Innovative Drugs**: With policy encouragement + capital influx + talent return, China’s innovative drugs have entered an explosive period of “from 1 to N”. In 2024, the total overseas licensing transactions of China’s innovative drugs will exceed US$50 billion.
3. **Payment Reform**: DRG/DIP payment method reform, transferring value from the drug side to the service side.

### 1.2 Market size

| Market segments | Size in 2025 (estimated) | Growth rate |
|----------|-------------------|------|
| China’s prescription drug market | ~1.8 trillion yuan | 5-7% |
| Innovative drug market | ~400 billion yuan | 20%+ |
| Generic drug market | ~800 billion yuan | -3~0% |
| Biosimilars market | ~80 billion yuan | 15%+ |

The main driving forces for industry growth: aging population, improved accessibility of innovative drugs, and unmet clinical needs.

### 2.2 Competitive positioning of China Biopharmaceuticals

The company is "middle of the first tier":
- Revenue scale is comparable to Hengrui and CSPC (~30 billion level)
- The transformation progress of innovative drugs lags behind that of Hengrui, but is faster than that of CSPC
- Rich pipeline (nearly 20 approved hospitality products) is a unique advantage
- Valuation is between Hengrui (55x) and Shiyao (10x)

### 2.3 Five Forces Analysis

**Supplier bargaining power: ★★☆☆☆ (low)**
- The supply of raw materials is sufficient, and the company is self-sufficient in some raw materials.
- API cost does not account for a high proportion of preparation cost

**Customer Bargaining Power: ★★★★☆ (High)**
- The National Medical Insurance Bureau is the largest single payer and has strong bargaining power
- Centralized purchasing and medical insurance negotiations continue to suppress drug prices
- However, when innovative drugs have no competing products, the bargaining power lies with the enterprise.

**Threat of substitutes: ★★★☆☆ (medium)**
- Generic drugs face competition of the same variety and substitution through centralized procurement
- Innovative drugs face competition from competing products for the same target and new technological routes
- Traditional Chinese medicine/health products constitute a substitute for some treatment areas

**Threat of New Entrants: ★★★★☆ (Higher)**
- Biotech companies are pouring into the field of innovative drugs
- License-in model lowers the R&D threshold for new entrants
- But large-scale commercialization capabilities are still a barrier

**Competition within the industry: ★★★★☆ (fierce)**
- Popular targets (PD-1, ADC, etc.) are seriously involved
- Price competition intensifies, and the problem of "involutionary innovation" becomes prominent
- Internationalization becomes the key to breakthrough

## 3. Analysis of moat evolution

### 3.1 Historical changes of moat

| 2018-2022 | Impact of centralized purchasing, moat erosion | ★★☆☆☆ |
| 2022-2025 | Innovation transformation + double barriers of scale channels | ★★★☆☆ |
| 2025+ | Innovation pipeline + commercialization platform | ★★★☆☆→★★★★☆? |

### 3.2 Current moat composition

**(1) Pipeline thickness barrier**

The company has more than 100 projects in its research pipeline, covering multiple fields such as oncology, immunity, respiratory, metabolism and so on. This "big and comprehensive" pipeline strategy prevents the company from suffering a fatal blow due to the failure of a single product.

But the problem is: a large number of pipelines does not mean a high quality pipeline. The market values ​​"a few truly blockbuster varieties" rather than "a bunch of mediocre varieties".

**(2) Commercial platform barriers**

The nationwide sales network (10,000+ sales personnel) and academic promotion capabilities are difficult for Biotech companies to replicate in the short term. This allows the company to both commercialize independently and become a partner with other biotechs.

**(3) Brand and doctor relationship**

In core areas such as liver disease and oncology, the company has established long-term relationships with key opinion leaders (KOLs) and prescribers. This trusting relationship is conducive to the rapid promotion of new products.

### 3.3 Threats to the moat

1. **Innovative drug "involution"**: too many drugs with the same target and insufficient differentiation
2. **The Rise of Biotech**: Small companies may make better products in specific areas
3. **Multinational pharmaceutical companies (MNCs) accelerate their deployment in China**: creating competitive pressure in the field of high-end innovative drugs
4. **Technological generational change**: New technologies such as AI pharmaceuticals and gene therapy may subvert the traditional drug research and development model.

## 4. Judgment of long-term industry trends

### 4.1 Trend with higher certainty

- The scale of China’s innovative drug market will continue to expand (aging + increasing affordability)
- Industry concentration will increase (small generic pharmaceutical companies will clear out)
- Internationalization will become the standard for excellent pharmaceutical companies
- The voice of payers will continue to increase

### 4.2 Factors with high uncertainty

- AI’s improvement in drug R&D efficiency
- The impact of Sino-US relations on biomedical cooperation
- The maturity speed of new technology routes such as gene therapy
- Long-term payment ability of medical insurance funds

## 5. Sustainability Assessment of Competitive Advantages

**Core question: Can China Biopharmaceuticals join the "first camp" of innovative drugs in China? **

Optimistic scenario (40% probability):
- Several blockbuster varieties were successfully launched, with innovative drugs accounting for over 70% of revenue
- Breakthrough in internationalization, overseas licensing/overseas direct sales contribute revenue
- Ranked among the "Class A" innovative pharmaceutical companies alongside Hengrui, with valuation revalued to 35-40x

Neutral scenario (probability 40%):
- The pipeline is advancing normally, and the proportion of innovative drugs is steadily increasing.
- Maintaining an upper-middle-class position in the industry, but unable to widen the gap with CSPC and others
- Maintain valuation at 20-25x and make money from profit growth

Pessimistic scenario (20% probability):
- Clinical failure of blockbuster pipelines or commercialization falling short of expectations
- Innovative drugs are also facing price pressure due to intensified competition
- Valuation revised down to below 15x
