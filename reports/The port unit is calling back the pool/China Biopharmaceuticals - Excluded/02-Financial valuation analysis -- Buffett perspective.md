## 2. In-depth analysis of profitability

### 2.1 ROE decomposition (DuPont analysis)

| Factor | Value | Evaluation |
|------|------|------|
| Net profit margin | ~14% | Above average, continuing to improve |
| Asset turnover rate | ~0.35 | Low, heavy on assets + large investment in R&D |
| Equity Multiplier | ~1.7 | Moderate Leverage |
| ROE | ~8% | Low, but on the rise |

The core reasons for low ROE:
1. **Net assets include a large amount of goodwill and intangible assets**: Historical mergers and acquisitions (such as Zhengda Tianqing, Nanjing Zhengda Tianqing, etc.) have accumulated a large amount of goodwill
2. **Capitalization ratio of R&D investment**: Part of R&D expenditure is capitalized as intangible assets
3. **Generic drug business drags down**: Margins of mature products decline

### 2.2 Profit quality

**Positive Signs:**
- Operating cash flow continues to be positive, with significant improvement trends in recent years
- Revenue growth from innovative products is recognized as real sales (non-channel sales)
- Accounts receivable turnover days are relatively stable

**Issues needing attention:**
- There is a big difference between adjusted profit and reported profit (2025H1 reported net profit attributable to parent company 3.39 billion vs adjusted 3.09 billion)
- Asset impairments (mainly related to generic drugs) continue but are reducing in scale
- The proportion of minority shareholders' equity is relatively high, reflecting that the subsidiary is not wholly owned

### 2.3 Gross profit margin trend

The gross profit margin is about 80%, which is normal in the pharmaceutical industry. But it needs to be distinguished:
- The gross profit margin of innovative drugs is usually 85-90%
- Gross profit margin may drop to 50-60% after centralized procurement of generic drugs
- The continued improvement of mixed gross profit margin essentially reflects the optimization of product structure

## 3. Capital allocation analysis

### 3.1 R&D investment

R&D investment in the first half of 2025 is 3.19 billion yuan (estimated to be 6 billion+ for the full year), accounting for approximately 18% of revenue, of which innovative drugs account for 78%.

Horizontal comparison:
-Hengrui Medicine: R&D expense rate is approximately 20%
- CSPC Pharmaceutical Group: R&D expense rate is approximately 15%
- Global large pharmaceutical company average: 15-20%

The company's R&D investment intensity is at a relatively high level in the industry, but the absolute amount is still orders of magnitude behind the world's major pharmaceutical companies.

### 3.2 M&A Strategy

The company has historically established multiple subsidiary platforms through mergers and acquisitions. In recent years, mergers and acquisitions have become more focused on:
- Acquire 55% equity of Haoubo (IVD Diagnostics) in 2024 to expand the diagnostic field
- Multiple License-in transactions introduced overseas innovation pipelines

M&A strategy evaluation: ★★★☆☆
- Advantages: Expand capability boundaries and acquire complementary pipelines
-Risk: Difficulty in integration, risk of goodwill impairment

### 3.3 Shareholder Return

The company's dividend ratio is about 30-40%, and the dividend rate is about 1.5-2%. For a pharmaceutical company in a period of high-growth transformation, this dividend level is reasonable - investing more funds in R&D and pipeline construction.

However, from the perspective of shareholder returns, the stock price performance has been mediocre in the past five years, and investors have not yet received satisfactory returns on capital appreciation.

## 4. Valuation analysis

### 4.1 Relative valuation

| Company | PE (TTM) | PEG | ROE |
|------|-----------|-----|-----|
| Sino Biopharma | ~24x | ~0.8 | ~8% |
| Hengrui Medicine | ~55x | ~2.1 | ~18% |
| CSPC Pharmaceutical Group | ~10x | ~1.0 | ~15% |
| Fosun Pharma | ~18x | ~1.5 | ~7% |

Compared with Hengrui, China Biopharmaceuticals' valuation is at a significant discount (24x vs 55x), but its ROE is also significantly lower (8% vs 18%). Hengrui has a higher proportion of innovative drugs and a more mature pipeline, so it is reasonable to enjoy a valuation premium.

Compared with CSPC, the company's PE is higher but its growth rate is also faster, and its PEG is actually better.

### 4.2 Thoughts on DCF valuation

Assumptions:
- Income CAGR in the next five years: 12-15%
- Net profit margin increased to 16-18% (the proportion of innovation further increased)
- Terminal growth rate: 5%
- Discount rate: 10%

Under optimistic assumptions, the current market value has 20-30% upside; under pessimistic assumptions (the pipeline fails to deliver as expected), there may be a 10-20% downside risk.

### 4.3 Key Valuation Drivers

1. **Can the revenue growth rate of innovative products be maintained at 25%+**: This is the core of whether the valuation can be maintained
2. **Can the net profit margin continue to increase**: From 14% to 18%
3. **Peak sales of major varieties are realized**: TQB3616, TQB2102 and other large varieties
4. **Profit recovery after centralized procurement clearance**: Generic drug sector stops bleeding

## 5. Financial risks

### 5.1 Goodwill risk ★★★☆☆
The goodwill accumulated through historical mergers and acquisitions is relatively large. If the performance of some subsidiaries does not meet expectations, there is a risk of impairment.

### 5.2 Risk of R&D Failure ★★★★☆
The R&D success rate in the pharmaceutical industry is inherently low (clinical phase III success rate is about 50-60%), and large R&D investments may not necessarily be converted into commercial products.

### 5.3 Policy Risk ★★★☆☆
Policies such as price reductions in medical insurance negotiations and expansion of centralized purchasing continue to put pressure on drug prices. Although the inclusion of innovative drugs in medical insurance can increase the volume, the price concession is also 30-60%.

### 5.4 Exchange rate risk ★★☆☆☆
Hong Kong stocks are listed but denominated in RMB, so the impact of exchange rate fluctuations is limited.
