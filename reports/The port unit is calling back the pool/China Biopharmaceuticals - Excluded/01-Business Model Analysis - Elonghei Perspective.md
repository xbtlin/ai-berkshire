# China Biopharma (1177.HK) – Business model analysis

> Analysis dimensions: nature of business model, user value, competition barriers, long-term economic characteristics

## 1. What is the essence of business?

The core business of China Biopharmaceuticals is "R&D-production-sales" of drugs, spanning the two fields of generic drugs and innovative drugs. The company operates through its subsidiaries such as Chia Tai Tianqing, Nanjing Chia Tai Tianqing, and Beijing Tide, covering multiple treatment areas such as liver disease, tumors, respiratory, cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases.

From the perspective of business model evolution, the company is transforming from a "generic drug scale manufacturer" to an "innovation-driven pharmaceutical company":

- **Generic drug model**: Relying on scale effect and channel coverage, volume is exchanged for price in a centralized procurement environment, and profit margins continue to be compressed.
- **Innovative drug model**: Obtain pipeline through independent research and development + License-in, and enjoy pricing rights and excess profits during the patent protection period

In 2025, the revenue from innovative products will be 15.22 billion yuan, accounting for nearly 48% of the total revenue, which is close to "half of the country." This transformation ratio determines the future economic characteristics of the company.

## 2. User value analysis

The "users" of pharmaceutical companies are multi-level:

1. **Patient**: The ultimate beneficiary, who needs drugs with accurate efficacy and controllable side effects.
2. **Doctor**: Prescription decision-maker, focusing on clinical evidence and medication safety
3. **Medical insurance/payer**: Focus on cost performance, the essence of centralized procurement is the bargaining behavior of the payer
4. **Hospital/pharmacy**: channel terminal, focusing on supply stability and profit margins

Sino Biopharmaceuticals has accumulated deep doctors’ wisdom and brand recognition in the field of liver diseases (Runzhong, Mingzheng, etc.). In the field of oncology (anlotinib, etc.), the company has established strong academic promotion capabilities.

**User stickiness evaluation: ★★★☆☆**

The user stickiness of drugs comes from: the irreplaceability of clinical efficacy (strong for innovative drugs), doctors’ prescribing habits (moderate), and patients’ brand recognition (weak for generic drugs). In a centralized procurement environment, generic drugs have almost no user stickiness at all—whoever wins the bid will sell them. Innovative drugs are different and enjoy exclusive status during the patent period.

## 3. Barriers to competition (moat)

### 3.1 R&D pipeline barriers ★★★☆☆

The company invested 3.19 billion yuan in R&D in the first half of 2025, accounting for 18.1% of revenue, of which innovative drug research and development accounted for 78%. Nearly 20 innovative products are expected to be approved from 2025 to 2027, including multiple blockbuster varieties with peak sales exceeding 2 billion yuan.

But please note: China's innovative drug industry has entered the "involution" stage, with many popular targets and many "me-too" drugs. Whether the company's pipeline has truly differentiated competitiveness (Best-in-Class or First-in-Class) is a key issue.

### 3.2 Scale and channel barriers ★★★★☆

As one of the top five pharmaceutical companies in China, the company has a nationwide sales network, a sales team of more than 10,000 people, and strong academic promotion capabilities in the fields of tumors and liver diseases. This channel capability is an important guarantee for the commercialization of innovative drugs - many Biotech companies have pipelines but lack commercialization capabilities.

### 3.3 Brand Barriers ★★☆☆☆

Brand barriers in the pharmaceutical industry are weaker than those in consumer goods. Generic drugs have almost no brand premium after centralized procurement, and the "brand" of innovative drugs is more reflected in clinical data and academic reputation.

### 3.4 Switching Cost ★★☆☆☆

Switching costs for patients and physicians are limited. There is a certain degree of prescription inertia for the use of drugs for chronic diseases, but the reality of forced switching of centralized procurement policies proves that this inertia is not strong.

## 4. Economic characteristics of business model

### Revenue Predictability ★★★☆☆

- Generic drug revenue: centralized procurement fluctuates cyclically, the winning volume is certain but the price pressure is high
- Innovative drug revenue: The path to volume expansion after medical insurance negotiations is relatively clear, but peak sales are uncertain.

### Profitability Trends

| Indicators | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|------|------|------|------|
| Gross profit margin | ~78% | ~79% | ~80% |
| Net profit margin | ~8% | ~10% | ~14% |

The profit margin improvement trend is obvious, mainly benefiting from: the increase in the proportion of innovative products (higher gross profit margin), the clearing of the impact of centralized procurement (decreased impairment pressure), and the optimization of operating efficiency.

### Capital efficiency ★★☆☆☆

ROE is about 8%, which is low in the pharmaceutical industry. The main reasons are:
1. A large amount of R&D investment cannot be converted into profits immediately.
2. Goodwill and intangible assets generated from historical mergers and acquisitions occupy a large amount of net assets
3. Declining profit margins of the generic drug business drag down overall returns

### Free Cash Flow ★★★☆☆

Capital expenditures of pharmaceutical companies are relatively controllable (unlike heavy industries that require large investments in fixed assets), but R&D expenses are high. The company's free cash flow is positive, but due to the impact of R&D investment, the FCF conversion rate is not outstanding.

## 5. Core questioning from Duan Yongping’s perspective

**"Where will this business be in ten years?"**

If the innovative transformation is successful (innovative drugs account for 70%+ of revenue, and annual sales of 3-5 blockbuster varieties exceed 2 billion), the company will transform from a generic drug company with a PE of 10-15 times to an innovative drug company with a PE of 25-35 times, and the market value is expected to double.

If the transformation does not go as expected (pipeline clinical failure, commercialization does not meet expectations, fierce competition leads to share losses), it may be stuck in the "neither up nor down" valuation range for a long time.

**"Is management trustworthy?"**

The fourth generation of the Xie family (Xie Chengrun, born in 1995) serves as CEO. He is young and well-educated but has limited industry experience. The advantage of family inheritance is long-term thinking, but the disadvantage is that decision-making may lack sufficient support from industry experience.

**"Is this price a good deal?"**

A PE of 24 times corresponding to the current profitability level is not cheap, but if you look at the profit growth in 2026-2027 (estimated growth rate of 20-30%), the dynamic PE will drop to 18-20 times. The key is the certainty of pipeline fulfillment.
