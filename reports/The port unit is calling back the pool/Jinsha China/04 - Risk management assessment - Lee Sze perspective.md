### Detailed explanation of key risks

**1. Policy risk - the only "killer" risk**
The peculiarities of the gambling industry in China: it is legal but not encouraged. This means:
- Policies can be tightened sharply in the short term (such as the gambling license renewal crisis in 2022)
-Anti-corruption campaigns may hit VIP business (e.g. 2021)
- The government can affect passenger flow through indirect means such as visas and entry restrictions.

This is the core reason for Sands China’s valuation discount. But please note:
- The new gambling license is valid until 2032, providing a 6-year window of certainty
- Macau gaming tax contributes approximately 80% of the Macau government’s fiscal revenue
- The gaming industry creates more than 50% of jobs in Macau
- Therefore, the probability of the government completely "killing" the gambling industry is extremely low

**2. Economic cycle risk**
Macau gaming revenue is highly correlated with the Chinese economy (especially middle-class consumer confidence). If China's economy continues to slow down, the number of tourists visiting Australia and per capita consumption may decline.

**3. Capital expenditure pressure**
The top 5 resorts need continuous renovation to remain competitive. Billions of Hong Kong dollars in annual capital expenditure suppress free cash flow and dividend capabilities.

Front:

2. MICE business layout leads its peers
3. Renovate properties to stay competitive

Negative:
1. Conservative dividend policy (dividend rate is only 1.6%)
2. Capital expenditure may be on the high side
3. Respond passively to policy risks – but this is indeed an uncontrollable factor

### Parent company relationship
Sands China is 80%+ controlled by LVS. This means:
- Strategic direction is determined by LVS
- Profit distribution is affected by the parent company
- Small shareholders have limited say
- But LVS’s operational capabilities are the core source of Sands China’s competitiveness

## 3. Li Lu’s “asymmetry” analysis

### Downstream protection
1. Gambling until 2032 - 6 years of certainty
2. Gambling "house edge" guarantees positive expected value
3. Mass business is more stable than VIP room
4. 10,829 rooms + conference and exhibition facilities have replacement value

### Upstream driver
1. China’s consumption recovery drives Macau tourism boom
2. Sands China increases its dividend payout ratio (from 1.6% to 4%+)
3. EBITDA increases after completion of new properties/renovations
4. The market returns to 20-25 times PE (the policy risk premium subsides)

### Asymmetry evaluation
**This is one of the most asymmetric targets among the 13 companies:**
- Limited downside (license monopoly + high ROE + property asset value)
- Huge upside (if policy risks subside + consumption recovers, PE can return from 15 times to 25 times = an increase of 60%+)
- The cost of "mistakes" is limited (even with continued undervaluation, the intrinsic value of 20%+ ROE is still growing)

## 4. Scenario Analysis

### Optimistic scenario (30% probability)
- Macau tourism recovers strongly
- Gambling revenue hits record high
- Management increased dividend payout ratio to 40%+
- PE rebounded to 22-25 times, and the stock price rose 50-70%

### Baseline scenario (probability 45%)
- Macau tourism grows moderately
- EBITDA stable at $2.3-2.5 billion
- Low dividends but reinvested profits
- Annualized return of 10-15% (profit growth + modest recovery in valuation)

### Pessimistic scenario (probability 25%)
- Policy tightening or severe economic recession
- Passenger traffic dropped by 20-30%
- EBITDA dropped 30%+
- PE maintains or declines, stock price drops 20-30%

## 5. Core Conclusions

**Risk Management Rating: ★★★★☆ (3.5/5)**

Sands China's risk characteristics are "high volatility + high return potential": policy risks make the stock price fluctuate, but the 20-25% ROE and monopoly barriers ensure long-term value creation capabilities.

From Li Lu's perspective: This is a "controversial but potentially seriously underestimated" target. PE 15-18 times is an obvious value mismatch for companies with ROE 20-25%. If you can tolerate 30% short-term fluctuations and are confident in the stability of Macau's policies in the next six years, Sands China may be the choice with the "highest expected return" among the 13 companies.

Key: It requires independent judgment on policy risks - this is not a question that can be answered through financial analysis, but requires an understanding of China's political and policy trends.
