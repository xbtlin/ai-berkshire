# Sands China (1928.HK) business model analysis

## 1. The essence of business

Sands China operates an integrated resort + gaming business. The core logic is: use non-gaming facilities such as luxury hotels, shopping malls, restaurants, and exhibitions to attract tourists, and then earn excess profits through gaming tables and slot machines. The gaming business is the core of profit, and the non-gaming business is the "drainage tool".

From Duan Yongping's perspective, the gambling business has a unique "mathematical advantage": **The casino always wins**. With every hand and every spin, the casino has a fixed probability advantage (house edge). This means that as long as enough guests come in to gamble, the casino's revenue is almost mathematically certain.

## 2. Moat assessment

**Rating: ★★★★☆ (4/5)**

### Powerful moat:
1. **License Monopoly**: Macau only has 6 gaming licenses. The issuance of new licenses requires the approval of the Legislative Council, which is extremely difficult to obtain.
2. **Property Barriers**: The five major integrated resorts (Venetian, Parisian, Londoner, etc.) cost tens of billions to build and cannot be easily copied.
3. **Brand and Positioning**: The Sands series is positioned as a high-end resort + business conference and exhibition, and has brand recognition in the Asian market
4. **Parent company support**: Las Vegas Sands (LVS) global operating experience and brand endorsement

### Moat defects:
1. Licenses depend on government renewal - the market panicked when new gambling licenses were renewed in 2022
2. The total capacity of the Macau market is limited – unlike the Internet, which can expand infinitely
3. Regulatory policies may be tightened at any time

## 3. User value and experience

Sands China's "users" are divided into two categories:

### VIP Lounge Customers (VIP)
- Accounts for approximately 20-30% of gaming revenue
- High single bets and huge profits
- But it is greatly affected by the economic cycle and anti-corruption policies
- The proportion has continued to decline in recent years

### Mass Market
- Accounts for approximately 70-80% of gaming revenue
- Low single bet but high customer flow
- More stable and less affected by policies
- Key drivers of growth

### Non-gaming revenue
- Hotels, restaurants, shopping, exhibitions
- Accounts for approximately 30% of total revenue
- The profit margin is lower than gambling, but it improves the overall experience
- Helps attract family/business customers

From the perspective of Duan Yongping's "user mind": Sands China's positioning in the minds of users is "Macau high-end vacation experience" - this is not just gambling, but also "experiential consumption". This positioning has more staying power than a pure casino.

## 4. Whether management is "doing the right thing"

### Correct strategic choice:
1. Transform from VIP room to mass market - reduce dependence on big customers
2. Vigorously develop MICE (convention and exhibition tourism) - increase non-gaming revenue
3. Continuously renovate properties – maintain competitiveness
4. The Londoner Macau Project – New Growth Drivers

### Doubts exist:
1. Net profit will fall by 3.3% year-on-year in 2025, and EBITDA profit margin will also decline - is it temporary or a trend?
2. Capital expenditures continue to be high (property renovation/construction), affecting free cash flow
3. The dividend distribution is conservative (1.6% dividend rate) and not friendly enough to value investors.
4. The capital needs of parent company LVS may affect Sands China’s dividend policy

## 5. Is it a good business?

**Rating: ★★★★☆ (4/5)**

This may be the one with the best business quality among the 13 companies:
- **ROE 20-25%**: Far higher than the other 12 companies, indicating an extremely high return on capital
- **License monopoly**: extremely high barriers to entry
- **Mathematical Advantage**: The casino's "house edge" ensures long-term stable profitability
- **EBITDA profit margin 30%**: a very high level in the asset-heavy industry
- **Beneficiaries of consumption upgrade**: China’s per capita consumption growth is good for tourism and gambling

But there are also things that are "not good enough":
- Reliance on the Macau single market
- The unpredictability of gambling regulation
- Capital intensive - property requires continuous large investment
- Dividends are low (only 1.6% dividend yield)

Duan Yongping's possible evaluation: This is a "good business" (high ROE, monopoly license), but the policy risks make people not completely reassured. If the regulatory environment is stable, it is an excellent investment target.

## 6. Core Conclusions

Sands China has the best business model quality (high ROE, monopoly barriers, stable mathematical advantages) among the 13 companies. The core question is not "Is the business good or not" (the answer is yes), but "How big is the regulatory risk" and "Is the valuation reasonable?" If you can buy it at a reasonable price during a period of policy stability, this is a target worth holding for the long term.
