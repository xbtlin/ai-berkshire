#ChinaFeihe: Business model analysis

> Analysis dimensions: business nature, user value, moat depth, business model sustainability

---

## 1. What kind of business is this?

### 1.1 Nature of business

Infant formula milk powder is essentially a high-trust consumer product business. Different from ordinary fast-moving consumer goods, the purchase decision of milk powder has the following characteristics:

- **Extremely high brand loyalty**: Once a mother chooses a brand, she rarely changes it unless something goes wrong (high switching costs)
- **Low price sensitivity**: Parents are willing to pay a premium for "safety" and "nutrition", and rarely switch brands because they are dozens of dollars cheaper.
- **Information asymmetry**: Consumers cannot directly verify the quality of milk powder and rely heavily on brand trust
- **Limited consumption frequency but high unit price**: The life cycle of users aged 0-3 is about 3 years, and the annual consumption is about 6,000-12,000 yuan

These characteristics mean that once brand trust is established, the company can achieve high gross profit and high repurchase, but the user pool itself is limited and is constantly being lost (children will no longer consume when they grow up).

### 1.2 Feihe’s user value proposition

Feihe's core positioning is "more suitable for Chinese babies' physiques." Although this slogan is scientifically controversial, it is extremely effective at the marketing level:

- Taking advantage of the trust-rebuilding window for domestically produced milk powder after the melamine incident in 2008
- Use the concept of "Chinese body constitution" to differentiate international brands
- Deliver quality signals through high-end pricing (a single can of Xingfeifan Zhuorui is about 400-500 yuan)

**Core Judgment**: Feihe’s user value is based more on brand mentality and emotional trust than on technical barriers. This is both an advantage (hard to break in the short term) and a risk (trust breaks down very quickly).

## 2. Moat analysis

### 2.1 Brand moat ★★★★☆

**Front**:
- The No. 1 brand of domestic infant formula, ranking No. 1 in sales across all channels for many years in a row
- High brand awareness, especially in second- and third-tier cities and lower-tier markets
- An asset of trust established through continuous investment for more than ten years after "Melamine"

**Hidden concerns**:
- Brand premium is based on the narrative of "domestic safety", but as time goes by, the differentiated value of this narrative is diminishing
- Yili quickly catches up with its stronger omni-channel coverage and lower prices
- The brand preferences of the younger generation of consumers (post-95s/post-00s mothers) may be different from those of the previous generation

### 2.2 Channel moat ★★★☆☆

**Front**:
- Established a single-layer dealer system covering the whole country, with approximately 2,700 dealers
- Deeply develop mother and baby store channels and build trust through "face-to-face" marketing
- QR code scanning systems, electronic fences and other technical means to control channelized goods

**Hidden concerns**:
- Maternity and baby store channels are shrinking (offline proportion dropped from 69% to below 60%)
- Feihe does not have an advantage in online channels, and its e-commerce gameplay is different from its traditional approach.
- The 1.2 billion milk powder subsidy (trade-in, etc.) for milk powder guarantees short-term supply, but it destroys the price system and dealer confidence.
- The number of dealers has dropped from 2800 to 2700, putting the channel ecology under pressure

### 2.3 Product/Technology Moat ★★★☆☆

**Front**:
- Whole industry chain layout: own ranch + factory, full control from milk source to finished product
- Launch "Milk Protein Fresh Extraction Technology" in 2025 to achieve independent production of 11 key milk protein raw materials
- The ultra-high-end product Xingfeifan Zhuorui will sell 6.7 billion in 2024 (+60% year-on-year), and its product upgrade capabilities have been verified

**Hidden concerns**:
- Infant formula formulas are highly homogeneous, making it difficult for consumers to perceive technical differences.
- R&D expenses account for a low proportion (about 3%), much lower than sales expenses (35%)
- The entire industry chain becomes a burden during the industry downturn (overcapacity, impairment of biological assets)

### 2.4 Economies of scale moat ★★☆☆☆

The infant formula industry has limited economies of scale. Although Feihe is the industry leader, its scale advantage is mainly reflected in the amortization effect of advertising, and the scale advantage on the production side is not significant (the industry's gross profit margin is generally higher).

## 3. Key contradictions in business models

### 3.1 High marketing dependence

The essence of Feihe's business model is "marketing-driven":

- Sales expense ratio in 2024 is 34.6% (approximately 7.2 billion yuan)
- In 2025, when revenue dropped by 12.7%, sales expenses did not drop by the same ratio.
- Invest heavily in local promotion activities, mother’s classes, KOL cooperation, etc.

**Comparison**: Kweichow Moutai’s sales expense rate is about 3%, and Haitian Flavor Industry’s is about 7%. Consumer products that truly have a deep brand moat do not require continued high-intensity marketing to maintain share. Feihe's high marketing expenses suggest its brand loyalty may not be as strong as it appears.

### 3.2 Market size continues to shrink

This is the most fundamental problem facing Feihe:

- The number of newborns in China will drop from 17.86 million in 2016 to about 9 million in 2024
- The stock of children aged 0-3 years shrank from 41.9 million to approximately 26.5 million
- Even if Feihe’s market share increases from 17% to 25%, the total volume will still decrease

**Key question**: In a market that continues to shrink, can the "share increase" of the leader offset the "shrinking cake"? Judging from the revenue growth rate of -12.7% in 2025, the answer is no for the time being.

### 3.3 Feasibility of the second curve

The second curves Feihe is trying include:

- **Adult Milk Powder**: Aiben Protein Series, targeted at middle-aged people aged 35+
- **Children's milk powder/snacks**: Fall in love with vegetable cheese series
- **Overseas markets**: layout in Canada and other places

In 2025, the revenue of "other dairy products" will be 2.06 billion yuan (accounting for 11.4%, +36% year-on-year), but the gross profit margin is only 1% - which means that the second curve is still in the stage of "burning money in exchange for scale" and is far from proving commercial feasibility.

## 4. Core questions from Duan Yongping’s perspective

### "If you don't look at the stock price, are you willing to buy 100% of this company?"

**Hesitation**:
1. The main business is in a structural downward channel, which is not a cyclical fluctuation.
2. High marketing expenses indicate that the "moat" needs continuous maintenance and is not deep enough.
3. The second curve has not yet been passed, and adult milk powder is a highly competitive Red Sea market.
4. Management still maintains high salaries and dividends even when profits have been cut in half, and the alignment of shareholders’ interests is questionable.

**Attraction**:
1. Baby powder is indeed a good business (high gross profit, strong cash flow, strong demand)
2. Industry concentration is still increasing, and Feihe is expected to gain a share of exiters
3. 7.5 billion cash in hand, financial security
4. If the birth population stabilizes and rebounds from 2026 to 2028 (promoted by policies), Feihe will be the biggest beneficiary.

## 5. Comprehensive assessment

| Dimensions | Rating | Description |
|------|------|------|
| Business quality | ★★★★☆ | Infant powder itself is a good business, but the shrinking market is dragging it down |
| Brand moat | ★★★★☆ | Strong but not unshakable, Yili is approaching |
| Channel moat | ★★★☆☆ | Offline advantages are weakening, and online is not prominent |
| Business model sustainability | ★★★☆☆ | The main business ceiling is clear, but the second curve has not been proven |
| User value creation | ★★★★☆ | Product quality is reliable, but the premium comes from marketing rather than technology |

**Overall Business Model Rating: ★★★☆☆ (3.5/5)**

Feihe is a good business that is going bad. The moat remains, but is slowly becoming shallower. Key observation points: When will the second curve contribute meaningful profits, and whether Yili will surpass Feihe in the next 1-2 years.
