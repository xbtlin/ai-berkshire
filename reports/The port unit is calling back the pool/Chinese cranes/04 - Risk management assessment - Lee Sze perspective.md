#China Feihe: Risk and Management Assessment

> Analysis dimensions: management quality and capabilities, corporate governance, key risk list, worst-case scenario analysis

## 1. Management evaluation

### 1.1 Leng Youbin himself

Leng Youbin is the soul of Feihe. He has been deeply involved in the dairy industry for nearly 40 years since he joined Zhaoguang Dairy Factory in 1987. His experience includes three key choices:

**First time (2001)**: Leading the team to leave a state-owned enterprise to start a business and found Feihe Dairy
**Second time (2005-2013)**: Listed in the United States → Privatization → Capital operation to prepare for Hong Kong stock listing
**The third time (after 2008)**: Feihe was not affected by the melamine incident and seized the historical window to rebuild trust in domestic milk powder.

**Ability Assessment ★★★★☆**:
- Strategic vision: Seize the key opportunity of domestic substitution after 2008, and propose a precise positioning that is "more suitable for the physique of Chinese babies"
- Execution ability: Establishing the entire industry chain, promoting channel sinking, and creating super high-end single products have all achieved results.
- Resilience: Experienced many tests such as delisting of US stocks and industry winter

**But ability ≠ character**. Character and alignment of interests often matter more than ability when evaluating management.

### 1.2 Interest alignment issue ★★★☆☆

**Leng Youbin’s interest structure**:

| Revenue Sources | Estimated Amount 2025 |
|----------|---------------|
| Annual salary | About 18.25 million yuan |
| Dividend income (43% shareholding) | About 870 million yuan |
| Total | About 900 million yuan |

**Key Questions**:

1. **Who benefits the most from excess dividends? ** The dividend rate in 2025 is 104.8% (dividends exceed net profit), and Leng Youbin, as the 43% major shareholder, received 870 million yuan in dividends. Choosing excessive dividends when net profits are cut in half, is this "giving back to all shareholders" or "major shareholders cashing out"?

2. **Salary and performance are decoupled**: Net profit dropped from 7.4 billion to 1.9 billion (a drop of 75%), and Leng Youbin’s annual salary was only slightly adjusted from 16.91 million to 18.25 million. Ordinary employees have suffered layoffs and salary cuts, while executive compensation has remained almost unchanged.

3. **The true purpose of the repurchase**: Is the 1 billion repurchase mainly to hedge against the dilution of management’s equity incentives?

### 1.3 Corporate governance structure ★★★☆☆

**Points of Concern**:

- **One share dominates**: Leng Youbin holds about 43-46% of the shares and has absolute control.
- **Family Color**: There are family members of Leng Youbin among the core management of the company
- **History of U.S. stock delisting**: When privatized from U.S. stocks in 2013, the consideration received by small shareholders was controversial.
- **The independence of independent directors is questionable**: When major shareholders are highly concentrated, can independent directors effectively check and balance?

**Positives**:
- Information disclosure after listing on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange is relatively standardized
- The company undergoes four major audits every year
- The high dividend policy at least shows that there is no "only in but no out" capital misappropriation

## 2. Management’s strategic assessment to deal with industry difficulties

### 2.1 Strategic Choice: Is it Right?

| Strategy | Evaluation | Risk |
|------|------|------|
| Stick to high-end positioning | ★★★★☆ Correct | The trend of consumption downgrade may invalidate the positioning |
| 1.2 billion subsidy guarantee share | ★★☆☆☆ Disputed | Undermining the price system and damaging dealer confidence |
| The second curve of all ages | ★★★☆☆ Direction | Questionable execution and profitability (gross profit margin 1%) |
| Excess dividends + buyback | ★★★☆☆ Useful in the short term | May consume ammunition in the long term and weaken investment capabilities |
| Overseas expansion | ★★☆☆☆ Early stage | Overseas demand for Chinese milk powder is extremely small |

### 2.2 Assessment of strategic execution capabilities

**Well done**:
- The success of Xingfeifan Zhuorui shows that product upgrade capabilities are still there
- Quality control throughout the entire industry chain continues to be stable, with no food safety incidents
- Overall conservative financial discipline (zero debt)

**Poorly done**:
- Still losing money after 2 years of advancing the second curve
- Slow transformation of online channels
- The subsidy strategy lacks systematicity and is highly emergency-oriented
- Insufficient prediction of industry shrinkage - the 2024 annual report is still talking about "growth", but it will plummet in 2025

### 2.3 Is management "honest to reality"?

Li Lu's research emphasizes whether management dares to admit difficulties. From public information:

- Feihe predicts in its financial report that "the number of newborns will rebound from 2026 to 2028" - this optimistic expectation lacks basis
- The progress of the second curve is less disclosed, especially the loss data is not transparent enough
- The negative effects of the subsidy war (channel conflicts, price system disorder) are downplayed in public disclosures

**Judgment**: The management has a certain degree of "selective optimism" and is not frank enough about difficulties.

## 4. Worst case scenario analysis

### 4.1 "Chronic Decline" Scenario (Most Likely Bad Scenario)

- The number of births continues to fall below 7.5 million
- Yili will surpass Feihe and become the first in the industry in 2027
- The second curve continues to lose money for 3-5 years and still has no profit.
- Feihe's revenue dropped to 12-14 billion yuan, with net profit margin maintained at 10-12%
- The stock price remains in the range of HK$2.5-3.5, and has been trading sideways for a long time.

**Investor outcome**: Relying on a 6% dividend rate to obtain annual returns, but the capital appreciation is close to zero. Total returns are around 6-8% per year - not bad but not great either.

### 4.2 "Accelerated deterioration" scenario (lower probability)

- The subsidy war is out of control → dealers rebel on a large scale → the channel collapses
- Or food safety incidents may occur → brand trust will return to zero
- Revenue quickly fell below 10 billion
- Management's "last party" - the company's value returns to zero after all the cash is distributed

**Investor outcome**: Permanent capital loss of 50-70%.

### 4.3 "Bottom reversal" scenario (requires luck)

- The number of births in 2026-2027 unexpectedly rose to 10 million+
- The industry is being cleared out at an accelerated pace, with Feihe’s market share rising to 25%
- The second curve achieves breakeven
- Profit margin rebounds to 15%+
- The stock price rebounded from HK$3.4 to HK$6-8

**Investor outcome**: 80-130% return in 2-3 years. But this scenario requires multiple assumptions to be true at the same time.

| Long-termism | ★★★☆☆ | The layout of the second curve is correct, but the execution is quick and easy |

### "Is this a business you understand?"

Infant milk powder is a relatively simple business with clear core variables: birth population × customer unit price × market share. Investors can track these key indicators to gauge direction. This is considered an "understandable" business.

### "Can you handle the worst?"

If the position is limited (for example, no more than 5% of the portfolio), the worst-case loss can be controlled. But if heavy bets reverse, the risk-reward ratio may not be superior enough.

| Downside protection | ★★★★☆ | Zero debt + high cash + high dividends provide bottom support |

**Overall Risk/Management Rating: ★★★☆☆ (3.2/5)**

Management is capable but alignment of interests is questionable. The biggest risk is not management mistakes, but the structural decline of the industry - something no management can change. Feihe's advantage lies in its strong financial safety cushion (7.5 billion in cash and zero debt), which determines that it will not "die", but "whether it will survive well" depends on when the industry bottoms out and when the second curve breaks through.
