#China Feihe: Industry Competition Analysis

> Analysis dimensions: industry structure evolution, competition landscape, end-game deduction, reverse thinking

---

## 1. Industry macro environment - a shrinking market

### 1.1 Demand Side: Structural Downturn

China's infant formula market is experiencing unprecedented contraction:

| Indicators | Data |
|------|------|
| Newborns in 2016 | 17.86 million |
| Newborn babies in 2020 | 12 million |
| Newborns in 2023 | 9.02 million |
| Newborns in 2024 | About 9 million (flat or slightly down) |
| The existing population aged 0-3 years | shrunk from 41.9 million to about 26.5 million |
| Market size | Shrinking from approximately 160 billion yuan to 120-130 billion yuan |

**Key judgment**: The decline in the birth population is not a cyclical fluctuation, but a structural trend. Even if maternity subsidy policies are implemented in various places, it is optimistically estimated that the number of newborns from 2026 to 2028 will only change from a decline to a "low stabilization" (8-9.5 million), rather than a reversal.

### 1.2 Supply side: serious overcapacity

The current capacity utilization rate of the infant formula industry is less than 50%. A large number of small and medium-sized brands are facing exit pressure, and the industry is entering a "clearance period":

- The number of infant formula brands will be approximately 400 in 2023, and is expected to shrink to less than 200 by 2027
- The new national standard (implemented in 2023) has raised the registration threshold and accelerated the exit of small and medium-sized brands.
- But the share release rate of exiters is < the industry shrinkage rate, and the leader may not benefit from it.

### 1.3 Price: High-end meets ceiling

The growth logic of the industry in the past few years has been "volume decreases and price increases" - the number of births decreases but the unit price per customer increases. But this logic begins to fail in 2025:

- The penetration rate of ultra-high-end milk powder (400 yuan +/can) is close to the limit
- Under the trend of downgrading consumption, some consumers have begun to "downgrade" or choose cost-effective brands
- Feihe’s 1.2 billion subsidy is essentially a disguised price reduction, indicating that the high-end pricing strategy is under pressure

## 2. Competitive Landscape Analysis

### 2.1 Main competitors

| Brand | Market Share (2024) | Features | Threat Level |
|------|---------------|------|----------|
| Feihe | ~17.5% | High-end positioning, strong offline | — |
| Yili (Golden Crown) | ~16.3% | Omni-channel coverage, price bandwidth | ★★★★★ |
| Junlebao | ~8% | Cost-effective route, fast growth rate | ★★★★☆ |
| Danone (Aptamil) | ~7% | International brand, high-end | ★★★☆☆ |
| a2 | ~4% | Differentiated positioning | ★★☆☆☆ |
| Wyeth | ~5% | Decline, price reduction | ★★☆☆☆ |

### 2.2 The biggest threat: Yili

The gap between Yili and Feihe has narrowed from "clear lead" to "within a millimeter" (17.5% vs 16.3%). Yili’s competitive advantages:

- **Broader channels**: Yili has 5 million+ terminal outlets, covering far more than Feihe
- **Wider price range**: Covering everything from mass to ultra-high-end, Feihe mainly focuses on mid-to-high-end and above
- **Category synergy**: liquid milk + milk powder + cheese, higher frequency of brand exposure
- **More resources**: Yili’s overall revenue exceeds 100 billion, and it can withstand the price war of infant powder

In the first half of 2025, Yili has claimed that its "overall infant milk powder retail sales market share has ranked first in the country" - although the statistical caliber is controversial, the trend cannot be ignored.

### 2.3 Junlebao’s low price impact

Junlebao has rapidly grown in volume with its "high quality and affordable" positioning, and has a structural advantage amid the consumption downgrade trend. Its threat to Feihe is mainly reflected in the competition for third- and fourth-tier cities.

### 2.4 Strategic changes of international brands

Wyeth, Danone and other international brands chose to cut prices and promote sales after their market share declined, further squeezing the market space. This "rather lose than exit" strategy increases the complexity of industry consolidation.

## 3. Changes in Feihe’s competitive position

### 3.1 Share change trend

Feihe’s market share will begin to loosen after peaking in 2022:
- 2020-2022: Rapidly increase to 20%+
- 2023-2024: Maintain at 17-18%
- 2025: Facing direct challenge from Yili

### 3.2 Feihe’s competitive strategy and its costs

**Subsidy war (1.2 billion trade-in)**:
- Sales volume was maintained in the short term, but gross profit margin was eroded
- Destroyed the dealer's price system
- Some subsidized products flow into the secondary market and are resold at low prices
- Essentially trading profits for share, which is dangerous in a shrinking market

**Ultra-high-end upgrade (Xingfeifan Zhuorui)**:
- Zhuorui’s sales in 2024 are 6.7 billion (+60%), replacing the classic Star Flying Sail as the largest single product
- Shows that Feihe still has the ability to upgrade its products
- But can it be sustained? Under the general trend of consumption downgrade, the space for ultra-high-end is limited

**Full-age transformation**:
- Cut into adult milk powder and children's food
- The logic is correct but the competition is more intense - Yili, Mengniu and Nestlé are all in this market
- The gross profit margin is only 1%, indicating that we are currently losing money to seize the market.

## 4. Industry Final Deduction

### 4.1 Munger’s thinking: What will this industry be like in 10 years?

**Scenario 1: Oligopoly (30% probability)**

- Industry concentration increased significantly to CR3 > 60%
- Feihe, Yili and Junlebao divide the world into three parts
- Price war ends, survivors enjoy higher profit margins
- Feihe maintains 15-18% market share

**Scenario 2: Moderate concentration (probability 45%)**

- Industry concentration is increasing but slowly, CR3 is about 50%
- Feihe remains first but its share drops to around 15%
- Yili tied or surpassed Feihe
- The industry scale is stable at 100-120 billion yuan
- Feihe's revenue fell to the range of 12-15 billion yuan

**Scenario 3: Feihe is surpassed (probability 25%)**

- Yili surpasses Feihe with omni-channel advantages
- Feihe’s market share dropped to 12-15%
- Online channels are reshaping the competitive landscape, with the influx of new players
- Feihe gradually "Wyethized" - from industry leader to follower

### 4.2 Key winner

Several key factors that determine Feihe’s future:

1. **Online channel capabilities**: Maternal and infant store channels are shrinking irreversibly, with online growth of 12.3%. Can Feihe establish the same competitiveness online as it does offline?
2. **Minds of Young Consumers**: Do mothers born in the 2000s still recognize "Feihe = No. 1 in China"?
3. **Yili’s offensive strength**: Will Yili regard infant formula as a strategic investment?
4. **Birth population trend**: If it stabilizes and rebounds from 2026 to 2028, Feihe will gain breathing space.

## 5. Reverse thinking: Why might the market be wrong?

### 5.1 Reasons why the market may be overly pessimistic

- Industry consolidation will eventually come, and the share of exiters will be divided among the leaders
- Even if the birth population drops to 7.5 million per year, the annual increase in infant formula consumption will still reach 60-70 billion yuan
- Feihe's brand awareness is still the most mentioned among mothers and infants
- 7.5 billion in cash + zero debt = extremely strong survivability, able to "survive" competitors

### 5.2 Reasons why the market may not be pessimistic enough

- The 10.7% net profit margin in 2025 may not be the bottom - what if it continues to decline in 2026?
- The probability of Yili surpassing Feihe may be underestimated
- The 1% gross profit margin of the second curve shows that diversification is not successful.
- Management’s interests (high salary + dividends) may be inconsistent with those of small shareholders

## 6. Mangg format checklist

### "Tell me where I will die, and I won't go there."

Scenarios where Feihe may "die" (permanent capital loss):
1. ❌ Food safety incident - brand trust collapsed overnight (low probability but catastrophic)
2. ⚠️ Yili continues to suppress + consumption downgrade - market share and profit margin both decline
3. ⚠️Management squanders cash – wrong diversification or excessive dividends use up cash
4. ❌ Policy risks – For example, the government’s forced breastfeeding leads to a sharp drop in demand for milk powder

### "Think the other way around"

What does it take to make Feihe a good investment?
1. Profit margins bottom out (sales expense ratio decreases or revenue stabilizes)
2. The second curve has a profit turning point
3. Unexpected rebound in birth population data
4. The exit of competitors accelerates

None of these 4 conditions are currently met.

From Munger's perspective, Feihe is in a classic dilemma of "a good company in a bad industry". The industry pie is shrinking, competitors are approaching, and Feihe’s response strategies (subsidy guarantees, second curve) currently cost more than the benefits. It might be wiser to wait for clear signals from the industry or for margins to stabilize.
