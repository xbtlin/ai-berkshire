# I. Systemic environment analysis

# 1.1 Hong Kong ' s Institutional Advantage

The first thing to assess is the institutional environment.

- ** Rule of law tradition**: property rights protection under the common law system is clear and the land contract system has been in operation for more than a century
- ** Free market**: Free movement of capital, no foreign exchange controls, equal access to foreign and domestic capital
- **Cracks: no capital gains, no value added tax, low real estate taxes
- **Financial infrastructure**: Exchange rate stability provided by the associated exchange rate regime and sound banking system

# # 1.2 System risk cannot be ignored

However, in recent years, changes at the institutional level have required sustained attention:

- ** Changes in the political environment**: Political events since 2019 have affected confidence in international capital, with some foreign institutions having reduced their allocation to Hong Kong
- **The Central American Games**: Hong Kong faces challenges as a "super-associate" between China and the United States
- ** Link exchange rate system**: while there are no signs of change in the short term, long-term sustainability remains a macro-risk
- **Variations in government policies**: Repeated policies to remove the hot (sugar-smoking) show policy uncertainty

# 1.3 Impact on New Hung Ki

New Hongji, a purely Hong Kong+Inland Real Estate company, is exposed to the above-mentioned institutional environment. Unlike the global diversity of the Protégé Group, there is no "back" for New Hungji – and if Hong Kong and the Mainland market deteriorate simultaneously, companies can hedge without overseas operations.

** Rating: <unk> (systems are well-established but risks are increasing)

# II. List of key risks

##2.1 Macroeconomic risk <unk> (Middle and high risk)

** Interest rate risk**:
- Hong Kong rates follow the US Federal Fund rates. The current market expects the Fed to continue to cut interest rates in 2026, but if inflation repeatedly delays or suspends interest cuts, it will directly hit the building recovery process.
- New Hongji ' s own liabilities are not high (13.5% net), but the buyer ' s mortgage rate has a direct impact on purchasing power

** Economic growth risk**:
- Hong Kong ' s GDP growth depends on the three pillars of finance, trade and tourism
- Trade friction and customs policies in Central America may affect Hong Kong ' s trade and transit actors
- If the global recession is taking place, demand for the writing building and retail consumption will be affected.

** Demographic risk**:
- The birth rate in Hong Kong has been falling for a long time, and natural population growth is nearing stagnation
- "High talent" brings short-term population inflows, but there is uncertainty about whether it can be sustained
- Will the trend of some Hong Kong residents migrating abroad be reversed?

##2.2 Industry policy risk # (medium risk)

** Changes in land policy**:
- A significant increase in the Government ' s land supply (e.g., Northern Metropolitan Area Plan) could change the long-term balance of supply and demand
- Changes in agrarian land conversion policies may affect the value of the large reserve of agricultural land in New Hungji

**Cluster management risk**:
- Although it's now completely out, if the price of the building rises too fast, the government may tighten again.
- Experience in the 2010s shows that the Hong Kong government has no hesitation in managing the market.

**Inland policy risk**:
- The problem of excess commercial property in the interior is serious and policy focus is on stock rather than on new development
- Land value added tax, corporate income tax, etc., is heavier than Hong Kong

#2.3 Operating risk (medium risk)

** The vacancy rate in the writing building has risen**:
- A-class-writing prices have been down by over 20% in 2023-2024
- The trend of teleworking reduces the need for traditional writing buildings
- Increased differentiation of quality: quality property in the core area is still good and the perimeter of the old writing building is under severe pressure

**Inland operational risk**:
- The February 2026 incident of Von Su-in had exposed the management gap in the mainland operations.
- Some 64.6 million square feet of inland land reserves, some of which are under de-demotional pressure
- The operation of the Mainland mall faces intense competition (local masters such as Wanda, Huayun, Dragon Lake, etc.)

** Retail trade transition**:
- Electricity penetration continues to erode physical retailing
- The downward trend in consumption affects the renter structure and rent levels of the high-end mall

#2.4 Corporate governance risks

** Family holding structure**:
- The Guo family has absolute control through a trust of 46.1%
- Minority shareholders have limited voice at the board level
- The interests of large shareholders are not fully consistent with those of small shareholders (e.g., large shareholders may prefer to retain profits rather than dividends)

** Intergenerational communication**:
- The current chairman, Kwok Bing-kung, is 72 years old.
- Third generation (Gou Ki-hui, Guo Ki-hoon) has joined the Board but has not yet proved their ability
- The historical brother-in-law incident is still in the dark.

**The meaning of corruption in governance**:
- The case of Feng Soo-Soo is not just a case, but a reflection of the regulatory challenges of the expansion of large family businesses.
- Rental search space exists for marketing bidding, branding cooperation, etc. in the operation of the inland mall
- The level of scrutiny and restructuring of the company is yet to be observed

# Six, the most critical question: Is the family discount reasonable?

The market generally imposes a "governance discount" on family-owned enterprises such as Shinjongji, which reflects the following concerns:

** Reasons for reasonable discount:**
1. Asymmetrical large- and small-share-shareholder interests are objective
2. ROE is chronically low (5-8 per cent) and inefficiently allocated
3. Large profits retained, neither fully rededicated nor actively repurchased
4. The uncertainty of succession may lead to a strategic direction swing
5. Families may enjoy demonetary gains (social status, control premium), while small shareholders cannot share

** Reasons for excessive discounts:**
1. 46.1% of the Guo family shares, with interests highly tied to all shareholders
2. The very conservative financial strategy objectively protects the principal security of all shareholders
3. Continuous red-score records attest to the basic commitment to shareholder returns
4. The rule of law environment in Hong Kong is somewhat restrictive in relation to associated transactions and the transmission of benefits
5. When the PB is 0.32 times lower, the discounts are sufficient to compensate for all the above concerns

# Eight, core conclusion

From a risk and management perspective, the Xinhong base has the following characteristics that combine the risk of "low probability high impact" with "high probability low impact":

**The greatest comfort**: The company is extremely hard to "kill." Thirteen.5% net indebtedness, 12 billion rents a year, 3.73 times the liquidity ratio -- even if all the disadvantages are present, the new Hongji will probably survive. In the investment, "inhabiting" is a huge advantage.

** The biggest trouble**: Management lacks the will and urgency to release value. When your company sells it for three-folds without buying back, without increasing dividends and without disaggregating good assets, this either means that management thinks that NAV is overestimated or that management does not attach enough importance to the interests of minority shareholders. Neither of these may be pleasant.

** The greatest uncertainty**: Intergenerational transfers. The 72-year-old Guo Bing-kwan cannot be at the helm forever, while the third generation’s abilities and willingness have not yet been truly tested. The investment of the new Hong Kong Foundation is also essentially a gamble that the third generation is not much worse than the second generation.

** Bottom line judgement**: The risk of a new Hongji is a "deep-down" rather than a "surge-down" risk. For long-term investors, this risk characteristic is actually acceptable - provided the purchase price is low enough to make money even if it is "low-down-off".
