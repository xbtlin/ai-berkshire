# II. Investment arguments

#2.1 Core positive arguments

** Argument I: Extreme discounts provide asymmetrical rates**

Even considering the possible overvaluation of NAV, family governance discounts, the current pricing is an over-pessive assumption. Historically, the median PB for Shining Hong is 0.55-0.65 times, and even if it returns to 0.45 times more, the implicit increase is more than 40%.

** Argument II: The downside is extremely protective**

- Net liability rate 13.5 per cent: financial risk is virtually non-existent
- Annual rental income of $12 billion + HK$: even if the property sales are zero, the rent is sufficient to cover operations and dividends
- 3.73 times the liquidity ratio: short-term solvency is far above the security line
- Land reserves are recorded at historical cost: implied value is not fully reflected

** Argument III: The cyclical turning point has emerged**

- Full withdrawal in February 2024, releasing the depressed demand.
- The Fed interest-reduction cycle is activated and mortgage rates are down
- 20,000 new ones in 2025.
- Eight months of price increases in 2026, with institutional upwards expected to be +12-15%
- Property sales revenue per year +94.3 per cent, profit elasticity already shown

** Argument IV: Market neglect of quality assets**

- The central malls and writing buildings such as ICC, IFC, APM, etc. are in an irreplaceable position
- Newcom, as the largest data centre operator in Hong Kong, benefits from the AI wave
- The potential value of large agricultural land reserves in the New World is not market-based

##2.2 Core negative argument

** Argument I: Typical features of the value trap**

- ROE has a long-term rate of 5-8 per cent and low capital returns
- Management has not actively released value (no buy-back, no increase in the red ratio)
- A lack of capacity of minority shareholders to drive change under the family-controlled structure
- Continued under-one PB may reflect a legitimate market challenge to the quality of assets

** Argument II: Limited growth prospects**

- The size of Hong Kong ' s 1,100 square kilometres and the 7.5 million people determine the ceiling
- Structural pressure on the market in the writing building, which is difficult to reverse in the short term
- The interior business is facing intense competition and regulatory loopholes are exposed
- It's not a high-growth industry or company.

** Argument III: Intergenerational risk cannot be ignored**

- Gon-Uen is 72 years old. The replacement is getting worse.
- Third generation ability is untested.
- The historical probability of the three-generation curse in the family business cannot be ignored.
- ICAC investigations and brother-in-arms counter-intelligence in 2012 revealed the fragility of family governance

** Argument IV: Macro uncertainty**

- Interest rate trends remain variable (inflation has delayed interest rate reduction)
- Geopolitical risks (China-United States relations, changes in Hong Kong ' s international position)
- Whether or not the outflow trend is reversed is not yet clear
- There are doubts about the strength of the economic recovery in the interior.

# Four against the masters of investment

# From a business model perspective

"Do the right thing, not the easy thing."

Is what New Hongji did right? In 50 years of business history, double-drive, extremely conservative finance, patiently holding high-quality assets -- these are "right things." But the "uneasy things" are: waiting in an environment that values long-term discounts and requires great patience.

# From the point of view of valuation analysis

"Bought a good company at a reasonable price is much better than buying a mediocre company at a cheap price."

Is New Hongji good or plain? It is not a great company (can't achieve exponential growth), but it is not a mediocre company. Buying it at 0.32 times the price of a true bankroll, and that is certainly "cheap." The key question is: Is it cheap enough to make up for its "not great enough" shortcomings?

# From the industry's competitive perspective

"Close the thought, always the thought."

If you think the other way around, what are the circumstances under which you will lose money buying a new Hongji?

1. Systemic crisis in Hong Kong (low probability but significant impact)
2. NAV continued to shrink and management inaction (team-cooked frog risk)
3. Family fighting leads to division of companies (with historical precedent but with institutional constraints)

And vice versa: under what circumstances would this investment be profitable?

1. Mandatory recovery of buildings + over-expected interest reduction + 0.5 times PB return (foreseeable)
2. Management begins to buy back or significantly increases the score (disrepute but catalytic)
3. Release value (possibly but not always) from the separation of quality assets (e.g., new web/market REITs)

# From a risk management perspective

"The real risk is not volatility, but permanent capital loss."

With a new financial position (13.5% net debt, 12 billion dollars annual rent), the probability of permanent capital losses is extremely low. Even at the highest point, if the dividends are held for a long enough time, they can be gradually recovered.

# Seven, final reading

# One sentence to sum up

The Ning Hong base is a company that "will not die, will not explode, but may slowly improve" and sells it at a 30% discount.

# And the detailed conclusion

** Positive**:
- It's one of the strongest and most financially sound companies in Hong Kong's real estate industry.
- The current valuation (PB 0.32 times) is at an extreme historical low, implying an over-pessimistic expectation
- Hong Kong City recovery has begun, and the profits from the sale of property are very flexible.
- 12 billion Hong Kong dollars per year rent and 13.5 per cent net liability provide solid downside protection
- Interest-rate-reduction cycles and policy easing provide a favourable macro-environment for industrial recovery

** Reverse**:
- Low ROE and conservative capital return strategies may keep valuation discounts sustainable for the long term
- Lack of willingness of management to actively release value (back-purchase, high-scoring red, split)
- Intergenerational exposure and corporate governance are long-term concerns
- Structural challenges to the writing-room market and interior operations
- Growth ceilings are clear, and it is difficult to achieve excess returns

** Core judgement**:

The Ning Hong base is closer to an opportunity for "in-depth value" rather than an "in-growth investment." Buying at current prices is likely not to cost (down-the-road hard base), but making a lot depends on the timing and extent of the valuation repair. This is not an exciting investment, but it may be a reassuring one.

# VIII. Key issues to be resolved

The following issues deserve further study before making final investment decisions:

1. **Nav authenticity**: Need for in-depth analysis of the assessment methods and assumptions of the investment property to determine whether the bookkeeping NAV is credible
2. **The real value of the agricultural land reserve**: conversion progress, intended use and timetable
3. ** Real profitability of Mainland operations**: Is the Mainland operations really of value after deduction of Headquarters cost sharing?
4. **The true management thinking about capital allocation**: Are there buybacks, raise dividends, splits etc.?
** Specific timetable and arrangements for the third generation succession**: Is there a clear transition programme?
