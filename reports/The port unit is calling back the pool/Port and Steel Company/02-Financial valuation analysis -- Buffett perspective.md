# MTR Corporation (0066.HK) Financial Valuation Analysis

> Analysis dimensions: financial quality, profitability, balance sheet health, valuation and margin of safety
> Core question: Does the current MTR price provide a sufficient margin of safety?

---

## 1. Profitability analysis

### Revenue structure and profit quality

MTR's total revenue in 2025 will be approximately HK$55.47 billion (a year-on-year decrease of 7.6%), and the net profit attributable to shareholders will be approximately HK$14.68 billion (a year-on-year decrease of 6.9%). The decrease in revenue is mainly due to the higher revenue base recognized in property development in 2024.

It makes more sense to break down MTR’s profits into two parts:

**Recurring operating profit (sustainable portion):**
- Hong Kong passenger transport services: The total passenger volume of local railways exceeds 1.9 billion, and passenger transport revenue is growing steadily
- Station Business: Revenue is approximately HK$5.34 billion, net profit is approximately HK$4.39 billion, and net profit margin exceeds 82%
- Property leasing and management: Net profit is approximately HK$4.17 billion, rental income is stable
- Total recurring operating profit was approximately HK$7.21 billion, a year-on-year increase of 68.4%

**Non-recurring operating profit (fluctuation component):**
- Property development profit: approximately HK$11.08 billion (a year-on-year increase of 8%)
- Investment property revaluation gain/loss: fluctuates with market

If we only look at the recurring operating profit of HK$7.21 billion, which corresponds to the current market value of approximately HK$220 billion, the recurring operating PE is about 30 times - which is much higher than the 10-18 times PE seen on the surface. This is key to understanding MTR's valuation.

### Profit quality of each business segment

**Station Business – Hidden Cash Cow**

This is MTR's most undervalued business. Revenue of HK$5.34 billion created a net profit of HK$4.39 billion, and a net profit margin of 82% is extremely rare in any industry in the world. In essence, the MTR has a monopoly on the "golden shops" that 5.2 million people pass through every day. The retail leasing and advertising spaces in the stations are pure profits with almost zero marginal cost. The quality of this business is comparable to that of toll booths.

**Property Rental - Stable Rental Income**

The MTR Corporation manages a number of large shopping malls (ifc mall, Elements, Telford Plaza, etc.). These properties are located in core transportation hubs, and the occupancy rate remains high all year round. The net profit of HK$4.17 billion provided a stable cash flow foundation.

**Property Development – High Profits but High Volatility**

Property development profits in 2025 will be HK$11.08 billion, accounting for nearly half of the total profits. But this type of profit is highly dependent on the pace of project delivery - in which year there are large projects delivered, profits will be higher. Property development profits in 2023 will only be about HK$2.2 billion, but will surge to HK$10.27 billion in 2024. This kind of volatility makes it easy to misjudge just looking at the PE of a certain year.

**Railway Passenger Transport - Low Profit Operation**

Although Hong Kong's railway passenger transport business has a large revenue scale, its profit margin is not high after deducting operating costs, depreciation and maintenance. Railways are essentially an asset-heavy, high-fixed-cost business, and profits come more from the marginal contribution brought about by passenger flow growth. MTR's passenger revenue-to-expense ratio reaches about 150% (revenue is 1.5 times cost), which is already at the top level among global subways - most subway systems operate at a loss.

## 2. Balance sheet health

### Debt status

At the end of 2025, MTR's total debt was approximately HK$88.9 billion, with a net debt-to-equity ratio of 22.5%. In 2025, US$3 billion of senior unsecured bonds and US$3 billion of subordinated perpetual securities were newly issued (the largest US dollar corporate subordinated perpetual securities issuance in the Asia-Pacific region), and a seven-year syndicated green loan of HK$30 billion was signed.

**Key Indicators:**
- Net debt to equity ratio: 22.5% (improved after including perpetual securities)
- Unused committed bank loan facilities: over HK$51.1 billion
- Credit Rating: Fitch AA- (Stable Outlook)

A net debt ratio of 22.5% is very healthy for an asset-heavy infrastructure company. The AA- credit rating is second only to the sovereign rating, reflecting the dual guarantee of government holdings and stable cash flow. MTR's financing costs are therefore much lower than those of ordinary enterprises.

### Future capital expenditure pressure

This is a risk point that needs to be focused on. MTR's capital expenditure in the next ten years will reach HK$165 billion, including:
- 2024-2026: approximately HK$25-35 billion per year
- About 80% is used for Hong Kong railway maintenance and new railway projects
- The total investment in six new railway projects is approximately HK$140 billion

Such large-scale capital expenditure means that MTR needs continued financing. Although the AA- rating ensures low-cost financing capabilities, the continued rise in debt levels still deserves vigilance. A positive hedging factor is that new railway projects will also bring new property development opportunities, and the R+P model can self-recover to a certain extent.

## 3. Cash flow analysis

MTR’s cash flow structure is rather special:

**Operating cash flow**: Railway passenger transportation, station business, and property leasing provide stable operating cash flow, approximately HK$10 billion or more per year.

**Investment Cash Flow**: New railway construction brings significant capital expenditure, but sales proceeds from property developments also provide important cash inflows.

**Financing cash flow**: Supplement capital needs through bond issuance and syndicated loans. The total of three large-scale financings in 2025 will exceed HK$60 billion equivalent, indicating that the capital market has fully supported the financing needs of MTR.

A structural issue that needs attention is: **The cash outflow from railway construction is certain, while the cash inflow from property development depends on the property market environment and project progress**. If Hong Kong's property market continues to slump, property development profits decline, and railway construction expenditures remain rigid, cash flow may become temporarily tight.

## 4. Return to shareholders

### Dividend policy

MTR Corporation implements a progressive dividend policy. The full-year dividend per share in 2025 will be HK$1.31, the same as in 2024. Based on the current stock price of about HK$35, the dividend rate is about 3.7%; based on the previous low of about HK$27, the dividend rate can reach 4.85%.

**Dividend payment record in recent years:**

MTR's dividend policy reflects the characteristics of a public utility - stable but not aggressive. In a massive capital expenditure cycle, it is prudent for management to choose to maintain rather than increase dividends.

### Stock buyback

MTR Corporation occasionally engages in small-scale share buybacks, but they are not its primary form of shareholder returns. Considering that the major shareholder (the government holds 74.4% of the shares) is unlikely to participate in the repurchase, the effect of the repurchase on the actual circulating shares is limited.

## 5. Valuation analysis

### Current valuation level

Based on the stock price of approximately HK$35 in May 2026:

| Valuation indicator | Value | Description |
|----------|------|------|
| Static PE (including property development) | About 10.7 times | Reflects years with higher profits from property development |
| Recurring business PE | About 30 times | Calculated based on recurring business profits only |
| PB | About 0.91 times | Breaking the net, historically low |
| Dividend yield | About 3.7% | Calculated based on current stock price |
| EV/EBITDA | About 12-14 times | Reasonable range for public utilities |

### The core contradiction of valuation

The biggest difficulty in MTR's valuation is: Which profit should be used to calculate PE? **

- If the total profit from property development is included, PE looks only 10-11 times, which seems very cheap.
- If you only look at recurring business profits, PE is as high as 30 times, which looks very expensive.
-Property development profits fall somewhere in between, depending on the abundance of future project pipelines

A reasonable approach is to assign different valuation multiples to different businesses:
- Recurring business (passenger transportation + station business + property leasing): quasi-public utility, 15-20 times PE → corresponding valuation of HK$108-144 billion
- Property development business: Based on the value of the project pipeline, the average annual profit in the medium term is about HK$6-8 billion, giving a PE of 8-10 times → corresponding valuation of HK$48-80 billion
- Total reasonable valuation range: approximately HK$156-224 billion, corresponding to approximately HK$25-36 per share

### The meaning of breaking the net

PB below 1x means that the market is pricing MTR assets below book value. This may reflect several factors:
1. The market is worried that large-scale capital expenditure will dilute returns
2. Uncertainty in Hong Kong’s property market has depressed the valuation of property assets
3. Losses and contraction of overseas businesses have affected market confidence
4. ROE is about 11%, which is only slightly higher than the cost of capital and does not support high premiums.

### Safety margin judgment

**Optimistic scenario (above HK$40 per share)**: Hong Kong property market recovers + new railway project progresses smoothly + property development profits continue to contribute + northern metropolitan area brings new growth

**Neutral scenario (HK$28-36 per share)**: The current operating status continues, recurring business grows steadily, and property development profits fluctuate with the pace of projects.

**Pessimistic scenario (HK$22-28 per share)**: Hong Kong property market continues to slump + capital expenditure exceeds expectations + debt pressure rises + overseas business continues to lose money

### Overall judgment

The current valuation of MTR is in the mid-to-low range in history, but it is not as cheap as the superficial PE looks. The core contradiction is that the valuation of recurring business is not low, and the "cheap" feeling mainly comes from the contribution of property development profits. If investors believe that MTR's future property development pipeline can continue to contribute an average annual profit of more than HK$6 billion to HK$8 billion, then the current price will be attractive. If you are pessimistic about the outlook for Hong Kong's property market, you need to wait for a greater margin of safety.

Below HK$30 may provide a better margin of safety, while below HK$25 enters a clear value range. The current price of HK$35 is reasonably expensive and requires relatively optimistic expectations for property development profits in the next few years to support it.
