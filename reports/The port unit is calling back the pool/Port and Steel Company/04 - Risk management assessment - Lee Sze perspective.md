# I. Management assessment

# Management change: from Kim Dae-taek to Yang Mi-jin

On 1 January 2026, Dr. Kim Zepei was transferred from the Executive President to the non-Executive Chairman and Yang Mi-Jen to the Executive President (CEO).

** Kim Zepee (2019-2025 CEO, as President since 2026)**

Kim Zepei joined the company in 1995, and has been working as a CEO from the engineer for over 30 years. During his tenure, he has faced the twin impacts of the new crown epidemic and social movements on passenger traffic, as well as the advancement of a number of large railway projects.

Objective evaluation:
- The financial stability of the company was maintained under the shock of the epidemic, and no significant cuts were made in the distribution.
- The construction of a roadway along the sand center line (Tuma and East Railways) was advanced despite previous quality disputes
- Launch of six new railway projects that will provide the basis for the growth of the port iron in the next decade
- Launched a major financing campaign in 2025 (over HK$ 60 billion) to prepare capital for expenditure
- The apparent contraction of overseas operations during their tenure (UK, SWE) is a negative record

Kim Zepei's transition to a non-executive chair is a noteworthy arrangement. His insight into the company helps to maintain strategic continuity, but the model of "former CEO as president" can sometimes also create role conflicts — the independence of the new CEO may be constrained.

** Yang Mi-jin (CEO since 2026)**

In 1999, Yang Mi-Jen joined the Hong Kong Railways as Chief of Commerce (2011-2021), Chief of Passenger Services (2021-2023) and Chief Executive (2023-2025), the first woman to be a Chief Executive Officer in the history of Hong Kong.

Key observations:
- **Intra-promotion**: 27 years of a railway career with a profound understanding of the company’s business and culture. The CEO of an internal promotion usually means strategic continuity, but may also lack the capacity to change from an external perspective
- ** Business background**: long-term responsibility for business plates (retailing, advertising, property management) with direct experience in managing the most profitable "cash cow" business in Hong Kong
- ** Clear strategic focus**: after taking office, public statements of emphasis on six new railway projects (a HK$140 billion investment), with emphasis on artificial intelligence applications and workforce development
- ** Fair pay**: HK$8.16 million per annum, with 120,000 shares awarded to a company upon expiry of the contract. For CEOs, a market company of US$220 billion, there is no excessive incentive for this level of remuneration.

In the critical period of six major railway projects, it is reasonable to choose an insider who knows how to run a company. But the strategic contraction and business transformation of Hong Kong's overseas economy requires leadership and observation.

# Corporate governance

** The special nature of the equity structure**

The HKSAR Government holds 74.4 per cent of the shares, which brings with it several important governance features:

1. **Policy stability**: Governments, as shareholders in holding shares, do not have malicious acquisitions, short-term arbitrage, etc., which ensure long-term corporate orientation
2. ** Finance advantages**: Government endorsement to achieve quasi-sovereign credit rating for iron and steel (AA-) with minimal financing costs
3. **Policy risk**: Government may impose non-commercial requirements on Hong Kong iron for political reasons (e.g. freezing of fares, community responsibility, etc.)
4. ** Minority shareholder interest protection**: 25.6% of the public holding shares in government absolute ownership has limited voice.

** Composition of the Board of Trustees**

The executive board, the government-appointed non-executive board and the independent non-executive board of directors, has a substantive influence on the strategic direction of the company through board appointments. This governance structure is in line with international standards in relation to the global state enterprise, i.e., the disclosure of information on the iron, financial reporting and compliance management.

** Does management incentives correspond to shareholders' interests?**

The remuneration structure of the Hong Kong iron administration is dominated by fixed pay, with lower equity incentives (Jan and Jan's three-year contract is awarded only 120,000 shares valued at approximately HK$ 4.2 million). This means that management's interests are less directly tied to the stock price, relying more on professional reputation and government evaluation systems.

# II. Risk identification and quantification

# Risk I: Capital expenditure cycle risk # (high)

** Description**: Capital expenditure over the next decade is HK$16.5 billion, and six new railway projects have invested approximately HK$140 billion.

**Quantified**:
- Annual capital expenditure in 2024-2026 is approximately HK$ 25-35 billion
- Total debt of HK$88.9 billion at the end of 2025 is expected to rise further
- Hong Kong Iron has recorded large project overruns in the past: the Hong Kong high-iron sector was originally estimated at HK$65 billion, resulting in an overspending of about HK$90 billion; the same overspending occurred on the sand line

** Potential impact**: If multiple projects exceed 20-30% at the same time, additional funding needs may amount to HK$10 billion, significantly raising the level of liability and reducing shareholder returns. The net liability equity ratio may increase from 22.5% to more than 30%.

** Mitigating factors**: AA - Credit ratings ensure access to finance; new railway projects under R+P model offer both physical development opportunities, partially offset by capital expenditures.

# Risk two: Hong Kong building and market risk # (high)

** Description**: Property development profits as a share of total iron profits in Hong Kong are about 40-55 per cent (average over the past decade), and highly dependent on the market climate of Hong Kong buildings.

**Quantified**:
- The profits from the development of the property in 2025 were approximately HK$ 11.18 billion
- In 2023, the profits from the development of the property were only about HK$2.2 billion (the low market period).
- The difference between the two is about HK$ 9 billion, corresponding to the difference in profits of about HK$ 1.5 per share

** Potential impact**: If Hong Kong building markets enter a long-term adjustment, profits from property development may remain low (K$3-5 billion/year), total profits from iron will shrink significantly and PE will rise significantly.

** Mitigating factor**: The industry model of the iron industry is a partnership with development agents, a profit-sharing model, where the iron itself does not take stock and sales risks, and is relatively well protected. But the profit-sharing ratio also means that the profits of the iron are reduced in parallel when the market goes down.

# Risk three: Political and regulatory risks #

** Description**: Harbour iron, as a government-owned utility, is under continuous political pressure.

** Specific performance**:
- The price adjustment is subject to the "add-and-a-half" mechanism, with the price remaining unchanged for 2025/2026
- The Government may require more social responsibility on the part of the company (e.g. elders ' preference, students ' preference, etc.)
- The choice of routes and timetable for the new railway project are influenced by policy
- The continuing tension between the high expectations of the community for the supply of iron services and commercial profit claims

**Quantified**: The impact of a one-year freeze on passenger transport revenue is not fatal in isolation, but if inflation rises and fares are limited in the long run, the cumulative effect may significantly erode the profitability of passenger transport operations.

** Mitigating factor**: Governments are also large shareholders of 74.4% and do not allow companies to get into financial distress.

# Risk four: contract risk in overseas operations #

** Description**: The successive losses of British and Swedish operations and the reduction of the overseas map of the port iron.

**Quantified**:
- The British Elizabeth Line contract is worth about £3 billion.
- Early termination of Swedish operations, with an estimated loss of HK$70 million confirmed
- It's probably not going to work out after the Southwest English railroad contract.

** Potential impact**: Overseas business revenues will decrease significantly, and Hong Kong’s growth narrative will become more dependent on the local market.

** Mitigating factors**: Australian operations (Melburn, Sydney) and Mainland operations (Beijing, Shenzhen, Hang) are still in operation, but the profit contribution is modest. Management may focus more resources on railway and property development in Hong Kong mainland.

# Risk five: interest rate and financing risk # (middle and low)

** Description**: In the large-scale capital expenditure cycle, the interest rate environment has a direct impact on the cost of financing the iron port.

**Quantified**: Total HK$88.9 billion debt, assuming an increase of one percentage point in financing costs, and an increase of approximately HK$890 million in interest expenditure per year, with an impact on net profits of about 6 per cent.

** Mitigating factors**: AA-credit ratings have made the financing spread of iron well below average enterprises; permanent securities issued in 2025 have allowed for some capital optimization; and 30 billion HK$ in green loans may benefit from interest rate preferences.

# Risk six: Engineering quality and safety risk # (middle and low, but high end impact)

** Description**: The mass simultaneous advancement of multiple railway construction projects, and challenges in engineering quality and safety management.

** Historical lessons**: The Shading Red Cross Station Project, which has revealed such quality problems as the cutting of the wire and the falsification of the construction records, which were ultimately assessed as structural security, seriously damaged the reputation of the port and public trust.

** Potential impact**: If major engineering quality problems recur, they may lead to project extensions, cost overruns, political crises and collapse of public trust.

# Overall judgment

The management of the port iron is a "stable" team that manages a public utility monopoly. They do not do amazing things and are unlikely to make fatal mistakes. This management style matches the business nature of the port iron.

The greatest risks are not in management itself, but in structural factors**: the long-term trends in Hong Kong building and implementation risks for excessive capital expenditure. Neither risk is fully controlled by management.

From the perspective of "buying shares is buying people," the management of the port iron is good enough to manage the business, but it is unlikely that it will do more than expected. The rationale for investing in the port iron should be based on judgement about the business itself (R+P model) and the city (Hong Kong), rather than expectations of management overperformance.
