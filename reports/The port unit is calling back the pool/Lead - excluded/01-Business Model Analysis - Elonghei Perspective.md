# Analysis of the business model of Link Real Estate Fund (0823.HK)

> Analysis dimensions: essence of business model, user value, moat depth, management’s "duty"
> Core question: What is the nature of this business? Why can it make money in the long run?
> Study date: May 16, 2026

---

## 1. The essence of business

Link's business can be summarized in one sentence: **holding and operating irreplaceable community retail properties within the "last mile" of Hong Kong citizens' living circles and charging stable rents**.

The essence of this business is "rent collection", but it is not a simple leasehold model. There are three levels of what Link does:

**Level 1: Hold scarce lots**. Link holds about 130 community commercial assets and about 57,000 parking spaces in Hong Kong, most of which are located next to public housing estates or near major transportation hubs. These properties were privatized through an IPO from the Hong Kong Housing Authority in 2005 and have a natural location monopoly - you cannot build another shopping mall in the same housing estate.

**Level 2: Proactive management enhances value**. Link does not hold assets statically, but continues to renovate properties, optimize the merchant mix, and increase rental levels through the Asset Enhancement Initiative (AEI). As of 2025, Link has completed 103 asset improvement projects, with an average return on investment of 18%. Projects such as Chung Shan Shopping Mall, Lok Fu Plaza, and T Town in Wong Tai Sin are all successful cases.

**Level 3: Capital circulation and expansion**. Link uses funds from the disposal of inefficient assets and low-cost financing to expand into asset classes and regions with higher returns - from community shopping malls in Hong Kong to commercial properties in first-tier cities in the Mainland, retail properties in Australia and Singapore, and office buildings in the UK. In recent years, it has launched LREP private equity fund business, extending in the direction of "light asset management".

### Business essence rating: ★★★★☆

The rent collection business naturally has high certainty and predictability, but Link is not a purely passive rent collection. Active management capabilities are its core differentiation. One star is deducted because the REIT's statutory dividend payment requirement (at least 90% of distributable income) limits the flexibility of retained earnings to be reinvested.

---

## 2. User value: What value is created for whom?

Link's "users" fall into two categories:

### 1. Tenant (merchant)

Link provides merchants with **high traffic community commercial locations**. A shopping mall in a housing estate close to tens of thousands of residents naturally has a stable customer flow. About 60% of Link's rental income comes from necessities consumer merchants such as wet markets, supermarkets and restaurants. These merchants are highly dependent on location - residents are unlikely to travel far to buy groceries, eat, or see a doctor.

Link has created a better operating environment for merchants by renovating shopping malls, optimizing the business mix, and introducing brand chains, but it has also pushed up rents, which has caused controversy at the social level.

### 2. Consumers (community residents)

Link provides consumers with **one-stop community life services**. Fresh markets, supermarkets, restaurants, clinics, banks, convenience stores - residents' daily needs can basically be met in Link Mall. Link's "fresh market" operating model is regarded as a benchmark in the Hong Kong market.

### User value rating: ★★★★☆

The user value of necessities consumption is extremely high and stable, and is not greatly affected by economic cycles and e-commerce shocks. However, as a monopolistic landlord, Link's strong rental pricing power also means that merchants have weak bargaining power, and may face political and regulatory pressure in the long run.

---

## 3. Moat analysis

### 1. Supply-side moat: scarce areas cannot be copied ★★★★★

This is Link's deepest moat. The supply of community shopping malls in Hong Kong is limited - it is impossible to build a new shopping mall next to an already established public housing estate. Link acquired approximately 180 properties from the Housing Authority in one go in 2005 (subsequently adjusted through disposals and acquisitions). This window of opportunity for "acquisition of scarce assets in bulk" has closed and cannot be replicated by any competitor.

### 2. Demand-side moat: community ecological lock-in ★★★★☆

The daily consumption behavior of community residents is highly inertial. Buying groceries, eating breakfast, picking up the kids and doing some shopping—these patterns of behavior won’t change just because a new mall opens next door. The "community embeddedness" of Link Mall creates a natural consumer lock-in effect.

But this is not absolute. The trend of Hong Kong residents "going north to consume" is eroding some non-essential consumption, especially catering and retail. This is a structural threat.

### 3. Scale and management moat ★★★★☆

Link is the largest REIT in the Asia-Pacific region, with assets of approximately HK$226 billion. Scale brings three advantages: (1) low financing cost (S&P A rating, financing cost is about 3.5%); (2) accumulation of talent and management capabilities (professional asset management team); (3) brand effect’s attraction to merchants.

### 4. Switching Cost ★★★☆☆

For merchants, relocation costs are relatively high - renovation investment, customer source reconstruction, and community connections. But if rent increases continue to exceed what merchants can afford, conversions will still happen. The recent negative rental adjustment rate (-6.4%) reflects the fact that Link is forced to give up profits in the current environment.

### Comprehensive moat rating: ★★★★☆

The overall moat is deep, and the core comes from the non-replicability of scarce areas on the supply side. But the moat is not inerodible - "northbound consumption", e-commerce penetration, and political regulatory risks have all weakened its pricing power at the margin.

---

## 4. Sustainability of business model

### Reasons to be bullish

**1. Resilience of consumption of necessities**. No matter how bad the economy is, people still have to buy food to eat. 60% of Link's revenue comes from necessities, and this demand will almost never disappear.

**2. Sustained room for asset improvement**. The average return on 103 completed projects was 18%, proving the effectiveness of this model. As properties age, the need for renovation and upgrades continues.

**3. Growth contribution of overseas portfolio**. The performance of Australia (occupancy rate 98.1%, lease renewal +16.3%) and Singapore (occupancy rate 99.8%, lease renewal +12.9%) is much better than that of the Hong Kong headquarters, and the diversification strategy is beginning to bear fruit.

**4. Benefits from the interest rate cut cycle**. As a leveraged REIT, falling interest rates directly reduce financing costs. Net financing costs have dropped by 7.3% in 2025/2026H1.

### Reasons to be bearish

**1. Structural pressure on Hong Kong’s retail market**. Hong Kong people's "going north to consume" is not a short-term phenomenon, but a long-term trend of the integration of Shenzhen and Hong Kong. The price difference between the strong Hong Kong dollar and low prices on the mainland has caused the continued outflow of non-essential consumption.

**2. Rent adjustment for Mainland properties**. The rental adjustment rate for renewal of properties in Beijing and Shanghai was -16.4%, reflecting that historically high rents are unsustainable. The problem of oversupply of commercial real estate in the Mainland is more serious than that in Hong Kong.

**3. The risk of transformation from "rent collection" to "asset management"**. LREP private equity fund business is a new field, which is very different from the circle of competence of the traditional rent collection model, and execution risks cannot be ignored.

**4. Uncertainty about management change**. George Hongchoy will retire at the end of 2025. Currently, John Saunders and CFO Ng Kok Siong are jointly acting as CEO. The permanent CEO has not yet been determined.

---

## 5. "Duty" perspective: Is the management doing the right thing?

Link’s management has several behavior patterns worthy of attention:

**Well done**:
- Continuously executing asset enhancement plans over the past 20 years, not pursuing short-term gains but long-term value creation
- Maintain conservative leverage (23.3%) well below regulatory caps and downgrade thresholds (30%)
- When rents in Hong Kong are under pressure, proactively lower rents to maintain the occupancy rate (97.6%) instead of forcing rents to lead to vacancies

**Worth Observing**:
- Whether overseas expansion and private equity fund business are beyond the circle of competence?
- Strategic continuity during management changes
- Whether executive remuneration is consistent with the interests of fund unitholders

### "Benfen" rating: ★★★★☆

Overall, Link's management has demonstrated a high level of professionalism and prudent style over the past 20 years. However, it is in a critical transition period, and the strategic direction and execution capabilities of the new management have yet to be verified.

---

## 6. Summary

| Dimensions | Rating | Highlights |
|------|------|------|
| Nature of business | ★★★★☆ | Community rent collection + active management, high certainty, but REIT structure limits reinvestment flexibility |
| User value | ★★★★☆ | The consumption of necessities is very rigid, but the social controversy over the monopoly position requires attention |
| Moat | ★★★★☆ | The supply-side moat is extremely deep, but consumption and e-commerce going north are eroding the margins |
| Sustainability | ★★★☆☆ | Hong Kong is under pressure but still resilient, overseas growth is promising but transformation is risky |
| The Duty of Management | ★★★★☆ | Historical performance is excellent, but the transition period increases uncertainty |
| **Comprehensive** | **★★★★☆** | **High-quality defensive assets with deep moats but facing structural challenges** |

**Summary in one sentence**: Link's business model is based on the three pillars of "scarce locations + necessities consumption + active management". The moat is deep but not inerodible. The core problem currently faced is not the problem with the business model itself, but the periodic headwinds brought about by the external environment (northbound consumption, interest rates, management changes).
