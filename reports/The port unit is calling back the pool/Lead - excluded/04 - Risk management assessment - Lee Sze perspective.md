# Lead property fund (0823.HK) risk and management assessment

> Analytical dimensions: core risk identification, paper-testing forgery, management quality and governance
> Core question: Under what circumstances will the investment papers be falsely proved? Is management worth entrusting them with capital?
> Date of study: 16 May 2026

---

# I. Core assumptions of the investment paper

Investors who are looking after the event usually have the following assumptions:

** The basic demand for essential goods in Hong Kong community is not lost** - people always have to buy food, eat, and visit the hospital.
** The monopoly of the contours cannot be subverted** — it is impossible to build new malls next to public houses.
** Interest-relief cycle will improve RIT valuation and financing costs** — interest-sensitive assets will benefit
**35% plus NAV concessions already reflect most bad news** - current prices provide sufficient security margins
** Management capacity to maintain quality of operations and implement strategic transformation** — overseas expansion and capital management operations can contribute increment

** If any of the above core assumptions are false, the investment paper needs to be revisited.**

# II. Core risk-by-project analysis

# Risk one: Structural contraction of the retail market in Hong Kong

** Risk level: <unk> (high)**

** Specific performance**:

- Total retail sales in Hong Kong continued to be under pressure from 2023-2025
- Hong Kong people's consumption has changed from "scaffles" to "normal".
- Retention rate adjustment for retail rent in Hong Kong - 6.4%
- 6.0 per cent decline in sales to general retail tenants

** Worst-case scenario**: If Hong Kong retail market is permanently reduced by 20-30% in the context of the accelerated integration of the deep ports, the value of Hong Kong retail property and rental income will be hit by a lasting blow. The impact, calculated as 74% of Hong Kong's combined product, is significant.

** buffers**:

- About 60% of the rent for the show comes from the consumption of essential goods (food market, supermarkets, food groceries), which is almost non-diversion.
- 97.6% of the rental rate means that the demand of the business is still strong.
- The rent ratio is 13.0 per cent sustainable, and the business is not overstretched
- Population ageing increases the dependency on community services

**Assessment**: This is the biggest structural risk to the collar, but it is partially hedged by the "consumption resilience" of the essentials. The risk is not that the base demand is gone, but that the rent growth may be stagnant for a long time - the collar can sustain rent collection, but it may be difficult to increase rents.

Risk two: the interior property continues to deteriorate

** Risk level: <unk> (high)**

** Specific performance**:

- Retention rent adjustment rate for the interior property sector - 16.4 per cent
- Beijing and Shanghai are facing oversupply of commercial properties
- The confidence of the interior is slowly recovering.

** Worst-case scenario**: In the event of a continuing economic downturn in the interior, the interior property sector (13.8 per cent of the portfolio) may face the double pressure of continuing downswings in rent and substantial decline in valuation.

** buffers**:

- Relatively manageable interior occupation (13.8 per cent) does not destabilize the whole
- The value of the core assets, such as Shenzhen Central City, is still being supported over the long term.
- The rehabilitation project in Guangzhou Tianhe and Beijing Tungzhou is expected to improve operations after completion by mid-2025

**Assessment**: The interior property industry is one of the main causes of short-term delays in performance, but the ratio is manageable. The concern is whether management has the strength to lose value or sell underperforming inland assets if necessary.

# Risk three: change of management risk

** Risk level: <unk> (high)**

** Specific performance**:

George Hongchoy retired on 31 December 2025, ending his 16-year CEO term. Under his leadership, he grew from a regional REIT to the largest real estate investment trust in Asia and the Pacific. His departure marked the end of an era.

The current transitional arrangements are:

- **John Sanders** (former Group Chief Investment Officer): Executive Director, responsible for investment and third-party capital operations
- **Ng Kok Siong** (Column Chief Financial Officer): Co-chairing CEO with Saunders
- ** Duncan Owen**: Chairman of the Board of Trustees, overseeing the transition
- **Permanent CEO**: Board of Trustees is in a comprehensive selection process, not yet published

** Key concerns**:

1. **The efficiency of the joint CEO model**: Historically, the joint CEO arrangements have rarely been effective in the long term.
** Uncertainty in strategic direction**: Has Hongchoy continued or reoriented to drive overseas expansion and LREP?
3. ** Brain drain risk**: core management teams may lose their jobs due to uncertainty during CEO rotation
4. ** Too long a selection period**: Long-term strategic ambiguity may affect investor confidence if the selection of the permanent CEO is delayed until the second half of 2026 or even later

**Assessment** This is the biggest source of uncertainty today. On the good side, leading business has a strong "run-by-run" capacity – 130 malls operate on a daily basis without the CEO’s personal decision-making. But strategic-level decision-making (overseas expansion rhythm, LREP input, asset disposal) requires clear leadership.

# Risk four: NAV continues down

** Risk level: <unk> (middle and high)**

** Specific performance**:

- The unit of NAV decreased from HK$74.97 in March 2023 to HK$63.30 in March 2025, a decline of 15.6 per cent in two years
- Pilgrim forecasts for a further 2-4 per cent average annual loss in the property valuation for the financial year 2025-2027
- Cap Rate still has room for expansion

** Impact on investors**:

The NAV decline does not in itself directly affect rental income and DPU, but it affects:
- Passive increase in leverage (debt/assets ratio)
- Market confidence in REIT and valuation multipliers
- Financing costs would increase if the threshold of below 30 per cent of credit ratings were to be reached

** buffers**:

- Current leverage rate of 23.3%, with a considerable buffer from the 30% threshold.
- The PBS valuation needs to drop another 20% from the current level before reaching the threshold
- Interest-rate-reduction cycles are theoretically favourable to capitalization and contribute to the recovery of valuations

**Assessment**: The downside of NAV is a risk that requires continuous monitoring but does not pose a fatal threat.

# Risk five: Political and regulatory risks

** Risk level: <unk> (medium)**

** Specific performance**:

The image of the leader has been controversial. As a monopolist landlord from privatization of public assets, the leader has been criticized by some Hong Kong citizens and politicians for "slowing prices up the community."

** Potential threat**:

- Introduction of regulations on rent control or rent protection
- The Government demands greater social responsibility on the lead company (e.g., by providing low-cost shops)
- Public opinion pressure influences rent pricing strategies

**Assessment**: This risk is long-standing but unlikely to change in the short term.

# Risk six: Implementation risks of overseas expansion

** Risk level: <unk> (medium)**

The expansion in Australia, Singapore and the United Kingdom is currently performing well (98 per cent rent +), but there are long-term risks as follows:

- Complexity of intercultural management
- Effects of exchange rate fluctuations on income and valuation
- Incomprehension with local market cycles may lead to a buy-in error
- LREP private fund-raising operations are entirely new and have a high probability of failure

- Development of the lead from RIT, a regional community mall, to the largest diversified real estate investment trust in Asia and the Pacific
- The size of assets increased from approximately HK$ 30 billion when it was listed to HK$ 226 billion
- 103 asset enhancement projects successfully implemented, with an average return of 18 per cent
- Maintain conservative leverage and investment-grade credit ratings
- Promotion of geographical diversity (Hong Kong to five countries/regions)

** Controversy**:

- The UNDF units diluted each unit NAV and DPU as a buy-off
- The timing and price of some overseas acquisitions are questioned (e.g., acquisitions in the British writing building in high interest rate environments)
- Whether retirement time points choose high-level departures, leaving the difficulty to the successors.

- The Board is more independent
- High transparency in information disclosure (bilingual, detailed operational data)
- Stakeholder communication is more proactive
- The level of management remuneration and interest-segment of fund unit holders needs to be strengthened, however

# # Management overall score: #

The management of Hongchoy can give four stars, but the current transition period can only give three stars. The model of joint management, the absence of permanent CEOs, and the indiscretion of new management to local business in Hong Kong are all sub-items.

# Six, historical stress test

The lead has undergone two major stress tests:

# 2019-2020: Social movements plus the new crown epidemic

- Community malls are relatively robust because of the consumption properties of the essential goods
- The rate of rent is always above 95%.
- DPU recovered rapidly after a brief downturn in 2020/2011
- Proves the resilience of the community mall model.

# 2022-2024: interest-added cycle + Hong Kong retail low

- NAV's down about 20% from the height.
- DPUs are volatile but still at a very high level
- Leveraging is safe.
- Credit ratings remain unchanged

** Apocalypse of pressure testing**: The collar shows reasonable resilience in adversity - not in the least, but in the absence of a scratch. This is consistent with the defensive attributes of its business model.
