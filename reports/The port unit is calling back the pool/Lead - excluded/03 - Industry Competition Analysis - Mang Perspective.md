# I. Definition of industry: Which industry does the lead compete in?

The question seems simple, and it needs to be considered carefully. The lead is ostensibly in the "ReIT" industry, but if you understand the show from the perspective of REIT alone, you'll miss a lot of important information.

The core business of the show can be broken down into three mutually embedded industries:

1. **Community retail property**: this is the basic drive for the show, which represents about 74% of the combined value
2. **Asia-Pacific commercial real estate**: As the expansion has taken place overseas, the lead has become more and more like a cross-regional commercial real estate platform
3. ** Real estate asset management**: launch of LREP private fund-raising operations and initiation of the diplomatic career in asset management

Each "industry" has different competition logic and structural trends that need to be analysed separately.

# II. Hong Kong Community Retail Property: Monopolies ' Worried

# Competition patterns: few direct competitors

In the Hong Kong Community Retail Realty segment, the competitive position of the leader can be described as "pre-monopoly".

** Why is it a quasi-monopoly?**

- The Hong Kong Housing Council privatized the retail property under the banner to the lead industry in 2005 and has since stopped developing new community businesses on a large scale
- The conductor holds approximately 130 Hong Kong Community commercial assets and 57,000 parking spaces
- These properties are embedded in public housing, and the location is not replicable.
- Other real estate providers (e.g., New World, Consignor, Huai Yuan, etc.), although also in retail, are concentrated in the core business or private residential areas, unlike the community mall location of the lead

** But the monopolists also have troubles:**

The competition facing the leaders is not from the same type of community mall operators, but from the structural shift in consumption behaviour**.

# Structural Challenge One: North-North Consumption

The number of Hong Kong residents who pass through the deep port north during weekends and holidays, according to the Immigration Department, is still high.

- Non-essential retail (cloths, electronics, make-up) is the hardest hit
- Partial outflow of catering consumption - Shenzhen food prices are clearly superior
- But the consumption of essential goods (food, supermarkets, daily services) is largely unaffected

This differentiation is evidenced by the data from the showcasing 2025/2026H1: supermarkets and food-type sales grew by +0.5 per cent, but retail sales generally declined by -6.0 per cent.

# Structural Challenge II: Power vendor penetration

New retail models such as instant distribution, community group purchasing, etc., are indirectly competing with traditional community malls. But the dominant business in the conservatory malls – the live market, the catering, the clinic, and the banking, etc., are themselves indistinguishable for electric power providers.

# Structurally good: population ageing and community service demand

The elderly are more dependent on the community market - they are less likely to consume in the north and to use electricity.

# Hong Kong Community Retail Rating: #

The position of the leader in this segment of the market is almost unshakeable, but the industry itself faces structural consumption drain. The good news is that the resilience of the consumer of the essentials provides a bottom line for the leader.

In overseas markets, the leader is no longer a monopolist, but one of many competitors. The advantages are:

- ** Low cost of funds**: A-class credit ratings give them a price advantage in the purchase competition
- **Managing brand**: The branding effect of the largest REIT in Asia and the Pacific helps attract business and partners
- ** Transregional platform**: experience in multi-market operations can be transferred across markets

- ** Inexperienced local operations**: The lead is later than Scentre's decades of deep-drawing in Australia

# Asia-Pacific commercial real estate rating: #

# IV. Structural trends in the REIT sector

Trends I: interest-reduction cycles - significant benefits

REIT is an interest-sensitive asset.

1. ** Reduction in financing costs**: Direct increases can be allocated revenue.
2. ** Valuation revaluation**: Lower capitalization rates drive property valuations back and NAVs are expected to stabilize or even rise

# Trend two: RITs are integrated into the deep port - potential catalyst

The Hong Kong CVM is actively promoting the inclusion of REITs in the Connectivity mechanism.

# Trends III: From "interest rate replacement" to "real asset allocation"

In an environment of high inflation and increased geo-risk, institutional investors increasingly value the allocation of physical assets. RIT, as the most liquid real estate investment instrument, may be favoured by more long-term funding. The A rating and the status of Asia-Pacific’s largest REIT make it a natural choice of institutional allocation.

# Trend IV: Property Technology and ESG

The digitization of property management and ESG requirements raise industry operating thresholds to facilitate large platforms like this. Small property operators may be pushed out of the market because they cannot afford the costs of technology inputs and ESG compliance.

# ReIT industry trend score: <unk>

Multiple structural trends favour the exercise, particularly interest and connectivity, but there is uncertainty about the timing and strength of these catalysts.

# V. Reverse thinking: What is the market worried about?

Understanding 35 per cent plus NAV concessions requires understanding what markets are worried about.

# Market terror list

1. ** Hong Kong retail sales have been permanently shrinking**: North consumption is not a cyclical phenomenon, but a structural trend towards deep-seated integration.

2. ** Data on the risk of collapse of commercial properties in the interior**: oversupply, weak consumption, renewal of 16.4 per cent are worrying.

3. ** Higher interest rate stays longer**: RIT valuation repairs may be delayed if interest rate reductions are not expected.

4. ** Management change failed**: George Hongchoy retired and could accelerate value destruction if the new management failed or failed to implement the strategy.

5. **Excreet dilution of fund units**: The increase in the last few years has been spread over each unit of NAV and DPU, and the market is concerned that this practice will continue.

# Is the market too pessimistic?

** Part of the possible over-pessimism**:

The market may underestimate the resilience of consumption of essential goods. Even if consumption is so hot in the north, Hong Kong residents will buy food, medical care, and breakfast locally.
- The market may not have a sufficient price-fixing effect on the performance. For every 50 basis points down, financing costs save the lead by nearly HK$300 million in annual interest payments.
- If connectivity is to land, the influx of incremental funds may quickly narrow NAV concessions.

** Part that may not be pessimistic enough**

- The inland property sector may be more problematic than the surface data. The lease renewal of 16.4% is a large figure, and the schedule for the recovery of the commercial real estate markets in Beijing and Shanghai is uncertain.
- The revenue contribution from LREP private fund-raising operations is currently minimal and may be a drag if significant management resources are invested but returns are limited.
- The downside of the property valuation (NAV) may not be over — the Pumps project a further 2-4 per cent annual valuation loss over the next two years.
