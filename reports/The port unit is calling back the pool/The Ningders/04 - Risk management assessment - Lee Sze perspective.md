#2.3 Succession issues: the greatest governance risk

** This is the most critical issue in the governance structure of the Ningde era.**

Recue repeatedly stressed:** "For the founder-driven company, succession planning is one of the most important governance issues."**

** Current status**:
- 58 years old, energetic. No sign of retirement.
- He said publicly in 2024, "Access is being sought."
- But there's no one else to come out.
- There's no obvious "big 2" in the management team.

**Risk evaluation**:
- If the former elite suddenly fails to perform its duties (health, political, etc.), companies will face a short period of uncertainty about directionalities
- No second person has the combined capacity of a group of past generations in terms of technical judgement, strategic planning, client relations, etc.
- It's a classic "key person risk."

** Mitigating factors**:
- The Ningde era has established mature organizational systems and processes that no longer rely solely on individual decision-making
- Corporate technological accumulation (60,000 plus patents) and customer relations (long-term contracts) have organizational inertia
- 58 years old is not very old among entrepreneurs, and 5-10 years of management window period is still available

**LiJan concluded**: The succession issue is a "medium-term risk" (a clear formula is needed for 3-5 years) and not an "urgent risk". But investors should keep track of this issue, and if no candidate for replacement is identified for three years, the risk level will need to be adjusted upwards.

#3.2 Detailed analysis of risks

**(1) US CMC List - Current most-at-large risks**

In January 2025, the Ningde era was added to the CMC list by the United States Department of Defense.

** Substantive impact**:
- The CMC list does not amount to a "list of entities", and does not directly prohibit trade
- The main effect is the prohibition of contracts between the United States Department of Defense and List Enterprises (from 30 June 2026)
- No direct restrictions on sales of civilian batteries to American customers in the Ninder era.

** Potential impact**:
- Agencies like the United States Pension Fund may be asked to take down listed companies ' shares
- Some United States clients may have reduced procurement due to compliance caution
- There is a risk of being upgraded to "list of entities" (low probability but high impact)

** Company response**:
- Ninder's expressed interest in communication and legal action.
- The Mi and the Racing companies have successfully removed them from the list, and there are precedents.
- Indirect market access through joint ventures, technology authorizations, etc. in the United States

**(2) Overcapacity in industry — structural pressures**

Global power cell capacity exceeded 2000GWh in 2025, while actual demand was about 1,200 GWh, with capacity utilization of less than 60 per cent.

This means:
- Second-line companies will start price wars to survive.
- The industry's profit margin is under downward pressure.
- But the leading enterprises are less affected by technological and scale advantages

The Ningde response:** does not engage in a vicious price war, but rather maintains a premium through technological upgrading**. This is evidenced by the rise in the reverse Māori rate to 26.3% in 2025.

**(3) Change of Solid-State Batteries Route - Long-term Technology Risk**

If full solid cell achieves output:
- Existing liquid lithium battery production lines may be subject to devaluation
- Competition patterns may be reshuffled.

But the Ningde response was reasonable:
- Global share of SOLDIC patents >30%
- Small batch production planned for 2027
- Sulphides + condensate polymers parallel routes

Reeling judgement:** Solid-state batteries are more like "progressive upgrades" than "subversive substitutions", and the Ningde era is likely to be a smooth transition.**

# Four, worst-case analysis

Lee Sze said, "Always, you have to know if you can handle the worst of the worst."

# 4.1 Worst scenario one: full decoupling

** Assumed**: The United States added the Ningde era to the list of entities and Europe increased the tariff on Chinese batteries by 50 per cent.

** Impact**:
- Significant decline in offshore earnings (30.6 per cent)
- Overseas factory investment could be stranded.
- The collection could fall back to about $300 billion.
- Net profits could fall to $40-50 billion.

** Response**: There is still significant room for growth in the domestic market (upper penetration + storage breakout) and companies will not "dead", but the growth logic needs to be re-formed.

** Impact on valuation**: Under this scenario, the reasonable market value is about $6000-800 billion, which would fall to about $140-180 for the current A share.** The probability is low (~10 per cent), but the impact is significant.**

# 4.2 Worst scenario two: technology route is being subverted

** Assumption**: Toyota or a start-up company achieved a large volume of fully solid batteries by 2028 with a capability far beyond liquid lithium batteries and with a manageable cost.

** Impact**:
- The 772 GWh liquid battery capacity in the Ningd era is subject to devaluation
- Technology's lead is wiped out.
- Urban coverage may fall from 39 per cent to less than 20 per cent

** Response**: Ninder itself is also laying solid-state batteries and has the most patented.

** Impact on valuation**: reasonable market value is approximately $500-700 billion.** The probability is extremely low (~5 per cent), but it is "fat tail risk".**

# 4.3 Worst scenario III: Management crisis

** Assumed**: The former group left the post suddenly for health or other reasons and did not have a qualified replacement.

** Impact**:
- Short-term stock prices may fall by 20-30%
- There may be a swing in strategic direction.
- But the company fundamentals (technology accumulation, customer contracts, capacity) are still in place

** Response**: The company has established a mature organizational system that can be "automated" in the short term. The core team (Panjian, Lee EQUAL) can maintain its day-to-day operations.

** Impact on valuation**: short-term shocks are significant (20-30%), but recovery is possible within 1-2 years if the successor is qualified.** Medium probability (~15%), impact is manageable**

## 4.4 Combining worst-case scenarios

If multiple risks mentioned above occur simultaneously (probability < 3%):
- The harvest could fall to $250-30 billion.
- Net profits could fall to $30-40 billion.
- Rationale market value of about 450-60 billion yuan
- Roughly $100-140 for Unit A

Even in the worst case scenario, the Ninder era was not "insolvency" -- it had a cash reserve of $392.5 billion to support any crisis. It was a ** "may fall a lot, but not die."
