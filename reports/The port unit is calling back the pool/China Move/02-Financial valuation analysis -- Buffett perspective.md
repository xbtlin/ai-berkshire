# China Mobile (0941.HK) – Financial Valuation Analysis

> Analysis dimensions: earnings quality, capital allocation efficiency, intrinsic value, margin of safety
> Core question: How much is this company worth? Is the current price a good deal?

---

## 1. Profit quality: Is the profit real money?

### 1.1 Revenue and Profit Overview (2025 Annual Report)

| Indicators | 2025 | Year-on-year change |
|------|--------|---------|
| Operating income | 1,050.2 billion yuan | +0.91% |
| Net profit attributable to parent companies | 137.1 billion yuan | -0.9% (+2.0% on the same basis) |
| Excluding non-net profit | 128 billion yuan | +4.27% |
| Operating profit | RMB 148.9 billion | +4.4% |
| EBITDA | 338.9 billion yuan | +1.6% |
| Free cash flow | 82 billion yuan | Healthy and abundant |

The first key observation: **Book net profit decreased slightly, but non-net profit and operating profit both increased**. This shows that core operating capabilities are improving, and the apparent profit decline mainly comes from non-operating factors.

### 1.2 Cash flow quality ★★★★★

EBITDA in 2025 will reach 338.9 billion yuan, far exceeding net profit of 137.1 billion yuan. This is because depreciation of assets in the telecommunications industry is huge—companies depreciate more than 200 billion yuan every year. Depreciation is a non-cash expense, meaning the company actually generates far more cash than paper profits.

The more important data is **free cash flow of 82 billion yuan**. Free Cash Flow = Operating Cash Flow - Capital Expenditures, representing the cash a company truly has at its disposal after keeping the business afloat. The free cash flow of 82 billion yuan corresponds to a market value of about 2 trillion yuan, and the free cash flow rate of return is about 4%.

The net cash flow from operating activities in Q1 of 2026 was as high as 71.4 billion yuan, a year-on-year increase of 128.1%. This is an extremely strong signal - in addition to seasonal factors, it also reflects the release of cash flow from lower capital expenditures.

**Judgment: The profit content is extremely high. ** Every penny earned by China Mobile is real money, and there is no problem of accumulation of accounts receivable or aggressive revenue recognition.

### 1.3 Profit rate trend

| Indicators | 2025 |
|------|--------|
| Net profit margin | 13% |
| EBITDA Margin | ~32% |
| Operating Margin | ~14% |

The characteristics of the telecommunications industry are: high EBITDA profit margin (due to large depreciation), but low net profit margin. China Mobile's net profit margin of 13% is at the mid-range level among global telecom operators. It is worth noting that the increase in the value-added tax rate from 6% to 9% (implemented from January 2026) may further suppress apparent profit margins.

## 2. Capital allocation: How does management spend money?

### 2.1 Capital Expenditure Trend ★★★★☆

| Year | Capital Expenditure | Year-on-Year Change |
|------|---------|---------|
| 2024 | 164 billion yuan | — |
| 2025 | 150.9 billion yuan | -8.0% |
| 2026 (planned) | 136.6 billion yuan | -9.5% |

This is a very positive trend: **capex continues to decline**. The peak of 5G network construction has passed, and future maintenance capital expenditures will be far lower than during the construction period. That means more cash available for dividends and buybacks.

The structure of capital expenditures is also changing: in 2026, investment in computing power networks will increase by 62.4%, and investment in intelligent networks will increase by 19.8%, but the total amount is still declining. The company is "reducing total volume and adjusting structure" - reducing investment in traditional networks and increasing investment in computing power.

### 2.2 Shareholder Return ★★★★☆

| Indicators | 2025 |
|------|--------|
| Full-year dividend | HK$5.27/share (+3.5%) |
| Payout ratio | 75% |
| H share dividend yield | About 6.5-7.0% |
| A-share dividend yield | About 5.1% |

The dividend payout ratio of 75% is relatively high among global telecom operators. The company promises that the dividend payout rate will be "stable and rising" in 2026, and the dividend payout rate will remain above 75% in the next three years.

**Key Calculations:**
- Net profit attributable to parent company in 2025 is RMB 137.1 billion
- 75% dividend payout ratio = NT$102.8 billion used for dividends
- Free cash flow 82 billion yuan
- Dividends exceed free cash flow by approximately 20 billion yuan

This means the company needs to use some of its balance sheet cash to maintain its dividend. However, considering that the company has abundant cash on its books (more than 300 billion yuan), it is completely sustainable in the short term. In the long term, as capital expenditures continue to decline, free cash flow will gradually cover dividend needs.

### 2.3 Balance Sheet ★★★★★

China Mobile's balance sheet is extremely healthy:
- Cash on book exceeds 300 billion yuan
- Very low interest-bearing debt, almost "zero leverage" operation
- Net cash position, no debt repayment pressure

This is a company with almost no financial risk. Even if the operating environment deteriorates, generous cash reserves will ensure that dividends continue for many years.

## 3. ROE analysis: How is capital efficiency?

### 3.1 ROE level ★★★☆☆

The ROE in 2025 is 9.94%, which is approximately equal to 10%. This is a **low rate of return**.

Teardown DuPont analysis:
- Net profit margin: 13% (medium)
- Asset turnover rate: about 0.5 times (low - asset-heavy characteristics)
- Equity multiplier: ~1.5x (low leverage)

The fundamental reason for low ROE is that large amounts of cash and heavy assets drag down capital efficiency. If the company can return excess cash on its books to shareholders in a greater way (higher dividends or large-scale buybacks), ROE can be increased to 12-14%.

**Comparison with peers:**
- China Telecom ROE: about 7-8%
- China Unicom ROE: about 5-6%
- Average ROE of global telecom operators: about 10-15%

China Mobile has the highest ROE among domestic peers, but it is at a mid-range level globally.

### 3.2 ROIC perspective

Considering that the company has almost zero leverage, ROIC (return on invested capital) is close to ROE, around 9-10%. This means that for every 1 yuan of capital invested by the company, it generates a return of approximately 0.1 yuan per year.

For a capital-intensive industry, a 10% ROIC is acceptable, but not excellent. The key question is: as 5G capital expenditures decline, can ROIC increase in the future? If the capital efficiency of the computing power business is higher than that of the traditional network business, the answer is yes; but if the computing power business falls into a price war, ROIC may actually decline.

## 4. Intrinsic value and safety margin

### 4.1 DCF valuation (simplified model)

**Assumptions:**
- Free cash flow in 2025: 82 billion yuan
- Annual growth rate of free cash flow in the next five years: 5% (capital expenditure decline + computing power business growth)
- Sustainable growth rate after 5 years: 2%
- Discount rate: 8% (required rate of return for H shares, taking into account the premium risk of Chinese state-owned enterprises)

| Year | Free cash flow (100 million yuan) |
|------|-------------------|
| 2026E | 861 |
| 2027E | 904 |
| 2028E | 949 |
| 2029E | 997 |
| 2030E | 1,047 |
| Future value (2% perpetual) | 17,799 |

The discounted enterprise value is approximately **1.52 trillion yuan**, plus net cash of approximately 300 billion yuan, the total is approximately **1.820 billion yuan**.

The corresponding intrinsic value per share is approximately **85 yuan** (approximately HK$93). The current price of H shares is about HK$75, corresponding to a discount of about 20%.

### 4.2 Dividend discount model

Calculated based on the full-year dividend of HK$5.27 in 2025:
- Assumes dividend growth rate of 3% per year
- Required rate of return 8%

DDM valuation = 5.27 * (1+3%) / (8%-3%) = **108.6 HKD**

The 9.7 times PE of H shares is the most obviously undervalued in the global telecommunications industry. Even taking into account the discount of Chinese state-owned enterprises, geopolitical risks and the liquidity discount of Hong Kong stocks, it still appears to be on the low side.

A-share PE of 14.5 times is basically reasonable and close to the industry average.

### 4.4 Safety Margin Assessment ★★★★☆

## 5. Key risk factors (financial perspective)

1. **Impact of the value-added tax increase**: The tax rate on basic telecommunications services will be raised from 6% to 9% starting in 2026, which may suppress the net profit margin by 2-3 percentage points.
2. **The return on investment in computing power is uncertain**: Investment in computing power network increased by 62.4%, but it is doubtful whether it can bring corresponding returns.
3. **ARPU growth stagnates**: Mobile ARPU has been hovering at 49-50 yuan for a long time, and revenue growth is weak.
4. **Exchange rate risk**: H shares are priced in Hong Kong dollars, and fluctuations in the RMB exchange rate will affect actual returns.
5. **Policy Risk**: As a central enterprise, it may assume more social responsibilities (such as profit concessions and fee reductions) and sacrifice profits.

## 6. Core Conclusions

### Financial Valuation Rating: ★★★★☆ (4/5)

**Attractive aspects:**
- Cash flow quality is extremely high, free cash flow is abundant and is expected to continue to grow
- Balance sheet is almost flawless, with zero leverage and plenty of cash
- H shares are significantly undervalued, with a dividend yield of 6.5-7% providing downside protection
-Capital expenditure has entered a downward cycle, and future cash flow releases can be expected

**Worrying aspects:**
- ROE is only 10%, capital efficiency has room for improvement but is limited by the central enterprise system
- Revenue growth is close to stagnant and lacks organic growth momentum
- VAT hikes and potential concession policies may erode profits
- The large price difference between AH (A shares premium is about 30-40%), reflecting the structural discount factor in Hong Kong stocks

**One sentence summary:** China Mobile H shares are a value stock of "low valuation + high dividend + low growth". By buying H shares at the current price, you can get a dividend return of about 7% plus room for modest capital appreciation. The core bet is: you believe that this cash machine can continue to operate, and that the market will eventually give it a more reasonable valuation.
