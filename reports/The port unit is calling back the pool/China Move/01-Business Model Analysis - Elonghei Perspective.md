#China Mobile (0941.HK)—Business Model Analysis

> Analysis dimensions: user value, competition barriers, corporate culture, long-term economic characteristics
> Core question: Is this a good business? Why can’t users live without it?

---

## 1. The nature of business: What creates value?

The essence of China Mobile's business is **infrastructure leasing**-building and operating communication networks and collecting monthly rental fees from 1 billion users. This is an extremely special business model:

**Revenue structure (2025):**
- Communication services revenue: still accounts for the majority, but growth is slowing down
- Computing service revenue: 89.8 billion yuan (+11.1%), of which smart computing service growth reached 279%
- AIDC revenue: 35.4% year-on-year growth
- Government and Corporate Business (DICT): continued expansion

From a user perspective, China Mobile serves 1.005 billion mobile customers and is the world's largest telecom operator by user base. There are 599 million 5G network customers, with a penetration rate of 59.6%. There are more than 300 million home broadband customers.

**Core judgment:** Telecommunications is a "necessity" business. Users pay phone bills every month, just like paying water and electricity bills, which is a necessary consumption. But the problem is that the pricing power of this business is not in the hands of enterprises - the government's "speed increase and fee reduction" policy continues to suppress ARPU. Mobile ARPU in 2025 will be 49.5 yuan, with almost no growth in the past five years.

---

## 2. Moat analysis: How deep are the barriers?

### 2.1 Economies of scale moat ★★★★★

China Mobile's first moat is its unreplicable network scale. By the end of 2025:
- There are more than 2.6 million 5G base stations, the largest in the world
- More than 4 million 4G base stations
- Wired broadband covers more than 400 million households

What does this mean? It would take hundreds of billions of investments and decades for any new entrant to replicate this network. This is not a financial issue, but a physical impossibility - spectrum resources are allocated by the state, tower sites are limited, and pipeline resources are scarce.

What's more critical is the **diminishing marginal cost effect**: after the network is built, the cost of serving one more user is extremely low. With a user base of 1 billion, China Mobile's unit network cost is much lower than China Telecom (400 million users) and China Unicom (340 million users). This is a structural cost advantage.

### 2.2 Conversion cost moat ★★★☆☆

Switching costs in the telecommunications industry are **moderate to weak**. After the implementation of the number portability policy, users can theoretically switch operators freely. However, actual data shows that the monthly churn rate of China Mobile’s postpaid users is only 0.8%, indicating that user stickiness is quite strong. Reasons include:
-The number is bound to a large number of social relationships and financial accounts
- Home broadband bundling reduces the possibility of losing a single service
- There is little difference in tariffs among operators, and switching power is insufficient.

But this is not "high switching cost", because users are more "too lazy to switch" rather than "cannot switch".

### 2.3 License/Policy Moat ★★★★★

In fact, there are only three full-service operators in China's telecom market (China Mobile, China Telecom, and China Unicom) plus China Radio and Television. The possibility of new licenses being issued is extremely low. This is the oligarchic pattern endowed by political policies and is one of the strongest moats.

### 2.4 Brand moat ★★☆☆☆

Telecom is a typical "undifferentiated service". Users choose operators mainly based on signal coverage and tariffs, and brand premiums are almost non-existent. China Mobile's brand advantage is more reflected in the user perception of "good signal", but this is essentially an extension of network scale rather than independent brand value.

## 3. User value: Do users really need this product?

**Front:**
- Mobile communications are the infrastructure of modern society, and the demand is extremely rigid.
- Data traffic consumption continues to grow, and 5G application scenarios are gradually enriched
- Enterprise digital transformation brings growth in government and enterprise markets

**Reverse side:**
- Individual user ARPU has been stagnant for a long time, and the "speed increase and fee reduction" policy has not changed.
- User growth has hit the ceiling - 1 billion users means almost everyone in China is a customer
- Communication services are becoming increasingly "public utility", and users will not pay a premium for better communication services

**Core Contradiction:** China Mobile provides services that are "necessary but not worth paying more for". Users can’t live without it, but they’re also unwilling to spend more money. This determines the ceiling of traditional business.

---

## 4. The second growth curve: computing power and the imagination space of AI

The biggest highlight of the 2025 annual report is that **computing service revenue is close to 90 billion yuan**, and the growth rate of intelligent computing services is as high as 279%. The company is transforming from a "communication pipeline provider" to a "computing power service provider":

**Positive Signs:**
- In 2026, investment in computing power networks will increase by 62.4%, and investment in intelligent networks will increase by 19.8%.
- AIDC business grew by 35.4%, and the mobile cloud brand continued to strengthen
- Has the largest data center network and fiber optic backbone network in the country
- Aim to build a "world-class scientific and technological service enterprise" by 2030

**Concerns:**
- Competition in the cloud computing market is extremely fierce, with Alibaba Cloud, Huawei Cloud, and Tencent Cloud all being strong competitors.
- The history of operators' cloud development is not successful, and the market competitiveness of mobile cloud is questionable.
- There is downward pressure on the gross profit margin of the government and enterprise cloud business
- Although computing power service revenue is growing rapidly, its absolute scale is still far smaller than the main communications business
- There are few successful examples among global telecom operators in the transformation from "pipeline" to "platform"

---

## 5. Corporate culture and organizational capabilities

China Mobile is a typical central enterprise. This has two sides:

**Advantages:**
- Strong execution ability, leading the world in 5G network construction speed
- National strategic support, advantages in spectrum allocation and policy preference
- A large workforce (approximately 450,000 people) and unrivaled coverage capabilities

**Disadvantages:**
- Innovation efficiency is low under the bureaucracy and slow to respond to competition from Internet companies
- The incentive mechanism is limited by the salary system of state-owned enterprises, making it difficult to retain top technical talents.
- Management replacement is arranged by organizational departments rather than market-based selection
- The decision-making chain is long and it is difficult to quickly adapt to technological changes

The new chairman Chen Zhongyue proposed the concept of "let front-line employees become small CEOs", showing his intention to reform, but the fundamental constraints of the central enterprise system are difficult to break through in the short term.

---

## 6. Core Conclusions

### Business model rating: ★★★★☆ (4/5)

**The good:**
- Just-in-demand products, 1 billion user base, oligopoly structure, abundant cash flow
- Economies of scale and license barriers form a very deep moat
- Capital expenditures have entered a downward cycle, and free cash flow is expected to continue to improve.

**The bad:**
- ARPU has long-term stagnation, lack of pricing power, and is essentially a "public utility"
- There is great uncertainty in the prospects of the second curve (computing power/AI)
- The central enterprise system limits organizational efficiency and innovation capabilities
- There are very few successful cases of global telecom operators transforming into technology companies

**One sentence summary:** China Mobile is a stable cash-generating machine with a deep and wide moat, but limited growth. It's more of a "bond-like" stock - you're buying certainty and cash flow rather than a growth story. The question is, how much are you willing to pay for that certainty?
