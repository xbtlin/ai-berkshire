# II. Institutional environmental risks

#2.1 Institutional risks in State-owned enterprises

China moves 100% of the state-owned holding enterprises (held through the China Mobile Communications Group). This means:

** System dividend:**
- National strategic support: spectrum allocation, policy bias, priority for government and business projects
- Low financing costs: highest credit ratings and very low borrowing costs
- Zero risk of insolvency: the country cannot let the largest operator fail
- Regulating asylum: as a "national priority", it is protected by a certain degree of competition.

** System cost:**
- ** Social responsibility burden**: UHAs are required (remote area coverage, speed-down fees, etc.) and these investments do not seek economic returns
- ** Risks of policy intervention**: The increase in VAT (6% - 9%) is a typical case - policy changes can change profit expectations overnight
- ** Non-economic objectives**: Management may be required to serve non-commercial objectives such as job stability, regional balance etc.
- ** Associated transactions**: transactions with parent companies and other state enterprises may not be fully marketable

##2.2 Deep meaning of the upturn in VAT

The value added tax on telecommunications basic services was increased from 6 to 9 per cent on 1 January 2026. The importance of this is not the difference in tax rates of 3 percentage points, but rather the key message that ** the government can change the rules of the game at any time.**

The inspiration to investors: The investor state business, you get certainty (not to fail, not to lose market position), but you give up autonomy (pricing power, capital allocation, strategic choice). The cost-effectiveness of this exchange depends on whether the certainty premium you receive is high enough.

#2.3 Geopolitical risks

The Chinese move was delisted by the United States stock exchange in New York in 2021 because the United States Government had included it on the list of "Chinese Military Corporation". The subsequent effects of the incident:
- Reduced willingness to allocate international funds to move China
- H share of liquidity and valuation is under pressure for long periods
- AH stock spreads are maintained at 30-40%

Geopolitical risk is one of the major reasons for the low valuation of Unit H. This risk is difficult to quantify, but must be taken into account.

# Three, long-term games: China's telecommunications industry's final game

#3.1 Communication from a civilizational perspective

Communications are the infrastructure of human civilization. Every revolution in communication technology, from letters to telegrams, telephones, the Internet, and mobile Internet, has contributed to social progress.

From this perspective, the industries in which China moves** will never disappear. Communications demand will only grow and not shrink. The question is not "need to communicate," but "who provides communications, what form they take, how much money they make."

# 3.2 The Fate of Telecommunications

The global telecommunications industry faces a common destiny:** social values are created far more than business values.**

Telecoms support the trillion-grade digital economy, but operators themselves have only a small share of their value. Apples, Googles, tee-tweeting, tremors, etc., have made far more profits on the telecommunications network than operators. This "predation" is a structural tragedy in the telecommunications industry.

China’s move to move up the value chain through arithmetic services, ADC, and so on, essentially seeks to escape the fate of the pipeline. But history has taught us that it is extremely difficult for infrastructure operators to move to the application level – railway companies have not become the logistics giant, electricity companies have not become the technology giants, and telecommunications operators have faced the same challenge.

#3.3 "Determinate" in Chinese

Unlike overseas telecommunications operators, China ' s movement has a unique advantage:** the tacit support of the Chinese Government**.

In the institutional environment of China:
- Operators are not acquired or split up.
- Price wars don't run out of control to threaten survival.
- Policy guarantees for spectrum resource allocation
- Priority for the use of operator capacity in major national works (new capital, East Timor and West Asia)

This "sure" is valuable. In a world of uncertainty, China moves one of the few companies you can be sure to be and still be profitable after 10 years.

# Five, "Assuring shares is a buyer" perspective

# 5.1 Speciality of management in the country

Investments in China are different in nature from investments in Birkhshire or Apple -- you're not "buyers," but in "buy systems."

The management of the company is a replacement. Chen will leave, and there will be a next chairman; and there will be a next general manager. As long as the system remains unchanged, there will be no fundamental change in course.

This is both a good (not dependent on individuals) and a disadvantage (without a Jobus-style vision leader).

# 5.2 Degree of alignment with shareholder interests

The management of the company is less aligned with shareholders’ interests. There is no significant personal ownership of the shares, the remuneration is delinked from the share price, and promotion depends on organizational evaluation rather than market performance.

However, in recent years the National Council has increasingly focused on "market value management" and has called for greater dividends and market value maintenance. This partly compensates for the lack of incentives at the institutional level. The 75% rate of distribution and the "stable upswing" commitment are the result of this policy pressure.

# Six, core conclusion

## Risk management rating:  (3/5)

** Rest assured:**
- The firm's "no-drop" is extremely certain.
- Financial risk is almost zero (zero leverage, cash abundance)
- The industry cannot be taken away by competitors.
- High-levelism offers a real shareholder return.

** A worrying aspect:**
- Low alignment of management with shareholder interests
- The risks of policy intervention are real and unpredictable
- Geopolitical repression of H share valuation
- Institutional constraints on strategic flexibility and innovative capacity
- Low valuations may be "structural" rather than "temporary."

** One sentence concludes:** China moves is a "system-driven" rather than "management-driven" company. You invest in it, essentially in the direction of the system’s stability and the reform of the State enterprise in the telecommunications industry. Management will not be particularly proud, but not particularly bad – and this may be the most realistic expectation under the system. The key question is: is the 6-7% dividend return sufficient to compensate you for the institutional risks and opportunity costs?
