# China Mobile (0941.HK) - Industry Competition Analysis

> Analysis dimensions: industry ecology, competition landscape, multiple thinking models, reverse thinking
> Core question: What is the final outcome of competition in this industry? Is China Mobile's location safe?

---

## 1. The nature of the industry: the "ecological niche" of the telecommunications industry

### 1.1 Use multiple thinking models to understand the telecommunications industry

**Physics Model - "Potential Energy" of Infrastructure:**
Telecom networks are like reservoirs, which require huge investment in the early stage to build "dams" (base stations, fiber optics, data centers). Once built, water (data traffic) will continue to flow through, generating continuous income. China Mobile's 2.6 million 5G base stations and 4 million 4G base stations are the "potential energy" it has accumulated for decades.

**Biological Model – Niche and Symbiosis:**
The relationship between China's three major operators is not a "life-or-death" competition, but more like three species in the same ecological niche - they share spectrum resources, build shared towers, and maintain a balance of "orderly competition" under policy regulation. This pattern is extremely stable, as aggressive behavior by either party will invite regulatory intervention.

**Economic Model – Natural Monopoly and Economies of Scale:**
Telecom networks have typical natural monopoly characteristics: extremely high fixed costs and extremely low marginal costs. In theory, it is most economical for one operator to serve all users. However, due to anti-monopoly considerations, China maintains the structure of three operators (Canada Radio and Television). This means that China Mobile will never monopolize the market, but its scale advantage will never disappear.

### 1.2 Where is the ceiling for industry growth?

The total number of mobile phone users in China has exceeded 1.7 billion, far exceeding the population of 1.4 billion - almost everyone has one or more mobile phones. This means **user growth is over**.

Traffic growth continues, but prices continue to fall. Under the "speed increase and fee reduction" policy, operators are forced to trade volume for price. The result is: traffic doubled, revenue remains unchanged.

The real growth points of the industry are:
- Digitalization of government and enterprises (DICT business)
- Cloud computing and computing power services
- IoT connectivity
- AIDC (AI Data Center)

However, the competitors faced by these new businesses are no longer China Telecom and China Unicom, but Internet/technology companies such as Alibaba Cloud, Huawei Cloud, and Tencent Cloud.

## 2. Competitive Landscape Analysis

### 2.1 Comparison of the three major operators (2025 data)

| Indicators | China Mobile | China Telecom | China Unicom |
|------|---------|---------|---------|
| Mobile users | 1.005 billion | ~400 million | ~340 million |
| 5G users | 599 million | 282 million | 214 million |
| 5G penetration rate | 59.6% | 65.2% | ~63% |
| Operating income | 1,050.2 billion | ~540 billion | 392.2 billion |
| Net profit | 137.1 billion | ~34 billion | 20.8 billion |
| Net profit margin | 13% | ~6% | ~5% |
| ROE | ~10% | ~7-8% | ~5-6% |

**Pattern Judgment:** China Mobile is in an absolute leadership position with approximately 60% of mobile user shares and 70% of industry profit shares. This pattern of "one super and two weak" has lasted for more than 15 years and is very stable.

### 2.2 Why is the pattern so stable?

**Historical path dependence:** China Mobile inherited the mobile business of the original China Telecom and has a huge first-mover advantage. The user base established in the 2G/3G era has formed a snowball effect - more users → high return on investment → good network quality → more user choices.

**Weak form of network effect:** Telecommunications does not have a strong network effect like social networks, but the reputation of "good signal" itself is a weak network effect. When everyone around you uses mobile, "the mobile signal is good" becomes a consensus, and new users choose mobile by default.

**Regulatory balance:** The government does not want any operator to fail, nor does it want one operator to become dominant. This implicit regulatory protection allows all three operators to survive, but it also limits China Mobile from further expanding its share.

### 2.3 Changes and Unchanges in the 5G Era

**Unchanging:**
- China Mobile’s user scale advantage
- The competitive landscape of "one super and two weak"
- Orderly competition under policy control

**Changed:**
- In terms of 5G penetration rate, China Telecom (65.2%) surpassed China Mobile (59.6%), indicating that latecomers are more active in 5G upgrades
- 5G private network business has become a new battlefield. China Unicom’s 5G private network revenue increased by 60% year-on-year, growing faster than China Mobile
- New tracks such as computing power services and AIDC have broken the competitive boundaries of traditional telecommunications

## 3. New battlefield: competition in computing power and AI fields

### 3.1 Operators vs. Internet companies

China Mobile’s competitors in computing power services are no longer just Telecom and China Unicom. The real threats come from:

| Competitors | Cloud Computing Market Share | Advantages |
|---------|--------------|------|
| Alibaba Cloud | ~33% | Leading technology and complete ecosystem |
| Huawei Cloud | ~19% | Technology + government-enterprise relations |
| Tencent Cloud | ~16% | Internet Ecology |
| China Telecom Tianyi Cloud | ~10% | Government and enterprise channels + security compliance |
| Mobile cloud | ~8% | Network + computing power synergy |

China Mobile's mobile cloud ranks around fifth in the public cloud market, with a market share of about 8%. Although the growth rate is fast (the growth rate of intelligent computing services is 279%), the absolute share is far behind Alibaba Cloud and Huawei Cloud.

### 3.2 Structural advantages and disadvantages of operators’ cloud development

**Structural Advantages:**
- Has the most dense computer rooms and fiber optic networks in the country
- Natural "cloud and network integration" capabilities
- Deep channels for government and enterprise customers (especially government and state-owned enterprise customers)
- Sufficient funds and able to withstand long-term investment

**Structural Weaknesses:**
- There is a clear gap between technical capabilities and Internet companies
- Organizational culture favors operations over innovation
- Talent attraction is weaker than that of Internet companies
- Cloud computing requires agile iteration, which conflicts with the operator's process-oriented culture

### 3.3 AIDC: possible breaking point

AIDC (AI Data Center) is an emerging market segment where operators are in a better competitive position than traditional cloud computing. The reason is:
- AIDC has extremely high requirements for power, heat dissipation, and network bandwidth, and operators have more obvious infrastructure advantages.
- Large model training requires massive GPU clusters and low-latency interconnection, and the operator's backbone network is a natural advantage
- Policies encourage operators to develop computing infrastructure

In 2025, China Mobile's AIDC revenue will increase by 35.4% year-on-year, and in 2026, computing power network investment will increase by 62.4%. This is the key bet direction of management.

## 4. Reverse thinking: Under what circumstances will China Mobile fail?

This is the most important question. Use the "think it over" method:

**Scenario One: Technological Disruption**
If there is a technology that can bypass traditional telecommunications networks to provide communication services (such as Starlink satellite Internet), China Mobile's network assets will depreciate significantly. But in China, where satellite internet is strictly regulated and spectrum resources are controlled by the state, this risk is extremely low.

**Scenario 2: Dramatic policy changes**
If the government decides to completely turn telecommunications into a public utility and force a significant reduction in fees or even provide basic communication services for free, profitability will be devastated. But this is inconsistent with the goal of maintaining and increasing the value of state-owned assets, and the possibility is extremely low.

**Scenario 3: All investment in computing power is lost**
If investments in cloud computing and AI continue to suffer losses, large amounts of capital expenditures cannot be returned, which will drag down overall profits. This is the **most real risk** - global telecom operators have a poor history of investing in non-core businesses.

**Scenario 4: Management makes major decision-making errors**
Such as large-scale inefficient acquisitions, excessive diversification, etc. Under the central enterprise system, major decisions must be approved by the State-owned Assets Supervision and Administration Commission, which reduces the probability of extreme mistakes to a certain extent.

**Overall judgment:** The probability of China Mobile's "complete failure" is extremely low. The more likely risk is that of "boiling a frog in warm water" - not sudden death, but lower long-term returns than investors expected.

## 5. Enlightenment from the global telecommunications industry

Looking around the world, the fate of telecom operators is not optimistic:

- **AT&T**: failed multiple transformations (acquisition of Time Warner, DirecTV), then split and returned
- **Vodafone**: forced to shrink after global expansion, share price halved in ten years
- **NTT Docomo**: privatized and delisted by the parent company
- **Deutsche Telekom**: The only case of successful transformation through T-Mobile US

**Common Lessons:** The core capability of telecom operators is "operating networks", not "innovation". When they try to become technology companies, they often pay a high price. China Mobile's vision of being a "world-class technology service enterprise" needs to be carefully evaluated against these lessons learned.

## 6. Core Conclusions

### Industry competition rating: ★★★★☆ (4/5)

**Pros:**
- The oligopoly structure is extremely stable and the risk of being subverted is extremely low
- China Mobile’s leadership position in the industry is unshakable
- AIDC and digitalization of government and enterprises bring structural growth opportunities
- Policies support operators in developing computing infrastructure

**Disadvantages:**
- The growth of traditional communications business has hit the ceiling
- Competitors in the cloud computing market are strong, and operators have a low chance of winning.
- There are few successful cases of transformation in the global telecommunications industry
- The industry as a whole is characterized by "low growth and high capital demand"

**One sentence summary:** China Mobile occupies the best position in an industry that cannot be disrupted but cannot grow quickly. Its competitive position is secure, but that security means more "high floor" than "high ceiling." The logic of investing in China Mobile is not "it will get better", but "it is unlikely to get worse".
