## 1. Investment thesis: Why might it be worth buying?

### Core logic

China Mobile is the world's largest telecom operator, with 1.005 billion mobile users, and is the absolute leader in China's telecom industry. The core of investment logic is not "growth", but "certainty + cash return"**.

**Three support points:**

**1. Cash Making Machine**
Free cash flow in 2025 will be 82 billion yuan, and operating cash flow in Q1 of 2026 will surge by 128% year-on-year. As the peak of 5G capital expenditures passes (CAPEX drops by 8% in 2025 and is planned to drop by another 9.5% in 2026), free cash flow will continue to be released. This is a machine that continues to generate cash without requiring significant reinvestment.

**2. H-share valuations are significantly low**
The PE of H shares is only 9.7 times, and the dividend yield is 6.5-7%, which is low among global telecom operators. DCF is valued at approximately HK$93, which is a discount of approximately 20% from the current price. Even if valuations never repair, a 6.5-7% dividend return provides a solid floor.

**3. Computing power transformation provides option value**
Computing service revenue was 89.8 billion yuan, smart computing services grew by 279%, and AIDC grew by 35.4%. If the computing power transformation is successful, China Mobile may be repriced from "public utility" to "technology infrastructure", and the valuation ceiling will be significantly increased. Even if the transformation is unsuccessful, the cash flow of traditional businesses will still be safe.

### Best investment scenario

- H-share valuation repaired from 9.7 times to 12-13 times (global peer level) → stock price rose 25-35%
- At the same time receive 6.5-7% dividend every year
- The computing power business grew beyond expectations, providing additional upside space
- Three-year compound return can reach 15-20% annualized

## 2. Negative paper: Why might it not be worth buying?

### Core concerns

**1. "Value Trap" Risk**
9.7 times PE may not be "cheap", but the market's reasonable pricing for the following factors:
- Discounted governance of state-owned enterprises (the interests of management and shareholders are not aligned)
- Geopolitical risks (history of US sanctions, uncertainty in Sino-US relations)
- Hong Kong stock liquidity discount
- Revenue growth is close to zero

If these factors are "structural", valuation repair will never happen.

**2. Weak growth**
In 2025, revenue will only increase by 0.91%, and net profit will decrease by 0.9%. The ceiling of 1 billion users has been reached, and ARPU has stagnated at 49-50 yuan for a long time. This means it's difficult to foresee organic revenue growth even under conservative assumptions.

**3. Unpredictable policies**
The value-added tax is raised from 6% to 9%, the policy of speeding up and fee reduction continues, and the social responsibility burden of central enterprises-these policy risks cannot be hedged, and the direction is unpredictable.

**4. The future of computing power transformation is uncertain**
There are very few successful cases of global telecom operators transforming into technology companies (only Deutsche Telekom's adoption of T-Mobile US is considered half-successful). The mobile cloud market share is about 8%, far behind Alibaba Cloud (33%) and Huawei Cloud (19%). Investment in computing power will increase by 62.4% in 2026, but the rate of return is questionable.

**5. Opportunity cost**
There are a large number of investment targets with better growth potential in the global and Chinese markets. Is holding China Mobile a 7% annualized return better than investing in other growth stocks?

### Worst investment scenario

- Valuation continues to hover around 9-10 times PE
- Revenue increased by 0-1%, profit increased slightly
- Dividend yield remains at 6-7% but no capital appreciation
- Three-year compound return is dividend return only (6-7% annualized)

## 7. Key indicators that need to be continuously tracked

1. **Mobile ARPU changes** — If ARPU exceeds 50 yuan and continues to rise, it means pricing power improves
2. **Computing service revenue growth** — whether it can maintain double-digit growth, especially the AIDC business
3. **Free cash flow** — should continue to grow as capital expenditures decline, and attention will be paid to whether it is released as scheduled.
4. **Dividend Payout Rate** — Promises "stable and rising" in 2026, pay attention to whether it can be fulfilled
5. **AH stock price difference** — If it narrows, it means that the valuation of H shares is recovering
6. **Policy Environment** — Value-added tax, fee reduction orders, and policies related to state-owned enterprise reform
7. **Management words and deeds** — New Chairman Chen Zhongyue’s strategic implementation

---

## 8. Final judgment

China Mobile is a company with "extremely high lower limit and limited upper limit".

It won't surprise you, but it will hardly disappoint you. A user base of 1 billion, oligopoly status, abundant cash flow, and continuously declining capital expenditures—these fundamentals form a solid value base.

H shares have a PE of 9.7 times and a dividend yield of 6.5-7%, which is clearly attractive in the current low interest rate environment. The key to the question is not "Is this company good or not" (the answer is yes), but "Are you satisfied with an annualized return of 7-10%" - this is the long-term return level that China Mobile is most likely to provide.

For investors looking for stable cash returns, China Mobile H shares are a choice with a reasonable risk-return ratio. But don't expect it to be the star of your mix - its role is that of "ballast", not "engine".
