Risk two: Australia EnergyAustralia -- bleeding wound

** Risk description**:
The profit from the operation fell sharply between 85.6% and 85 million HK$ in 2025. This is not a one-time event — the challenges that Energoprojekt has faced in recent years:

- Retail markets are highly competitive and user conversion costs are extremely low

** Worst-case scenario**:

**Assessment**: This is the most realistic risk of the moment. Australian business is the most vulnerable link in the medium- and electronic business model, and investors need to follow it closely.

## Risk three: The complexity of implementation of decarbonization transition

** Risk description**:
Medium-power promises to phase out coal by 2040 and to achieve zero net emissions by 2050. This means that a full transition from fossil energy to clean energy will be needed in the next 15-25 years.

** Specific challenges**:
1. ** Cost uncertainty**: The costs of large-scale construction of renewable energy, energy storage and gas facilities may be higher than expected
2. **Technology route risk**: bet which technology (wind, solar, hydrogen, nuclear) is a gamble in itself
3. ** Supply stability**: Challenges to grid stability from intermittent renewable energy sources (wind, photovoltaic)
4. ** Grounded asset risk**: coal power and gas assets that are retired early may face impairment
5. ** Hong Kong ' s special difficulties**: land scarcity and limited deployment of large-scale renewable energy sources

** Positive perspective**:
In other words, decarbonization is not only a cost, but also a driver of profit growth. Management’s attitude to decarbonization should be “positive but prudent” – exactly what is being observed.

# # Risk four: Policy and market risks in the Mainland

** Risk description**:
The local business surplus fell by 13.7 per cent in 2025, mainly affected by three factors:
1. Lower prices for nuclear power on the Internet in Yangjiang
2. Renewable energy consumption restrictions (resort from wind)
3. Competitive pressure from electricity market reform

** Deep question**:
- China is mostly involved in the interior, not in the holding, and has limited influence on operational decisions
- The Chinese electricity market is fast marketing, and the protection of the former "plan price" is fading.
- Uncertainty in the policy environment of foreign investment in China ' s energy sector

** Mitigating factors**:
- The long-term value of nuclear power assets (Ooya Bay, Yangjiang) remains significant
- 75 per cent of non-carbon energy, consistent with China ' s carbon neutral and policy orientation
- Inland operations contribute about 15 per cent to group profits, impact is manageable

# II. Management assessment

#2.1 Governance structure

** Kaji family holding shares**:
- Shareholding: approximately 35%

Family holding + professional managers are more commonly combined in Hong Kong. The centennial reputation of the family provides a certain reputation, but 35% of the holding ratio means that the family has substantial control over the company, with limited voice for small shareholders.

# Six, key conclusions

** Trusting place for management**:
- The Kaji family has been running for centuries, and values long-term reputation.
- As the first Chinese to take the helm, Chiang Dongqiang is right in his direction (focus on Hong Kong and the Mainland)
- Transparency in information disclosure and sound dividends policy
- Financial discipline is good. No risk.

** Places where management needs proof**:
- Will there be a decisive decision on Australia ' s business (sale, curtail or reorganize)
- Whether the ability to implement the decarbonization transition is sufficient
- The ability to remain competitive in the context of accelerated marketization in the Mainland

** Core risk management judgement**:
The biggest risk for electricity and electricity in China is not short-term profit fluctuations, but longer-term "can the bond-like attributes maintain." If Hong Kong’s regulatory framework remains stable, with 8% of the permitted returns not substantially reduced, the central investment logic of electricity and electricity is established.

From a probabilities-weighted perspective, the core investment logic is relatively high (80 per cent+) in the next 5-10 years, but investors should be prepared for the small probabilities disadvantage scenario, which means that buy-in should not be made at a price that lacks a safe margin.
