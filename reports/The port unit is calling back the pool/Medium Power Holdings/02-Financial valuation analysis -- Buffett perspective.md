## 1. Profitability analysis

### 1.1 Revenue and Profit Trends

| Indicators | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | Trends |
|------|--------|--------|--------|------|
| Operating income | About 93 billion | 90.96 billion | 88.02 billion | Continuous decline |
| Operating profit (excluding changes in fair value) | — | 10.95 billion | 10.69 billion | -2.4% |
| Net profit attributable to parent company | — | 11.74 billion | 10.47 billion | -10.8% |
| Earnings per share | — | Approximately HK$4.64 | Approximately HK$4.14 | Decline |

*Unit: Hong Kong dollar*

Revenue in 2025 will decrease by 3.2% year-on-year, and net profit attributable to the parent company will decrease by 10.8%, but operating profit ("core profit" excluding changes in fair value) will only decrease by 2.4%. This gap shows: **Core operations are actually relatively stable, and the sharp decline in profits is more caused by non-recurring items and fair value fluctuations. **

Focusing on operating profit rather than net income attributable to shareholders is a more reasonable way to evaluate utility companies.

### 1.2 Segment Profit Quality

The composition of operating profit of HK$10.69 billion in 2025:

| Sector | Amount (HKD 100 million) | Proportion | Year-on-year |
|------|---------------|------|------|
| Hong Kong | ~95 | ~89% | +7% |
| Mainland China | 16.0 | ~15% | -13.7% |
| Australia | 0.85 | ~1% | -85.6% |
| India | 2.2 | ~2% | -32.8% |
| Other/Adjustments | ~-7 | — | — |

**Core findings**: Hong Kong business contributed approximately 89% of operating profit and grew 7% year-on-year. This is the "fixing needle" for the entire profit structure. If we only look at the Hong Kong business, CLP's profits are actually improving.

But on the other hand, this also means that the three overseas markets (Mainland China, Australia, and India) have collectively invested a large amount of capital, but only contributed about 11% of profits. **There are obvious problems with capital allocation efficiency. **

### 1.3 ROE analysis

| Indicators | Data |
|------|------|
| ROE（TTM） | ~10.2% |
| Shareholders’ Equity | ~HK$117.4 billion |
| Net profit attributable to parent companies | ~HK$10.47 billion |

The ROE of 10.2% is above average for a utility company. But there are several structural factors to note:

- The permitted rate of return for the Hong Kong business is 8% of net fixed assets, but due to the leverage effect (financing part of the fixed assets with debt), the actual ROE can be higher than 8%
- The ROE of overseas business is significantly lower than that of Hong Kong business, and the overall ROE is pulled down.
- If the Australian business can return to normal profitability, the overall ROE may return to 11-12%

### 2.3 Cash flow quality

In the first half of 2025, the group invested approximately HK$7 billion in capital expenditure, of which approximately HK$4.5 billion was invested in Hong Kong. Full-year capital expenditure is expected to be at the level of HK$13-15 billion.

The power industry is characterized by "heavy capital and stable cash flow". CLP's operating cash flow is stable in the long term and is sufficient to cover daily operations and dividend payments, but large capital expenditure projects still require external financing.

## 4. Capital Allocation Assessment

### 4.1 Past capital allocation

CLP’s capital allocation logic has always been: **take Hong Kong as its foundation and expand overseas to seek growth**.

Judging from the results, the effectiveness of this strategy is mixed:

| Market | Capital investment | Return evaluation |
|------|---------|---------|
| Hong Kong | Continuous and stable investment | ★★★★★ Regulatory protection, guaranteed returns |
| Mainland China | Massive investment (nuclear power, wind power, photovoltaic) | ★★★☆☆ Strategic value, but rising policy risks |
| Australia | Heavy investment | ★☆☆☆☆ Long-term poor performance, almost no profit in 2025 |
| India | Medium investment | ★★☆☆☆ Not large enough to be competitive |

**Core Issue**: CLP’s capital allocation in overseas markets is inefficient. If the capital invested in Australia and India were recycled and fully invested in Hong Kong (to the extent permitted by the regulatory framework) or returned to shareholders in a special dividend, the overall rate of return could be even higher.

### 4.2 Future capital expenditure direction

Management has stated that it will focus capital on Hong Kong and mainland China, and projects in Australia and India will need to be self-financing. This is a move in the right direction, but the key is execution.

Decarbonization investments are the largest capital spending driver over the next decade:
- Phase-out of coal power generation by 2040 will require significant replacement investment
- Construction of renewable energy and energy storage facilities
- Natural gas transition plan

These investments can earn an 8% return through the regulatory framework in Hong Kong, but they face market-oriented competitive pressure in overseas markets.

### 5.2 Valuation rationality assessment

**Comparison with history**:
The historical fluctuation range of CLP's PE is roughly between 14-20 times. The current 18.6 times is at a moderately high level, which is not cheap, but it is not a bubble either.

**Comparison with similar**:
- Hong Kong Electric (2638.HK): PE is about 14-15 times, dividend rate is about 6-7%, purely Hong Kong business
- Yangtze River Infrastructure: PE about 12 times
- Global utility leader: PE usually 15-20 times

CLP's valuation is higher than that of Hong Kong Electric, partly because the market gives it a "growth premium" (the possibility of overseas business). But judging from actual performance, whether this premium is reasonable is questionable.

### 5.3 Calculation of intrinsic value

**Method 1: Dividend Discount Model (DDM)**

Assumptions:
- Current dividend per share: HK$3.20
- Dividend growth: 2% (conservative estimate, close to inflation)
- Discount rate: 7% (considering Hong Kong risk-free interest rate + equity risk premium)

Intrinsic value = 3.20 / (7% - 2%) = HKD 64

**Method 2: Adjusted DDM (optimistic scenario)**

Assumptions:
- Dividend growth rate: 3% (fixed asset growth under regulatory framework + slight electricity price adjustment)
- Discount rate: 6.5%

Intrinsic value = 3.20 / (6.5% - 3%) = HKD 91.4

**Method Three: Profit Valuation Method**

Assuming that CLP’s reasonable PE is 15-18 times (a reasonable range for public utilities), earnings per share in 2025 will be approximately HK$4.14:
- Conservative valuation: 4.14 x 15 = HKD 62
- Neutral valuation: 4.14 x 17 = HKD 70
- Optimistic valuation: 4.14 x 18 = HK$74.5

### 5.4 Valuation conclusion

| Scenario | Valuation Range | Description |
|------|---------|------|
| Conservative | HKD 62-64 | Assuming stagnant growth and continued sluggish overseas business |
| Neutral | HKD 70-74 | Moderate dividend growth assuming current conditions continue |
| Optimistic | HKD 85-91 | Assuming Australia improves, mainland China stabilizes, and dividend growth accelerates |

**The current stock price of HK$74 is at the upper edge of neutral valuation**, and the margin of safety is insufficient.

## 7. Key conclusions

**Financial Certainty**:
- Hong Kong business is protected by regulation and profits are highly certain
- Healthy balance sheet and controllable debt
- The dividend policy is stable and has been growing or maintaining for many consecutive years.

**Financial concerns**:
- Australian business will almost "return to zero" in 2025, with a large amount of capital unable to generate returns
- The overall return on capital of overseas business is much lower than that of Hong Kong business
- Future decarbonization investments may push up debt or compress dividend space

**Valuation Judgment**:
The current stock price of HK$74 corresponds to a dividend rate of approximately 4.3% and a PE of 18.6 times, providing a rate of return of approximately "dividend rate 4.3% + growth rate 2% = annualized 6.3%". That's not a bad return forecast for a utility stock, but it's not cheap either.

If you pursue a margin of safety, a more reasonable purchase price should be in the range of HK$60-65 (corresponding to a dividend rate of more than 5%), which will provide more sufficient downside protection.
