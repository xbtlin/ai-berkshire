# I. Understanding the electricity industry with a multi-thinking model

# 1.1 Physics model: energy persistence and irreplaceable

Electricity is the "basic energy" of modern society. Whatever digitalization and AI evolves, the bottom needs power-driven power. This insulation means that electricity demand is only increased or decreased and not lost.

But "demand is not an irreplaceable supplier." In competitive power markets (e.g. Australia), competition between suppliers can be very intense. Only in monopolistic markets (e.g. Hong Kong) can suppliers be truly irreplaceable.

##1.2 Economics model: efficiency boundaries of natural monopolies

Electricity distribution is a typical natural monopoly - building two parallel grids is not only wasteful of resources, but physically unworkable. The Hong Kong government has chosen the model of "private monopoly plus regulatory returns" which is a second-best but stable arrangement in economics:

- Advantages: private enterprises operate more efficiently than State-run ones and regulation ensures that consumers are not over-exploitated
- Disadvantages: lack of competitive incentives may lead to a weakening of efficiency, with 8 per cent of the permitted return likely to be higher than the best in society

#1.3 Biology model: eco-position and adaptability

The eco-position of China and electricity in Hong Kong is the "top predator" -- no predator, top of the food chain. But in the Australian market, it is just one of many competitors who have to compete with other electricity retailers, which is another ecological position.

A key biological lesson:** Success in one eco-position cannot automatically be transferred to another.** The ability of the Chinese and Chinese electricity to operate in Hong Kong’s monopoly market does not help it to win in the Australian competition market. The latest note on this lesson was the 85.6% drop in profits in Australia’s business in 2025.

# II. Industry competition patterns: depth analysis of the sub-markets

#2.1 Hong Kong Market: Monopoly Gold Pattern

** Market structure**:
- Medium: Kowloon, New Territories, Ex-Island, approximately 2.83 million clients
- Port lights: Hong Kong Island, cranes, approximately 590,000 customers
- The two can be connected by submarine cables, with no remaining complementarities, but no competition from the customer

** Regulatory framework** (Control plan agreement 2018-2033):
- Authorized rate of return: 8 per cent of net fixed assets
- Five-year development plan approval system: 2024-2028 for the current cycle
- Fuel cost adjustment mechanism: shift of fuel cost fluctuations to consumers
- Energy efficiency incentives and sanctions: encouraging power companies to promote energy conservation

** Pattern evaluation **:
This is one of the best electricity market patterns around the world. Monopolies, government guarantees, LTAs are locked in – almost impossible to subvert.

Whenever Hong Kong’s economy is depressed, voices are calling for lower rates of return or for the opening of the electricity market. But since 1964, the regulatory framework has been revised several times, and the core structure has never been shaken.

##2.2 China ' s inner land market: a policy-driven fast-changing landscape

** Market structure**:
China’s electricity market is in a critical phase of transition to a market. The electricity spot market is running in several provinces, and new rules for spot trading have been introduced in China and elsewhere in 2026.

The distribution of China and China in the interior is dominated by participation:
- Nuclear power: Grand Asian Gulf nuclear power plant (25 per cent interest), Yangjiang nuclear power plant (17 per cent interest)
- Renewable energy: Project 50+ in 14 provinces, total equity installed 8,098 MW
- 75% of non-carbon energy sources

** Challenges ahead**:
1. The lower prices of nuclear power on the Internet in Yangjiang directly impact profits
2. Renewable energy consumption restrictions (winding) affecting power generation
3. Accelerated electricity market reform, competitive Internet access, compressed profit space
4. Limited voice in policy games as foreign equity participants

** Pattern evaluation **:
Strategic values persist, but in the short term there is policy uncertainty. In the interior, China is more like a "financial investor" than an "operational controller," which limits its strategic flexibility.

#2.3 Australian Market: The Mound of the Red Sea Competition

** Market structure**:
The Australian electricity market is highly marketed and retail competition is extremely high. EnergyAustralia (Small and Medium Power All-In-Commercial) faces direct competition from its own giants, such as Origin Energy and AGL Energy.

** The dilemma of 2025**:
- Curbing profit margins in retail markets: low price for competitors
- Accelerated client loss: low user conversion costs and poor integrity
- Increased coal electricity costs: double squeeze on fuel prices and carbon emissions
- Increased bad debt: arrears of electricity bills due from consumers under economic pressure
- Compliance penalties: marketing and customer communication violations are fined

** Pattern evaluation **:
This is a market where business is hard to do. Full competition, tight regulation, consumer sensitivity, complex energy transformation -- almost all the bad things are going on. EnergyAustralia takes up a lot of management resources and capital, but it pays little.

##2.4 Indian market: high growth but high risk

** Market structure**:
The operation is operated through Apprava Energy, which has 1,974 MW of equity installed. Operations include wind power, solar power, and transmission.

** Pattern evaluation **:
High growth potential is hedged by high risk - policy uncertainty, currency devaluation risk, infrastructure challenges, and difficulties in collecting receivables. The size of electricity in medium is too small in the Indian market and there are no competitive barriers.

# Three, in reverse thinking: Why shouldn't we buy electricity?

In the "think first why not buy" way of thinking, the most important negative argument:

#3.1 Will the permitted return be further reduced?

In 2008, 9.99% fell to 8% in 2018. When the 2033 agreement expires, the pressure on society to further reduce the rate of return may be high if Hong Kong’s economy continues to be depressed.

** Probability assessment**: Medium. 8% is no longer high (global utility average) and the possibility of a substantial downward adjustment is not significant, but a small downward revision (e.g., 7 to 7.5%) cannot be ruled out.

# 3.2 The "double blow" of energy transformation

The Chinese electricity is facing a dilemma:
- To phase out coal power (2040 years ago), but coal power is still an important power supply base
- I'm going to need a lot of investment in renewable energy and storage, but these technologies are still evolving.
- "New and old swaps" in the investment process may result in impairment of assets and a reduction in efficiency

If the transition is too fast, it may face problems of supply stability and soaring costs; if it is too slow, it may face regulatory sanctions and reputational risks.

# 3.3 Australian business is a "value trap"?

EnergyAustralia has been performing poorly since 2019, with profits falling sharply to HK$85 million in 2025. If this is not a cyclical fluctuation but a structural problem, the 10 billion HK$ in Central and Central Power invested in Australia may be at risk of impairment.

Morningstar's analysis also notes that "EnergyAustralia's improvement is key to the further improvement of the valuation of Medium Electricity" — which also means that the valuation may be suppressed if EnergyAustralia continues to deteriorate.

#3.4 Growth ceiling is clear

Under the regulatory framework, the growth of Hong Kong operations is limited to:
- Slow growth in the demand for electricity in Hong Kong (the city has grown highly)
- Fixed asset expansion is subject to approval of the five-year plan
- A flat rate of return.

Overseas markets lack competitive advantages. So the growth story of electricity in medium is actually weak.

#3.5 Interest rate sensitivity

As a class of bond assets, the median value is highly sensitive to interest rates. If global interest rates remain at a high level for a long time, the attractiveness of 4.3 per cent of dividends will decline and the valuation hub may move down.

# Four, thinking: Why should we consider buying electricity?

##4.1 Extremely certain cash flow

In a world of uncertainty, there is little certainty about the cash flow from the Sino-Hong Kong operation. The regulatory framework guarantees a profit base, 2.83 million customers’ electricity demand is not lost, and 99.99% of electricity reliability ensures stability of operations.

# 4.2 "Purpose of real purchasing power"

The HK$3.20 per dividend is a 4.3 per cent rate of return, which provides a moderate inflation protection if the company continues to increase the dividends slightly (with a 1-2 per cent rate of increase over the past few years).

# 4.3 The windfall benefits of decarbonization investments

If China and China invest massively in renewable energy and storage facilities in Hong Kong, these additional fixed assets will also enjoy an acceptable return of 8%. That is, decarbonization is not just a cost, but a driver of profit growth.

# 4.4 "To be bored so much that it's underestimated"

Utilities are often unattended by the market, analysts are poorly covered and hot money is not popular. Such "neglect" sometimes means more reasonable pricing and patient long-term investors.

# V. Long-term trends in industry

#5.1 Electricity demand: structural growth

Even if Hong Kong’s economic growth slows, the trend towards data centres, electric cars, and full electrification will drive a moderate increase in electricity demand. In the long run, "Electrification Everything" is a global trend.

##5.2 Regulatory trends: moderate to moderate somewhat tight

The trend in global utility regulation is to focus more on consumer protection and environmental responsibility, and to allow a downward pressure on returns. But the special nature of Hong Kong’s political system means that much radical reform is less likely.

# 5.3 Technological change: acting and not destabilizing

Technology such as distributed power generation, storage, and smart grids are evolving, but the infrastructure nature of power transmission is not undermined. The monopoly of power transmission and distribution in medium power is not subject to technological shocks in the short term.

## 5.4 Competition trends: increasing fragmentation

Monopoly markets (Hong Kong) will remain stable, and competitive markets (Australia, part of the interior) will continue to divide. Intra-electric electricity needs to find its own strategies in these two distinct competition environments.

# VII. Key findings

** Industry pattern is good for electricity in the middle**:
- The situation of the two oligopolies in Hong Kong is extremely stable.
- The regulatory framework provides profit "floor."
- The trend of electrification supports long-term demand
- The transmission and distribution monopoly is not to be sabotaged for a short period of time.

** Industry patterns are unfavourable for electricity in China**:
- Multimarket operations bring complexity and implementation risks
- Australian competition market performance reveals the power boundaries.
- The uncertainty of the decarbonization transition, although a trend of great magnitude, cannot be ignored
- Permitted rates of return may be subject to downward pressure in the long term

** Summary in Mandry: If you just have to answer "How's the business in Hong Kong?" The answer is certainly good. But if you have to answer "How's the business in Hong Kong?" The answer is much more complicated. Its dilemma in Australia suggests that even a good management team, once it enters a non-competition environment, can act as ordinary. This is not a moral question for management, but a border issue for the "competence circle".
