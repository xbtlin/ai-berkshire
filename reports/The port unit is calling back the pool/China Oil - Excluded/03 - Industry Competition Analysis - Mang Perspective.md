# PetroChina (0857.HK) industry competition analysis

## 1. Industry Ecology

### Characteristics of the global oil and gas industry
The oil and gas industry is one of the largest single industries in human society, with annual revenue exceeding US$4 trillion. Use Munger’s framework to understand:

**Industry Attractiveness Rating: ★★☆☆☆ (2/5)**

Reason:
1. **Extremely high capital intensity**: The investment in a deepwater well can reach hundreds of millions of dollars
2. **Uncontrollable Price**: Determined by OPEC+, geopolitics, and global demand
3. **Natural attributes of depletion**: Once an oil field is put into production, it will become exhausted.
4. **Uncertain long-term demand prospects**: The global energy transition will gradually reduce the share of fossil energy

### China’s oil and gas industry landscape

| Enterprise | Domestic crude oil production | Natural gas production | Core positioning |
|------|------------|-----------|----------|
| PetroChina | ~100 million tons/year | ~150 billion cubic meters | Upstream is king |
| Sinopec | ~35 million tons/year | ~35 billion cubic meters | Refining is king |
| CNOOC | ~65 million tons/year (oil equivalent) | ~40 billion cubic meters | Offshore oil and gas |

PetroChina’s dominant position in domestic oil and gas production is almost unshakable—accounting for more than 50% of the country’s crude oil production and 65%+ of its natural gas production.

## 2. Multidimensional assessment of competitiveness

### 1. Resource endowment
**Rating: ★★★★☆ (4/5)**

PetroChina owns the highest quality oil and gas fields in China:
- Changqing Oilfield: Annual oil and gas production exceeds 65 million tons (the largest in China)
- Daqing Oilfield: Although declining, it still produces 30 million tons+
- Tarim Oilfield: Rich deep-seated natural gas reserves
- Southwest oil and gas fields: shale gas development accelerates

Overseas assets cover Central Asia, the Middle East, Africa and the Americas, with equity production of approximately 100 million tons of oil equivalent.

### 2. Natural gas business competitiveness
**Rating: ★★★★☆ (4/5)**

This is PetroChina’s biggest differentiated advantage:
- Accounts for more than 65% of domestic natural gas production
- China's natural gas consumption is still growing (annual growth of 5-8%)
- LNG receiving terminal + pipeline network forms infrastructure barrier
- Natural gas is "cleaner" than oil and is a transitional energy source on the path to carbon neutrality

### 3. Refining and sales competitiveness
**Rating: ★★☆☆☆ (2/5)**

This is PetroChina’s shortcomings:
- Refineries are mostly located in areas with weak demand such as the Northeast and Northwest.
- The number of gas stations (approximately 22,000) and quality are weaker than those of Sinopec (30,000+)
- Refining and chemical profit margins lower than industry average

### 4. Cost control
**Rating: ★★★☆☆ (3/5)**

- The cost of domestic oil field production is about US$35-45/barrel, which is higher than that in the Middle East but lower than the average in the world.
- The cost of overseas assets varies, and the cost of some projects (such as Canadian oil sands) is relatively high
- Improvements have been made in recent years through cost reduction and efficiency increase.

## 3. Key issues of Munger format

### "What is the biggest mistake this industry makes?"
1. **Large-scale acquisitions at high oil prices**: PetroChina’s overseas acquisition wave from 2005 to 2013, some assets have been impaired
2. **Underestimating the speed of energy transition**: If the popularity of electric vehicles is faster than expected, upstream oil and gas assets may face the risk of "stranding"
3. **Ignore return on capital**: long-term pursuit of "production growth" rather than "value growth"

### "What force may subvert the existing structure?"
1. **Popularization of global carbon tax**: Increase the cost of fossil energy and accelerate the shrinking of demand
2. **Nuclear fusion/hydrogen energy breakthrough**: a "black swan" that is distant but cannot be ignored
3. **Shale gas revolution expands**: If China’s shale gas is commercially developed on a large scale, it may change the competitive landscape

### "What are the disadvantages of PetroChina compared to CNOOC?"
- The average cost of CNOOC’s offshore oil and gas barrels is about US$30, which is lower than PetroChina’s onshore US$35-45
- CNOOC's assets are more concentrated and management incentives are more market-oriented
- CNOOC ROE (15%+) far exceeds PetroChina (5%)

## 4. Natural gas: the only structural bright spot

China’s natural gas industry is in a growth stage:
- Consumption volume in 2025 will be approximately 420 billion cubic meters, and the penetration rate is still lower than the global average
- Expected to reach 550-600 billion cubic meters in 2030
- PetroChina, as the largest supplier, will directly benefit

The strategic significance of natural gas to PetroChina:
1. Provide a profit buffer when oil prices fall
2. Long-term contract pricing, income is more stable than crude oil
3. Comply with ESG trends and help improve valuations
4. However, gas prices in China are currently low and profit margins are limited.

## 5. Core Conclusions

**Industry competitiveness rating: ★★★☆☆ (3/5)**

PetroChina’s competitive position in the oil and gas industry is solid (resource monopoly + license barriers), but the attractiveness of the industry itself is declining. From the perspective of Munger's "reverse thinking": **If the world accelerates decarbonization, the value of PetroChina's upstream assets will be revalued. ** This is the greatest long-term risk.

In the short and medium term, natural gas business growth and improved capital discipline are positive factors, but they are not enough to fundamentally change the industry dilemma of "low ROE and high capital consumption". In the same track, CNOOC's business model and execution quality are better.
