# PetroChina (0857.HK) Risks and Management Assessment

## 1. Core risk identification

### Risk Matrix

| Risk type | Severity | Probability of occurrence | Comprehensive rating |
|----------|----------|----------|----------|
| Prolonged low oil prices (<$50) | Very high | Medium | ★★★★☆ |
| Global decarbonization acceleration | Very high | Medium to high | ★★★★☆ |
| Overseas asset political risk | High | Medium | ★★★☆☆ |
| Domestic oil field production is accelerating decline | High | Medium | ★★★☆☆ |
| Natural gas price reform falls short of expectations | Medium | Medium | ★★★☆☆ |
| Major safety/environmental accident | Extremely high | Low | ★★☆☆☆ |

### Detailed explanation of key risks

**1. Risk of long-term downturn in oil prices**
PetroChina's breakeven oil price is about US$45-50 per barrel. If Brent stays below $50 for a long time, profits will shrink significantly. Although the current geopolitical situation supports oil prices, the peak of global demand + the elasticity of U.S. shale oil supply have put the oil price center under downward pressure.

**2. "Stranded Asset" Risk**
If the global process of carbon neutrality accelerates, some oil and gas assets with a long medium-term development period may not be able to recover their investment during their economic life cycle. This is one of the core reasons why the valuations of international oil companies continue to be discounted.

**3. Geographical risks of overseas assets**
CNPC has a large number of assets in Central Asia (Kazakhstan), the Middle East (Iraq), Africa (Sudan/Chad), South America (Venezuela) and other places. Geopolitical uncertainty is high in these areas.

## 2. Management evaluation

### Governance Structure
**Rating: ★★☆☆☆ (2.5/5)**

As one of the world's largest state-owned oil companies, PetroChina's governance faces:
- Management is appointed by the State-owned Assets Supervision and Administration Commission/Organization Department, and the tenure system leads to short-term decision-making preferences
- The number of employees exceeds 1 million, and the "reducing staff and increasing efficiency" progress is slow
- There are corruption cases in history (such as the 2013 Zhou Yongkang case involving the PetroChina system)

### Improvements in recent years:
- Capital expenditure discipline has been significantly strengthened, and we no longer blindly pursue "output first"
- The dividend ratio increased from 35-40% in the past to 54.7%
- Propose "value creation" assessment orientation to replace pure output KPI

### Capital allocation capability
**Rating: ★★☆☆☆ (2.5/5)**

Historical questions:
- The wave of overseas acquisitions from 2005 to 2013, most of which were purchased at high prices (such as the acquisition of Peruvian assets in 2013)
- Some overseas projects have been devalued by tens of billions of dollars
- Heavy capital investment in the refining and chemical sector but low returns

Improvements in recent years:
- Be more cautious about overseas investments
- Focus on strategic directions such as natural gas and shale gas
- Start considering asset divestiture (such as pipeline network independence)

## 3. Li Lu’s "Civilization Evolution" Perspective

Li Lu emphasized that corporate value should be judged from the "evolutionary direction of civilization". From this perspective:

### Positive factors:
1. As a "transitional energy", natural gas still has a growth period of more than 20 years in China.
2. China’s energy security needs ensure policy support for domestic exploration and development
3. The demand for petrochemical products (plastics, chemical fibers, etc.) is positively related to GDP and will not completely disappear.

### Negative factors:
1. The direction of civilization evolution is "decarbonization", and fossil energy is to be replaced.
2. The younger generation of investors has higher requirements for ESG, and funds are flowing out of fossil energy.
3. Technological advances (batteries, solar energy) are accelerating the substitution process

### Core judgment:
PetroChina is not on the side of "civilization and progress" - it is a representative of the old energy system. This doesn't mean it will disappear, but it means: 1) valuations are under long-term pressure; 2) room for growth is limited; 3) investors are more inclined to "harvest" (high dividends) than "sow" (long-term holding).

## 4. Scenario Analysis

### Optimistic scenario (probability 20%)
- Oil prices remain at $80+/barrel
- Natural gas price changes were implemented, and gas prices increased by 20%+
- Dividend rate increased to 60%+
- Stock price rises 30-50%, dividend yield + capital gains return 15%+

### Baseline scenario (50% probability)
- Oil prices center around US$60-70/barrel
- Steady growth in natural gas
- Profit maintained at around 150 billion yuan
- Investors receive 5% dividend return every year, and the stock price is basically flat

### Pessimistic scenario (30% probability)
- Oil prices fall below $50
- Global recession suppresses demand
- Profits fell below 100 billion yuan
- Share price fell 20-30%, partially offset by dividend gains

## 5. Core Conclusions

**Risk Management Rating: ★★☆☆☆ (2.5/5)**

The fundamental contradiction facing PetroChina is: as a fossil energy company, it is in an industry trend that has been replaced for a long time, and management has limited room to respond to this (it is impossible to transform into a new energy company). Its status as a central enterprise ensures that it will not "die", but it also ensures that it will not "live a wonderful life".

From the perspective of Li Lu's safety margin, PetroChina's safety margin mainly comes from: PB's 0.5 times asset discount, 5% dividend rate, and the certainty of natural gas growth. But these are not enough to constitute an "exciting investment opportunity."

Suitable investor profile: A conservative allocation that is bullish on oil prices, can accept a bottom line of 5% annual return, and does not pursue high growth.
