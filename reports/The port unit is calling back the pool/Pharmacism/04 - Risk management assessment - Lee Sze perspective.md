# II. Risk level analysis

## First layer: Deadly risk (which could result in permanent value destruction)

Risk 1: BIOSECURE Bill full upgrade

** Risk description**: The US Biosafety Act was signed in December 2025. While the current version is relatively modest (grandfather’s clause protection until 2032, with increased flexibility in administrative review), there is a potential for the future of the Act to expand its scope – for example, from federal procurement restrictions to cooperative restrictions on all companies that receive US federal funding.

** Worst-case scenario**:
- The bill extends to prohibit any drug company receiving NISH funding from cooperating with China ' s CDMO
- Full withdrawal of U.S. clients (over half of the U.S. client newly signed projects are from the U.S.)
- 30-40 per cent reduction in revenue and significant contraction of commercial pipelines
- The three-star, Longsa and other competitors take orders for transfer, and the drug-specify organisms are permanently reduced in their competitive position.

** Current mitigation factors**:
- Grandfather's clause protects the existing contract until 2032.
- The administrative review process has increased the margin of discretion
- The name of the drug-specify creature is not directly included in the bill.
- Technical barriers (especially complex biological drugs) increase the difficulty of customer transfer

** Probability assessment**: The probability of full escalation to lethality is about 15-20%. But even if it is moderate and implemented, the cumulative effect of a prolonged, gradual loss of customers may be significant.

Risk 2: China-US relations are getting worse

** Risk description**: Biosafety is only one dimension of the Chinese-American technological competition. If China-US relations deteriorates in general, broader sanctions or restrictions may arise, such as:
- Pharmacism is on the list of entities
- Limited availability of critical equipment and raw materials
- The dollar settlement is blocked.

** Worst-case scenario**: China-like total decoupling, with companies forced to withdraw completely from the United States and ally markets and to retain only China and some emerging market operations.

** Probability assessment**: The probability of extreme decoupling is about 5-10%, but the probability of "progressive decoupling" (a gradual accumulation of restrictions) is higher.

# # Risk 6: Technological route change #

** Risk description**: Existing capacity and technological accumulation of drug-edicting organisms may depreciate if new generation therapies (mRNA, cell therapy, gene therapy, etc.) are replaced on a large scale by traditional macro-molecular antibody drugs.

** Assessment**: The risk is low in the short term (five years). Traditional macro-molecular antibodies, bi-resistance and ADC are still the dominant biopharmaceutical developments.

Risk 7: Brain drain, <unk>

** Risk description**: The core asset of the CDMO industry is the human being. Samsung biology and other competitors are actively recruiting technical talent from Chinese CDMO enterprises. Geopolitical pressures may accelerate the brain drain.

# # Risk 8: Exchange rate fluctuations #

** Risk description**: Income is predominantly US dollar-based and costs are predominantly renminbi. The appreciation of the renminbi or the depreciation of the dollar can reduce profit margins. But companies have hedge tools, and the spread of the medium-United States spreads has limited the probability of extreme exchange rate movements over the long term.
