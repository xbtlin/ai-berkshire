# Three: Competition power analysis

# 3.1 Port 5 Power Analysis

** Vendor bargaining capacity **

The suppliers of the main raw materials (cultures, disposable bioreactors, etc.) are dispersed, and drug-specify organisms have some bargaining power as large customers. However, suppliers of critical equipment (e.g. bioreactors) are concentrated.

** Customer bargaining power (medium)**

- Large drug companies (e.g. Pfizer, Rhodes) have a large number of CDMOs, and they're very competitive.
- Small and medium-sized Biotech has limited options and weak bargaining power
- Once the clinical stage is advanced, the client ' s bargaining power is drastically reduced (the switching cost is too high)

** Alternative threat <unk> (low)**

- Self-generating capacity of pharmaceutical companies is a theoretical alternative, but the trend is a continuous increase in outsourcing rates
- Emerging technologies (continuing stream production, etc.) may change the production paradigm but do not pose a threat in the short term

** New entrants threat (low, but with attention to regional changes)**

- Building a biopharmaceutical plant that meets GMP standards requires billions of dollars in investment and years of compliance experience
- But governments are subsidizing indigenous CDMO construction (USA, EUR, IND) with the potential to diversify markets over time

** Co-operative strength (high and increasing)**

This is the most critical force. Industry is undergoing the following changes:
1. Three Stars biologically aggressive expansion, productive arms race
2. BIOSECURE Act to break the old balance of competition
3. The new crown is being used to stock the CDMO industry, which is partially overcapacity
4. Increased price competition on low-end projects

# Four: BIOSECURE Act: The Outer Impact of Changing the Rules of the Game

# 4.1 Status of the Bill

In December 2025, the President of the United States signed the Biosafety Act, which was incorporated into the National Defence Authorization Act (NDAA) for the financial year 2026.

** Core elements of the bill**:
- Restricted cooperation between United States federal government agencies and the designation of the Chinese Biotech Corporation
- Contains "Grandfather Clauses": the existing contract is enforceable until 2032
- The final version added an administrative review process, giving the White House discretion to apply restrictions
- The name of the drug-specify creature and the drug-smuggling-conde was not named directly in the final version.

##4.2 Actual impact assessment

** The direct impact may be limited**:
- U.S. direct mining accounts for less than 5% of the proceeds of drug-edicting organisms
- Grandfather's clause protects the existing contract until 2032.
- Increased flexibility in the administrative review process

** The indirect effects may be significant**:
- United States clients may prioritize new projects to non-China CDMOs for compliance reasons
- Three star organisms signed a new $4.1 billion order in 2025, which may have partially reflected the transfer of orders
- Market pricing of "China Risk" may be a long-term disincentive to the explicit biovaluation

# 4.3 Response of the drug-specify creature

- Suspend construction of the Worcester base in Massachusetts, United States
- Strengthening the capacity of European Irish factories (as a non-Chinese production base)
- Continue to work on non-United States markets (Europe, Asia and the Pacific)
- Technical barriers to complex biological drugs (double resistance, ADC) reduce customer transfer willingness
