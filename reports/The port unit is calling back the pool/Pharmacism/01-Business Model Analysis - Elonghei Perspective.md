# WuXi Biologics (2269.HK) Business Model Analysis

> Analysis Dimension: Business Nature and Moat
> Core question: Is this a good business? What do you think about user experience and business common sense?
> Data cutoff: May 2026

---

## 1. The nature of business: What exactly is WuXi Biologics doing?

The essence of WuXi Biologics' business is to help global pharmaceutical companies turn biopharmaceutical molecules in the laboratory into products that can be mass-produced.

More specifically, the company provides CRDMO (Contract Research, Development and Manufacturing) one-stop services. From discovery to launch of a biologic drug, it usually takes 8-12 years and billions of dollars of investment. WuXi Biologics’ value proposition is: You give me the molecules, and I will help you with cell line development, process optimization, and clinical sample production, all the way to commercial large-scale production. Customers do not need to build their own teams and factories, which can significantly reduce initial investment and risks.

The underlying logic of this business is **professional division of labor**. Just like TSMC is to chip design companies, WuXi Biologics is to innovative pharmaceutical companies. They are essentially professional collaborations in which "you focus on innovation and I am responsible for manufacturing."

### Three major business sectors

| Sector | Content | 2025 Performance |
|------|------|------------|
| Research Services (R) | Drug Discovery, Cell Line Development | Record High Down Payments and Total Payments |
| Development Services (D) | Process development, clinical phase I/II/III samples | Supported 156 IND applications and added 209 new comprehensive projects |
| Production Services (M) | Commercial Production | Revenue increased by 26.4% year-on-year, and 28 PPQs were completed |

Key features: **Once customers enter the development stage, switching costs are extremely high**. After the production process of a biopharmaceutical is approved by the FDA, changing the CDMO means re-process transfer, verification and declaration, which takes 1-2 years and costs tens of millions of dollars. This is the most valuable part of WuXi Biologics' business model - the back-end lock-in effect.

## 2. "Follow and win molecules" strategy: an exquisite funnel model

WuXi Biologics' core business strategy is called "Follow and Win the Molecule".

The logic of this strategy is:

1. **Cast a wide net on the front end**: Undertake a large number of early-stage research projects (preclinical and phase I) at competitive prices and build customer relationships
2. **Mid-end follow-up**: Provide continuous development services as the customer's drug enters Phase II and Phase III.
3. **Back-end harvest**: Once the drug is approved for marketing, locking in a commercial production contract is the most profitable part.

Funnel data as of end of 2025:

- Total number of comprehensive projects: 945
- Clinical phase III projects: 74
- Commercial production projects: 25
- New projects in 2025: 209 (record high, 148 in 2024)
- PPQ (process verification) completed in 2025: 28 (+75% year-on-year)
- PPQ scheduled in 2026: 34

**The beauty of this funnel model is**: the large influx of early-stage projects means that there will be a continuous stream of projects advancing to the commercialization stage in 3-5 years. Orders on hand are US$23.7 billion (including US$11.5 billion in service orders and US$12.2 billion in potential milestone payments), of which US$4.5 billion are visible orders within 3 years, providing strong revenue visibility.

## 3. Moat analysis: wide or narrow?

### Moat 1: Technical capabilities and platform accumulation ★★★★☆

WuXi Biologics has established significant technological advantages in the field of complex biopharmaceuticals:

- There are 196 bispecific antibody projects and 252 ADC projects. Complex biological drugs account for more than 50% of the total projects.
- Each dual-antibody and ADC addition is approximately 30%, indicating that technical capabilities continue to gain market validation.
- Has the world's leading one-stop CRDMO platform, covering the entire chain from discovery to commercialization

**But note**: The moat of technical capabilities is not insurmountable. Samsung Biologics is already quite competitive in terms of production capacity and yield, and Lonza is also strong in the ADC field.

### Moat 2: Customer switching cost ★★★★★

This is WuXi Biologics’ deepest moat.

Once the production process of biopharmaceuticals is registered with FDA/EMA, the cost of replacing the CDMO is extremely high:
- Process transfer takes 12-18 months
- Cost tens of millions of dollars
- Requires re-process validation and regulatory filing
- Risk of supply disruption

945 projects on hand mean that 945 "pipelines" continue to generate switching costs. Especially with 74 Phase III projects and 25 commercialization projects, it is almost impossible for these customers to leave easily.

### Moat Three: Scale and Cost Advantages ★★★☆☆

WuXi Biologics' total production capacity is planned to exceed 580,000 liters (2026), ranking among the top among global CDMOs. Scale brings cost sharing advantages, but this moat is quickly being caught up or even surpassed by Samsung Biotech (845,000 liters).

**China's manufacturing cost advantage** is weakening - on the one hand, labor and operating costs continue to rise, on the other hand, geopolitical risks have led some customers to be willing to pay a premium for "de-Chinaization."

### Moat Four: Network Effect ★★☆☆☆

There is no network effect in the traditional sense in the CDMO industry. More customers don't directly make the service better. But there are indirect effects: more projects bring more process experience accumulation, improving the efficiency and success rate of subsequent projects.

### Comprehensive moat rating: ★★★★☆ (wider)

The core moat comes from customer switching costs and technology platform accumulation. But geopolitical risks are systematically eroding this moat—if customers are forced to leave for policy reasons, the switching cost line of defense becomes ineffective.

## 4. User experience perspective: Why do customers choose WuXi Biologics?

From the perspective of customers (pharmaceutical companies), the core values of choosing WuXi Biologics are:

1. **One-stop service**: full coverage from drug discovery to commercial production, avoiding the risks and costs of multiple technology transfers
2. **Speed Advantage**: The company claims that it can file from DNA to IND within 15 months, which is several months faster than the industry average.
3. **Cost-effectiveness**: Compared with European and American CDMOs, the price still has certain advantages (but the gap is narrowing)
4. **Rich project experience**: Accumulation of experience in 945 projects, especially in the field of complex biopharmaceuticals

**But customers’ concerns are also very real**:
- After the signing of the U.S. Biosafety Act, customers receiving U.S. federal funds face compliance pressure
- Concerns about intellectual property protection have weakened but not been eliminated
- Risks of supply chain concentration in China

## 5. Business common sense test

### Is this business profitable?

The gross profit margin in 2025 will be 46%, and the adjusted net profit margin will be approximately 30%. This is a business with good profit margins, but requires large capital expenditures to maintain capacity expansion. Capital expenditures in 2024 will be approximately 4.8 billion yuan, and free cash flow will be only 1.3 billion yuan. Most of the money earned by ** was invested back into building production capacity, and the actual money shareholders can get is limited. **

### Is growth sustainable?

The biopharmaceutical CDMO industry is expected to grow at an annual rate of 10%+ until 2030, and the market size is expected to increase from US$14 billion to more than US$20 billion. If WuXi Biologics can maintain its market share, its growth will be supported by fundamentals. But we need to be vigilant: industry growth does not equal the growth of individual companies, especially under geopolitical interference.

### Do I understand this business?

**Higher complexity**. Biopharmaceutical CDMO involves highly specialized fields such as cell engineering, process amplification, and quality control. Investors need to admit that most people's understanding of "CHO ​​cell culture optimization" or "double antibody process development" is limited. This increases investment uncertainty - it may be difficult to independently judge changes in technological competitiveness.

**Bottom line judgment**: WuXi Biologics is indeed a good business - the logic of professional division of labor is established, customer stickiness is high, and industry growth is certain. But it is not the kind of business that "any fool can run well": it is asset-heavy, technology-intensive, and geopolitically sensitive. In Duan Yongping's framework, this is more like a business that "needs to continue to be done right" rather than a business that is "hard to do wrong."
