# BOCHK (2388.HK) – Financial Valuation Analysis

## 1. Profit quality analysis

### Revenue structure (2025)
- Operating income: HK$77.019 billion (+8.1% YoY)
- Net interest income: accounting for approximately 65-70%, which is the core source of income
- Non-interest income: insurance, wealth management, trade finance fees

### Profit Trend
- Net profit attributable to parent companies: HK$40.121 billion (+4.9% YoY)
- Net profit growth rate is slightly lower than revenue growth rate, which may be due to rising credit costs or fluctuations in investment income

### Profit quality judgment ★★★★☆

**Front**:
- Steady revenue growth (+8.1%) and increased non-interest income contribution
- The cost-to-income ratio is only 20.76%, which is extremely outstanding in the global banking industry (peers usually 30-50%)
- Means that for every HKD of revenue earned, only HKD0.2 of operating cost is required

**Negative**:
- ROE is about 10.19%, which is not outstanding for a bank with a PE of 9.3 times.
- The reason for low ROE: CET1 is as high as 23.69%, and capital efficiency is dragged down by "over-capitalization"
- If CET1 is reduced to 15% level (still extremely safe), ROE can be increased to 14-15%

## 2. Capital efficiency and return

### Key Capital Indicators
| Indicators | 2025 | Industry Reference |
|------|--------|----------|
| ROE | 10.19% | 12% is a good level |
| CET1 ratio | 23.69% | Far exceeding regulatory requirements (8%+) |
| Total capital ratio | 25.69% | Extremely abundant |
| Cost-to-income ratio | 20.76% | Among the lowest in the world |

### Capital Efficiency Assessment ★★★☆☆

**Core Contradiction**: BOCHK is a bank with "excess capital".

- CET1 23.69% means there is about 8-10 percentage points of "excess capital"
- If this capital is returned through buybacks or special dividends, it can significantly increase ROE and value per share
- However, as a subsidiary of Bank of China, the decision-making power of capital allocation does not entirely lie with BOCHK itself.

**Potential free space**:
- If CET1 is reduced from 23.69% to 16% (still double the regulatory requirement), approximately HK$50-60 billion in capital will be released
- Corresponding to an additional return space of approximately 10-12% of the current market capitalization

## 3. Balance sheet health

### Capital Adequacy
- CET1 23.69% is one of the highest levels in the global banking industry
- There is no risk of insufficient capital, or even the problem of "over-adequacy"
- Extremely high capital buffer means weather any crisis

### Asset Quality
- Hong Kong mainly focuses on mortgage loans, with a very low historical default rate (<0.1%)
- Corporate loans are concentrated in Hong Kong and the Greater Bay Area, and the overall quality is controllable
- Focus: Credit risk of Mainland-related loan exposures

### Liquidity
- As a note-issuing bank, liquidity management has natural advantages
- The current deposit base in the Hong Kong market is extremely stable

## 4. Valuation analysis

### Current valuation level
| Indicators | Values |
|------|------|
| PE (TTM) | ~9.3x |
| PB | ~1.0-1.1 times |
| Dividend Yield | ~5-6% |
| ROE | ~10.19% |

### Valuation rationality judgment ★★★★☆

**The meaning of PE 9.3 times**:
- For a bank with ROE of 10%, PB≈1x is reasonable pricing (ROE≈COE)
- The current PB is about 1.0-1.1 times, which means that the market's valuation of BOCHK is basically "in place"
- But if the space released by CET1 is considered, the real ROE can reach 14-15%, then PB should be ≥1.3 times

**Implicit "value trap" or "value discovery"**:
- If parent bank never releases excess capital - current valuations are reasonable, no excess returns
- If the parent bank gradually releases capital (increases dividend rate, initiates repurchases) - there is 20-30% upside potential
- Key variable: the parent bank’s wishes, not BOCHK’s own operations

### Safety Margin Assessment

**Downside Protection**:
- 1.0 times PB is already the bottom area (bank net assets are a "hard bottom")
- 5-6% dividend yield provides holding period returns
- Very high CET1 means there is no risk of insufficient capital leading to financing

**Upside Flexibility**:
- Depends on capital release, cross-border business growth, NIM stabilization

## 5. Cash flow and shareholder returns

### Dividend History
- BOCHK’s historical dividend payout rate is approximately 40-50%
- Calculated based on a profit of HK$40.1 billion and a dividend payout rate of 50% in 2025, the dividend will be approximately HK$20 billion.
- Corresponding dividend rate 5-6%

### Possibility of repurchase
- CET1 23.69% provides ample room for buybacks
- But as of now, BOCHK has not launched large-scale repurchases
- Whether the parent bank (Bank of China) allows it is a key issue

### Shareholder Return Sustainability ★★★★☆
- The current dividend payment is completely sustainable (the dividend payout ratio is only 50%, and the profit coverage is more than enough)
- Potential positive catalysts: increase dividend payout ratio to 60-70%, or initiate buybacks
- This will be the core trigger for revaluation
