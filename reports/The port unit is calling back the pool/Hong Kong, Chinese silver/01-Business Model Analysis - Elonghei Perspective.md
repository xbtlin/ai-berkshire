# BOCHK (2388.HK) – Business model analysis

## 1. The essence of business

BOCHK is essentially one of the largest local full-featured banks in Hong Kong, and its business model is extremely classic:

1. **Deposit Absorption**: With its status as one of the three major note-issuing banks and its branches all over Hong Kong, it can obtain deposits at very low cost.
2. **Credit Grant**: Mainly Hong Kong mortgage loans, corporate loans, and trade financing
3. **Fees income**: insurance distribution, fund sales, cross-border remittances (relying on the network of the parent bank, Bank of China)

**Core positioning**: BOCHK is "the bank for Hong Kong people" - covering the financial needs of Hong Kong people throughout their life cycle, from opening accounts for students, paying wages for working people, to purchasing properties and mortgages, and retirement financial management.

## 2. Moat Assessment ★★★★☆ (4/5)

**Strengths:**

- **Note-issuing bank privilege**: One of the three major note-issuing banks in Hong Kong (HSBC, Bank of China, Standard Chartered), which gives strong brand recognition and credibility. The status of a note-issuing bank itself is an insurmountable barrier.
- **Customer stickiness is extremely high**: Hong Kong people’s banking relationships often last for decades from account opening to mortgage. Migration costs are high—changing to a mortgage bank requires legal fees, re-approval, and other friction.
- **Branch Density**: Approximately 200+ branches + smart banking centers in Hong Kong, with unparalleled physical coverage
- **Parent Bank Network Empowerment**: Bank of China’s global network provides BOCHK with a natural channel for cross-border business

**Weaknesses:**

- **Interest rate sensitive business**: Net interest margin fluctuates with HIBOR/federal funds rate and is beyond the control of management
- **Hong Kong market ceiling**: The market size of 7 million people is limited, and growth is restricted by population and economy.
- **Parent Company Related Transactions**: As a subsidiary of Bank of China, its independence is questionable and it may be required to cooperate with the parent bank's strategies.

## 3. "Simplicity" test of business model

This is a **simple business** that Duan Yongping would appreciate:

- **Positive**: deposit-taking-lending-charging, a banking model that has remained unchanged for a century. The business area focuses on Hong Kong and does not require the management of global complexity. The cost-to-income ratio is extremely low at 20.76%, indicating extremely high operating efficiency.
- **Negative**: The only complexity comes from the parent company relationship - does BOCHK truly operate in the best interests of its own shareholders? Or is it partially responsible for the strategic tasks of the parent bank?

**Conclusion**: The business model itself is extremely simple, but the "governance structure" adds a layer of uncertainty.

## 4. User value and "differentiation"

The value BOCHK creates for customers:
- **Convenience**: The widest physical + digital channel coverage in Hong Kong
- **Trust**: Endorsement from the note-issuing bank + century-old brand
- **Cross-border advantages**: For Hong Kong people to go north for consumption/investment, BOCHK is the most natural choice (the parent bank has the widest network in the mainland)
- **Comprehensive Service**: One-stop deposit, loan, exchange insurance and investment

The main source of differentiation is the natural advantages of the cross-border (Mainland China-Hong Kong) channel, which HSBC and Standard Chartered do not fully possess.

## 5. Long-term sustainability ★★★★☆ (4/5)

**Pros:**
- Hong Kong’s status as an international financial center is irreplaceable in the short term
- The integration trend of the Greater Bay Area is beneficial to BOCHK’s cross-border business
- The CET1 ratio is extremely high at 23.69%. Capital is extremely abundant and there is room for substantial increase in dividends or repurchases.
- The cost-to-income ratio of 20.76% is one of the best levels in the global banking industry

**Disadvantages:**
- Hong Kong interest rates follow the Federal Reserve, and NIM is under pressure during the interest rate cut cycle
- If the Hong Kong property market continues to adjust, the quality of mortgage assets may be affected.
- The "discount" caused by the parent company's governance exists for a long time
