# I. Business landscape: the basics of the water and drink track

#1.1 China ' s market for packaged drinking water

The market for bottled water in China is expected to break 300 billion yuan in 2025. The industry has several core features:

- ** There is still room for improvement in penetration**: per capita consumption of bottled water in China is much lower than in Europe and Japan and South Korea
- ** Sustained growth**: annual compound growth rate of about 8-10 per cent maintained over the past decade
- **Expendance **: clear trend from pure water to natural water, mineral water upgrading
- **Focused up **: combined, five major brands (farmer spring, IPo, 100 years old, Vahaha, Master Kang) account for about 60%

It's a good industry — big, steady, growing.

# 1.2 Chinese beverage industry

The beverage industry is more disaggregated and diversified, with several key trends:

- ** Healthy**: Sugar-free, low-sugar, zero-addition mainstream demand
- ** Classification**: Tea beverages, functional beverages, juices, coffee, etc., with growth logic
- **New consumer brands are emerging**: new players like the gas forest, the fruit, etc.
- ** Channel change**: new channels such as instant retailing, community group purchasing, changing competition rules

The central feature of the beverage industry is** the growth driven by innovation in the goods category** — who can capture the next trend in the major goods category and who can achieve excess growth.

# Three, the final thinking of the industry

#3.1 The final end of the water wrapping

Experience in the global mature market shows that the packaging water industry will eventually form a ** oligarchy or trio-** pattern:

- France: D'Ann + Nestlé (departed)
- United States: Dasani (Cola) + Aquafina + Polish Spring
- Japan: Trinity + Assun + Coca Cola

China’s final odds are probably: ** Farmers’ Hill Springs + Ebo + one or two regional brands. In this final position, farmers’ mountain springs are probably the biggest winners with their natural water positioning and a complete product matrix.

# 3.2 The final end of the beverage industry

The beverage industry is not as concentrated as water packaging, because of the diversity of products and the dispersion of consumer demand. But the "platform" beverages companies – those with multiple successful products and powerful channels – will be the ultimate winners.

The global example is Coca-Cola: it not only sells coke, it also has hundreds of beverage brands covering almost all categories. The farmers' springs are moving in this direction — from water to tea to functional drinks to juice — to gradually build the prototype of the Chinese version of Coca-Cola.

# Four: Reverse thinking: What are the circumstances under which the farmers' springs will lose?

##4.1 Scenario I: substitution of goods

If there is a fundamental change in consumer drinking-water habits – such as the spread of straight drinking-water systems and a significant increase in water purification-purification rates – the need for packaging water may be hit. But this scenario is less likely to occur in China in the short term, because water pollution fears and consumption habits change slowly.

# 4.2 Scenario two: sugarless tea track collapses

If sugar-free tea is a windfall rather than a long-term trend, growth in eastern leaf will slow considerably. But, from experience in the Japanese market, sugar-free tea has been in use in Japan for 30 years and is still growing, and the trend in China is likely to be sustained.

## 4.3 Scenario III: Channel subversion

If the offline channel is completely dismantled – for example, when no one sells a machine, community buys, or instant retailing becomes the main channel for drinking consumption – the traditional farmer springs network’s advantage is weakened. But the drink is typical of the "in-time consumption" category, and the importance of the offline channel will not change fundamentally in the short term.

##4.4 Scenario IV: Regulatory or policy risk

Water policy tightening, environmental requirements raising, and restrictions on water access permits - all of which may increase the operating costs of farmers' springs - are low-probability policy risks, given the size and influence of companies.

## 4.5 Scenario V: Sustained public opinion attacks

Lessons from 2024 suggest that the brand reputation of the social media era is fragile. If the farmer spring is once again confronted with a massive public opinion attack (especially on issues related to national emotions), it may hit performance again. This is a risk of a medium probability and a medium impact.

- ** Water distribution**: national network of 16 water sources, which competition cannot replicate quickly
- ** Channel depth**: 2.37 million plus terminal network maintenance and management capacity

- **Competition pressure**: All big guys are adding sugar-free tea, price wars could break out
- ** Success rate for new items**: Not every new product can become an Eastern leaf.

# VII. Core conclusions

From the point of view of competition in the industry, the farmers' springs occupy an enviable position:

1. ** Good industry**: Packing water and drinks are big lanes, long ramps, and not subject to subversion by technology
2. ** Good position**: Double track tap (water package first + sugar-free tea first)
3. ** Possession of good weapons**: Waterfield + brand + Triple Barrier to Competition
4. ** Faced with weak rivals**: declining share of I Po, unrest in the management of the Wakhaha, lack of access to new brands

But it also needs to be clear:

** The high growth period of the sugar-free tea track may be only 2-3 years** and will then enter steady-state competition
2. **The share of Eastern leaves is likely to decline**, from 50 per cent + to 30-40 per cent is normal industry evolution
3. ** Where is the next "Oriental Leaf"?** The sustainability of corporate innovation pipelines in producing large monoliths is key to long-term growth.
4. ** Channel change** is a variable that requires sustained attention but does not pose a real threat in the short term

** To sum up, the farmer's mountain springs are the best "horse races" in the Chinese beverages industry, riding on the best track, but the steepest slopes of the track may have passed.**
