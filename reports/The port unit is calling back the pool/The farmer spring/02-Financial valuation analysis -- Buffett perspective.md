> Analytical dimensions: financial quality, profitability, capital allocation and valuation

# I. Financial quality: "Is it true money?"

#1.1 Income quality

The farmer springs, which in 2025 generated 52,553 million yuan, increased by 22.5% over the same period, breaking the 50 billion mark for the first time. The driving force behind the growth of income is clear:

- ** Tea drinks**: 21.596 billion, year-on-year + 29%, contributing the maximum increment
- **Package water**: 18.709 billion, year-on-year + 17.3%, out of 2024, when public opinion was down
- ** Functional beverages**: approximately 5 billion, year-on-year + 13.6%
- ** Juice drinks**: about 4 billion, year-on-year + 21.3%

Income quality evaluation

- Growth comes from real consumption demand and is not dependent on mergers or accounting instruments
- Double-digit growth in all groups of goods, growth in structural health
- Tea drinks go beyond packaging water to become the first source of income, with a more balanced income structure
- No risk of concentration of large clients, with highly dispersed sources of income (millions of end consumers)

# 1.2 Profit quality

The net profit return to the mother in 2025 was $15.868 billion, a 30.9 per cent increase in the year, and the profit increase was higher than the income increase — which means that earning efficiency is improving.

# 1.3 Cash flow quality

The profits of the farmer springs are of great quality, with several key signals:

- ** Operating cash flows **: The company maintains a business cash flow that greatly exceeds net profits over the years, which means that profits are "real silver."
- ** interest-free liabilities**: companies do not need to borrow money to run their business and do not need to be bothered by interest payments
- ** Asset-liability ratio is lower than industry average**: below industry level of about 9 percentage points
- ** High Red **: 11,134 million in gold in 2025, about 70%

This is a "typist" — the profits that can be made can be directly cash, without the need for substantial capital investment to sustain operations.

# II. Deepness of profitability

# 2.1 ROE Dismantling: 44% secret

In 2025, ROE reached 44.2%, which is an excellent figure.

**ROE = net interest rate x turnover of assets x equity multiplier**

- **NIG 30.2%**: product has premium capacity and costs are properly controlled
- ** Liquidity ratio**: while heavy assets such as waterfield plants are available, high-frequency consumer goods are still working well
- ** equity multiplier**: Low liability, low equity multiplier, which suggests that ROE is driven mainly by "profit power" rather than "leveraging"

A company that can do 44 per cent REE without leverage is a global consumer industry with a glimmer of power.

#2: Sustainability of profitability

What needs to be considered is whether 60 per cent of the Mäori rate and 44 per cent of ROE are sustainable?

**Favourables**:
- Brand premium is solid.
- The increase in the share of tea beverages has a positive impact on the Māori rate (the Māori rate for tea beverages is higher than the rate for packing water)
- 16 water sources are spread out to cover logistics costs
- The scale effect continues to show.

** Adverse**:
- Price volatility of PET raw materials (the price of PET started to rise in 2026)
- Increased competition may reduce price space
- Companies indicate that there is no product-change plan, which indicates that pricing is not absolute

** Sensitization**: The probability of a Maori rate remaining short-term between 58% and 62% is high. If the tea drink ratio continues to rise, the Maori rate may be slow to rise.

# Three: Capital allocation: How does management spend money?

#3.1 Capital expenditure

The farmers' springs require capital to expand their waterfield plants, equipment and capacity every year. Three new water sources (Hunan-Sun-Shun, Yong-Mong, Tung-Gura, Tibet) were added in 2025, and a new Yunnan sedan snow mountain source in 2026.

Capital expenditure evaluation: ** Reasonable and disciplined**. Companies do not expand blindly, and the selection of new water sources combines water quality with location advantages (reducing logistics costs in close proximity to supply).

#3.2 Reding policy

The proposed cash share in 2025 is 11,134 million yuan, or about 70 per cent of the net profit from the mother. Considering that the bell shares are about 84.4 per cent, the individual will receive about 9.4 billion yuan.

The high red rate means two things:
1. The company has a strong cash flow and a large share of its capital
2. Management (mainly Jong-soo) does not tend to invest its money blindly in the company

For shareholders, the high red rate is friendly – indicating that management is not motivated by "imperialist construction." There are also concerns that the high rate of red distribution will affect future capital investment capacity.

##3.3 Repurchase

By the end of 2024, Jong-soo announced plans to increase farmers’ market stocks by HK$2 billion, a move to convey confidence to the market after the wave of public opinion. The real control of the increase in real silver and silver is a positive sign.

# V. Financial risk points

#5.1 Cost risk of raw materials

PET is the main packaging material cost, and its price is associated with the price of international crude oil. Prices of PET began to rise in 2026, and companies have responded to the price lock, but if they continue to rise, they will erode the Māori rate.

# 5.2 High-scoring controversy

Some investors question that this is "the blood of the major shareholders." But, from another perspective, companies are not short of money, do not borrow money, and have sufficient capital spending capacity to split the money, and high dividends do not in themselves constitute financial risk.

#5.3 Single market risk

Almost 100% of farmers’ income comes from mainland China, without an internationalization. If China’s consumer market is chronically depressed, companies lack a hedge.

# VII. Core conclusions

The farmers' financial statements are almost textbook-grade "good company" features:

1. ** High ROE + Low Leveraging**: 44% ROE is almost entirely profit-driven and not amplified by borrowing
2. ** High Māori + High Net Benefit**: 60% Māori, 30% net interest rate, compared to only high-end white wine in the food and beverage industry
3. ** Strong cash flow + high score **: The money earned is real and willing to share with shareholders
4. ** interest-free liabilities**: very low financial risk, with sufficient financial buffers in any crisis

** But good companies are not good prices.** The current 40+PE means that the market has already fully priced the company’s excellence. If you buy it, investors are essentially betting that the company will maintain the current high growth rate (15-20%) for at least three to five years.

**Ideal timing**: If the PE falls to 25-30 times the price (comparable shares are about HK$ 30-38), it will provide a better ratio of risk gains. This opportunity usually occurs when markets panic, public opinion waves or performance falls short of expectations – a good purchase window when the price fell to HK$ 34.85 in 2024.
