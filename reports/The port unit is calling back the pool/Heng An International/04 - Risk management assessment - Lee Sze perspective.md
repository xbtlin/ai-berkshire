# Hengan International (1044.HK) Risk and Management Assessment

> Analysis dimensions: management quality, corporate governance, long-term value creation capabilities, factors with Chinese characteristics

## 1. Management evaluation

### Founder and Successor

Hengan was co-founded by Shi Wenbo and Xu Lianjie in 1985. The two have cooperated for nearly 40 years and are a rare example of long-term partnership in the history of Chinese enterprises.

#### Shi Wenbo (Chairman of the Board of Directors)
- Holds approximately 20.6% of shares
-Conservative and steady business philosophy
- Died in April 2025 (at the age of about 70), and his son Shi Huangjian took over relevant positions

#### Xu Lianjie (one of the founders)
- Holds approximately 23.16% of shares
- Core promoter of corporate strategy
- His son Xu Qingchi has entered management

**Key events**: After Shi Wenbo’s death in 2025, Hengan faces a true “generation handover.” This is an important turning point for a 40-year-old family business.

### Second generation management

- **Shi Huangjian** (Shi Wenbo’s son): has served as president and publicly pointed out the "malignant involution" of the industry.
- **Xu Qingchi** (son of Xu Lianjie): serves on the board of directors

**Assessment**: The second generation management is in the "prove themselves" stage. Judging from Shi Huangjian’s public remarks, he has a clear understanding of industry competition, but whether he can demonstrate the same strategic vision and execution capabilities as the founder in actual operations still needs time to be verified.

### The process of de-familialization reform

Hengan’s investment in corporate governance reform deserves recognition:
1. **2001**: Recruit Thomas Group for process transformation
2. **2008**: Hired Booz Allen for management and control optimization
3. **2014**: Hiring PricewaterhouseCoopers to conduct strategic review

Core achievements of the reform:
- A large number of family members and entrepreneurial veterans were persuaded to leave management positions
- Established a professional manager system
- Performance appraisal and internal control systems have been strengthened

**But still need to pay attention**:
- The two major families hold about 44% of the shares in total, and the actual control is concentrated
- Are family interests and minority shareholder interests always aligned?
- How to fill the power vacuum after the death of a founder?

## 2. Corporate governance risks

### Historical flaws

Hengan has faced serious governance challenges in the past:

1. **2018-2019 Short Selling Report**:
   - Bonitas Research once questioned the authenticity of Hengan’s cash
   - Allegation that the company inflated deposits through related parties
   - Although the company rebutted, the stock price was significantly affected.

2. **Non-operating current accounts**:
   - At the end of the 2019 reporting period, non-operating current accounts and fund lending totaled 6.666 billion yuan.
   - Accounts for 47% of consolidated net assets
   - Far above normal levels

3. **Auditor concerns**:
   - Large capital transactions between related parties have always been the focus of audits

### Current Governance Status

Governance has improved in recent years:
- The scale of related-party transactions has decreased
- Information disclosure is more standardized
- The proportion of independent directors meets the standard
- Robust dividend policy reflects a certain shareholder-friendly attitude

**Li Lu's possible concerns**: For a company that has had governance flaws, investors need a longer "observation period" to confirm that the improvements are real and lasting. One or two years of good performance is not enough to offset more than a decade of trust-building issues.

## 4. Long-term value creation ability

### Can Hengan be "built to last forever"?

**Pros**:
1. Product demand is unlikely to disappear – humans will always need paper towels and sanitary products
2. There is no risk of technological disruption in the industry - there will be no "new inventions that replace paper towels"
3. The top position will not be easily shaken without major mistakes.
4. The Chinese market is huge. Even if the growth rate is zero, the absolute size is enough to support the leader.

**Disadvantages**:
1. No growth = no compounding effect = long-term returns depend on dividends
2. The industry lacks the characteristics of "winner takes all" - it is difficult to significantly increase the share
3. Brand value may be consumed faster than it is accumulated (price wars erode brand image)
4. Can second-generation operators maintain the stability and prudence of the founders?

### Expectations in the 10-year dimension

- **Best Scenario** (20% probability): Successful high-endization + industry integration + continuous improvement of governance, doubling the market value
- **Baseline Scenario** (55% probability): Maintain the status quo, earn 6% dividends + 5% organic growth every year, and the total return is about 80%
- **Worst case scenario** (25% probability): Governance issues or price wars lead to halved profits and a 40% drop in market value

Expected annualized return: approximately 6-8% (plus dividends)

## 5. Factors with Chinese characteristics

### Governance Culture of Fujian Private Enterprises

Hengan is a typical Fujian Jinjiang private enterprise. Fujian private enterprises have their own unique business culture:
- **Positive**: pragmatic, low-key, focused on family inheritance, strong resilience
- **Negative**: Family interests are given priority, information is not transparent enough, and external supervision is insufficient.

Li Lu emphasized "the positive effects of entrepreneurship" when investing in Chinese companies, but was also wary of "the risks of lack of institutions." Hengan is in between the two - it has the inheritance of entrepreneurial spirit, but there is still room for improvement in institutionalization.

### The "blank area" where state-owned enterprises/foreign capital does not participate

One characteristic of China's daily necessities market is that large state-owned enterprises do not participate (look down upon it) and foreign giants have limited participation (P&G/Kimberly-Clark has a small share in China). This gives private enterprises like Hengan a relatively free space to compete - neither having to face the pressure of the "national team" nor the global resource crushing of multinational giants.

## 7. Core Conclusions

Evaluating Hengan International from the perspective of Li Lu’s investment philosophy:

**Front**:
- There is no policy risk or technological disruption risk in the industry
- Product demand is rigid and cash flow is highly predictable
- Reasonable valuation and sustainable dividends
- Have a background as a "good student" in China's consumer goods field

**Negative**:
- Not the "long slope and thick snow" type of company that Li Lu likes - no potential for exponential growth
- Historical flaws in governance make trust take longer to build
- Uncertainty about second-generation succession
- The competitive structure of the industry determines that this is not an area where excess returns can be obtained

**Final judgment**: Hengan International is suitable as a "stable allocation" rather than a "core position". It provides the investment attributes of "low volatility + guaranteed dividends + moderate appreciation", with an expected annualized return of 8-10%. For investors who pursue long-term high compound returns (15%+), Hengan lacks sufficient "explosive power"; but for conservative investors who pursue "not losing money and outperforming inflation", it is a qualified choice.
