# Hengan International (1044.HK) Financial Valuation Analysis

> Analysis dimensions: earnings quality, capital return, free cash flow, safety margin

## 1. Profitability analysis

### Revenue and Profit Trends

|------|-------------|------|-----------------|------|
| 2021 | 227.0 | -4.5% | 27.4 | +7.5% |
| 2022 | 225.1 | -0.8% | 18.3 | -33.2% |
| 2023 | 237.6 | +5.5% | 28.0 | +53% |

**Key Findings**:
- Revenue fluctuates between 22 billion and 24 billion yuan, with almost no growth
- Profit fluctuations are greater than revenue, mainly affected by raw material costs and competitive pricing
- Profits surged by 53% in 2023 and then fell by 18% in 2024, indicating that profits are not stable enough

### Gross profit margin analysis

- Overall gross profit margin: 32-34% range
- Gross profit margin of sanitary napkins: 60%+ (main contributor to profit)
- Tissue gross profit margin: approximately 22% (squeezed by price war and pulp costs)
- Diaper gross profit margin: medium

**Core Contradiction**: The high-profit sanitary napkin business has limited growth (affected by population decline), while the low-profit paper towel business accounts for a large proportion and faces fierce competition. The profit structure has "ceiling above and floor below".

## 2. Capital return analysis

### ROE level

Hengan International's ROE is about 15% - an "above average" level among consumer goods companies:
- Higher than the general manufacturing industry (8-12%)
- But lower than top consumer products companies (20-30%)

ROE breakdown (DuPont analysis):
- Net profit margin: about 10% (medium)
- Asset turnover: approximately 1.0x (medium)
- Financial leverage: ~1.5x (moderate)

### Stability of ROE

Hengan's ROE fluctuates between 12-18%. This fluctuation mainly comes from:
1. Cyclical fluctuations in pulp prices affect gross profit margins
2. Year-to-year differences in competitive promotional spending
3. One-time expenses (such as impairment, restructuring)

A long-term average ROE of 15% is "good enough" for a low-growth consumer products company - the key issue is not the ROE level, but the growth rate.

### Capital allocation

Hengan’s capital allocation is relatively stable:
- **Dividend**: The dividend payout rate is about 60-70%, and the dividend rate is about 6%
- **Capital Expenditure**: Moderate, mainly for capacity maintenance and upgrades
- **Buyback**: Occasionally conduct small-scale buybacks
- **Mergers and Acquisitions**: No major acquisitions in recent years

**Points that Buffett will focus on**: A dividend rate of 6% means that the company returns most of its profits to shareholders, which is the right thing to do for a company with limited growth - more "duty" than wasting money on low-return expansion.

## 3. Free cash flow analysis

### Cash flow quality

Consumable goods manufacturing companies generally have good cash flow quality:
- Reasonable turnover of accounts receivable
- Inventories are raw materials and finished products, with good liquidity
- Fixed asset depreciation roughly matches capital expenditures

Hengan's annual operating cash flow is approximately 3-3.5 billion yuan, capital expenditure is approximately 8-1.2 billion yuan, and free cash flow is approximately 2-2.5 billion yuan.

Calculated based on the current market value of approximately HK$28 billion (approximately RMB 26 billion):
- FCF Yield ≈ 20-25/260 ≈ 7.7-9.6%

**This is an attractive FCF yield**, significantly higher than most consumer goods companies.

### Cash Flow Sustainability

The rigid demand for Hengan's products determines the high sustainability of cash flow:
- The demand for paper towels/sanitary napkins will not disappear
- No need for large additional investments
- Stable channels and stable customers

But be careful: if the price war intensifies further, profit margin compression may cause FCF to decline.

### Safety Margin Assessment

**Conservative valuation** (perpetual annuity method):
- Assume FCF is 2 billion yuan, no growth, discount rate 8%
- Intrinsic value = 20/8% = 25 billion yuan ≈ 27 billion Hong Kong dollars
- Basically the same as the current market capitalization (HK$28 billion)

**Neutral valuation** (considering moderate growth):
- Assuming FCF of 2.2 billion yuan, sustainable growth of 2%, and discount rate of 8%
- Intrinsic value = 22/(8%-2%) = 36.7 billion yuan ≈ 39.5 billion Hong Kong dollars
- Approximately 40% upside potential from current market capitalization

**Pessimistic Valuation** (price war intensifies):
- Assume FCF is 1.5 billion yuan, no growth, discount rate 9%
- Intrinsic value = 15/9% = 16.7 billion yuan ≈ 18 billion Hong Kong dollars
-The current market value has dropped by about 35%

## 5. Core issues of Buffett’s framework

### 1. Does it have a lasting competitive advantage?

Partially available. It has strong brand stickiness in the sanitary napkin category (seven-dimensional space), but lacks a differentiated moat in the paper towel category. The overall competitive advantage is "sufficient but not outstanding".

### 2. Is management honest and competent?

It remains to be seen. Related party transactions and governance flaws in the past are negative points, but recent reforms and steady dividends are plus points. After the death of co-founder Xu Lianjie in 2025, the company's governance structure faces new tests.

### 3. Will this company be stronger or weaker in ten years?

Most likely it will be similar to today. The industry will not disappear and Hengan will not go bankrupt, but it is unlikely to grow significantly. This is a "forever but never big" company.

### 4. Does the current price provide a margin of safety?

**Provides a modest margin of safety**. 10-11 times PE, 6% dividend yield, 8-9% FCF Yield - for a stable consumer goods company, this price is not expensive. However, the margin of safety is not "huge" and requires maintaining confidence in corporate governance and industry competition.

## 7. Core Conclusions

Is Hengan International a subject of debate as to whether it is a classic value trap or a value depression?

**Arguments for low value**:
- 10 times PE, 6% dividend yield, 15% ROE, 8-9% FCF Yield
- Product demand is rigid and cash flow is sustainable
- Does not require large amounts of reinvestment and can continue to be returned to shareholders

**Value Trap Argument**:
- Zero growth means that you can only make money with "same valuation + dividends"
- Price war may further squeeze profits
- Governance of historical flaws reduces premium space
- Hong Kong stock liquidity discount

**Buffett's possible judgment**: This is an acceptable "interest-earning" investment. If you buy a stable consumer goods company with a ROE of 15% and a dividend rate of 6% at 10 times PE, without considering the increase in valuation, the annualized return will be approximately 6% (dividends) + 3-5% (appreciation of retained earnings) = 9-11%. Sufficient for investors seeking solid returns, but not "the greatest investment opportunity of a lifetime."
