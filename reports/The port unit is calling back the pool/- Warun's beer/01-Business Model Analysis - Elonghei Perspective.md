## 1. What kind of business is this?

### 1.1 Nature of business

Beer is essentially a consumer goods business that combines "brand + channel + scale". It has the following characteristics:

- **High-frequency low-price consumption**: The single consumption amount is low, the repurchase rate is extremely high, and consumer price sensitivity decreases as high-end products move up
- **Asset-heavy start, asset-light operation**: A large amount of production capacity investment is required in the early stage, but once the regional density advantage is formed, the marginal cost will decrease significantly.
- **Logistics Radius Limitation**: Beer has high water content and a large proportion of transportation costs, which naturally forms regional barriers (general economic transportation radius is 150-300 kilometers)
- **Enhanced brand consumption attributes**: Under the trend of high-endization, beer has shifted from "thirst-quenching beverage" to "social consumer goods", opening up space for brand premiums

China Resources Beer's core business can be summarized as follows: using China's largest beer distribution network to sell products at all price ranges from mass to high-end, and obtain incremental profits through structural upgrades. **

### 1.2 Is liquor the second curve or a strategic mistake?

China Resources spent approximately 12.3 billion yuan in 2021 to acquire liquor assets such as Jinsha Liquor Industry in an attempt to create a "beer + liquor" dual platform. From the results:

- Liquor revenue will shrink from the high point of about 3.5 billion yuan at the time of acquisition to 1.496 billion yuan in 2025
- Provision for goodwill impairment of RMB 2.877 billion in 2025
- EBITDA plummeted from RMB 807 million to RMB 264 million

**Objective evaluation**: The timing of this acquisition is just at the top of the sauce craze, and it is a typical "high-position acquisition". However, after the impairment is cleared, the liquor business is no longer a hidden danger on the balance sheet, but a low-base option - if it does well, it will be a surprise, but if it does not, the impact will be limited.

## 3. Differentiation capabilities and moat

### 3.1 Moat Assessment

**★★★★☆ Channel network barriers (strong)**

China Resources Beer has the largest beer distribution network in China:
- Sales volume market share is approximately 31.9%, ranking first
- Covering most county-level markets across the country
- Industry-leading terminal reach capabilities (restaurants, nightclubs, convenience stores)
- The logistics and service efficiency created by channel density is difficult for new entrants to copy

**★★★★☆ Advantages of economies of scale (strong)**

- The annual sales volume exceeds 11 million kiloliters, and the scale effect is fully reflected in the procurement, production and logistics links.
- The space for optimizing the capacity utilization of individual plants has been released, and plant closures are in the later stages to improve efficiency.
- The bargaining power brought by scale is especially obvious in the procurement of raw materials (barley, packaging materials)

**★★★☆☆ Brand premium ability (medium to strong)**

- Strong brand power in the mass market ("Snowflake" national recognition)
- The brand power in the high-end mark returns on Heineken, and its own brands such as "silent" are still in the development stage
- There is still a gap in brand potential with Budweiser in the ultra-high-end market

**★★☆☆☆ Switching costs (weaker)**

- Beer consumers have low switching costs, and brand loyalty depends on channel accessibility and consumption scenarios
- However, there are certain exclusivity agreements and freezer placement lock-ins on the B-side (catering stores).

### 3.2 Evolutionary direction of moat

China Resources Beer's moat is being upgraded from a "channel + scale" type to a "channel + scale + brand" type. The essence of premiumization is to superimpose brand premium on scale advantages. This process is irreversible but takes time.

## 4. Sustainability of business model

### 4.1 Positive factors

1. **The industry structure is solidified**: China’s beer CR5 reaches 92%, and it is almost impossible for new entrants to break the pattern
2. **High-end is irreversible**: Under the trend of consumption upgrading, the increase in ton price is a certain trend
3. **Capital expenditure decline period**: Plant closures and efficiency improvements are basically completed, the peak of production capacity investment has passed, and free cash flow is released
4. **Management Incentive Alignment**: China Resources Group’s assessment system focuses on profits and cash flow, rather than pure scale

### 4.2 Negative factors

1. **Total volume peaks**: China’s total beer production has dropped from 50.62 million kiloliters in 2013 to approximately 35 million kiloliters, continuing to shrink.
2. **Concerns about the high-end ceiling**: During the economic downturn, consumption downgrade may delay the pace of high-end development.
3. **Heineken dependence risk**: High-end increment is highly dependent on the Heineken brand, and there is uncertainty after the expiration of the cooperation agreement.
4. **Liquor business has not yet proven value**: After the acquisition logic is falsified, management’s "second curve" narrative needs to rebuild trust

## 5. Core judgment from Duan Yongping’s perspective

### 5.1 Is this a good business?

**★★★★☆ (4/5 stars)**

The beer industry in China has entered a steady state of competition, and China Resources, as the sales leader, has significant scale and channel advantages. High-endization brings structural profit growth, and reduced capital expenditure releases cash flow. This is a good business where "you can make money while lying down, but you can't make huge profits."

The deduction points are: the decline in total volume is a long-term trend, and the brand barriers are not as "eternal" as Moutai and Coca-Cola.

### 5.2 Doing the right thing vs. doing things right

- **Do the right things**: The direction of high-endization is correct, the main business of focusing on beer is correct, and the clearing of impairment is correct
- **Doing things right**: Channel intensive cultivation, brand building, and regional layout at the execution level are all moving in the right direction.
- **Things done wrong**: Misjudgment of the timing and price of liquor acquisitions, indicating management’s lack of discipline in cross-border expansion

### 5.3 Core Conclusions

China Resources Beer is the target with the highest certainty and the strongest high-end execution capabilities in the Chinese beer industry. It's not a "ten-fold in ten years" growth stock, but it's also not an over-hyped story stock. It is more like a cash flow machine with stable output and is continuously improving output efficiency through product structure upgrades**.

For investors seeking certainty and reasonable returns, this is a business worth holding for the long term.
