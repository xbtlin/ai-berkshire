## 1. Panoramic view of industry ecology

### 1.1 Life cycle positioning of China’s beer industry

China's beer industry has completely gone through four stages:

1. **Barbaric Growth Period (1990-2013)**: Production increased from less than 10 million kiloliters to a peak of 50.62 million kiloliters
2. **Capacity clearance period (2014-2019)**: Total output continues to decline, the industry closes factories and cuts production capacity
3. **Structural Upgrading Period (2020-2025)**: The total volume stabilizes at around 35 million kiloliters, and high-end becomes the main theme
4. **Steady-state game period (2026-)**: The pattern is solidified, and the source of growth is completely switched from "quantity" to "price" and "profit"

**Current positioning**: The industry is in the transition stage from "structural upgrade period" to "steady-state game period". The total volume is shrinking slowly (annual decrease of 1-2%), but the industry profit pool continues to expand. The total profit of China's beer industry is expected to be approximately 30 billion yuan in 2025, an increase of approximately 50% from five years ago.

### 1.2 Economic characteristics of the industry

| Features | Performance |
|------|------|
| Product homogeneity | The mid- and low-end are highly homogeneous, while the high-end is differentiated |
| Capital intensity | Medium (capacity is already overcapacity, new entrants do not need to invest heavily) |
| Regional attributes | Strong (logistics radius restrictions form regional barriers) |
| Cyclicality | Weak (rigid consumption, limited macro impact) |
| Risk of technological change | Extremely low (mature technology, low probability of disruptive innovation) |
| Regulatory risk | Low (no special policy restrictions) |

## 2. In-depth analysis of competitive landscape

### 2.1 Portraits of the Five Giants

| Enterprise | 2024 Revenue | Sales Share | Core Strengths | Strategic Direction |
|------|----------|----------|----------|----------|
| China Resources Beer | 38.6 billion | ~32% | First in channel density + scale | "China + International" dual brand high-end |
| Budweiser Asia Pacific | 45.7 billion | ~20% | High-end brand matrix | Super high-end + craft beer |
| Tsingtao Beer | 32.1 billion | ~21% | Brand inheritance + export | Premiumization of classic brands |
| Chongqing Beer | 14.7 billion | ~8% | Carlsberg System + Southwest | Regional Deepening + International Brands |
| Yanjing Beer | 14.7 billion | ~11% | Beijing base camp + reform dividend | Management reform + product upgrade |

**CR5 totals about 92%**, and the industry structure has been highly solidified.

### 2.2 Analysis of competition dimensions

**Price with competition**:
- Super high-end (20 yuan+): Dominated by Budweiser (Corona, Fujia White, Goose Island)
- High-end (12-20 yuan): Budweiser vs China Resources (Heineken) vs Qingdao (Classic 1903)
- Mid- to high-end (8-12 yuan): China Resources (SuperX, Pure Life) vs Qingdao vs Chongqing (Tuborg)
- Volkswagen (under 8 yuan): China Resources (Snowflake) vs Yanjing vs Qingdao

**Key findings**: China Resources is most competitive in the mid-to-high-end price range (8-15 yuan), which happens to be the main battlefield for consumption upgrades. In the ultra-high-end market (20 yuan+), Budweiser still has an overwhelming advantage.

**Regional Competition**:
- China Resources: Nationwide coverage, strong in Northeast/South China/Southwest
- Qingdao: Shandong base + nationalization
- Budweiser: East/Central China high-end market
- Chongqing: Southwest + Central China
- Yanjing: Beijing + Guangxi

China Resources' "no blind spots" geographical coverage is the basis of its scale advantage.

### 2.3 Competition intensity assessment

**★★★☆☆ Medium to low**

Reason:
1. The oligopoly structure is stable and all parties have a tacit understanding to avoid a comprehensive price war.
2. During the Hou Xiaohai era, he publicly called for "staying away from price wars" many times, and an industry consensus is forming.
3. Premiumization is essentially about “making the cake bigger” rather than “grabbing the cake”
4. However, there is still promotion competition in some markets, especially in the ready-to-drink channel.

## 3. Analysis of multiple thinking models

### 3.1 Supply and demand model

**Supply side**:
- National beer production capacity is still about 30-40% surplus
- However, the closure of factories has come to an end, and there is very little new production capacity.
- The supply-side reform has essentially been completed, and the remaining excess capacity is "zombie capacity"

**Demand side**:
- Demographic changes (aging + declining birth rate) have led to a shrinking of the total number of drinking people
- Per capita consumption has dropped from 36 liters/year to approximately 30 liters/year
- With the rise of health awareness, the trend of "drink less and drink better" is clear
- Consumption scene differentiation: home drinking increases, food stalls decrease

**Supply and demand conclusion**: The total volume has definitely declined, but there is still plenty of room for structural upgrades. The key variable is not "how much to sell", but "how much to sell for."

### 3.2 Game theory model

China's beer industry has entered a typical "oligopoly game" stage:

- **Balanced Cooperation**: All parties focus on high-end development, do not engage in price wars, and the industry profit pool jointly expands
- **Temptation of betrayal**: Grab share in the existing market by lowering prices
- **Punishment Mechanism**: Any party launching a price war will trigger full retaliation

Current observation: The industry is in a "fragile cooperative equilibrium." China Resources and Qingdao both emphasize "no price war", and Budweiser maintains its price strategy at a high level. However, Yanjing's radical reforms and competition and friction in local regions may undermine the balance.

**Risk Points**: If the macroeconomic downturn continues and consumption downgrades lead to a slowdown in high-endization, companies may be forced to return to the old model of "price for volume".

### 3.3 Psychological model – consumer behavior

**The Psychology of Beer Consumption**:
- Socially driven: 70%+ of beer consumption occurs in social situations
- Bandwagon effect: consumers tend to choose "safe choices for social situations"
- Anchoring effect of brand upgrade: Once consumers choose a high-end brand in social situations, it is difficult to downgrade (face psychology)

This explains why premiumization is a "ratchet effect" - when it goes up, it is difficult to come down. Even if the economy goes down, consumers may reduce the frequency of consumption, but they will not easily lower their consumption level.

### 3.4 Evolutionary Model—Industry Adaptability

China's beer industry is experiencing an "adaptive radiation" after a "mass extinction":

- Low-end homogeneous products ("dinosaurs") are dying
- Differentiated and high-end products ("mammals") are occupying the ecological niche
- Craft beers ("small mammals") occupy niche market but struggle to scale

China Resources' evolutionary strategy is to "learn differentiation from large enterprises" - while maintaining its scale advantage, it will occupy new ecological niches through brands such as Heineken/Li/SuperX.

## 4. Reverse thinking: Under what circumstances will investment fail?

### 4.1 Scenario of peaking of high-endization

If China's economy is in a long-term downturn (similar to Japan's "lost 30 years"), consumption downgrade will become mainstream:
- Ton prices may stagnate or even fall back
- High-end product sales growth slowed to low single digits
- The industry switches from "high-end logic" back to "share logic", and the risk of price war increases

**Probability Assessment**: Moderately Low. China's per capita GDP is still growing, and the penetration rate of high-end beer (sub-high-end and above accounts for about 25%) is far lower than Japan (50%+) and the United States (60%+), and structural space is still there.

### 4.2 Scenario of worsening competitive landscape

- Budweiser launches aggressive price cuts to regain market share in China
- Yanjing launched a challenge to China Resources in the northern market after the successful reform
- The disruptive rise of craft beer cannibalizes the high-end market

**Probability Assessment**: Low. Budweiser's global strategy is "high-end" rather than "low-price". It is difficult to scale up craft beer, and the size of Yanjing is huge.

### 4.3 Management Execution Risk

- The ability of the new management (Zhao Chunwu) has not been verified
- The liquor business continues to consume management energy and resources
- Renewal conditions worsened after the Heineken cooperation expired

**Probability Assessment**: Moderate. Management change is the biggest uncertainty and requires an observation period of 1-2 years.

## 5. Core judgment from Munger’s perspective

### 5.1 "Finding a good industry is more important than finding a good company"

Is China's beer industry a "good industry"?

**Front**:
- A highly concentrated oligopoly structure with orderly competition
- Product demand is stable and not subject to technological disruption
- High-endization brings expansion of profit pool
- Capital expenditures fall, free cash flow improves

**Backside**:
- Total shrinkage is a long-term trend
- Predictable growth ceiling
- Not a "forever growing" industry

**Conclusion**: Beer is not a "great industry", but it is a "good industry" - stable, predictable, and profitable. When valuations are reasonable, good companies in good industries are worth holding.

### 5.2 "Think upside down"

**If you want to short China Resources Beer, what is the best logic? **

1. Population collapse leads to accelerated shrinkage of beer consumption
2. Long-term economic recession leads to reversal of high-endization
3. Management continues to make strategic mistakes (such as another large-scale acquisition)
4. Heineken takes back operating rights in China

Among these risks, (1) and (2) are systemic risks and cannot be dispersed; (3) require continuous monitoring; (4) have extremely low probability but high impact.

### 5.3 "Circle of Competence" Judgment

Is the beer industry within investors’ circle of competence?

**Pros**:
- The business model is simple and easy to understand
- Financial data is transparent and there are no complicated accounting treatments (except for liquor impairment)
- The competitive landscape is clear and the number of participants is small
- Can be verified through daily consumption

**Conclusion**: Beer is an "easy to understand" industry, suitable for long-term tracking and research.
