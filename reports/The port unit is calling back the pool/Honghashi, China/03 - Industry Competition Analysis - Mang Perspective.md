# I. The properties of the electrolytic aluminium industry

##1.1 Industry attribute positioning

Electrolysis aluminium is a heavy industry between resource extraction and basic materials. Understanding this industry requires three essential features:

** First, very high energy density.** Electricity costs account for about 34% of the production costs of electrolytic aluminium, making energy prices and energy policies core variables that influence the industry landscape. Whoever has access to affordable and stable electricity, he or she has a dominant position on the cost curve.

** Second, high homogenization of products.** Electrolysis aluminium is a standardized bulk commodity, with downstream customers having no preference for brands and industry competition having only one core dimension — cost.

** Third, the very high capital threshold.** Investment in a 250,000-ton electrolytic aluminium production line is billions of yuan, with a construction cycle of 2-3 years.

##1.2 Global electrolytic aluminium industry pattern

Global electrolytic aluminium production in 2025 was about 70 million tons, with China accounting for about 60 per cent (approximately 42 million tons). The remaining major producers include India, Russia, Canada, the United Arab Emirates, Norway, etc.

The world ' s leading producers are ranked as:
1. **Hongbridge, China**: approximately 6545 million tonnes (the largest private aluminium company in the world)
2. **Aluminium industry in China** (including Aluminium shares): approximately 7 million tonnes + (the largest global but state enterprise system)
3. **Team: approximately 5 million tonnes + (unlisted, Shandong Man-GmbH)
4. **Rosal**: approximately 3.8 million tons
5. **Rio Tinto**: approximately 3.2 million tons
**Alcoa**: approximately 2.2 million tons

Although not the world ' s largest production, Honghashi has long been the top global aluminum commodity in profitability, thanks to its integrated layout and low-cost advantages.

# Two, Chinese electrolytic aluminium "supply side revolution"

#2.1 Energy ceiling policy: turning the corner of the industry

In 2017, the country introduced a cap on the capacity of electrolytic aluminium, which locks the total capacity of the country at about 45 million tons. This is a policy arrangement that has far-reaching implications for the industry.

As of April 2025, the domestic electrolytic aluminium production capacity was approximately 44.1 million tons, with a capacity utilization rate of 97.55%.
- Equivalent or reduced replacement of old capacity
- Aluminium fluid exchange electricity consumption not exceeding 13,000 kWh/t
- Environmental performance is A-level

** This means that China’s electrolytic aluminium industry has shifted from “uptake competition” to “stock competition.”** In this pattern, existing capacity holders – especially those with large capacity – have gained similar value as “licensings”.

#2: Comparison with other resources industry

The energy ceiling policy makes the supply end characteristics of the electrolysis aluminium industry more similar to "scarce resources" (e.g. coal, iron ore), rather than "manufacturing" (e.g. steel, chemicals).

- ** Steel industry**: Despite the degenerative energy policy, total capacity remains above 1 billion tons, supply elastic and industry is often caught in price wars
- ** Coal industry**: also productively regulated, but new mining development cycles are long, environmentally restrictive and supply rigids are similar to electrolytic aluminium
- ** Electrolytic aluminium industry**: clear energy ceiling, near 100% capacity utilization, most rigid supply

##2.3 Regional transfer trends

Policies require that priority areas for air pollution control (Kyojin, Long Triangle, Pearl Triangle) no longer add new electrolytic aluminium production and encourage a shift to clean energy-rich areas. Yunnan (hydropower), Sichuan (hydropower), and Inner Mongolia (wind power/photovolt) are the main destinations for energy migration.

China ' s Honghashi actively participated in this trend by building hydroelectric aluminium production capacity in Yunnan, while building a photovoltaic power station at Shandong base (two GWs have been built and four GWs are under construction) to promote green transformation of the energy structure.

# 3.2 Stability of competition patterns

The current pattern of competition is highly stable because:

** Supply end lockdown**: Under the energy-producing ceiling policy, the market share of existing firms is largely solid and no new large competitors are emerging. Productive capacity can be transferred only through merger restructuring, not by new ones.

** Cost differentials solid**: The cost structure of enterprises depends on long-term factors such as power sources (self-contained/hydro/offer), aluminum self-sufficiency, and scale effects, which are difficult to change in the short term. The cost advantage of China’s macro-bridge is structural.

** Industry does not have an inner volume**: because of the near-calculus capacity and the growing demand, there is no risk of a vicious circle of "excessive capacity and price-wart profit-0". This contrasts sharply with industries that compete too much, such as steel and light.

#3.3 The competitive position of the Chinese Lung Bridge

On the cost curve, the Chinese Hong Bridge is located in the lowest cost range:
- 2024, about 13232 tons of electrolytic aluminium.
- Average industry cost of approximately $15,000-16,000 per ton
- High-cost energy (mainly electricity and aluminium oxide outsourcing) can cost more than $18,000 per tonne

This means that Honghashi has the largest "sustainable buffer" in the lower aluminium price cycle. When aluminium prices fall to 15,000 yen per tonne, a large amount of high-cost production capacity has already been lost, while Honghashi still has about 1800 yuan per tonne of profit space.

# IV. Structural changes at the demand end

# 4.1 Traditional demand: property down, but bottom

The traditional use of aluminium in construction (doors and windows, curtain walls, decorations) is being dragged down by property, with a projected reduction of 600,000 to 800,000 tons in 2025. However, the proportion of aluminium used in real estate as a proportion of total demand has continued to decline, affecting the marginal decline.

## 4.2 Emerging demand: three main incremental engines

**New energy vehicles:** A bicycle with a volume of more than 200 kg, far higher than the traditional car of about 130 kg. China’s new energy vehicle sales are expected to exceed 12 million in 2025 and will contribute to the increase in demand for a million tons of aluminium by 2026. Aluminium is not an alternative in battery casings, body structures, chassis, heat management systems.

** Photovolt and storage:** Aluminum border for photovoltaic assemblies, with a large number of aluminiums used in the frame. Global storage core production in 2025 was about 540 GWh (+48.8% compared to +48.8% compared to +4.8% compared to 730 GWh projected for 2026. A total of about 300,000 tons of aluminium in 2025 and a 105,000 increase in 2026 in the field of storage energy. Morgan Stanley expects an additional 1.44 million tons of aluminium in storage capacity to be needed.

**AI and Power Infrastructure:** The core conductor material for the supervoltage transmission line is aluminium (copper too heavy to fit long distances for high-altitude installation). The AI data centre construction has contributed to a surge in global electricity demand, with the data centre using a power supply of up to 6 per cent compared to the expected level, and indirectly pulls aluminium into the power facility.

#4.3 Balance of supply and demand outlook

The gap between supply and demand for electrolytic aluminium in China is projected to be as follows for 2025-2027:
- 2025: approximately 1,82 million tons
- 2026: approximately 2.130,000 tons
- 2027: approximately 2.86 million tons

The gap will be covered by the import of the original and the regenerative aluminium components, but the supply-demand balance will continue to support aluminium prices.

# V. Industrial chain games and risks

# 5.1 Upstream: Aluminium Oxide's Double-edged Sword

The price of aluminium oxides in 2024-2025 experienced significant fluctuations (from 3,000 to 6,000 tons, after falling). The impact on Hong Kong has been twofold:

- **positive**: Aluminium oxide self-sufficiency rate over 160%, with a significant increase in profits from the sale of aluminium oxide when prices increase
- ** Negative**: Aluminium oxide prices fell, although the internal cost of aluminium was lower, but the profit from the sale of aluminium oxide was reduced

The fall in performance in H2 in 2025 was due to a high return in aluminium oxide prices. In the long run, high levels of self-sufficiency of aluminium oxide are a central advantage, but short-term fluctuations can lead to a uneven profit pace.

##5.2 Downstream: Extension of the aluminium processing industry chain

The Chinese Hoonbridge is currently based on liquid aluminium alloys and aluminum ingots, and aluminium processing (board-banded slabs, materials, etc.) is limited. A future extension to high-value-added aluminium is likely to increase the profits and resistance of tons of aluminium.

#5.3 Trade policy risks

Aluminium is an important commodity in global trade, and changes in tariffs and trade barriers may affect global aluminium prices and trade flows. China is now a net importer of aluminium (the original aluminium dimension), and the tight balance between domestic supply and demand makes international aluminium prices more direct to the domestic market.

#5.4 Long-term impacts of carbon costs

The EU Carbon Border Control Mechanism (CBAM) officially introduced a carbon tariff in 2026. Although China exports a small amount of electrolytic aluminium, the global trend in carbon costs will be:
- The cost of pushing up the high carbon emitting aluminium business is not favourable to the Hong Kong base in Hong Kong.
- Promotion of alumina enterprises (e.g., shares in alumina) to promote further industrial transformation towards green energy
- The green energy layout of Hong Kong in Yunnan is a strategic response to this trend

# Six, long-term evolution of industry

# 6.1 Supply: From "domestic" to "globalization"

As domestic production is close to the ceiling, the global increase in the supply of electrolytic aluminium will be mainly due to:
- Middle East (UA, Bahrain, Saudi Arabia): low-cost natural gas for electricity generation
- India: coal power + demographic dividend
- Indonesia: nickel aluminium composite industrial chain
- Africa (Guinea, Ghana): Hydropower rich

China ' s Hong Kong-Bing has been involved in the competition for globalization ahead of schedule through the overseas deployment of Guinea and Indonesia.

# 6.2 Demand end: Aluminium is "the new age of steel."

Aluminium is becoming one of the most important building blocks of the new energy age. Light quantification, conductivity, and recyclability make aluminium irreplaceable in the areas of automobiles, photovoltaics, storage, power transmission, etc. In this sense, investing in electrolytic aluminum taps is an investment in the upstream infrastructure of the new energy chain.

#6.3 Trends in industrial integration

As a leader, China’s Honghashi is either a unitizer or a target for integration. It is a matter of concern that, in February 2026, China’s Honghashi completed a major asset restructuring, which indicates that industrial integration is accelerating.
