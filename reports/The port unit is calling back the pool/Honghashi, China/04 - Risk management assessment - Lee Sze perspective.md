II. Corporate governance risks

##2.1 Associated transactions <unk> (risk level: medium-high)

This is one of the most market-focused governance issues in Hong Kong.

** Scale of associated transactions:**
As of March 2025, long-term liabilities to related parties (China ' s Hoonbridge Group, the Long Bridge Lease) amounted to approximately 32.53 billion yuan, while other related parties were due to approximately $9.112 billion. The amount was significant, which is significantly higher than the company ' s annual net profit (22.6 billion yuan).

** Main types of associated transactions:**
- Procurement of raw materials (bauxite, coal, etc.) from related parties
- Arrangements for the rental of power-owned assets
- Assets and business transactions with Wei Bridge Enterprise

**Risk evaluation:**
Related transactions are not necessarily negative per se – upstream and downstream transactions within integrated enterprise groups have their commercial reasonableness. But the key issue is ** fair pricing and transparency of information. ** External investors have difficulty in accurately judging whether these transactions are actually conducted at fair market prices.

Historically, the question of the link deal was questioned by the empty institutions (Emerson Analytics and others) and, despite the company's response, the issue was always the "grizzian" in the investor's mind.

# III. List of core risks

#3.1 Aluminium price cyclical risk

The average price of aluminium is about $20,600 per ton in 2025, and management expects to reach 23,000 in 2026. But aluminium prices are influenced by multiple factors such as global macroeconomics, the dollar exchange rate, and China’s capital investment, which have fluctuated significantly over 30% on several occasions in history.

** Scenario analysis:**
- Optimism scenario (aluminum at $25,000 per ton): net profit can exceed $40 billion
- Baseline scenario (aluminum price 23,000 yuan): net profit of approximately 34.8 billion yuan
- Pessimistic scenario (aluminum at $18,000 per tonne): net profit may fall back to $15-18 billion

The low-cost advantage of the bridge makes it more resilient than its peers in the down-cycle, but it does not allow for a volatile immune cycle.

#3.2 Policy risk <unk> (risk level: medium-high)

**The risk of self-supporting electricity policy:** The company ' s self-supporting rate of about 50%, with the cost of self-supporting electricity significantly lower than that of outsourcing. If the state adjusts its own power policy (upturning cross-subsidization and promoting integration into a unified grid), it will directly increase the cost of electricity for the company. This risk has been discussed over the past few years and has not been substantially settled, but the likelihood is not zero in the long run.

** Energy ceiling policy risks:** The current cap policy is extremely favourable for macrobridge. If future policy liberalization (though short-term probability is extremely low), it will change the supply and demand patterns of the industry and reduce the productivity scarcity premium for macrobridge.

** Carbon emission policy risk:** Shandong base is dominated by fire power and faces carbon cost-increasing pressures in carbon neutral and large trends. Companies are moving forward with photovoltaic projects and Yunnan Green Power transition, but completing the full transformation will take time and capital.

#3.3 Environmental protection and ESG risk <unk> (risk level: medium)

The electrolytic aluminium is a high energy-consuming and high carbon-emitting industry. The production base of Honghashi in Shandong, China, is largely dependent on fire power, with high carbon emission intensity.

** Response measures taken:**
- Hydro-aluminum production in Yunnan
- Construction of a photovoltaic station at Shandong Base (two GWs in progress and four GWs in progress)
- Zhang Bo has been Chairman of the Commission on Sustainable Development since 2025

** Unfinished issues:**
- Green transformation takes a lot of time and investment
- There is still room for the ESG rating to be raised from the global investor perspective
- If the EU CBAM imposes high carbon tariffs on aluminium products, the export competitiveness of companies (although currently not significant) may be affected

#3.4 Geopolitical risks

**The political risks of Guinea:** The company has large-scale bauxite mining operations and the interests of the West Mandou mine in Guinea. Although Guinea's political situation, which was the subject of a military coup in 2021, has not been significantly affected by its operations, the political stability of African countries remains uncertain.

** Trade friction between China and the United States:** If trade frictions rise, this could affect global trade flows and prices for aluminium products. China, as a net importer of aluminium, has a relatively limited direct impact, but indirect effects (declining global demand) cannot be ignored.

#3.5 Risk of volatility of aluminium oxide prices

Aluminium oxide prices in 2024-2025 have soared from $3,000/t to $6,000/t, causing delays in company H2 performance. While high levels of self-sufficiency of aluminium oxide are a long-term advantage, sharp price fluctuations can lead to large fluctuations in profits in a single quarter or a half year, affecting market confidence.

#3.6 Succession and succession risk

Zhang Bo is currently 55 years old and is in his prime. But as a family business, the third generation of transmission is a long-term risk. There is no public information showing a clear plan for a third generation.

- Low percentage of independent directors
- The chairman and CEO are not separated.
- The scale and transparency of associated transactions need to be improved
- ESG information disclosure depth and third-party auditing could be enhanced

But the level of governance in Hong Kong is not bad — at least it has been consistently redred, complied with, and no major irregularities were recorded — compared to the average for China's national companies.

# Core conclusion

The investment logic of Hong Kong's Honghashi is "buy assets" instead of "buy management" – you buy the lowest-cost electric aluminium production in the world, the scarcity of policy locks, and the high-quality mineral resources overseas. Management (Shang Bo) is doing well, but family governance structures, associated transactions, and transparency of information are constant sub-elements.

But for investors who can accept a "governance discount" and value the asset and industry patterns, these risks have been partially priced in the lower PE valuation (12-13 times as much as ROE 22%). The key question is whether this "governance discount" is sufficient and whether there is room for underestimation.
