## 2. Risk Assessment

### Risk 1: Interest rate loss risk ★★★★☆

**Specific performance**:
- There are a large number of 3.5% + predetermined interest rate products in existing life insurance policies
- If the investment return rate is lower than 3.5% for a long time, "interest spread loss" will occur
- This was the core risk of the "lost decade" of Japan's insurance industry in the 1990s

**Extent of impact**:
- There will not be a one-time outbreak, but profits will be eroded year by year.
- The predetermined interest rate for new orders has been lowered to 2.5%, but the existing balance will naturally mature in 15-20 years.
- It is a "chronic disease" rather than an "acute disease"

**Release factors**:
- On the investment side, the "dividend + Hong Kong stocks" strategy is increasing investment yields
- The reduction in new order booking interest rates has eased incremental pressure
- The dead spread/fee difference can partially compensate for the lack of spread

### Risk 2: Equity market fluctuations ★★★★☆

**Specific performance**:
- Of the net profit of 53.5 billion in 2025, approximately 17 billion will come from "excess returns" on the investment side.
- If the capital market weakens, net profit may fluctuate significantly
- Operating profit of 36.5 billion is a "reliable" profit base

**Scale of impact**: Net profit may fluctuate between 40 billion and 70 billion

**Release factors**:
- Management has adjusted its investment strategy (increased allocation of dividend stocks and bonds)
- The impact of investment fluctuations on P&L is decreasing under IFRS9/17
- Investors should base their valuation on operating profit rather than net profit

### Risk Three: Agent Transformation Risk ★★★☆☆

**Specific performance**:
-The total number of agents is still declining
- Although per capita production capacity has increased, if the rate of decline exceeds the rate of increase in production capacity
- NBV growth may hit a bottleneck at some point

**Extent of impact**: There may be a slowdown in NBV growth in the medium term (3-5 years)

**Release factors**:
- The current effect of "supplementing quantity with quality" is significant (NBV +40%)
- Bancassurance channels and Internet channels are being supplemented
- Increase in average annual salary per agent will help retention

### Risk 4: Risks of implementing the health care strategy ★★★☆☆

**Specific performance**:
- Senior care communities require large investments and long return cycles
- Capacity building in healthcare services takes time
- Synergies with the main business (insurance underwriting) need to be verified

**Extent of impact**: If you burn money but don’t see returns, it will drag down the overall ROE.

### Risk five: regulatory and policy risks ★★★☆☆

**Specific performance**:
- Predetermined interest rate adjustment policy affects product competitiveness
- New solvency regulations (C-ROSS Phase 2) may increase capital requirements
- Internet insurance regulation may affect channel layout

**Influence**: Controllable, will not fundamentally change the business model

## 3. Institutional environment analysis (Li Lu framework)

### Rule of law and property rights protection
- Insurance industry supervision (State Administration of Financial Supervision) is highly professional
- The governance of listed companies is subject to dual supervision by the Securities and Futures Commission and the Hong Kong Stock Exchange
- **Evaluation**: ★★★★☆

### Capital market openness
- A+H dual listing, sufficient liquidity
- Southbound Hong Kong Stock Connect enables mainland investors to participate in H-shares
- **Evaluation**: ★★★★★

### Industry regulatory clarity
- Improved insurance regulatory framework (solvency + actuarial + product approval)
- Policy direction generally supports the development of the insurance industry
- Supervision’s guidance on protection products is consistent with CPIC’s transformation direction
- **Evaluation**: ★★★★☆

### Industry Policy Environment
- The state encourages the development of commercial pension insurance (personal pensions, etc.)
- The positioning of commercial insurance as a supplement in the reform of the medical security system is clear
- **Evaluation**: ★★★★★ Highly supportive policy

## 4. Investment risk and return assessment

### Worst case scenario (5% probability)
- Concentrated interest rate losses + sharp decline in equity market + massive loss of agents
- PEV dropped to 0.3-0.4x
- Downside: -20-30%

### Pessimistic scenario (20% probability)
- NBV growth slows to 5-10% + investment income is flat
- PEV maintained at 0.5 times
- Annualized return: 5-8% (including dividends)

### Baseline scenario (50% probability)
- NBV maintains growth rate of 15-20% + investment side is stable + COR continues to improve
- PEV fixed to 0.7-0.8 times
- Annualized total return: 15-25%

### Optimistic scenario (25% probability)
- The health care strategy is effective + interest rates stabilize + insurance stock valuations are restored
- PEV fixed to 1.0x
- Annualized total return: 30-50%
