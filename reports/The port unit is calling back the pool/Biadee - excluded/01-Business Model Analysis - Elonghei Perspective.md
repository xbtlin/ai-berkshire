# BYD (1211.HK) Business Model Analysis

> Analysis dimensions: business essence, user value, differentiated competitiveness, long-term sustainability
> Data cutoff: May 2026

---

## 1. The essence of business model

### What kind of business is this?

The essence of BYD's business is to be a vertically integrated platform in the manufacturing industry. Unlike traditional car companies that source core components from outside, BYD has built an almost complete internal industrial chain from batteries, motors, electronic controls, power semiconductors to vehicle manufacturing.

A UBS report dismantling the BYD Seal shows that about 75% of the model's parts are produced by BYD itself, 17% come from Chinese suppliers, and only 8% come from overseas. This ratio is extremely rare among global car companies.

**Core Business Logic**: By internalizing high cost-proportion links (battery accounts for 30-40% of the cost of the entire vehicle, and power semiconductors account for nearly 50% of the cost of the electric drive system), we can achieve:
1. Initiative in cost control
2. Synergy effects of technology iteration
3. Independent and controllable supply chain security

### Revenue structure (2025)

- Automobiles and related products: accounting for approximately 75% of revenue (including new energy vehicles + fuel vehicles + external parts supply)
- Mobile phone components and assembly: accounting for approximately 20% of revenue (including consumer electronics OEM)
- Battery and energy storage: accounting for about 5% of revenue (Fudi battery external supply + energy storage business)

## 2. User value and demand satisfaction

### What needs does BYD satisfy?

BYD's core user value proposition is "technological equality" - providing technology originally belonging to high-end models to ordinary consumers at popular prices.

**Specific performance**:
- The safety of blade batteries (no fire in acupuncture test) eliminates consumers’ anxiety about spontaneous combustion of electric vehicles
- DM-i super hybrid system achieves real fuel consumption of 3.8L/100km and solves mileage anxiety
- Goal for 2026: High-speed NOA is standard on models below 100,000 yuan, urban NOA is standard on models above 100,000 yuan, to achieve "equal rights in smart driving"
- Second-generation blade battery + megawatt flash charging, "recharge 400km in 5 minutes"

### Do users really like this product?

Judging from sales data, vehicle sales in 2025 will be 4.6024 million units, of which 2.26 million pure electric vehicles will surpass Tesla. But it needs to be viewed objectively:

**Positive Signs**:
- Sales volume has increased for many consecutive years, ranking first in global new energy vehicle sales in 2025
- The overseas market has grown from scratch to 1 million+ units, indicating that the product strength has been internationally recognized
- The Dynasty series (Han and Tang) have a brand premium in the price range of 200,000-300,000

**Signs worth alerting**:
- Price war is an important driving force for sales growth, and net profit in 2025 will be -19% year-on-year
- Low price and high volume strategy (Seaou, Qin PLUS, etc.) lowered the brand tone
- The high-end attempts of the Look Up series have not yet achieved scale effects.

## 3. Analysis of differentiated competitiveness

### Is vertical integration a moat or a burden?

**As a moat side** (★★★★☆):

1. **Cost Advantage**: Vertical integration gives BYD the "last line of defense" in the price war. When competitors' profits fall to zero, BYD can still maintain positive profits through transfer pricing of internal parts. The gross profit margin of automobiles in 2025 will be 20.49%, significantly higher than most independent brands.

2. **Technical collaboration**: The collaborative optimization of battery, electric drive, and electronic control enables the vehicle to continue to lead in energy consumption and performance. The DM5.0 hybrid system's fuel consumption of 2.9L/100km requires deep collaboration of the battery, motor, and electronic control to achieve it.

3. **Iteration Speed**: Without relying on the schedule of external suppliers, the cycle from R&D to mass production of new technologies is shorter. R&D investment of 63.4 billion yuan in 2025 and a R&D team of 120,000 people will support rapid iteration.

**As a Burden Side** (Risks Worth Watching):

1. **Capital Intensive**: Maintaining the entire industry chain requires continuous heavy asset investment, and there is great pressure on depreciation of fixed assets.
2. **Efficiency issue**: When the technical route of a certain link is misjudged, the adjustment cost is much higher than that of the outsourcing model.
3. **Scale dependence**: The cost advantage of vertical integration is based on huge production and sales. Once sales decline, fixed cost sharing pressure increases sharply.

Duan Yongping’s preferred business model usually has: light assets, high gross profit, strong brand premium, simple and understandable. BYD's performance in these dimensions is as follows:

## 4. Long-term sustainability assessment

### "Do the right thing" - direction judgment

BYD made the right strategic choices at several key nodes:
- Entered the automotive industry in 2003, 20 years before the industry transformation
- Adhering to the lithium iron phosphate route in 2008, which later proved to be safe and cost-effective
- Launching blade batteries in 2020 to redefine power battery safety standards
- 2021 DM-i hybrid system will open up the mass market of 100,000-200,000 yuan
- Fully invest in intelligence in 2024 to make up for the shortcomings of smart driving

### "Doing things right" - execution ability

- R&D investment of RMB 63.4 billion per year, nearly twice as much as net profit, reflecting long-termism
- From batteries to complete vehicles to intelligence, every technological leap can be quickly implemented in mass production
- Establishing factories overseas (Hungary, Türkiye, Brazil, Thailand), with strong global execution capabilities

### Long-term concerns

1. **The ceiling of profit margin**: In the history of the automobile manufacturing industry, few companies have been able to maintain gross profit margins above 20% for a long time, and Toyota was only about 25% at its peak. Are BYD's current profit margins sustainable?

2. **Technology route risk**: If disruptive technologies such as solid-state batteries and hydrogen energy emerge, will BYD's heavy position in lithium iron phosphate become a burden?

3. **Organizational complexity**: 600,000+ employees, spanning multiple automotive/electronics/battery/semiconductor industries. Is the management radius too large?

## 6. Key conclusions

**BYD is a "good but not perfect" business. **

Good things: Vertical integration has created unique cost and technical advantages in the era of new energy vehicles, and the moat is deepening rather than weakening. Globalization has opened up a second growth curve.

Imperfect aspects: This is still essentially an asset-heavy manufacturing industry that relies on scale, continuous capital investment, and profit margins are suppressed by industry competition. It does not have the characteristics of "making money while lying down" in the consumer goods business.

**Core question**: What investors need to judge is - is BYD's vertical integration advantage enough to allow it to "stand last" in a long-term price war and enjoy a recovery in profit margins after competitors withdraw? If the answer is yes, the current profit trough is exactly the time to make plans.
