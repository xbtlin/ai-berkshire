## 1. Industry Nature and Competition Structure

### What stage is the new energy vehicle industry at?

Analysis using the industry life cycle framework:

- **Introduction period** (2015-2020): Penetration rate 0-5%, policy-driven, many players
- **Growth Period** (2020-2025): Penetration rate 5-40% (China), demand explodes, and winners emerge
- **Current Location** (2025-2026): China's penetration rate has exceeded 50% and has entered the **integration stage before maturity**
- **Future** (2027+): Domestic storage competition is the main competition, and the increase mainly comes from overseas

**Core Judgment**: China's new energy vehicle market is shifting from "making the pie bigger" to "grabbing share". This means that the overall profit margin of the industry will systematically move downwards, and only the players with the lowest costs and the largest scale can obtain reasonable profits.

### Industry economic characteristics

| Characteristics | Performance | Impact on BYD |
|------|------|---------------|
| Capital density | Extremely high (factory + R&D) | Benefiting players who have established scale |
| Technology iteration speed | Fast (one platform generation every 2-3 years) | Requires sustained high-intensity R&D investment |
| Economies of scale | Significant (only one million vehicles can be profitable) | BYD’s scale advantage of 4.6 million vehicles is obvious |
| Brand premium | Medium (not as good as luxury goods but exists) | BYD brand is strong in the mid- to low-end and weak in the high-end |
| Exit barriers | High (heavy assets are difficult to transform) | Industry "zombie companies" will drag down overall profit margins |

## 2. In-depth analysis of competitive landscape

### Global Competitor Matrix

| Competitors | 2025 Sales | Core Strengths | Threats to BYD |
|----------|---------|---------|---------------|
| Tesla | 1.64 million units (pure electric) | FSD autonomous driving, brand premium | Dimensionality reduction in the intelligent dimension |
| Toyota | 10 million vehicles (total) | Hybrid technology, global channels | Channel barriers in overseas markets |
| Volkswagen Group | 5 million vehicles + | European home stadium, brand matrix | Local protection in the European market |
| Geely | 2.7 million vehicles | Multi-brand strategy, Volvo technology | Domestic mid- to high-end market competition |
| Xiaomi Auto | ~200,000 vehicles (new entry) | Ecological chain, Internet genes | Intelligence + user experience dimension |

### Game theory analysis of domestic price war

Use the **Prisoner's Dilemma** model to understand the current price war:

- Participants: BYD, Geely, Changan, Chery, Xpeng, Weilai, etc.
- Equilibrium state: everyone reduces prices and everyone’s profits decrease
- BYD's strategy: As the lowest-cost player, it proactively initiates a price war with the goal of "eliminating weak competitors"

**Manger asked**: Can this strategy be successful?

Historical analogy – China’s home appliance industry in the 2000s:
- Gree and Midea eliminated a large number of weak brands through price wars
- Industry concentration increased from approximately 30% in CR3 to 60%+
- Winners ultimately enjoy margin recovery

**But the automotive industry is different**:
- Higher exit barriers (local governments protect "zombie car companies")
- New entrants continue to emerge (Xiaomi, Huawei ecological chain)
- Diversified technical routes (pure electric, hybrid, and extended range each have their own markets)

**Conclusion**: The price war may last 2-3 years and eliminate some weak players, but it is unlikely that there will be a "complete victory" like the home appliance industry. BYD is more likely to gain 35-40% of the domestic market share in the competition, but the industry's overall profit margin will be difficult to recover significantly.

## 3. Depth and width of moat

### Five forces model analysis

**1. Competitive intensity of existing competitors (extremely high)★☆☆☆☆**
- There are 200+ new energy brands in China, with the top 10 accounting for 80% share
- The intensity of price wars is rare in history, and gross profit margins have systematically declined.
- The dimension of competition has shifted from "whether there are electric vehicles" to "intelligent experience"

**2. Threat of new entrants (medium-high) ★★☆☆☆**
- Xiaomi, Huawei and other technology companies cross-border entry
-Although Apple has given up building cars, other technology giants are eyeing it
- The threshold for car manufacturing is lowered due to the OEM model

**3. Threat of substitutes (low) ★★★★☆**
- Electrification is a definite trend, and fuel vehicles will be replaced
- Hydrogen energy cannot be replaced in the short term
-Solid-state batteries are an upgrade within the technical route, not a replacement

**4. Bargaining power of suppliers (low, compared to BYD) ★★★★★**
- Vertical integration makes BYD almost independent of external core suppliers
- Prices of upstream resource products such as lithium ore have fallen sharply
- This is one of BYD’s strongest structural advantages

**5. Customer bargaining power (medium)★★★☆☆**
- C-end consumers are highly price sensitive
- Major B-side customers (taxi, online ride-hailing) have certain bargaining rights
- Brand power is not enough to support continued price increases

### Moat types and evaluation

| Moat type | BYD performance | Rating |
|-----------|-----------|------|
| Cost advantage | Vertical integration + scale effect, lowest cost in the industry | ★★★★★ |
| Switching costs | Switching costs in the automotive industry are naturally low | ★★☆☆☆ |
| Network effect | Charging network + smart ecology is still under construction | ★★☆☆☆ |
| Brand premium | Strong in low-end and weak in high-end | ★★★☆☆ |

## 4. Application of multiple thinking models

### Niche thinking: What position does BYD occupy?

BYD's position in the new energy vehicle ecosystem is "the technology leader in the mass price segment" -- similar to Toyota's positioning in the era of fuel vehicles.

The advantage of this position: the largest market capacity (the price range of 100,000-250,000 accounts for 60%+ of China’s auto market)
Disadvantages of this position: limited profit margins, difficulty in establishing real brand premium

### Evolutionary thinking: Who is the ultimate "fittest"?

In the evolution of the automobile industry, the core capabilities that determine "survival of the fittest" are changing:
- The past 10 years: electrification capabilities (battery, electric drive) → BYD’s home field
- The next 10 years: Intelligent capabilities (autonomous driving, AI cockpit) → BYD is catching up

**Key Risk**: If "intelligence" becomes the first factor for consumers to choose a car (similar to the smartphone era), then Tesla and Huawei, which are stronger in AI/software, may gain structural advantages.

### Reverse thinking: Under what circumstances will BYD fail?

1. **Intelligent driving is completely lagging behind**: If Tesla FSD or Huawei ADS reaches the L4 level, but BYD is still stuck at L2+, consumers may collectively switch to
2. **Fragmentation of the global trade system**: If Europe and the United States completely block Chinese car companies (100% tariff), the logic of overseas growth will break
3. **Solid-state battery disruption**: If Toyota or other players take the lead in mass-producing solid-state batteries, BYD’s investment in lithium iron phosphate will become a sunk cost
4. **The price war cannot end**: If the industry continues for 5 years + price war (local protection leads to weak players not exiting), everyone’s profit margin will be depressed for a long time

## 5. Structural opportunities and risks in overseas markets

### Opportunity

- Overseas sales will exceed 1 million vehicles in 2025 and target 1.3 million vehicles in 2026
- Electrification in Southeast Asia, the Middle East, and Latin America has just started, similar to China in 2018
- BYD's price/performance ratio is extremely attractive in emerging markets
- Localized production is gradually implemented to reduce the impact of tariffs

### Risk

- The EU imposes an additional 17.4% tariff (superimposed on 10% basic tariff), compressing profit margins
- Uncertainty over the Trump administration’s imposition of additional tariffs on Chinese goods
- Countries may follow the EU's lead in introducing localization ratio requirements
- The construction of overseas channels requires a large amount of initial investment, which will drag down profits in the short term.

### Mang format judgment

Overseas markets are the core of BYD's "second growth curve". If globalization succeeds (the probability is about 60%), BYD may be upgraded from "Chinese leader" to "global leader", corresponding to a revaluation. If globalization is blocked (the probability is about 40%), the company will be trapped in China's existing competition, and its valuation may be further compressed.

## 7. Key conclusions

** BYD is in the most advantageous position in the competition, but the structural characteristics of the industry itself limit profit margins. **

In Munger's words: "Even the best racer will not be able to achieve good results if the track itself is muddy." BYD is undoubtedly one of the strongest players on the new energy vehicle track, but the intensity of competition on this track determines that the winner can only obtain "reasonable" rather than "excessive" profits.

Investors need to distinguish between "good companies" and "good investments" - BYD is a good company, but a good investment also requires a good price.
