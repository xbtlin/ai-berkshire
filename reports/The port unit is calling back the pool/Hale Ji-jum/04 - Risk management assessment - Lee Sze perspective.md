# I. KEY RISK I.

#1.1 North American Customs and Geopolitical Risks (Major Concern)

** Risk description**:
Once it lands, China’s combined merchandise tariffs may rise to about 61%. Although GE Applides is manufactured mainly on the mainland, parts and product lines are still dependent on Chinese imports, and tariff escalation will directly increase costs.

** Actual impact**:
- 2026Q1 North American market sector demand dropped by about 10 per cent on a year-on-year basis (partly due to storm weather, but customs expectations also discourage consumer confidence)
- The company collected 73,687 million yuan in 2026Q1 and the mother's net profit was $4,652 million, which was the same amount of money.
- If tariffs are further upgraded, the GE Applides supply chain needs to accelerate "de-Chinaization" and short-term costs will increase significantly

** buffers**:
- GE Applides has nine manufacturing bases in the United States, and is highly home-grown.
- The flexible supply chain of the company "1+1+N" is shifting from China's main supply to localization, regionalization and global synergy
- Hale Jijit was a strategic asset in addressing single market risks in a distributed layout of 163 manufacturing centres around the world

**Defence**: This is the greatest external risk today, but Haile's globalisation has made it more resilient than a pure export-oriented enterprise. Tariff risk is "manageable difficulty" rather than "lethal threat".

# # 1.2 Risk of increased cost of raw materials #

** Risk description**:
Copper prices have exceeded 95,000 yuan per ton, and key plastic particles such as ABS and PC have been more than half-percently affected by oil prices. These raw materials are the core inputs for white power.

** Actual impact**:
- 26.7 per cent Māori in 2025, a decrease of 1.1 percentage points in comparison with the previous year
- If raw materials continue to be priced at high prices, the Maori rate may be further pressured

** buffers**:
- Companies can transfer part of the cost through price increases (although there is limited scope for price increases in a competitive environment)
- Digital change continues to reduce operating costs, partly hedge cost pressures
- Globalized procurement systems provide more bargaining options

# 1.3 Risk of weak domestic demand

** Risk description**:
The Chinese white power market continued its weak recovery, with a decline of about 6.2 per cent in the industry as a whole in 2025, characterized by a low property cycle, low consumer confidence and a decline in population, which constrained domestic demand growth.

** Actual impact**:
- Domestic revenue in 2025 was 146.036 billion yuan, a 3.07 per cent increase over the previous year, which slowed considerably
- The policy dividend of "old for new" is at marginal risk of regressiveity.

** buffers**:
- Overseas income has surpassed domestic income and reduced reliance on a single market
- The high-end strategy (Casati) can partially resist the industry.
- Increased concentration in the industry is conducive to a higher share of the head

#1.4 Risk of exchange rate fluctuations

** Risk description**:
Overseas income accounts for 51.1%, and the renminbi appreciation will directly reduce the amount of overseas profits in the accounts. The renminbi has appreciated against the dollar since 2026, putting pressure on overseas profits to convert.

**Quantitative estimate**: RMB 1% appreciation, impacting on net profits of approximately RMB 200-300 million (approximately 1-1.5% net profit).

# # 1.5 Merger integration and goodwill risk

** Risk description**:
The historical large acquisitions (GE Applicances, etc.) have created large goodwill and intangible assets. There is a risk of impairment if overseas operations continue to fall short of expectations.

** Current assessment**:
- GE Applides have achieved good operations (North America share first) with low probability of significant impairment
- Candy's integration in Europe is still ongoing and requires sustained attention.

# 1.6 Competition increases risk

- The beauty of the world is constantly catching up with globalization and pluralism.
- Cross-borderers like Mi are encroaching on the lower-middle market.
- Air conditioning is under a strong, beautiful oligarchic squeeze.

##2.2 Management competency assessment #

**Globalization capacity**: This is the biggest management item. Turning GE Applides’ losses from the acquisition into the top North American market share is the case of China’s globalisation.

** Strategic implementation**:
- High-end.
- Digital change (full push since 2025, optimised 17 per cent swing days for air-conditioning inventory)
- Increase in shareholder returns (48 per cent to 55 per cent, with no less than 60 per cent planned for 2028)

** Shortcoming**:
- Profitability improvement is less rapid than beauty (ROE 17% vs 23% beauty) and organizational efficiency is more likely to improve.
- The "one person" model has increased the complexity of management to some extent.
- Zhou Yunjie's tenure of office, where Hale's market value has not been over $250 billion, has been questioned by some investors

#2.3 Management integrity and interest consistency

** Positive signal**:
1. **Significant partnership**: 14,885 creative partners hold "Signit equity" and management and core staff and corporate interests are highly bound.
2. ** Continuously increasing shareholder returns**: annual increases in the red ratio and greater buy-backs, demonstrating the importance that management attaches to shareholder interests.
3. **Packa **: Annual reports are relatively complete and overseas sub-areas data are more detailed.

** Focus**:
1. ** Complex equity structure**: Hale Group controls Heil think-tank through multilayered structures, core management (Chou Yunjie, Liang Haishan, Tan Lixia, etc.) indirectly holds approximately 48.8 per cent of Heir Group equity (through the Ocean Soft Partnership Platform) through limited partnerships, and Zhang Rimin through GP 51.1 per cent control. This multilayered equity structure increases the potential risks associated with trading and profit delivery.
2. ** Linked transaction**: There is a daily connection transaction between Hale Jiist and other entities under the Heir Group, with attention to the fairness of the transaction.
3. ** Partial defect**: Analysis indicates that there are areas where the information is not sufficiently disclosed in the 2025 annual report that require attention.

#2.4 Corporate governance assessment

** Governance improvement history**:
- By 2020, the dual listing structure of Hale Jie A+H led to a breakdown in governance
- Privatization of Hale Electric (H-Unit) and integration into a unified Hale Jiist A+H architecture by 2020, with a significantly simplified governance structure
- International capital markets were further expanded by listing D shares in Frankfurt, Germany, 2024

** Current level of governance**:
- Independent directors are in line and audit committees function properly
- But the majority shareholder (Heir Group) has a strong control and limited voice for the small and medium shareholders.
- Ports have relatively limited liquidity and may increase stock price volatility

** Comparison with peers**:
Governance is moderate in White Power Three. The governance structure is clearer (who owns the healthy family and the team of professional managers) and more controversial (Tong Ming-ju’s personal influence is too high).

# Three, key person risk

# 3.1 The influence of Zhang Rimmin

Zhang Reming retired in 2021, but as founder of the One-Man model and Helmian spiritual leader, his influence remains far-reaching. He retains effective control of the Hel Group through the GP structure (51.1%). The risk is that if Zhang's influence is completely withdrawn, the culture of "one-personness" will be sustainable.

# 3.2 Chao Yunji's challenge

After his shift, Zhou Yunjie faced the twin challenges of "care" and "entrepreneurship." He had a good deal in integrating globalization, but still had a weakness in stimulating market confidence and raising valuations.

#3.3 Management ladder

Hal’s Inventor Partnership has addressed the issue of succession development to some extent – by developing talent through mechanisms rather than relying on individual heroes. But the effectiveness of this model still needs to be seen at the corporate level.
