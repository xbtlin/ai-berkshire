# Five, the fragile point of the moat

The same emphasis was placed on finding ways of breaking the moat:

# 5.1 The risk of "displasticity" by the first generation

The fire from the first generation in China is largely rooted in the "social currency" attribute -- the identity labeling of the first generation. But all consumer behavior that relies on the "fashion attributes" rather than "functional demand" is cyclical. If the "tamp-hotness" of the first generation is cooled, its high growth may be difficult to sustain.

Historical reference: FILA has experienced a similar cycle – a high growth rate in 2019-2021, which began to slow significantly in 2022. Will the first-born bird repeat the FILA trajectory?

# 5.2 The "marginal reduction" of the multi-brand strategy

The problem of dissipating management efforts is exacerbated by each brand that already has 8-10 brands. Internal competitions between brands (such as running shoes vs. FILA) and conflicting resource allocations can occur.

#5.3 Consumption downgrade risk

In a macroeconomic uncertainty, high-end sports branding may be the first to be hit. A grand-old bird sells a sub-coupled suit for $5,000-10,000, and a Disant single item is in the 2000-5,000 range – these are "luxury" products for optional consumption, and consumers in the next economic period may turn to cheaper alternatives.
