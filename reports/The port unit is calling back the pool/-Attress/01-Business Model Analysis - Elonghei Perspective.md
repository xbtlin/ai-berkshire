# ANTA Sports (2020.HK) Business Model Analysis

> Analysis Dimension: Examined from the perspective of "good business" | Date: May 16, 2026 | Currency: RMB (unless otherwise specified)

## Overall rating: ★★★★☆ (4/5)

---

## 1. Is this a "good business"?

Duan Yongping's core criteria for judging a good business are: highly differentiated products, pricing power, a simple and replicable business model, and long-term occupation of consumers' minds. **

The essence of Anta Sports' business is not "selling sports shoes", but a brand operation platform**. This is the most critical point to understand Anta - it is essentially a brand management company, and its core capabilities are "acquiring brands + local transformation + large-scale operations".

### 1.1 Core logic of business model

Anta's business model can be broken down into three layers:

**First level: Anta main brand (Popular Sports, revenue of 34.8 billion yuan)**
- Covering the largest sports consumer group in China
- More than 7,000 stores across the country, with channels reaching third- and fourth-tier cities
- High cost-effective positioning, low unit price but wide coverage
- The growth rate will slow down to 3.7% in 2025 and enter the mature stage

**Second level: FILA (fashion sports, 28.5 billion yuan in revenue)**
- Acquired FILA's Chinese business from Belle for approximately RMB 600 million in 2009
- Repositioned as "high-end fashion sports", increasing unit price by 3-4 times
- Going from a loss to nearly 30 billion in revenue is the best proof of Anta’s brand operating capabilities
- Growth rate of 6.9% in 2025, growth slows down but still contributes stable profits

**Third level: High-end outdoor matrix (Archateryx/Descente/Kolon, etc., 17 billion yuan in revenue)**
- Participated in the acquisition of Amer Sports (parent company of Arc'teryx) in 2019 for a total price of 4.66 billion euros
- Descente's revenue will exceed 10 billion yuan in 2025, a year-on-year increase of 40%
- Kolon grew by 55%, MAIA ACTIVE grew by 25-30%
- The overall growth rate of this segment in 2025 is 59.2%, making it the group’s strongest growth engine

**Level 4 (new): Global expansion**
- Acquisition of Jack Wolfskin in 2025, 2.1 billion yuan
- In January 2026, it acquired 29% of Puma's shares and became the largest shareholder with 12.2 billion yuan.
- Invested in Korean fashion e-commerce company MUSINSA

### 1.2 About "differentiation"

Anta's differentiation is not at the single product level, but at the **system capability level**:

- **Brand Matrix Differentiation**: From 99 yuan Anta running shoes to tens of thousands of yuan Arc'teryx jackets, covering the entire price range, it is difficult for competitors to copy this breadth
- **Channel operation differentiation**: Direct operation transformation is basically completed, and the control over the terminal is much stronger than the wholesale model
- **Supply chain differentiation**: Flexible supply chain of self-owned factory + outsourced production to support the differentiated needs of multiple brands

### 1.3 About "Pricing Power"

This needs to be discussed by brand:

| Brand | Pricing Power Strength | Description |
|------|-----------|------|
| Arc'teryx | ★★★★★ | Luxury-level pricing, almost no discounts, consumers queue up to buy |
| Desante | ★★★★☆ | High-end professional sports, low price sensitivity |
| FILA | ★★★☆☆ | Once had strong pricing power, but in recent years the discount rate has increased, and the pricing power has been weakened |
| Anta's main brand | ★★☆☆☆ | Mass positioning, fierce price competition, head-on competition with Li Ning and others |
| Puma | ★★☆☆☆ | Global performance is sluggish, and the brand needs to be reshaped |

The group's overall gross profit margin is 62%, indicating that the comprehensive pricing power is acceptable. But what needs to be vigilant is that Anta, the brand with the most pricing power in the country (Arc'teryx), only holds about 40% of the equity, while Anta, the brand with the weakest pricing power (Anta's main brand, Puma), holds 100%. ** This mismatch of "profit attribution" deserves attention.

## 2. User value and consumer mentality

### 2.1 What user needs does Anta solve?

ANTA Group has solved the different needs of different consumers through a multi-brand matrix:
- **Anta main brand**: allowing ordinary consumers to obtain good sports equipment at reasonable prices
- **FILA**: Meet the mixed needs of urban young people for "sports + fashion"
- **Archa'teryx/Descente**: Meets the pursuit of professional outdoor equipment by middle- and high-income groups while providing social identity
- **Kelon/MAIA ACTIVE**: Meet the needs of niche scenarios (camping, yoga)

### 2.2 Will consumers continue to pay?

**Positive judgment:**
- China’s sports consumption penetration rate is still lower than that of developed countries, and there is room for long-term growth
- Outdoor sports (skiing, camping, hiking) have just started in China, and there are clear structural opportunities
- Arc'teryx has formed a brand effect similar to "social currency" in China

**Worry:**
- FILA’s fashion attributes mean that consumer loyalty is not as strong as that of professional sports brands
- Arc'teryx's "luxuryization" may be a double-edged sword - when the trend recedes, can the brand premium be maintained?
- The mental status of Anta's main brand among young consumers is not as clear as Li Ning's "national trend" concept

## 3. Sustainability and replicability of business model

### 3.1 Can the "acquisition + transformation" model be sustained?

This is Anta's core strategic issue. The success of FILA has built confidence in the market, but it needs to be evaluated calmly:

**FILA’s success factors (replicable parts):**
- Anta’s channel operation capabilities (can be copied)
- Dividends of the times from China’s consumption upgrade (conditions are changing)
- The historical heritage and design genes of the FILA brand itself (different for each brand)

**FILA’s success factors (non-replicable parts):**
- FILA's Chinese business was almost zero when it was acquired in 2009, and Anta acquired it at a very low price (it is difficult to reproduce now)
- At that time, China's high-end sports fashion market was almost blank (now the competition is fierce)
- It took FILA 15 years to grow from 0 to 30 billion. Can this patience be reproduced under the pressure of the capital market?

### 3.2 "Duan Yongping-style" questioning of Puma's acquisition

If Duan Yongping evaluates this deal, he might ask:

- **"How much do you know about Puma's business?"** Puma is a global brand with operations in Europe, America, Southeast Asia, and Latin America. Anta's core capabilities are in the Chinese market and its global operations experience is limited.
- **"12.2 billion to buy a 29% minority stake, what can you change?"** As the largest shareholder but not a controlling shareholder, Anta has limited influence on Puma's actual operations. This is completely different from FILA (100% holding and full management).
- **"If Puma does not improve in three years, what are you going to do?"** Puma will have a net loss of 247 million euros in the first half of 2025, and its performance is deteriorating rather than improving.
- **"What will happen to Anta if we don't do this?"** Anta's core business (Arc'teryx and Descente are growing rapidly) is growing healthily. Does it really need to expand aggressively at this time?

### 3.3 Natural limitations of the model

- Each acquisition requires a large amount of capital investment and the integration cycle is long (FILA took 5-6 years to explode)
- As the number of brands increases, management complexity increases exponentially
- It is becoming increasingly difficult to find targets like FILA’s "low-price acquisition + huge room for transformation"

## 4. Culture and organizational capabilities

### 4.1 "Dare to do" corporate culture

Anta's corporate culture can be summed up in one word: **Dare to be**.

- Dare to spend 600 million to acquire FILA in 2009, when Anta’s annual revenue was only a few billion
- In 2019, Dare took the lead in acquiring Amerfin for 4.66 billion euros, using debt as a leveraged buyout.
- Dare to spend 12.2 billion yuan to become the largest shareholder of Puma in 2026

This "can-do" culture is a double-edged sword: "vision" when it succeeds, and "adventure" when it fails. Historically, Anta's major decisions have a high success rate, but the bets are getting bigger every time.

### 4.2 Can organizational capabilities keep up?

Anta currently operates a very large number of brands: ANTA, FILA, Arc'teryx (through Amer), Descente, Colon, MAIA ACTIVE, Wolf Claw, and Puma (shareholding). This places extremely high demands on the management radius of the organization.

The positive signal is that Ding Shizhong voluntarily stepped down as CEO in 2023 and introduced a joint CEO system, which shows that he is aware of the limitations of his personal management radius and has made adjustments.

---

## 5. Summary: Is Anta a "good business"?

**Conclusion: Anta’s core business (ANTA + FILA + high-end outdoor) is a good business, but the group level is becoming more and more complex. **

Characteristics of a good business Anta has:
- High gross profit margin (62%)✓
- Strong cash flow (operating cash flow 16.7 billion yuan) ✓
- Brand equity forms a mental barrier for consumers ✓
- Multi-brand matrix diversifies single brand risks ✓

Requires continuous observation:
- Strategic rationality and integration prospects of Puma acquisition ⚠️
- How to break through the growth dilemma of Anta’s main brand ⚠️
- Can management efficiency be maintained after the number of brands expands ⚠️
- Is Arc'teryx's "luxury" craze sustainable ⚠️

Duan Yongping's possible evaluation: **"Anta's past performance proves that it has strong brand management capabilities, which is real. But now the challenge it faces is whether its proven effective capabilities are suitable for a larger and more complex global stage. If I were to invest, I would wait until the results of the Puma deal are clear before making a decision."**
