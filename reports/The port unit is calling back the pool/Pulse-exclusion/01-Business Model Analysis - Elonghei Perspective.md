# BeiGene (6160.HK) business model analysis

> Analysis Dimension: Business Nature and Moat
> Core question: Is this a good business? Will users buy competing products just because they are 10% cheaper?
> Study date: May 16, 2026

---

## 1. The nature of business: What kind of business is innovative medicine?

Innovative drugs are a high-investment, high-barrier, high-return but also high-risk business. Its business logic is completely different from that of consumer products: it does not rely on brand loyalty to sell products, but relies on clinical data and efficacy to obtain doctors’ prescription rights. Once a drug proves superior in head-to-head clinical trials, it can achieve a market position similar to a "natural monopoly" - doctors will not put patients at greater risk of side effects just to save a few percentage points on drug costs.

BeiGene’s core product zanubrutinib (BRUKINSA) is the beneficiary of this logic. In the ALPINE clinical trial, zanubrutinib demonstrated statistically significant superiority to ibrutinib in relapsed/refractory chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL), including higher overall response rate and lower cardiotoxicity. This is not something a marketing war can change, but hard data written into clinical guidelines.

**Positive judgment**: In the field of oncology, "efficacy is the moat." Zanubrutinib has strong competitive barriers based on its prescription advantage established by clinical data. Patients and doctors will not change treatment options just because a competing product is 10% cheaper.

**Reverse view**: But the moat of innovative drugs is time-sensitive. Expiration of patents, emergence of new generation drugs, and disruptive therapies with new targets may erode existing advantages. The core patent of zanubrutinib will expire in the mid-2030s, and new therapies such as CAR-T are also eating away at some of the indications of BTK inhibitors.

### 2.3 Cost structure

In 2025, R&D expenses will be US$2.146 billion (accounting for 40.2% of revenue), and sales and administrative expenses will be US$2.081 billion. Although the expense ratio has dropped significantly from 125% in 2021, the absolute amount is still high. This reflects the inherent characteristics of innovative pharmaceutical companies: they must continue to invest heavily to maintain the competitiveness of their pipelines.

## 3. Moat assessment: How wide is it? How long can it last?

### 3.1 Clinical data barriers ★★★★☆

Zanubrutinib defeated ibrutinib in the ALPINE trial and was the first drug in the world to demonstrate superiority over a first-generation BTK inhibitor in a head-to-head trial. This data barrier is real:
- Higher overall response rate (PFS significantly better than ibrutinib)
- Lower cardiotoxicity (significantly lower incidence of atrial fibrillation)
- Recommended by NCCN guidelines as one of the preferred first-line treatments for CLL

But this moat is not insurmountable. Lilly/Loxo's pirtobrutinib, as a non-covalent BTK inhibitor, has shown efficacy in BTK-resistant patients and may cannibalize part of the market for zanubrutinib.

### 3.2 Global commercialization capabilities ★★★★☆

BeiGene is one of the very few Chinese pharmaceutical companies that has built its own global commercialization team. As of 2025, the company has a sales team of more than 500 people in the United States, and has also established its own teams in major European markets. This capability is not possessed by most Chinese innovative pharmaceutical companies.

The significance of building a self-built team is: to control pricing power, obtain front-line market feedback, and not be restricted by the distribution of benefits from partners. But maintenance costs are high - selling and administrative expenses of US$2.081 billion are not a small amount.

### 3.3 Pipeline depth ★★★☆☆

There are 30 clinical stage pipeline products and 96 clinical trials, which is a considerable number. Key points to watch:
- **sonrotoclax (BCL-2 inhibitor)**: has received priority review from NMPA, and combined with zanubrutinib is expected to become a first-line gold combination for CLL, benchmarking AbbVie's Venclexta
- **Solid tumor layout**: CDK4 inhibitor (BGB-43395) is planned to enter Phase III in 2026, and the PRMT5/MAT2A combination is in progress
- More than 20 milestones expected in the next 18 months

But the number of drug candidates in the pipeline that can truly become "blockbuster" drugs is still limited. Sotokra is the most promising second growth curve, but its layout in the field of solid tumors is relatively late and the competition it faces is more intense.

### 3.4 Production and Supply Chain ★★★☆☆

The company has production bases in Guangzhou, Suzhou, China and New Jersey, the United States. The opening of the New Jersey plant in the United States is crucial to coping with geopolitical risks and tariff policies. However, compared with the global manufacturing networks of large multinational pharmaceutical companies, there is still a gap in production capacity and redundancy.

## 4. Key question: Will users (doctors/patients) change medicines just because they are 10% cheaper?

In the field of innovative drugs, the answer is usually "no" - but there are conditions:

**Situation where dressing cannot be changed**:
- Patients who are receiving zanubrutinib and have good results will not switch medications easily
- When clinical guidelines clearly recommend zanubrutinib, physicians have the basis to insist on prescribing it
- In markets dominated by commercial insurance such as the United States, drug net price differences are not directly passed on to patients

**Possible dressing changes**:
- After the ibrutinib patent expires in 2026, the price of generic drugs will drop significantly, which may lead to diversion in price-sensitive markets (such as some emerging markets)
- If next-generation BTK inhibitors with better efficacy or safety emerge
- In markets such as China where health insurance prices are negotiated, price pressure persists

## 5. Comprehensive Rating

| Dimensions | Rating | Description |
|------|------|------|
| Nature of business | ★★★★☆ | Curative effect-driven prescription drugs, customer stickiness is strong, but cyclical |
| Moat width | ★★★★☆ | Clinical data + global commercialization capabilities constitute a wider moat |
| Persistence of the moat | ★★★☆☆ | Patents expire and new technology paths may be eroded, requiring continuous innovation |
| Business model predictability | ★★★☆☆ | High dependence on a single product, uncertainty in pipeline fulfillment |
| User switching cost | ★★★★☆ | Doctors and patients have strong inertia in decision-making, but they are not absolutely locked in |

**Overall Rating: ★★★★☆ (3.6/5)**

BeiGene is running a good business - driven by efficacy, high switching costs, and outstanding globalization capabilities. But it is not a Moutai-style "sustainable money printing machine": innovative drugs naturally have a life cycle, and the moat requires continuous R&D investment to maintain. When investors buy BeiGene, they are essentially betting on its ability to continue to produce clinically superior drugs, not just the stock value of its existing products. The "goodness" of this business is conditional - the condition is whether management can successfully incubate the next blockbuster product within the life cycle of zanubrutinib.
