## 2. In-depth analysis of profitability

### 2.1 ROE decomposition (DuPont analysis)

| Factor | Value | Evaluation |
|------|------|------|
| Net profit margin | ~1.9% | Extremely low |
| Asset turnover rate | ~1.6x | High (characteristics of distribution industry) |
| Equity Multiplier | ~3.8x | High Leverage |
| ROE | ~12% | Relying on leverage and turnover |

**Sinopharm Holdings' ROE "Quality"**

The ROE of 12% seems okay, but after dismantling it, we found:
- Very low net profit margin - earn little
- Extremely high leverage - borrow more
- Turnover is acceptable - but subject to downstream accounting periods

This is a "fragile" ROE structure - any slight decline in net interest rates will lead to a significant deterioration in ROE.

### 2.2 Profit quality

**Positive Signs:**
- Operating cash flow is generally positive, but fluctuates greatly
- Income is highly correlated with industry totals, and there is no fictitious income risk
- Impairment provisions are relatively conservative

**Issues needing attention:**
- The scale of accounts receivable is huge (ratio to annual revenue is about 25-30%), and the risk of bad debts
- Large impairment losses will occur in 2024, resulting in short-term pressure on profits
- Minority shareholders have a relatively high proportion of profits (~35%), and actual attributable profits are limited

### 2.3 Working capital management

| Accounts receivable turnover days | ~100 days | Industry normal |
| Inventory turnover days | ~35 days | Better |
| Accounts payable turnover days | ~70 days | Medium |

The core pain points of the pharmaceutical distribution industry: the downstream (hospital) account period is long, the upstream (pharmaceutical company) requires quick payment, and the gap needs to be filled with its own funds or borrowings.

## 3. Capital allocation analysis

### 3.1 Capital Expenditure

Capital expenditures are mainly used for the construction of warehousing and logistics facilities and mergers and acquisitions. In recent years, the capital expenditure rate (CAPEX/revenue) is about 0.5%, and the absolute amount is about 3 billion/year.

### 3.2 M&A Strategy

Sinopharm has historically expanded its network coverage through a large number of acquisitions of local distributors. This "volume map" M&A strategy:
- Advantages: Quickly obtain local channels and customer resources
- Disadvantages: High integration costs, poor quality of some acquired assets

In recent years, the pace of mergers and acquisitions has slowed down, and more emphasis has been placed on stock integration and efficiency improvement.

### 3.3 Shareholder Return

| Indicators | Values |
|------|------|
| Dividend ratio | ~30% |
| Dividend Yield | ~4-5% |
| Repurchase | Occasionally in small quantities |

The dividend yield of 4-5% is a relatively attractive level among Hong Kong stocks. For an industry leader with limited growth, dividends are the main form of shareholder returns.

## 4. Valuation analysis

### 4.1 Relative valuation

| Company | PE (TTM) | PB | ROE | Dividend Yield |
|------|-----------|-----|-----|--------|
| Sinopharm Holdings | ~7x | ~0.8x | ~12% | ~4.5% |
| China Resources Pharmaceutical | ~8x | ~0.9x | ~11% | ~4% |
| Shanghai Pharmaceuticals | ~10x | ~1.0x | ~10% | ~3.5% |
| Jiuzhoutong | ~12x | ~1.5x | ~13% | ~3% |

The valuation of Sinopharm Holdings is relatively low in the industry. The main reasons are:
1. The Hong Kong stock market as a whole gives a "governance discount" to large state-owned enterprises
2. Weak growth (slight decline in revenue)
3. No improvement expected in profit margins

### 4.2 Absolute valuation

PB 0.8x means that the market is pricing the company's net assets at a 20% discount. For a company with an ROE of 12% and stable operations, this pricing is conservative.

If the company is considered a "bond-like" investment:
- Dividend yield 4.5% + profit growth rate 1-3% = potential annualized return 5.5-7.5%
- With room for valuation repair (PB from 0.8 to 1.0), potential returns could be even higher

### 4.3 DCF thinking

Assumptions:
- Revenue CAGR in the next five years: 2-3%
- Net profit margin maintained at 1.8-2.0%
- Terminal growth rate: 2%
- Discount rate: 9%

The DCF valuation is equivalent to or slightly higher than the current market value, indicating that the current pricing basically reflects the expectations of "low growth + high certainty".

### 4.4 Valuation drivers

Factors that can lead to a revaluation:
1. **Profit margin improvement**: The increase in the proportion of retail business drives the overall profit margin
2. **Dividend ratio increased**: from 30% to 40-50%
3. **Catalysis of state-owned enterprise reform**: equity incentives, mixed ownership reform, etc.
4. **Industry concentration is accelerating**: Mergers and acquisitions bring economies of scale

## 5. Financial risks

### 5.1 Accounts receivable risk ★★★☆☆
The scale of accounts receivable is large, and bad debts may occur if the hospital's ability to pay is reduced or local finances are tight.

### 5.2 Downward risk of profit margin ★★★☆☆
Centralized purchasing continues to reduce drug prices, compressing the circulation spread space; competition in distribution rates intensifies.

### 5.3 Risk of growth stagnation ★★★☆☆
Revenue has already experienced negative growth in 2025. If the industry total continues to be under pressure, revenue may continue to decline.

### 5.4 Leverage risk ★★★☆☆
Highly leveraged operations mean that rising interest rates will directly erode profits. But as a central enterprise, financing costs are relatively controllable.
