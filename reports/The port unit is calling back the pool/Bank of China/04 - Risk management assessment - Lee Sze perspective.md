# Bank of China (3988.HK) Risk and Management Assessment

## 1. Core risks

| Risk type | Severity | Probability of occurrence | Comprehensive rating |
|----------|----------|----------|----------|
| Net interest margin continues to narrow to less than 1% | High | Medium | ★★★★☆ |
| Massive defaults on real estate loans | Extremely high | Low | ★★★☆☆ |
| Local government implicit debt risk | High | Low to medium | ★★★☆☆ |
| The policy requires greater efforts to transfer profits | Medium | Medium | ★★★☆☆ |
| Overseas business geopolitical risk | Medium | Medium low | ★★☆☆☆ |

### About systemic risk
The biggest potential risk to China’s banking system is the concentrated outbreak of non-performing loans in real estate and local government bonds. But Li Lu’s analysis should focus on:
1. The Chinese government has a strong will and ability to prevent systemic crises
2. Bank provision coverage ratio is about 190% - with cushion
3. Risks can be gradually resolved through time + growth
4. The probability of complete collapse is extremely low

## 2. Management evaluation

**Rating: ★★★☆☆ (3/5)**

Characteristics of central bank management:
- Strong execution ability and implementation of policies in place
- Improved risk control system
- Limited space for innovation
- Salary incentives are out of touch with the market

Positive performance of Bank of China’s management in recent years:
1. The proportion of non-interest income increased to 33%, indicating that the transformation direction is in the right direction.
2. Asset quality remains stable and improving
3. Continue to develop international business

## 3. Asymmetry analysis

**Extremely strong downside protection:**
- "Too big to fail" - national credit endorsement
- PE 6 times + PB 0.5 times - the valuation has fully reflected the pessimistic expectations
- 6-7% dividend yield - considerable returns even if the stock price does not rise
- Adequate provision coverage

**Upstream driver:**
- Economic recovery → recovery in credit demand → profit growth
- The central bank’s interest rate cut cycle ends → the net interest margin stabilizes
- "Market Value Management of Central Enterprises" Policy → Valuation Repair
- RMB internationalization drives overseas business growth

**Asymmetry Rating: ★★★★☆ (3.5/5)**

On the downside, there is national credit to cover the bottom, and on the upside, there is room for valuation repair. The 6-7% dividend yield provides excellent compensation while you wait.

## 4. Scenario Analysis

### Optimistic scenario (probability 25%)
- Economic recovery + net interest margin stabilization
- Profit returns to 5-8% growth
- PB increased from 0.5x to 0.7x
- Stock price increased by 40%+

### Baseline scenario (probability 55%)
- Low profit growth (1-3%/year)
- Net interest margin slowly declines
- Dividend maintained at 6-7%
- Annualized return 7-9% (dividends + small growth)

### Pessimistic scenario (probability 20%)
- Accelerated decline in net interest margin + rising bad debts
- Profits dropped by 5-10%
- Share price fell 10-15%
- But the dividend can still be maintained at 5%+

## 5. Core Conclusions

**Risk Management Rating: ★★★☆☆ (3.5/5)**

Bank of China is a target that is "almost impossible for you to lose a lot of money" - 6 times PE, national credit, and 6-7% dividend rate together form an iron-clad downside protection. But it's almost impossible for you to "make big money" - falling net interest margins and low growth limit the upside.

Li Lu's conclusion: Bank of China is suitable for "bottom positions" - it provides a stable annualized return expectation of 7-9% (dividends 6-7% + slight growth), and the downside risk is extremely low. In an environment of declining interest rates, a deterministic dividend yield of 6-7% has high relative value.
