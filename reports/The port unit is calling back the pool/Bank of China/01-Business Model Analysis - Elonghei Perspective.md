# Bank of China (3988.HK) business model analysis

## 1. The essence of business

The essence of a bank's business is extremely simple: borrow money (deposits/bonds) at low prices, lend it out (loans/investments) at high prices, and earn interest differentials. ** As one of the four major state-owned banks, Bank of China has total assets of approximately 30 trillion yuan and is one of the largest banks in the world.

From Duan Yongping's perspective, banking is a business that "seems simple but has hidden risks": On the surface, it makes money every year, but once the economic crisis leads to a large number of bad debts, the profits for many years may disappear (such as the U.S. banking industry in 2008).

## 2. Moat assessment

**Rating: ★★★☆☆ (3.5/5)**

### Existing moat:
1. **License Barrier**: Large commercial bank licenses are extremely scarce
2. **Scale advantage**: The capital cost and risk diversification advantages brought by 30 trillion assets
3. **National Credit Endorsement**: It is almost impossible for the four major banks to go bankrupt
4. **Outlet network**: tens of thousands of outlets + huge customer base
5. **Internationalization**: Bank of China has the highest degree of internationalization (overseas assets account for about 25%)

### Moat defects:
1. Product homogeneity - a loan is a loan, no differences among banks
2. Subject to policy – interest rates, deposit reserves, and credit lines are all determined by supervision
3. The policy task of benefiting the real economy restricts profit maximization.
4. Net interest margin continues to narrow, and core profitability is declining

## 3. The special positioning of Bank of China

Among the four major banks, Bank of China is uniquely positioned as an "international bank":
- Overseas business revenue accounts for about 20-25%
- With branches in 60+ countries around the world
- Advantages of foreign currency business and cross-border settlement
- Core participant in RMB internationalization

This positioning has positive significance under the "Belt and Road Initiative" and the trend of RMB internationalization, but overseas business also faces geopolitical risks.

## 4. User value analysis

The "users" of a bank include depositors and borrowers. From Duan Yongping's "differentiation" standard:
- Depositors choose banks mainly based on interest rates and convenience - there is almost no difference between the four major banks
- Borrowers choose banks based on interest rates, quotas and approval speed - large companies have bargaining power, while small companies rely on relationships
- No one will "warmly recommend" a bank - brand loyalty is next to zero

## 5. Net interest margin—continuous deterioration of core profit logic

The net interest margin in 2025 will be 1.26%, a year-on-year decrease of 14 basis points. Here are the numbers that deserve the most attention:
- Net interest margin is the core profitability indicator of banks
- A continued downward trend means "it's getting harder and harder to make money"
- Reason: central bank interest rate cut + loan interest rate competition + policy concessions
- If the net interest margin falls below 1.0%, the bank's profit model will face fundamental challenges

Increasing the proportion of non-interest income to 33% (financial management, agency sales, intermediary business) is management's response strategy, but it is currently not enough to fully make up for the impact of narrowing interest margins.

## 6. Is it a good business?

**Rating: ★★★☆☆ (2.5/5)**

Banks are "ordinary businesses" in Duan Yongping's framework:
- There are barriers but no differentiation
- Profitable, but the quality of profits is greatly affected by the macroeconomics
- There is scale, but scale does not necessarily bring excess returns
- Stable but lack of growth

What's special about Bank of China is that it is one of the four "international" banks and plays a unique role in the general trend of RMB internationalization.

## 7. Core Conclusions

Bank of China is a "stable but not growing" large bank stock. The combination of PE 6x and ROE 9% provides a reasonable margin of safety in valuation. However, the continued narrowing of net interest margin is the biggest fundamental risk - if this trend continues, profits will gradually be eroded. The logic of investing in Bank of China is "earn dividends but not growth".
