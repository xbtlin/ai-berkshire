## 2. Summary of investment discussion

### 2.1 Bullish logic (mainstream market view)

1. **Domestic substitution is irreversible**: China imports US$300 billion in chips every year, and independent substitution is a definite trend.
2. **Scarcity of the only target**: The only advanced process foundry platform for A-shares/Hong Kong stocks, enjoying a valuation premium
3. **Technological breakthroughs catalyze**: 7nm yield is close to 85%, 5nm is expected to break through, and every breakthrough is a catalyst for stock prices
4. **Unlimited policy support**: Large funds continue to invest, and the government will not let SMIC fail.
5. **Industry Recovery**: 2025-2026 Semiconductor Industry Upcycle

### 2.2 Bearish logic (the core point of this study)

1. **The gap between valuation and reality**: PE 160 times, ROE 3%, no dividends - the expectations implicit in market pricing are almost impossible to realize
2. **Structural flaws in capital return**: Annual capital expenditure of US$8.1 billion, net profit of 5 billion yuan, negative free cash flow - this is not a short-term problem, but an inevitable result of the business model
3. **Asymmetry of geo-risks**: No matter whether sanctions are maintained/upgraded/relaxed, it will be difficult for investors to make profits
4. **The end of JD.com’s approach**: Even if it eventually becomes the “number one”, it may still have ROE in single digits for a long time like BOE.
5. **The depreciation cliff has not yet arrived**: The depreciation peak corresponding to the US$8.1 billion/year capital expenditure will continue to be released in 2026-2029

### 2.3 Key issues that have been ignored

**Question 1: Whose money is paying for SMIC? **

SMIC's expansion funds come from: government subsidies + shareholder investment + bank borrowings. When ROE is only 3%, shareholders are actually "advancing funds" for the national strategy. This is an investment that has value for the country but no return for shareholders.

**Question 2: Is large scale a good investment? **

SMIC's global market share in 2025 will be 4.8%, with revenue of US$9.3 billion. TSMC has a 72% share and revenue of approximately US$90 billion. The size difference is 10 times. Even if SMIC reaches the second place in the world (it already is), its profit margin and return rate will still be far inferior to UMC, which focuses on mature processes.

**Question 3: Where is the boundary of the "domestic substitution" narrative? **

The end result of domestic substitution is not "China uses its own chips" but "China uses the best chips." If sanctions are relaxed one day, will customers return to TSMC? The answer is most likely "yes". So how will the valuation premium paid today for "domestic replacement" be digested by then?

## 3. Key Assumptions and Scenario Analysis

### 3.1 Baseline scenario (50% probability)

- Sanctions maintain the status quo and do not escalate significantly
- Annual revenue growth of 12-15% from 2026 to 2028
- Gross profit margin fluctuates between 18-22% (depreciation pressure)
- Net profit in 2028 will be approximately 8-10 billion yuan
- Reasonable valuation: 300-400 billion yuan (PE 30-40 times)

**Conclusion:** The current market value of 800 billion is overestimated by 50-60%

### 3.2 Optimistic scenario (probability 20%)

- The advanced process was successfully commercialized and gross profit margin increased to 30%
- Explosive demand for AI chips in China drives revenue to double
- Net profit reaches 20 billion yuan in 2028
- Optimistic valuation: 600-800 billion yuan (PE 30-40 times)

**Conclusion:** Even in the most optimistic scenario, current valuations are only "reasonable" rather than "cheap"

### 3.3 Pessimistic scenario (30% probability)

- Escalating sanctions and restricted supply of DUV equipment
- Overcapacity in mature processes, gross profit margin fell below 15%
- Semiconductor cycle downturn superimposed on depreciation peak
- Incurred quarterly losses
- Market value fell back to 300-400 billion yuan

**Conclusion:** More than 50% downside

### 3.4 Risk-benefit ratio

- Upside (optimistic-current): 0% to up to 50% (requires extremely optimistic assumptions)
- Downside (pessimistic-current): -50% to -60%
- Risk-benefit ratio: **Severe asymmetry, downside is much greater than upside**

## 4. Comparison with the classic investment framework

### 4.1 Does it meet the standard of "good company, good price"?

| Standard | Judgment | Explanation |
|------|------|------|
| Good industry | Partly | Wafer foundry is a good industry, but SMIC’s location is not good |
| Good company | Conditional | The strongest domestically, but weak in global competitiveness |
| Good management | Partly | Excellent management but constrained |
| Good price | No | PE 160 times, PB 5.3 times, ROE 3% |
| Good time | No | During peak depreciation period, profit margins will continue to be under pressure |

**Conclusion:** Only 0-1 of the five "good" conditions are met, and the buying conditions are not met.

### 4.2 Is there any reason to "hold for ten years"?

The premise of holding for ten years is that the company can continue to create value and return value to shareholders.

- Create value: ROE 3%, ROIC <2% - currently destroying value
- Give back to shareholders: No dividends or repurchases in a row - no feedback whatsoever
- The situation after ten years: revenue may reach 200 billion yuan, but ROE is still below 10% (refer to BOE)

**Conclusion:** Not a long-term holding value (at current prices)

### 4.3 "Think the other way around" - under what circumstances will the judgment change?

1. **Stock price drops by 60-70%** (PE drops to 40-50 times): there is a margin of safety.
2. **Gross profit margin exceeds 30% and continues**: Proving the improvement of the business model
3. **Free cash flow turns positive**: Proving that the capital cycle is on the right track
4. **Start dividends or buybacks**: Management begins to focus on shareholder returns
5. **Advanced process achieves major commercialization breakthrough**: Opening up the growth ceiling

It is not recommended to intervene before any of the above 2-3 conditions are met at the same time.

## 5. Final conclusion

### 5.1 Investment Decision

| Decision | Content |
|------|------|
| Current Position | **NO BUY** |
| Core reasons | The valuation is extremely expensive and the risk-return ratio is seriously imbalanced |
| Conditions of concern | The stock price falls by 60%+, or there is a substantial improvement in fundamentals |
| Suitable for the crowd | Thematic investment/transactional investors (non-value investors) |

### 5.2 Evaluation of the "domestic substitution" narrative

Domestic substitution is a real trend, and SMIC’s strategic value is undeniable. But:

- **Strategic value ≠ Investment value**
- **Good Company ≠ Good Investment** (You will also lose money if you buy a good company at the wrong price)
- **Certainty Trend ≠ Certainty Return** (trend may be fully priced)

The market's current pricing for SMIC is not paying for the "current SMIC", but for the "imagined future SMIC" - a SMIC with an ROE of 20%+, a gross profit margin of 30%+, and abundant free cash flow. But there is huge uncertainty and a very long time between the current ROE of 3% and that state.

### 5.3 Monitoring list

Continue to pay attention to the following indicators and wait for better buying opportunities:

- [ ] Quarterly gross profit margin trend (whether it exceeds 25%)
- [ ] Annual free cash flow (whether turned positive)
- [ ] Advanced process revenue proportion (whether it exceeds 20%)
- [ ] Dividend/Repurchase Action
- [ ] Geopolitical changes (signals of escalation/relaxation of sanctions)
- [ ] Degree of valuation fallback (target PE 40-50 times)
