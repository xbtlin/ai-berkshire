# SMIC (0981.HK) Risk and Management Assessment

> Analysis dimensions: management quality, geo-risk, institutional environment, safety margin
> Analysis date: May 16, 2026

## 1. Management evaluation

### 1.1 Core Management Team

SMIC implements a "joint CEO" system, and its core executives include:

| Position | Name | Background | Role |
|------|------|------|------|
| Chairman | Liu Xunfeng | 30 years of management experience in large industrial groups | Strategic decision-making, government relations |
| Co-CEO | Zhao Haijun | Operations management expert | Market, customers, operations |
| Co-CEO | Liang Mengsong | Former TSMC/Samsung technology executive | Technology research and development, process breakthroughs |

### 1.2 Management capability assessment

**Zhao Haijun——Operation and Market**

Front:
- Promote the absolute value of industrial and automotive revenue to increase by more than 60% year-on-year in 2025
- Accurately grasp the trend of "localization of industrial chains" and promote the upgrading of customer structure
- Capacity utilization rate increases from 85% in 2024 to 93.5% in 2025
- Effectively managed the complex situation of simultaneous construction of multiple new plants

Negative:
- Revenue growth of 15-16%, lower than some competitors in the industry
- Gross profit margin improvement is limited (+3pct), and depreciation pressure cannot be effectively digested

**Liang Mengsong——Technical Soul**

Front:
- At the age of 68, he still sticks to the front line of research and development, pushing the 7nm process yield to nearly 85%
- Achieve technological breakthroughs through DUV multiple exposure without EUV equipment
- Three major directions of layout: FinFET iteration, mature process characteristic platform, and advanced packaging
- Established an advanced packaging research institute to demonstrate forward-looking layout

Negative:
- Whether the cost and yield of the DUV solution can be commercialized remains to be verified.
- 5nm breakthrough has been reported but the actual commercialization progress is unknown
- The long-term economics of technology path selection (DUV multi-exposure vs waiting for EUV) are questionable

### 1.3 Management stability

**Historical Lessons - SMIC's "History of Palace Fights"**

SMIC has experienced serious management turmoil in its history:
- Founder Zhang Rujing was forced to resign
- After six chairmanships
- The conflict between Jiang Shangyi and Liang Mengsong once became public
- Zhao Haijun also resigned as executive director

The current team (Zhao Haijun + Liang Mengsong) has a relatively stable cooperation that has lasted for many years, but it needs attention:
- Liang Mengsong’s age (68 years old), successor issue
- Long-term sustainability of the joint CEO system
- The constraints of government influence on management autonomy

**Management Rating: ★★★☆☆ (3/5)**

Reason: The current team has outstanding capabilities (a combination of technology + operations), but the management has a poor history of stability, and company decisions are greatly affected by non-market factors.

## 2. Governance structure and shareholder interests

### 2.1 Equity Structure

SMIC's shareholding structure is relatively dispersed, with major shareholders including:
- Datang Telecom (state-owned assets background)
- National Integrated Circuit Industry Investment Fund (Large Fund)
- State-owned capital such as Ziguang Group

**Key issue:** The actual controller of the company is the "state will" rather than a specific major shareholder. This means:
- The company's strategy first serves the national semiconductor independence goal
- Shareholder returns are not the first priority
- Capital allocation may be biased towards "strategic expansion" rather than "profit maximization"

### 2.2 Shareholder Return Record

| Year | Dividend status | Buyback status |
|------|----------|----------|
| 2023 | No dividend | None |
| 2024 | No dividend | None |
| 2025 | No dividend | None |

**No dividends or repurchases for many consecutive years. ** This is highly unusual for a company with a market value of 800 billion. Management's explanation is that "funds need to be reserved for production expansion," but this also reflects the company's prioritization of shareholder interests.

### 2.3 Alignment of management incentives and shareholder interests

- Management compensation includes equity incentives and is linked to company performance
- But the definition of "performance" is biased towards revenue growth and capacity expansion, rather than profit margins and capital returns
- Management has no clear ROE target or return on capital commitment

**Governance Rating: ★★☆☆☆ (2/5)**

Reasons: State-owned assets control, strategy takes precedence over returns, continuous no dividends are given, and shareholder interests are not the first priority.

## 3. Geopolitical risks (core risks)

### 3.1 Current status of US sanctions

**Sanctions implemented:**
- December 2020: Added to the US Entity List
- 10nm and below technologies: Export license refused in principle
- 14nm and above technologies: approval on a case-by-case basis (but enforcement is becoming increasingly strict)
- 2025: The United States plans to change the restriction clause from "unique needs" to "availability", expanding the scope

**New sanctions in Taiwan in 2025:**
- Taiwan adds SMIC and 600+ related entities to its export control blacklist
- May affect the supply of some equipment parts and materials

### 3.2 Possible paths for escalating sanctions

| Upgrade Level | Content | Probability | Impact |
|----------|------|------|------|
| Current levels maintained | Maintain existing restrictions | 40% | Controllable |
| Moderate upgrade | Restrict more equipment types | 30% | Slow down production expansion |
| Significant upgrade | Cut off the supply of DUV equipment | 20% | Severe blow |
| Extreme Scenario | Total Technology Decoupling | 10% | Survival Challenge |

### 3.3 DUV equipment supply risks

SMIC relies on ASML's DUV lithography machines to achieve advanced processes. If the DUV device is also restricted:
- Existing production capacity can maintain operations (installed equipment)
- But the expansion of new production lines will be seriously affected
- Equipment maintenance and parts supply may be disrupted
- Advanced process research and development will come to a standstill

**2026 estimate:** Mainland China needs about US$13 billion in etching + CVD + measurement equipment, and the domestic substitution rate needs to increase from 25% to more than 50% to offset the impact of the new ban.

### 3.4 Asymmetry of geopolitical risks

**Asymmetric characteristics of risk:**
- Sanctions escalation: The probability is not low and the impact is huge (non-linear downside risk)
- Sanctions are relaxed: the probability is extremely low, and after relaxation, SMIC will face direct competition from TSMC
- Regardless of the escalation or relaxation of sanctions, SMIC will not be the beneficiary

This is a "lose-lose" situation - maintaining/escalating sanctions restricts development, while relaxing sanctions removes the moat.

**Geo Risk Score: ★☆☆☆☆ (1/5, very high risk)**

## 4. Other key risks

### 4.1 Technical path risks

- The cost of a DUV multiple exposure solution is 3-5 times that of a single exposure
- Although the yield rate has improved, the economics of mass production have not been proven
- If EUV can never be obtained, the ceiling of advanced manufacturing processes will be certain

### 4.2 Risk of overcapacity

- Mainland China's production capacity will account for 34.4% of the world's production capacity in 2026
- A large number of new production capacities are put into production at the same time
- If terminal demand falls short of expectations, there will be a serious surplus
- Historically, every time overcapacity has resulted in a sharp decline in industry profit margins

### 4.3 Cyclic Risk

The semiconductor industry is highly cyclical:
- 2022: Core shortage, gross profit margin 38%
- 2023: Downturn cycle, revenue -13%
- Current (2025-2026): Moderate recovery

In the next downward cycle, SMIC may suffer quarterly losses due to the superimposed depreciation peak.

### 4.4 Customer concentration risk

- Huawei's ecological chain is an important source of income
- If Huawei itself is subject to more severe sanctions, it will also affect SMIC's orders
- China accounts for 84%+ of revenue, with extremely high geographical concentration

### 4.5 Risk of brain drain

- The global competition for talent in the semiconductor industry is fierce
- Liang Mengsong is old and there is a problem with his successor.
- Under the sanctions environment, it is difficult to recruit international talents

## 5. Safety margin analysis

### 5.1 Downstream protection

| Protective factors | Strength | Description |
|----------|------|------|
| Asset value | ★★★☆☆ | PB 5.3 times, lack of asset protection |
| Profit certainty | ★★☆☆☆ | Profit is thin and greatly affected by depreciation/cycle |
| Policy support | ★★★★☆ | The country will not let SMIC go bankrupt |
| Cash flow protection | ★☆☆☆☆ | FCF continues to be negative |
| Valuation protection | ★☆☆☆☆ | The current valuation is extremely high and the downside is huge |

### 5.2 If I buy, what price has a margin of safety?

Rough estimate:
- Assuming net profit reaches 10 billion yuan in 2028 (optimistic assumption)
- Give 30 times PE (reasonable valuation for growth manufacturing industry)
- Reasonable market value = 300 billion yuan
- Consider safety margin (30% discount): 210 billion yuan
- Current market value is 800 billion yuan

**The "reasonable value" of the relatively conservative estimate of the current price is still overestimated by about 60%. **

### 5.3 Under what conditions is it worthy of attention?

1. The stock price drops to 1/3-1/2 of the current level (i.e. PE 40-60 times range)
2. Gross profit margin continues to improve to above 25%
3. Free cash flow turns positive
4. Start dividends or buybacks
5. Substantial progress has been made in the commercialization of advanced processes

| **Comprehensive** | **★★☆☆☆** | |

**One sentence summary:** SMIC's management has performed well under extremely difficult conditions, but the geopolitical risks faced by the company are structural, asymmetric, and uncontrollable. From the perspective of risk-return ratio, "maintenance of sanctions = limited growth, escalation of sanctions = major blow, relaxation of sanctions = disappearance of moat" - it is difficult for investors to obtain excess returns under the three scenarios. Combined with the current extremely high valuation levels, the margin of safety is almost zero. This is not a problem that management capabilities can solve, but a structural risk-return asymmetry problem.
