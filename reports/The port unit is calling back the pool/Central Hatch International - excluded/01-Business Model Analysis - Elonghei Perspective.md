# SMIC (0981.HK) business model analysis

> Analysis dimensions: business essence, user value, moat, business model sustainability
> Analysis date: May 16, 2026

---

## 1. What kind of business is this?

### 1.1 Nature of business

The essence of foundry is an asset-heavy manufacturing service industry. The customer (chip design company) provides the design drawings, and the foundry is responsible for turning them into physical chips. The revenue model is based on the number of wafers charged. The core competitiveness lies in: process technology advancement, yield, production capacity scale, and delivery reliability.

This business has several natural characteristics:

- **Extremely high capital density**: The investment in an advanced process production line can easily reach tens of billions of dollars.
- **Significant scale effect**: Every 1% increase in capacity utilization contributes huge contribution to marginal profit
- **Fast technology iteration**: Equipment depreciation takes 5-7 years, but the process update cycle is 2-3 years
- **Strong customer stickiness**: The tape-out verification cycle is long (6-18 months), and the cost of switching foundries is extremely high

### 1.2 Positioning of SMIC

SMIC is the only wafer foundry in mainland China that can provide advanced processes of 14nm and below. It also has a large amount of production capacity in mature processes of 28nm and above. In 2025, the company will achieve:

- Revenue of 67.3 billion yuan (US$9.327 billion), a year-on-year increase of 16.5%
- Equivalent to 8-inch monthly production capacity of 1.059 million pieces
- Average capacity utilization rate 93.5%
- Revenue from China accounts for more than 84%

**Business Rating: ★★★☆☆ (3/5)**

Reason: Foundry is essentially a good business (high barriers, high stickiness), but SMIC's situation makes it a "constrained good business" - it is unable to obtain the most advanced equipment due to sanctions and can only compete in a limited space.

## 2. User value analysis

### 2.1 Who is the customer? Why do customers need SMIC?

SMIC’s customers are mainly chip design companies in mainland China, and its core needs are:

1. **Secure supply chain**: Under geopolitical risks, using domestic foundries is a "must" rather than a "choice"
2. **Mature process demand**: Consumer electronics, IoT, and automotive chips use 28nm-55nm processes extensively.
3. **Localization of advanced processes**: Huawei and other companies need domestic foundry capabilities at the 14nm/7nm level

### 2.2 Customer Structure

| Application areas | Revenue share | Growth trends |
|----------|----------|----------|
| Consumer Electronics/Smartphones | About 66% | Stable |
| Industrial & Automotive | About 10% | Rapid growth (+60%) |
| Others (IoT, etc.) | About 24% | Moderate growth |

Geographically, China's revenue share will increase from 80.9% in 2024 to 84.2% in 2025, and the reliance on a single market is deepening.

### 2.3 Customer stickiness assessment

**Positive factors:**
- The tape-out verification cycle is long and the switching cost is high
- Under the trend of domestic substitution, customers "have to use it"
- The vehicle certification period is 2-3 years, and once imported it is difficult to replace

**Negatives:**
- Customers choose SMIC more because of "no other choice" than "the best choice"
- If sanctions are relaxed, some customers may return to TSMC
- Relatively limited price competitiveness (yield and cost disadvantages)

**User Value Rating: ★★★★☆ (4/5)**

Reason: In the current geopolitical environment, SMIC’s value to China’s chip industry chain is irreplaceable. But this value comes more from "monopolistic scarcity" than "excellent service."

## 3. Moat analysis

### 3.1 Source of moat

| Moat Type | Strength | Description |
|------------|------|------|
| Technical barriers | ★★★☆☆ | 14nm mass production, 7nm breakthrough, but about 5 generations behind TSMC |
| Scale advantage | ★★★★☆ | The largest in mainland China, with a monthly production capacity of 1.05 million pieces |
| Customer switching cost | ★★★★☆ | Long tapeout verification cycle and high IP adaptation cost |
| Policy Barriers | ★★★★★ | "Passive Moat" under the Entity List |
| Capital barriers | ★★★★★ | New entrants require tens of billions of dollars in investment |

### 3.2 The particularity of the moat

SMIC's moat has a unique feature: it is a "moat created by sanctions".

- Looking on the positive side: Sanctions prevent Chinese customers from using TSMC’s advanced manufacturing processes and can only turn to SMIC
- On the flip side: Sanctions also restrict SMIC’s access to advanced equipment, preventing it from truly narrowing the gap with TSMC.
- Going deeper: If sanctions are relaxed in the future, this moat could quickly disappear

This is a **fragile moat** - one that relies on the continued deterioration of the external environment rather than the continued improvement of the company's own capabilities.

### 3.3 The gap with TSMC

| Dimension | TSMC | SMIC |
|------|--------|----------|
| The most advanced mass production process | 2nm (2025) | 7nm (DUV multi-exposure) |
| Global market share | 66-72% | 4.8% |
| 2025 Revenue | Approximately $90 billion | $9.3 billion |
| Gross profit margin | 55%+ | 21% |
| Return on Capital (ROIC) | 25%+ | <5% |

The gap is comprehensive and has a tendency to expand in advanced processes.

**Moat Rating: ★★★☆☆ (3/5)**

Reason: The moat exists but is not strong. Policy barriers are uncontrollable, and technical barriers are catching up but with a big gap. Scale and customer stickiness are the most reliable parts.

## 4. Business model sustainability

### 4.1 The core contradiction of the profit model

SMIC faces a fundamental problem: In order to maintain its strategic position, it must continue to invest huge amounts of money, but the return cycle of the investment is extremely long and full of uncertainty. **

2025 data:
-Capital expenditure: US$8.1 billion (approximately 59 billion yuan)
- Net profit: 5 billion yuan
-Capex is **nearly 12 times net profit**

This means that almost all of the company's cash flow is invested in expanding production, and shareholder returns are close to zero (no dividends will be paid in 2025).

### 4.2 Two sides of sustainability

**Optimistic scenario:**
- The production capacity value of mature processes is stable in the long term (the demand for IoT and automotive chips is highly certain)
- Gross profit margin is expected to increase after breakthroughs in advanced processes
- China’s semiconductor autonomy is an irreversible trend

**Pessimistic scenario:**
- The depreciation peak lasts for more than 5 years, and it is difficult to improve profit margins
- The cost of advanced process DUV solutions is too high and commercialization is difficult
- Mature manufacturing processes face price wars from competitors such as Huahong and Jinghe

### 4.3 Summary of business model

SMIC is more like a national infrastructure project rather than a commercial company in the traditional sense. Its mission is to "ensure the existence of China's chip manufacturing capabilities" rather than "create maximum returns for shareholders."

From the nature of the business, this is not an "easy to make money" business. It requires continuous huge investment, faces technological blockade, and the lead of competitors (TSMC) is still expanding.

**Business Model Sustainability Rating: ★★★☆☆ (3/5)**

As a national strategic asset, it is inevitable to exist; as an investment target, the "profit-making ability" of its business model is far from proven.
