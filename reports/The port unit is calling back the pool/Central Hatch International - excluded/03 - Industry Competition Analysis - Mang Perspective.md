# Semiconductor Manufacturing International Corporation (0981.HK) industry competition analysis

> Analysis dimensions: competitive landscape, industry chain positioning, multiple thinking models, reverse thinking
> Analysis date: May 16, 2026

---

## 1. Panorama of wafer foundry industry

### 1.1 Industry scale and growth

The global wafer foundry industry is expected to reach US$203.2 billion in 2026, a year-on-year increase of approximately 19%. Growth mainly comes from:
- Explosive demand for AI chips (advanced processes)
- Increased automotive electronics penetration rate (mature manufacturing process)
- IoT devices continue to grow (mature manufacturing processes)

### 1.2 Industry Concentration – Winner Takes All

| Ranking | Company | Market Share 2026 | Core Strengths |
|------|------|---------------|----------|
| 1 | TSMC | 72% | Advanced process monopoly |
| 2 | Samsung | 6.8% | Storage + OEM collaboration |
| 3 | SMIC | 4.8% | Chinese market + policy support |
| 4 | UMC | ~5% | Mature process efficiency |
| 5 | GLOBALFOUNDRIES | ~5% | Differentiated and featured craftsmanship |

**Key insights:** TSMC alone accounts for 72%, and its share is still expanding (66% in 2025 to 72% in 2026). This is an extremely concentrated industry, and its leading advantages continue to strengthen.

### 1.3 Economic characteristics of the industry

There are several cruel economic laws in this industry:

1. **The "Matthew Effect" of advanced manufacturing processes**: The leader gets the largest order, the largest order brings the highest yield, the highest yield brings the lowest cost, and the lowest cost attracts more customers - the positive cycle continues to strengthen
2. **The "arms race" of capital expenditure**: TSMC's capital expenditure in 2025 will exceed US$30 billion, and SMIC's US$8.1 billion. The gap is not narrowing, but widening
3. **The "homogenization trap" of mature processes**: There is a small technological gap between processes above 28nm, and competition will tend to be price wars.

## 2. In-depth analysis of competitive landscape

### 2.1 SMIC’s competitive positioning

SMIC is in a "squeezed from both ends" position:

- **Above (Advanced Process)**: TSMC and Samsung are far ahead, and SMIC cannot catch up due to equipment bans
- **Bottom (mature process)**: Domestic competitors such as Huahong and Jinghe are rapidly expanding production, and price competition is intensifying

SMIC's "comfort zone" is the "middle zone" of **14nm-28nm** - advanced enough to have certain technical barriers, but without the need for EUV equipment.

### 2.2 Comparison with major competitors

**vs TSMC (global dominant player):**
- Technology gap: about 5 generations (TSMC has mass-produced 2nm, SMIC is the most advanced 7nm)
- Scale gap: nearly 10 times difference in revenue
- Efficiency gap: gross profit margin 55% vs 21%
- Conclusion: SMIC cannot challenge TSMC in advanced processes if it is not in the same competitive dimension.

**vs Hua Hong Semiconductor (domestic rival):**
- Huahong focuses on unique processes (BCD, IGBT) and differentiated positioning
- Huahong’s gross profit margin is slightly lower (about 12%), but it is also rapidly expanding production
- SMIC has an advantage in process advancement (14nm vs Huahong 28nm)
- There is direct competition in mature processes (40nm-55nm)

**vs Global Mature Foundry (United Microelectronics, GlobalFoundries):**
- UMC announced that it will not catch up with advanced processes and focus on mature processes with high profit margins.
- GLOBALFOUNDRIES’ differentiated positioning and characteristic craftsmanship
- The ROE and profit margins of these companies are much better than SMIC
- Implications: Focus and restraint may be more beneficial than chasing

### 2.3 Mainland China’s production capacity pattern

Mainland China's wafer production capacity is expanding rapidly:
- In 2026, monthly production capacity is expected to exceed 4 million pieces (8-inch equivalent), accounting for 34.4% of the world's total
- Compound annual growth rate of 13.4%, the fastest in the world
- It is expected to account for 30% of global production capacity in 2030, surpassing Taiwan (23%)

**Hidden concerns: Risk of overcapacity**

A large amount of production capacity is put into production at the same time. If the demand cannot be matched, it will lead to:
- Decreased capacity utilization
- Price war intensifies
- Gross profit margin is further under pressure

## 3. Analysis of multiple thinking models

### 3.1 Supply and demand model

**Supply side:**
- SMIC will have a monthly production capacity of 1.059 million pieces by the end of 2025 and will continue to expand production in 2026
- 12-inch production capacity target 1.17 million pieces/month
- Rapid increase in production capacity across the industry

**Demand side:**
- Moderate recovery in consumer electronics
- Demand for AI inference chips is growing (but most require advanced manufacturing processes)
- The demand for automotive chips is highly certain but limited in magnitude

**Supply and demand judgment:** There is a risk of periodic surplus in mature processes. There is demand for advanced processes but SMIC cannot fully meet it.

### 3.2 Incentive mechanism model

Analyze the incentives of all parties:

- **Government**: Regardless of cost support, semiconductor autonomy is a national security issue
- **Management**: Salary is linked to performance, but more important is "sense of mission" and "political correctness"
- **Customer**: forced to use SMIC (due to sanctions), rather than actively choosing
- **Competitors**: TSMC does not need to pay attention to SMIC, UMC/GF withdraw from advanced process competition

**Key Insight:** When neither a company's largest backer (the government) nor its largest customer base (domestic design firms) are motivated by "profit maximization," the company's shareholder returns tend not to be a priority.

### 3.3 Economies of Scale and Anti-Economies of Scale

**Favourable economies of scale:**
- The greater the production capacity, the lower the fixed cost allocation
- The more customers there are, the faster the process iteration will be
- R&D investment can be reused across product lines

**Adverse scale effects (anti-scale):**
- Building factories in multiple locations increases management complexity
- Each new factory requires a long ramp-up period of production capacity
- Depreciation peaks are superimposed, and short-term profit margins continue to be under pressure

### 3.4 Path dependency model

SMIC has embarked on an irreversible path of production expansion:
- Already committed to multiple new factories in Shenzhen, Lingang, Beijing, Tianjin, etc.
- Equipment has been ordered and is being installed
- Government funds are already in place

Even if it finds that it is expanding production too quickly or that demand is insufficient, it will be difficult for the company to stop. This "path dependence" means that the depreciation pressure in the next 3-5 years is certain, but revenue growth is full of uncertainty.

## 4. Reverse thinking

### 4.1 Under what circumstances would SMIC be a good investment?

If the following conditions are met simultaneously:
1. China’s semiconductor demand continues to explode (AI, automobiles, and IoT are all growing)
2. Advanced process breakthroughs (the cost of 7nm/5nm DUV solutions has dropped significantly)
3. Capacity utilization remains above 90%
4. Gross profit margin restored to above 30%
5. The sanctions environment does not deteriorate further

**Probability assessment:** The probability of 1 and 4 being realized simultaneously is low. When demand breaks out, advanced processes are the main battlefield, and SMIC is limited in advanced processes. The growth in demand for mature processes cannot bring high gross profit margins.

### 4.2 Under what circumstances would SMIC be a disastrous investment?

1. Sanctions are further escalated and the supply of DUV equipment is cut off
2. Overcapacity in mature processes and price wars have caused gross profit margins to fall below 15%.
3. The yield and cost of advanced process DUV solutions have never been commercialized
4. Escalating geopolitical conflicts lead to market panic
5. Semiconductor industry cycle downturn coupled with depreciation peak

### 4.3 Historical Analogy

**Analogy 1: BOE (BOE)**
- It is also the "national team" in the panel industry and has suffered huge losses for many years while expanding production.
- Eventually became the world's largest panel manufacturer, but ROE has been 5-8% for a long time
- Optimistic expectations when the market capitalization has never reached "the world's first"
- Lesson: First in scale does not equal first in return on investment

**Analogy 2: CRRC (after merger)**
- Enjoy the highest valuation of "domestic substitution" during the merger
- Valuations then returned and stock prices remained depressed for a long period of time
- Lesson: The pinnacle of narrative is often the pinnacle of valuation

## 5. Industrial chain perspective

### 5.1 Upstream dependencies

SMIC’s upstream dependence is extremely high:
- Lithography machine: ASML (Netherlands), DUV equipment supply at risk
- Etching equipment: American Applied Materials, Lam Semiconductor (restricted)
- Silicon wafer: Japan's Shin-Etsu, SUMCO

The domestic substitution rate is currently about 25%, and the target needs to reach more than 50%. The progress of equipment localization will directly affect SMIC’s production expansion pace.

### 5.2 Downstream customer concentration

- The top five customers account for an estimated 40-50% of revenue
- Huawei’s ecological chain is an important customer group
- The risk of a single large customer exists

**Summary in one sentence:** Wafer foundry is a winner-takes-all industry, and TSMC’s advantages are still expanding. SMIC has an irreplaceable position in the Chinese market, but this position comes from "the closed market created by sanctions" rather than "winning in open competition." Judging from the rules of industry competition, a foundry that has no access to the most advanced equipment, a gross profit margin of only 21%, and is trapped in a mature process is difficult to become an excellent long-term investment—unless you believe that the "closed market" will exist permanently and the profit margins in the closed market can continue to improve.
