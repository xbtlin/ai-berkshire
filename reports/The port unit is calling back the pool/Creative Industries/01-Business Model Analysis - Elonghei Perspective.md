# Chuangke Industrial (0669.HK) business model analysis

> Analysis Dimension: Good Business Judgment | Research Date: May 16, 2026 | Currency: United States Dollar (USD), unless otherwise noted in Hong Kong Dollar (HKD)

---

## 1. Is this a good business?

### 1.1 The essence of business

The core business of Techtronic Industries (TTI) is the R&D, manufacturing and sales of global power tools and outdoor power equipment**. Its two core brands:

- **Milwaukee**: Positioned in the professional market, targeting professional users such as electricians, plumbers, mechanics, etc., revenue will increase by 7.9% (local currency) in 2025, contributing most of the profits of the power tool business
- **Ryobi**: Targeting the consumer market, selling through Home Depot’s exclusive channel, revenue growth of 5.4% in 2025 (local currency)

The full-year revenue in 2025 will be US$15.3 billion, of which the power tool business will contribute US$14.4 billion (accounting for 94%), and the floor care and cleaning business will contribute approximately US$900 million.

**Judgement criteria for good business**: Duan Yongping repeatedly emphasized that a good business must have "a brand that consumers are willing to pay a premium for" and "sustainable competitive advantage." Chuangke Industrial meets these two conditions - Milwaukee's brand loyalty among professional users is extremely high, and professional workers are far less sensitive to tool price than their requirements for reliability and efficiency. An electrician would not switch to an unfamiliar drill brand to save $50 because the time lost due to tool failure far outweighs the price difference of the tool itself.

### 1.2 "Simple" and "understandable" business model

The logic of this business is very clear:

1. **R&D of new products** (approximately 3.5% of revenue is invested in R&D each year)
2. **Pricing through brand premium** (Milwaukee product prices are usually 15-30% higher than competing products)
3. **Battery platform locks users** (more than 300 products on the three major platforms of M18/M12/MX FUEL)
4. **Continue to expand categories** to gain greater wallet share (expanding from electric drills to lighting, storage, measurement, and personal protective equipment)
5. **Scale effect** reduces procurement and manufacturing costs

This "blade + tool holder" model means that once a user buys the first product of a battery platform, the marginal decision-making cost of subsequent purchases of products on the same platform is extremely low. Milwaukee's M18 platform already has more than 300 tools, forming a strong ecological lock-in effect.

### 2.1 Cash flow quality

| Net cash position ($100 million) | — | — | 7.0 |

*Data source: TTI 2025 Annual Report, BusinessWire*

TTI has achieved free cash flow of more than US$1.2 billion for three consecutive years, and will be close to US$1.4 billion in 2025. This means that the cash generated by the company each year far exceeds the capital expenditures required to maintain and expand the business.

What's more, the company is in a **net cash position** -- $700 million in net cash at the end of 2025. For a manufacturing company with more than $15 billion in revenue, that means an extremely healthy balance sheet and little financial leverage risk.

### 2.2 Capital Allocation

Management shows discipline in capital allocation:

- **Dividend**: The full-year dividend for 2025 is HK$2.57 per share (HK$1.25 for the interim period + HK$1.32 for the final period), with a dividend rate of approximately 2.1%
- **Buyback**: The Board of Directors announced that it will repurchase up to $500 million in shares over the next 18 months.
- **R&D investment**: Continuous investment in core technologies (brushless motors, high energy density batteries, intelligent interconnection)
- **Capacity Construction**: Expanding factories in Vietnam and Mexico to cope with tariff risks

The priority of capital allocation is "R&D-driven growth > buyback > dividends", which is consistent with the long-term compound interest logic of good business.

## 3. Customer stickiness and user mentality

### 3.1 "Path dependency" for professional users

Milwaukee's competitive advantage is built on the work habits of professional users:

- **Battery platform lock**: If an electrician buys an M18 platform drill, chainsaw and light fixture, he is unlikely to buy DeWalt's 20V MAX platform for a new category - which means buying a whole new set of batteries and chargers
- **PACKOUT SYSTEM**: Milwaukee’s modular tool storage system has become standard on construction sites, further enhancing ecological stickiness
- **One-Key Intelligent Platform**: More than 100 tools support Bluetooth connection, which can track tool position, set torque parameters, perform predictive maintenance, and form data-level locking

### 3.2 Channel locking

- Milwaukee: Sales through a network of professional dealers, establishing deep channel relationships
- Ryobi: Exclusive partnership with Home Depot. Home Depot is the world's largest home building materials retailer. This exclusive relationship is both a moat and a risk point - if the relationship breaks down, the impact will be huge, but because Ryobi contributes important sales to Home Depot's power tool category, the interests of both parties are highly bound.

### 3.3 Brand Mind

In the North American professional power tool market, brand choice is almost a "professional identity". Milwaukee's red has become as iconic to professional electricians and plumbers as Caterpillar's yellow is to heavy machinery. Once this kind of brand mentality is formed, it is difficult for competitors to subvert it through simple price wars or feature catch-up.

## 4. Quantitative verification of "good business"

### 4.1 Gross profit margin continues to increase

| Year | Gross profit margin |
|------|--------|
| 2015 | 35.7% |
| 2018 | ~37% |
| 2020 | ~38% |
| 2022 | ~38.5% |
| 2024 | 40.3% |
| 2025 | 41.2% |

*Data sources: TTI annual reports, Huatai Securities research reports*

Gross profit margin has steadily increased from 35.7% in 2015 to 41.2% in 2025, an increase of 550 basis points in 10 years. This is not a cyclical fluctuation, but a structural trend - reflecting the continued strengthening of Milwaukee's brand power, the tilt of the product mix towards the high end, and the optimization of procurement costs brought about by economies of scale.

### 4.2 ROE continues to be high

ROE remains at about 19-20% (about 19.2% in 2025), and the company uses almost no financial leverage. A high ROE achieved purely by operating efficiency is of much higher quality than an ROE leveraged by high debt.

## 5. Potential concerns

Even if it’s a good business, you have to see the other side:

1. **Organic electrification penetration has entered the second half**: The dividend from wired to wireless conversion is weakening, and future growth will rely more on category expansion and share grabbing.
2. **High dependence on the North American market**: North America contributes about 75% of revenue. Once the U.S. economy declines or the housing market drops sharply, the impact will be significant.
3. **Tariff risks materialize**: Milwaukee has suspended the sales and promotions of some products due to tariff issues in the second half of 2025.
4. **Ryobi’s exclusive reliance on Home Depot**: Excessive channel concentration is a double-edged sword

---

## 6. Conclusion

**Good Business Rating: ★★★★☆ (4/5)**

Chuangke Industrial is a typical good business: strong brand, high customer stickiness, abundant free cash flow, and healthy balance sheet. Milwaukee's "battery platform ecosystem" is the most ingenious design of this business - it turns a one-time purchase into an ecological relationship of continuous repurchase. It is similar to Apple's iOS ecosystem. Once you enter, it is difficult to leave.

Reasons for deducting one star: (1) The high concentration of the North American market brings cyclical risks; (2) Tariff uncertainty may suppress profit margins in stages. But in the long term, these are "challenges that a good business faces in a specific period" rather than "structural flaws in the business itself."
