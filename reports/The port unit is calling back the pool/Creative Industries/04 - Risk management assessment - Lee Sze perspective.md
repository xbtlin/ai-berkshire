# I. Management assessment

# 1.1 Founder: Horst Julius Pudwill

The creative business was founded in Hong Kong in 1985 by Horst Julius Pudwill, a German businessman. Pudwill, a sales representative of the popular car in Asia, had a great deal of experience in international trade and operations. He led the company from the original OEM/ODM plant to a global power tool leader with an autonomous brand — a rare transformation path that has enabled the world to successfully complete the "forework-to-brand" leap.

** Key contribution of the founder**:
- Acquisition of Milwaukee and Ryobi brands in the early 2000s, laying the strategic foundation for a two-brand matrix
- Stick to brand plus technology, not rely on low-cost manufacturing.
- Maintain financial discipline and maintain healthy balance sheets even during high-speed expansion

** Current role**: Pudwill is still the company's main shareholder (directly holding 8.02%, controlled by Sunning Inc., 9.76%, totalling 17.78%), but no longer holds a day-to-day management position. The presence of the founder provides an "a anchoring effect" for the company's culture and strategic direction.

*Data source: TTI Investor Relations Page, Investment.com*

##1.2 Former CEO: Joseph Galli Jr. (2006-2024)

Galli, 18 years in office (16 of which was CEO), was the most important career manager in the company's history. He worked 19 years in Black & Decker, and was President of the Electric Tools and Parts Department, successfully launching the DeWalt Heavy Power Tool brand — ironically, he later beat Milwaukee at TTI to create DeWauke's strongest rival.

** Achievements of the Galli era**:
- Revenue growth from about $3 billion to over $14.6 billion
- Milwaukee has grown from a second-line brand to a first brand of a global professional electric tool
- Māori ratio increased from 30% to over 40%
- The creation of a globalized supply chain and manufacturing system

** Separation**: decision to leave in May 2024 due to individual retirement.

#1.3 Present CEO: Steven Richman (May 2024)

Richman has 35 years or more of experience in the industry of electrical tools and outdoor power equipment, and is a manager of the deep-farming industry, electrical, water heating and retail distribution channels. He is a manager of the promotions within TTI and has long been responsible for the development of the Milwaukee brand in North America before joining the TTI team.

** Preliminary performance assessment**:
- in 2025, Richman was the first full financial year of CEO, and the company handed over the report cards for revenue + 4.4%, profit + 6.8%
- Successfully advanced transfer of manufacturing capacity from China to Viet Nam and Mexico to meet tariff challenges
- The announcement of the first large-scale buy-back (18 months, $500 million) showing a positive attitude towards capital allocation
- Milwaukee grew by 7.9 per cent in 2025 (local currency) and continued to win the industry

** Uncertainty**:
- Richman has been in office for less than two years and long-term strategic implementation capacity has not been tested for full economic cycle
- Whether he has the same product instinct and branding as Galli is building up, still to be seen.
- The CEO transition period is the key window for corporate governance - a number of good firms have been strategically moving since they changed.

*Source: TTI officer network Ore Board page, Bloomberg, Crunchbase*

# Three, risk list

#3.1 Tariffs and trade war risks (high risk)

** This is the greatest known risk at present.**

- **Status**: TTI has completed its plan to transfer products to the United States market from Chinese factories to local factories in Viet Nam, Mexico and the United States, but CEO made it clear at the 2025 press release that "the tariff situation is still changing and we are closely monitoring it."
- ** Impact of substantiveization**: Milwaukee suspended sales and promotions of products that were partially heavily affected by tariffs in late 2025, directly delaying the pace of North America ' s growth
- ** Advance stock accumulation**: US stock increased from $4.03 billion to $4.29 billion at the mid-year report of the company in 2025, partly to import earlier to avoid customs duties
- **Assessment**: Energy transfer can mitigate but not completely eliminate tariff shocks. TTI will face a new challenge if the US imposes additional tariffs on Vietnam or Mexico.

#3.2 US Housing Market Cycle Risk (Middle and High Risk)

- North America contributes about 75% of the TTI revenues.
- Electrical tool requirements are highly correlated with the start of new houses and renovation of houses
- In a high interest rate environment, the United States housing market is being held back
- Demand may fall significantly if interest rates remain high for long periods or the economy is in recession

#3.3 CEO handover risk (moderate risk)

- Steven Richman has been in office for less than two years and has not been through a full down-cycle.
- The 16-year term of the former CEO Gali has created a very strong personal stamp, and the new CEO needs time to build up his own management style
- Key observation indicator: whether Milwaukee can maintain the momentum of double-digit growth under Richman

#3.4 Single market concentration risk (moderate risk)

- North American markets account for about 75 per cent of the total, while Europe and Asia-Pacific markets continue to grow but to account for a low share
- The company has clearly proposed a strategy for "accelerating European and Asia-Pacific expansion", which will take time to move forward through local product planning and new distribution centres

#3.5 Home Depot channel is risk dependent (moderate risk)

- Ryobi brand works with Home Depot's exclusives.
- If Home Depot adjusts branding strategies or introduces competitions, Ryobe will be hit
- But the interests of both sides are highly bound and the probability of a short-term break-up is extremely low.

#3.6 Raw materials and battery supply risk (low risk)

- Lithium cell core is the core raw material.
- TTI procurement is large and has a bargaining advantage in the global core supply chain
- In extreme cases, however, the cost of lithium mining may still be soaring (e.g., as a result of a geographical conflict that has disrupted the supply of the mine)

#3.7 Risk of technological subversion (low risk)

- New technologies like solid-state batteries may change battery ecology, but all major players are following up.
- The possibility of cross-border players (e.g., Big Auto-Tel Bialdi) entering electric tools is not high - technical barriers to electric tools are not only in batteries, but also in electrical control, mechanical design and branding channels

# 4.3 Survival in the worst scenario

Even in the worst-case scenario, TTI has:
- ** Net cash $700 million** security advance without maturity pressure
- **Free cash flow of $500-7 billion per year** sufficient to sustain operations and basic R & D
- ** Brand assets and customer base** will not disappear due to short-term shocks — professional users in Milwaukee will not change brands because of a recession
- ** Globalized production and distribution** provides flexibility for adjustment

** Conclusion**: TTI does not "down" in the worst case scenario, but stock prices may be withdrawn to HK$80-90. This is exactly the ideal buy-in for long-term investors.

# Six, conclusions

** Risk management rating: <unk> (4/5)**

** Management evaluation**:

The founder Pudwill's "re-engineering" strategy is excellent, with the former CEO Gali's 16-year implementation of a textbook level. The new CEO Richman's initial performance is reassuring – still giving up good report cards in the face of the customs wind and offering to promote the transfer of capacity and the first buy-back.

**Risk evaluation**:

Tariff risks are the greatest recent uncertainty, but companies have responded positively. The housing market cycle in the United States is a medium-term concern. In the long run, brand power, platform locking, and industry structures provide solid downside protection.

** Reason for withholding a star**: (1) Uncertainty in the CEO's handover - History has proved on numerous occasions that the change of CEOs in good firms is the most problematic moment; (2) Unpredictability of tariff situations is beyond the control of companies.
