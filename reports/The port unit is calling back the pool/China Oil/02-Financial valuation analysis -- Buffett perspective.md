# CNOOC (0883.HK) Financial and Valuation Analysis

> Analysis dimensions: earnings quality, balance sheet, cash flow, valuation safety margin
> Data as of: May 2026 | Based on 2025 annual report

## 1. Profitability: Testing true strength amid falling oil prices

### 1.1 Key data of income statement (2025)

| Indicators | 2025 | 2024 | Year-on-year change |
|------|--------|--------|----------|
| Oil and gas sales revenue | ~398.2 billion yuan | ~420.5 billion yuan | -5.3% |
| Net profit attributable to parent company | 122.1 billion yuan | 138 billion yuan | -11.5% |
| Average realized price of barrel of oil | ~$68 | ~$79 | -13.9% |
| Barrel of oil primary cost | $27.9 | $28.5 | -2.2% |
| Net production (million barrels) | 777.3 | 726.6 | +7.0% |

**Core Observations:**

**(a) Resilience test of "volume increases and price decreases". ** 2025 is a very good "stress test" year: the average price of Brent crude oil fell by about 15%, but CNOOC's net profit fell by only 11.5%. This was helped by two hedging factors - a 7% increase in production and a 2.2% decrease in costs.

**(b) Margins remain strong. ** Even in years when oil prices fell, the net profit margin was still about 30%, and the profit margin per barrel of oil was about $40 (realized price of $68 - full cost of about $28). This "safety cushion" means that oil prices need to fall below $30 to threaten the company's profitability.

**(c) Changes in income structure. ** Natural gas production increased 11.6% year over year, surpassing 1 trillion cubic feet for the first time. The increase in the proportion of natural gas will help reduce the company's dependence on a single oil price, and the long-term demand prospects for natural gas as a "transitional energy" will be more certain.

### 1.2 Earnings Quality Assessment

**★★★★☆（4/5）**

The profit content is high - operating cash flow is 209 billion yuan, far exceeding the net profit of 122.1 billion yuan, indicating that almost all profits are recovered in the form of cash, and there is no "paper profit" problem. However, attention should be paid to: whether the provision for depreciation and amortization is sufficient (the depreciation policy of oil and gas assets directly affects the income statement), and whether the impairment provision is conservative enough.

## 2. Balance sheet: "clean" sample among central enterprises

### 2.1 Core indicators

| Indicators | Values | Evaluation |
|------|------|------|
| Asset-liability ratio | 26.7% | Extremely low, far lower than the industry average of 40-50% |
| Net debt ratio | Close to net cash | Abundant cash on hand |
| Interest-bearing liabilities to total assets | <15% | Almost no pressure on financial leverage |

### 2.2 Asset Structure Analysis

CNOOC's asset structure is very "clean":

**Left side (asset side):** Mainly oil and gas assets (PP&E) and construction in progress related to exploration and development. There is less goodwill (the goodwill brought by the Nexen acquisition has been significantly impaired).

**Right side (liability side):** Interest-bearing liabilities are extremely low, mainly operating liabilities such as accounts payable. This is a large state-owned enterprise that barely relies on borrowing money to do business - very rare among Chinese listed companies.

**Key Questions from Buffett's Perspective:** Low debt is a good thing in itself, but it may also indicate that capital is not efficient enough - is the company leaving too much cash on hand without returning shareholders more efficiently?

### 2.3 ROE analysis

The median ROE in the past five years is about 19%, and it will be about 16-17% in 2025 (affected by the decline in oil prices).

ROE dismantling (DuPont analysis framework):
- **Net profit margin:** ~30%, very high, mainly due to low cost and pure upstream positioning
- **Asset turnover rate:** ~0.45, medium to low, characteristics of asset-heavy industries
- **Equity Multiplier:** ~1.36, extremely low leverage

**Key findings:** CNOOC's ROE is mainly driven by "high profit margins" rather than leverage. This is the healthiest source of ROE. Even if falling oil prices lead to profit margin compression, low leverage means the company will not have a debt repayment crisis.

**ROE evaluation: ★★★★☆ (4/5)**

An ROE of 16-19% is excellent in cyclical industries. The main risk is that a long-term downturn in oil prices will drag ROE to 12% or even below.

## 3. Cash flow: This is the most beautiful thing about this business

### 3.1 Key cash flow data

| Indicators | 2025 | 2024 |
|------|--------|--------|
| Operating cash flow | ~209 billion yuan | ~220.9 billion yuan |
| Capital expenditure | RMB 120.5 billion | RMB 132.5 billion |
| Free cash flow | ~88.5 billion yuan | ~97.5 billion yuan |
| Dividend payment | ~57 billion yuan | ~61.7 billion yuan |

**Core Observations:**

**(a) Operating cash flow far exceeds net profit. ** Operating cash flow of 209 billion vs. net profit of 122.1 billion, a ratio of approximately 1.7 times. This shows that the company has a large number of non-cash expenses (depreciation and amortization) and the quality of profits is extremely high.

**(b) Free cash flow more than covers dividends. ** Free cash flow is about 88.5 billion, dividend payment is about 57 billion, and the coverage multiple is about 1.55 times. Even if oil prices drop another 20%, the company can still maintain its current dividend level.

**(c) Capital spending begins to "peak." **Capital expenditure in 2025 will be 120.5 billion, a decrease of 12 billion from 2024; the 2026 budget will further drop to 112-122 billion. This implies that the investment peak of "increasing reserves and production" may have passed, and free cash flow is expected to improve in the future.

**Cash flow assessment: ★★★★★ (5/5)**

This is the biggest financial highlight of CNOOC. Operating cash flow is abundant, free cash flow is substantial, and dividend coverage is sufficient. For a resource-based company, cash flow quality is the core financial indicator, and CNOOC has nearly perfect scores in this dimension.

## 4. Valuation analysis: Does the current price have a margin of safety?

### 4.1 Current Valuation Level (May 2026)

| Valuation Metrics | Current Value | Evaluation |
|------|------|------|
| PE-TTM | ~9.3x | Low, 12-15x lower than the median of global oil and gas giants |
| PB | ~1.4x | Reasonable, reflecting 16-17% ROE |
| Dividend yield | ~4.8% (static) | Higher than the Hong Kong stock market average |
| EV/EBITDA | ~3.5-4x | Low among global peers |

### 4.2 Historical valuation range

The historical PE center of CNOOC Hong Kong stocks is approximately between 6-12 times. The current 9.3 times is in the middle and low position.

It is important to note that low PE does not necessarily mean "cheap":
- If oil prices fall further, net profit may drop to 100-110 billion yuan in 2026, and the corresponding PE will rise to 10-11 times
- On the contrary, if oil prices rebound to above US$80, net profit may return to 130-140 billion yuan, and PE will drop to 7-8 times

**CNOOC’s valuation is in the middle of the pack among global peers. ** Compared with international oil and gas giants, there is no obvious discount (considering that Hong Kong stocks themselves have liquidity discounts). But if you take into account its higher ROE and faster production growth, it can be considered that the valuation is reasonably low.

### 4.5 Safety margin judgment

**Optimistic Scenario (Oil Price Center $70+):** Current valuations provide a margin of safety of approximately 20-30%. With an annual output growth of 5-7% and a dividend of 4-5%, investors can obtain an annualized return of approximately 10-12%.

**Neutral scenario (oil price center at $60):** Limited margin of safety. Dividends can still be maintained, but there is less room for capital appreciation, and the annualized return may be around 6-8%.

**Pessimistic scenario (oil prices below $50):** There is little margin of safety at current prices. Profits and dividends will shrink significantly, but the company will not lose money due to its low cost advantage.

**Valuation safety margin assessment: ★★★☆☆ (3/5)**

The current valuation is not expensive, but it cannot be said to be "extremely undervalued." The size of the margin of safety depends entirely on your judgment of oil prices. This is the essential difficulty of investing in cyclical stocks - you cannot determine the margin of safety through static valuation because the numerator (profit) is highly uncertain.

For value investors, the key "hitting zone" is: **When oil prices are at cycle lows and the market is extremely pessimistic, CNOOC's low-cost advantage ensures that it is still profitable, and the safety margin of intervention is the largest at this time. ** The current oil price is in the middle area of ​​60-70 US dollars, which is neither the best buying point nor a dangerous area.
