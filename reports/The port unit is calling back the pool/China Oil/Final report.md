## 3. Core investment arguments

### Three reasons to go long

**1. The cost-effective safety mat is the best in the world. ** A barrel of oil costs $27.9, which means that even if Brent falls to $40, there is still a profit of $12 per barrel. Under the pessimistic oil price assumption of US$50-60, the company can still maintain tens of billions of annual profits and considerable dividends.

**2. The two-wheel drive of "volume increase + dividend". ** In the past five years, the compound growth rate of output has been approximately 6%, and the dividend payout ratio has increased from 30% to 45%. If the production growth rate is maintained at 3-5% in the next 3-5 years and the oil price is centered at 60-70 US dollars, investors can obtain approximately 4-5% dividends + 3-5% intrinsic value growth, with an annualized return of 8-10%.

**3. The valuation already implies pessimistic expectations. ** With a PE of less than 10 times, a PB of about 1.4 times, and a dividend yield of nearly 5%, the market has given a significant discount to energy transition and oil price uncertainty. Downside is limited.

### Three reasons to be short/cautious

**1. Oil prices are unpredictable and fatal. ** If the average Brent price falls below US$50 in the next 2-3 years (global economic recession + OPEC production increase), net profit may be halved to 60-70 billion yuan, and the current valuation is not cheap.

**2. Growth may slow down. ** The capital expenditure budget for 2026 has begun to decline, the "Seven-year Action Plan to Increase Reserves and Production" is coming to an end, and the high growth stage of 5-7% may come to an end. If output growth drops to 2-3%, the "growth premium" will disappear.

**3. Uncertainty about management change. ** The new chairman Zhang Chuanjiang came from a central power enterprise. Although he has an early petrochemical background, it will take time to prove whether he can continue his predecessor's operating efficiency and shareholder return strategy.

## 5. Judgment of investment suitability

**Suitable for what type of investors:**
- Long-term investors looking for high dividend income
- Able to accept profit and valuation fluctuations caused by oil price fluctuations
- Treat it as a defensive, income-earning position in the portfolio (rather than a core growth position)

**Not suitable for what type of investors:**
- Investors pursuing deterministic compound interest growth (oil price fluctuations break the compound interest curve)
- Investors with an extremely pessimistic view on the energy transition
- Investors who cannot bear a 20-30% stock price drawdown

## 6. Key indicators that need to be continuously tracked

1. **Brent crude oil price** — the most core single variable, directly determines the profit level
2. **Change trend of barrel oil cost** — If the cost starts to trend upward, it means that the quality of resources is declining.
3. **Quarterly/Annual Production Growth** — Is the growth achieved as expected?
4. **Strategic signals from new management** — Will Zhang Chuanjiang change the dividend policy, capital expenditure pace or strategic direction?
5. **Dividend ratio policy** — whether the 45% bottom line is maintained or further improved
6. **Changes in the proportion of natural gas** — An increase in the proportion of natural gas will help reduce cyclicality
7. **Geopolitical Updates** — South China Sea situation, US sanctions risk

---

## 7. Final judgment

CNOOC is a "good company on an uncertain track." It has almost done the best it can—the lowest costs in the world, the fastest growth, the cleanest balance sheet, and increasing dividends year by year. But as an oil company, its fate ultimately hangs on the price of oil.

**At the current valuation level (PE about 9 times, dividend yield about 5%), CNOOC provides an "asymmetric" risk-return structure: cost safety cushion and dividend protection on the downside, and production growth and oil price elasticity on the upside. ** This structure is suitable as a "defense + interest income" position in a value investment portfolio, and the allocation ratio is recommended to be controlled at 5-10% of the portfolio.

What needs to be emphasized is that this is not a "buy and hold forever" target, but a cyclical value target that requires "continuous tracking of oil prices and operating data, increasing positions when they are extremely undervalued, and reducing positions when they are extremely optimistic." Discipline is more important than belief.
