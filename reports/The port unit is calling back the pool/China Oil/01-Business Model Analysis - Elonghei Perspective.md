# Analysis of China National Offshore Oil Corporation (0883.HK) business model

> Analysis dimensions: business essence, user value, moat, business logic
> Data as of: May 2026 | Based on 2025 annual report

---

## 1. What kind of business is this?

CNOOC's core business can be summarized in one sentence: find oil and natural gas in China and global waters, extract it, and then sell it. **

This is an "upstream pure" business. Different from the "exploration-refining-sales" entire industry chain of PetroChina and Sinopec, the listed company of CNOOC is almost exclusively engaged in exploration and production (E&P) and does not engage in refining or gas stations. This positioning determines several key characteristics:

**1. Income is directly linked to oil prices. ** In 2025, the average Brent price will fall by about 15% year-on-year, the company's revenue will fall by 5.3%, and net profit will fall by 11.5%. Oil prices are the biggest "uncontrollable variable" in this business.

**2. No product differentiation. ** Crude oil and natural gas are standardized commodities, there is no brand premium, and there is no "pricing power". You can't make customers pay a penny more for CNOOC's crude oil.

**3. Asset-heavy, long-term. ** It usually takes 5-8 years for an offshore oil field to be discovered and put into production. The capital expenditure in 2025 is 120.5 billion yuan, and the budget in 2026 is 112-122 billion yuan. This means that a large amount of money has to be "buried" in advance, and the payback period is very long.

**From the perspective of business attributes, this is not the kind of "differentiated consumer goods" business that Duan Yongping likes. ** It has no brand, no pricing power, and cannot "make its products the best to make users scream." But that doesn’t mean it can’t be a good investment – ​​the key is: can the cost advantage be sustained? Does management honestly reward shareholders? **

## 2. Moat analysis: Cost is the core competitiveness of this business

### 2.1 Cost of barrel of oil: lowest quartile in the world

The main cost of a barrel of oil in 2025 is US$27.9, a year-on-year decrease of 2.2%. This figure is in the lowest quartile among the world's major publicly traded oil and gas companies. Comparison reference:

- CNOOC: US$27.9/barrel
- Global peer median: approximately US$38-42/barrel
- North American shale oil companies: US$35-50/barrel

**Where do the costs come from? **

**(a) Natural endowments of offshore oil fields. ** The single well output of offshore oil fields is usually much higher than that of onshore oil fields. The large-scale integrated oil fields in Chinese waters, especially the Bohai Sea and the South China Sea, have high development efficiency and naturally low unit costs.

**(b) Continued amplification of scale effects. ** Net production in 2025 will be 777.3 million barrels of oil equivalent, a year-on-year increase of 7%, with daily production exceeding 2.13 million barrels for the first time. The greater the output, the greater the dilution of fixed costs.

**(c) Ongoing cost control culture. ** The company's barrel oil cost continued to decline from US$45 in 2013 to US$27.9 in 2025, a 38% drop in 12 years. This is not the luck of one or two years, but the result of systematic management.

**Cost Moat Assessment: ★★★★☆ (4/5)**

The cost advantage is real and sustainable, but it should be noted that as the "Seven-Year Action Plan to Increase Reserves and Production" advances, subsequent development will inevitably shift to deeper waters and more complex blocks, and there will be upward pressure on marginal costs.

### 2.2 Resource endowment: monopoly in China’s waters

CNOOC is the only large-scale operator of oil and gas exploration and development in Chinese waters. This is a monopoly determined by law and licenses. China's maritime area is about 3 million square kilometers, equivalent to one-third of its land area.

Key figures for 2025:
- Net proven reserves are 7.77 billion barrels of oil equivalent, a year-on-year increase of 6.9%
- Reserve life is maintained for more than 10 years
- 6 new discoveries were made throughout the year, and 28 hydrocarbon-bearing structures were successfully evaluated.
- Natural gas production exceeded 1 trillion cubic feet for the first time, an increase of 11.6% year-on-year

**Resource Moat Assessment: ★★★★★ (5/5)**

The monopoly of offshore resources is CNOOC’s deepest moat. As long as China continues to promote its energy security strategy and "increase reserves and production" policy, CNOOC will be the most direct beneficiary.

### 2.3 Technical Capabilities: Deepwater Development

"Deep Sea No. 1" (Lingshui 17-2 Gas Field) is China's first self-operated ultra-deep water gas field, with a water depth of more than 1,500 meters. Deepwater technology barriers are extremely high, and there are no more than 10 companies in the world that can independently operate deepwater projects.

**Technical moat assessment: ★★★☆☆ (3/5)**

Deepwater technology is improving, but there is still a gap compared with international giants (Shell, BP, Total). Technical capabilities are more like "necessary conditions" than "sufficient barriers".

## 3. The core contradiction of business logic

### 3.1 The seesaw of "increment" and "price"

CNOOC's growth logic is very clear: annual production growth is 5-7%, and "price fluctuations" are hedged through "volume growth". 2025 is a typical case: oil prices fell by 15%, but production increased by 7%, and profits only fell by 11.5% instead of more than 15%.

But the question is: Can the growth in volume be sustainable? ** The capital expenditure budget for 2026 has begun to decline (112-122 billion yuan vs. 120.5 billion yuan in 2025), which implies that the company itself is aware that the marginal difficulty of maintaining high growth is increasing.

### 3.2 Return to shareholders: from "growing bigger" to "spending money"

This is the biggest positive change of CNOOC in recent years:

- The bottom line of dividend payout ratio is increased from 40% to 45%
- Full-year dividend for 2025 of HK$1.28 per share (tax included)
- Operating cash flow is 209 billion yuan, with abundant free cash flow
- The asset-liability ratio is only 26.7%, with almost no financial leverage pressure

**Shareholder Return Assessment: ★★★★☆ (4/5)**

The dividend payout ratio of 45% is already at an upper-middle level among central enterprises. However, as a state-owned enterprise, its repurchase efforts are limited, and it is worth paying attention to whether the dividend space will be squeezed due to policy requirements to increase new energy investment in the future.

## 4. Looking at CNOOC from the perspective of "doing the right thing"

Duan Yongping emphasized that "doing the right thing" is more important than "doing things right". For CNOOC:

**What you did right:**
- Adhere to the upstream E&P positioning and do not engage in diversified expansion
- Continuing to reduce costs and increase efficiency, the cost of barrel of oil dropped by 38% in 2012
- Increase dividend payout ratio and reward shareholders
- Domestic "increasing reserves and production" contributes to energy security

**Probably not done right:**
- As a state-owned enterprise, it has a history of premium acquisitions in overseas asset allocation (such as the US$15.1 billion acquisition of Nexen in 2013, which is still controversial)
- The pace of new energy transformation is slow and the path to carbon neutrality is not clear yet
- There are frequent management changes (the chairman will just change in 2025), and the continuity of the long-term strategy is in doubt

## 5. Comprehensive assessment of business model

| Dimensions | Rating | Description |
|------|------|------|
| Nature of business | ★★★☆☆ | Bulk commodities, no pricing power, strong cycle |
| Cost moat | ★★★★☆ | The lowest quartile in the world, continuous optimization |
| Resource moat | ★★★★★ | China’s maritime monopoly |
| Technology moat | ★★★☆☆ | Making progress but not yet leading the world |
| Shareholders’ willingness to return | ★★★★☆ | The dividend payout ratio increases, but the nature of state-owned enterprises restricts buybacks |
| Long-term certainty | ★★★☆☆ | Oil prices are uncontrollable and energy transition is a long-term variable |

**Business model overall rating: ★★★★☆ (3.5/5)**

CNOOC is not the ideal "perfect business" - it lacks differentiation and pricing power. But in the field of commodities, it has almost done the best it can: the lowest costs in the world, monopoly resources, continued growth in output, and increasingly generous dividends. If you accept the premise that "this is a cyclical resources business", then CNOOC is one of the highest quality targets in this category.

The core problem is not the company itself, but your judgment on the long-term trend of oil prices. If the oil price remains above US$60 in the next 5-10 years, CNOOC's business model will continue to generate considerable free cash flow. If oil prices fall below US$50 for a long time, even at a cost of US$27.9, profit margins will be significantly compressed.
