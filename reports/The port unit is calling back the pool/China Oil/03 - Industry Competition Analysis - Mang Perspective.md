# China National Offshore Oil (0883.HK) Industry and Competition Analysis

> Analysis dimensions: industrial structure, sustainability of competitive advantages, industry trends, reverse thinking
> Data as of: May 2026 | Based on 2025 annual report and industry data

## 1. Industrial structure: first figure out "who is on the poker table"

### 1.1 Basic pattern of global oil and gas extraction industry

The global oil and gas upstream industry is roughly divided into four types of players:

| Category | Representative Enterprise | Characteristics |
|------|----------|------|
| International super giants | ExxonMobil, Shell, Total, BP, Chevron | Whole industry chain, global layout, market-oriented decision-making |
| National oil companies | Saudi Aramco, Abu Dhabi Petroleum, Petrobras | Resource monopoly, huge output, greatly affected by government policies |
| China's "Three Barrels of Oil" | PetroChina, Sinopec, CNOOC | Background of central enterprises, taking into account business and policy objectives |
| Independent E&P company | Pioneer Natural Resources, Devon Energy, Hayes, etc. | Purely upstream, focusing on specific areas |

What is unique about CNOOC is that it is the listed entity of a national oil company, but its business model is more like an international independent E&P company - it focuses on the upstream and does not engage in downstream refining and retail. ** This combination of "state-owned enterprise status + pure E&P positioning" is almost impossible to find anywhere else in the world.

### 1.2 Competitive landscape of China’s oil and gas industry

"Three barrels of oil" form an oligopoly in China's oil and gas market:

| Indicators | PetroChina | Sinopec | CNOOC |
|------|--------|--------|--------|
| Net profit attributable to parent company in 2024 | 164.7 billion | 50.3 billion | 138 billion |
| Crude oil self-sufficiency rate | ~70% | <30% | >100% |
| Main areas | Onshore oil and gas + refining | Refining + retail | Offshore oil and gas |
| Net profit growth rate in 2025 | To be determined | To be determined | -11.5% |

**Key differences:** CNOOC is the only "pure upstream" listed entity, producing entirely its own crude oil. PetroChina and Sinopec have invested a lot of resources in refining and sales, and their profit sources are more dispersed but also more complex.

## 2. Sustainability of competitive advantage: test with "reverse checklist"

The core of Munger's methodology is "thinking the other way around" - instead of asking "why CNOOC is good", ask "under what circumstances will CNOOC's advantages disappear".

### 2.1 Will the maritime monopoly be broken?

**Current reality:** CNOOC is the de facto monopoly of oil and gas exploration and development in Chinese waters. This status is established by regulations such as the Regulations of the People's Republic of China on Foreign Cooperation in the Exploitation of Offshore Petroleum Resources.

**Possible threats:**
- Policy level: Oil and gas system reform may introduce more competition. However, judging from the current trend, the reform direction is "pipeline network independence" rather than "opening upstream", and the maritime monopoly will not be broken in the short term.
- Technical level: Onshore oil and gas companies (PetroChina) are also trying to enter the offshore industry, but the technical barriers and professional equipment requirements for offshore operations are extremely high, making it difficult to form effective competition in the short term.

**Conclusion: The monopoly position will be stable within 5-10 years. ★★★★★**

### 2.2 Will the cost advantage be eroded?

**Current reality:** The cost of a barrel of oil is US$27.9, the lowest quartile in the world.

**Possible threats:**
- Deepwater trend: As high-quality shallow water areas are gradually developed, companies must move to deep water and ultra-deep water. Deepwater development costs are significantly higher than shallow water (often 50-100% higher).
- Decline of old oil fields: The production of old oil fields in mature areas such as the Bohai Sea will naturally decrease, requiring continuous injection of funds to maintain production, and unit costs may rise.
- Labor and material cost inflation: The long-term trend of steel, equipment, offshore labor and other costs is rising.

**Conclusion: The cost advantage is stable in the short term (3-5 years), but there is upward pressure in the long term. ★★★★☆**

### 2.3 Can production growth be sustained?

**Current reality:** Production will increase by 7% year-on-year in 2025, with daily production exceeding 2.13 million barrels for the first time.

**Possible threats:**
- A decrease in the capital expenditure budget in 2026 (112-122 billion vs 120.5 billion in 2025), suggesting that the pace of growth may slow down.
- Global offshore oil and gas exploration investment is expected to grow by only 3%, and the period of substantial expansion may have passed.
- The "Seven-year Action Plan to Increase Reserves and Increase Production" is coming to an end. What will be the growth engine in the next stage?

**Conclusion: High growth of 5-7% may drop to moderate growth of 3-4%. ★★★☆☆**

## 3. Industry trends: three key variables

### 3.1 The long-term trend of the oil price center

This is the single variable that has the greatest impact on CNOOC’s investment value.

**The logic of bullish oil prices:**
- OPEC+ still has the ability to adjust production cuts
- Global upstream investment has been insufficient for nearly 10 years, and the supply side lacks elasticity.
- Geopolitical risk premium (Middle East, Russia) persists
- Growing energy demand in emerging countries (India, Southeast Asia)

**The logic of bearish oil prices:**
- Slowing global economic growth (expected to be about 3.0% in 2026)
- The penetration of electric vehicles is accelerating, and oil demand in OECD countries has reached its peak
- There is still room for release of U.S. shale oil production capacity
- OPEC+ internal cohesion has declined and production has begun to increase in 2025

**Neutral judgment:** In the next 3-5 years, the Brent oil price center is likely to fluctuate in the range of 55-75 US dollars. CNOOC can maintain good profitability within this range, but it will be difficult to return to the level of huge profits when oil prices exceed US$100 in 2022.

### 3.2 Structural opportunities for natural gas

CNOOC’s natural gas strategy deserves attention:

- Natural gas production will exceed 1 trillion cubic feet for the first time in 2025, a year-on-year increase of 11.6%
- Under China's "double carbon" goal, the demand for natural gas as a transitional energy source will continue to grow.
- Increased contribution from deepwater gas fields in the South China Sea ("Shenhai No. 1", etc.)
- LNG receiving terminals and trade layout are gradually improved

Natural gas is less volatile than crude oil and demand growth is more certain. Increasing the proportion of natural gas is an important way for CNOOC to reduce cyclicality.

### 3.3 Long-term impacts of energy transition

**Short term (2026-2030):** Limited impact. Global oil demand is still growing (IEA predicts the peak in 2028-2030). CNOOC's "increased reserves and production" strategy is highly bound to national energy security, and policy support is highly certain.

**Medium term (2030-2040):** Needs attention. If the penetration rate of electric vehicles exceeds 50% and alternative energy sources such as hydrogen make breakthroughs, oil demand may begin to decline substantially.

**Long term (2040+):** There is a lot of uncertainty. But for investors, at a valuation level of less than 10 times PE and a dividend yield of nearly 5%, the market has already "priced" this forward risk to a large extent.

## 4. "Multi-dimensional comparison" with global peers

### 4.1 Growth comparison

| Company | Compound growth rate of output in the past three years | Output growth rate in 2025 |
|------|-------------------|----------------|
| CNOOC | ~6% | +7.0% |
| Exxon Mobil | ~3% | ~2% |
| Shell | ~1% | ~0% |
| Total | ~2% | ~2% |
| Petrobras | ~4% | ~3% |

CNOOC is one of the world's largest oil and gas companies with the best growth potential. This is mainly due to the fact that China's waters are still in the middle stage of development and there are still a large number of developable reserves.

### 4.2 Cost comparison

CNOOC's full cost of US$27.9/barrel ranks among the highest among global E&P companies. Only the national oil companies in the Middle East (Saudi Aramco about $10/barrel) and some low-cost blocks can achieve lower prices.

### 4.3 Valuation comparison (re-examination)

CNOOC's PE is about 9 times, Shell and Total are about 8 times, and Exxon is about 13 times. If you look at PE alone, CNOOC is not particularly cheap. But if you consider:
- Higher production growth (7% vs 0-3%)
- Lower gearing ratio (27% vs 30-40%)
- Higher ROE (16-17% vs 14-18%)

Then CNOOC's "cost-effectiveness" is at an upper-middle level among global oil and gas stocks.

## 5. "Where could I go wrong?" - reverse testing

Any analysis can have blind spots. The following are scenarios that may cause the logic of being bullish on CNOOC to go awry:

**1. Oil prices fell below $50 and remained there for a long time. ** If OPEC+ loses its price control, the global economy is in deep recession, and the oil price center moves sharply downward, CNOOC's profits will be significantly compressed. The cost advantage of US$27.9 ensures that there will be no loss, but the profitability and dividend capabilities will be greatly reduced.

**2. The resource quality of China’s maritime areas is accelerating. ** If newly discovered oil and gas fields become smaller and poorer in quality, the cost of a barrel of oil may trend upward and production growth will slow down.

**3. The nature of state-owned enterprises leads to inefficient capital allocation. ** If the company is required to invest heavily in new energy or undertake non-commercial policy tasks, shareholder returns may be squeezed.

**4. Geopolitical risks intensify. ** CNOOC’s substantial assets in the South China Sea face potential geopolitical risks. In addition, the company has been blocked from overseas listings due to U.S. sanctions (it was included in the list of military-related companies in 2021).

**5. Management turnover brings strategic uncertainty. ** Chairman Wang Dongjin will step down in 2025, and Zhang Chuanjiang (former general manager of Datang Group, power background) will take over in July 2025. There is uncertainty about whether an "airborne" chairman with no background in the oil and gas industry can maintain the previous operating efficiency.

## 6. Comprehensive assessment of industry competition

| Dimensions | Rating | Description |
|------|------|------|
| Industry structure | ★★★★☆ | Oligopoly, limited competition |
| Competitive position | ★★★★★ | Maritime monopoly, lowest cost quartile in the world |
| Growth | ★★★★☆ | 7% growth rate leads global peers, but may slow down |
| Industry Trends | ★★★☆☆ | Oil prices are neutral, natural gas is improving, and there is transformation pressure in the long term |
| Global comparative valuation | ★★★☆☆ | Not too expensive, not too cheap, neutral but reasonable |

**Industry competition overall rating: ★★★★☆ (3.5/5)**

CNOOC has the top competitive position in its field - the lowest cost, fastest growth and cleanest balance sheet. But the track itself faces long-term headwinds (energy transition, oil price uncertainty). In Munger's words: This is a good horse, but the length and direction of the track are not entirely certain.

The judgment that investors need to make is not "whether CNOOC is a good company" (the answer is obviously yes), but "how many years of growth will global oil demand have left" and "whether the current valuation has fully reflected the forward risks."
