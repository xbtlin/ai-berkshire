# I. Core risk list: from the deadliest

The risks to which sea oil is invested can be divided into four levels: lethal, material, intermediate and monitorable.

#1.1 Fatal level risk: long-term collapse of oil prices

** Risk description:** If Brent crude oil prices remain below $40 for a long period of time (more than three years), China and China will not lose (27.9 per cent at cost), but profits will be reduced to the current 20-30 per cent, dividends will be significantly reduced and valuations may be further reduced.

** Probability: ** Low (10-15%). Historically, oil prices have been below $40 for a very short period (a 2020 epidemic, excess supply in 2016), and the supply constraints of OPEC+ ' s reduction adjustment mechanism and insufficient investment upstream globally make it difficult to sustain the very low oil prices over the long term.

** Responsive capacity:** The cost of 27.9 United States dollars in oil barrels in China and China provides the thickest "safe mat" in the industry. There is still about $12/bp at $40 oil prices. The asset liability rate of 26.7 per cent means no debt service pressure.

** Risk rating: <unk> (4/5) — significant impact but low probability**

##1.2 Critical level risk: geopolitical and sanctions

** Risk description:** China Sea oil is exposed to multiple geopolitical risks:

** (a) Sovereign dispute over South China Sea assets.** China Sea oil has a large number of exploration and production assets in the South China Sea, and sovereignty dispute in the South China Sea involves a number of countries. While China currently controls the development of the relevant maritime areas, the deterioration of international relations may affect the progress of the development of some of the blocks.

**(b) US sanctions risk.** In 2021, China Sea oil was added to the US "List of China's Military Related Enterprises" which led to its ADR being withdrawn from the market in New York. Although no further sanctions escalation has taken place since then, this label limits the scope for corporate financing and cooperation in the US.

**(c) Overseas asset operating risks.** The company has overseas assets in Guyana, Nigeria, Indonesia and Canada, and changes in host country policies (resource nationalism, tax adjustment, etc.) may affect overseas earnings.

** Probability of occurrence:** Medium (20-30%). Geopolitics are a continuing risk and will not disappear, but the probability of large-scale deterioration (e.g., full sanctions or military conflict) is limited.

** Responsive capacity:** China’s oil revenues are mainly from China’s maritime waters, and the overseas share is relatively manageable. Even if all overseas assets are damaged, the company’s core profitability is sustainable.

** Risk rating: <unk> (3/5) - ongoing attention but non-core threat**

##1.3 Significant level risk: long-term erosion of energy transformation

** Risk description:** The global energy transition is a slow but well-directed trend. The acceleration of the penetration rate of electric vehicles (China ' s new energy vehicle penetration rate is over 50%), the continuing decline in renewable energy costs, carbon neutrality and policy tightening across countries, which will gradually reduce oil demand after 2030.

** Time dimension:**
- 2026-2030: limited impact, and global oil demand is expected to continue to grow slowly
- 2030-2035: oil demand in OECD countries may begin to decline significantly
- After 2035: Global oil demand may enter the downward cycle

** Response to China Sea Oil:** The company's new energy layout is relatively conservative - there are some explorations of the offshore wind power, but total investment is extremely low. This is both a good (a good thing is not to burn money for something that is not good) and a risk (the company may not be well prepared if the transition is accelerated).

** Risk rating: <unk> (3/5) — forward determined risk, but the current valuation has been partially priced**

##1.4 Medium risk: safety incidents and environmental protection

** Risk description:** Sea oil and gas development is naturally at risk for safety and environmental protection. The 2010 BP oil spill in the Gulf of Mexico cost BP more than $65 billion. China Sea oil itself also experienced an oil spill in 2011 in the 19-3 oil field in Punley.

** Responsive capacity:** The company has significantly strengthened its HSE (Healthy and Safe Environment) management system in recent years, and its safety record has improved continuously. But the inherent risks of maritime operations can never be reduced to zero.

** Risk rating: <unk> (2/5) — high-probability impact, to be tracked continuously through security records**

##1.5 Controllable risk: governance and policy burden of state enterprises

** Risk description:** As a listed company, China Sea oil may face:
- Requiring non-commercial tasks (e.g. low-cost supply, social responsibility projects)
- Investment decisions are influenced by administrative interventions
- Lack of incentives for top pay and market-related incentives
- State-owned shareholder claims may conflict with the small shareholder

** Current performance:** The level of governance of China and China oil in the firm is objectively greater.

** Risk rating: <unk> (5) — China’s disease, but China’s oil performance is relatively good**

# III. Corporate governance assessment

#3.1 Equity structure

China Ocean Oil Group holds about 64% of the shares of listed companies and is an absolute shareholder. This means:
- Small shareholders have little voice in major decisions.
- Related transactions require sustained attention (although no significant profit channel is found at present)
- The allocation policy is ultimately determined by the major shareholders (i.e. the NRC through the group).

#3.2 Independence of the Board of Directors

The company has independent non-executive directors, but the independence of the board of directors is naturally limited in the company’s system. On the good side, China Sea oil, a dual listed company with a port and A share, needs to meet both information disclosure and governance requirements and is relatively transparent.

#3.3 Governance assessment

**★★★☆☆（3/5）**

Among the companies that are better governed, there is still a gap between the governance standards of the world’s top-class enterprises. The core short-sheet is absolute control of major shareholders, inadequate managerial incentives, and the risk of non-commercial factors influencing business decisions.

# IV. Assessment of the suitability of long-term investments

How appropriate is the oil of China and China as a long-term warehouse price from the point of view of value investment?

# 4.1 Reasons suitable for long-term holding

** (a) "No alternative."** China's maritime oil and gas development is only possible with China Sea oil. This status will not change in the foreseeable future under the framework of national energy security.

** (b) Cash flow creativity.** Even during low oil prices, middle-sea oil can generate a positive free cash flow and maintain a red. This is the bottom line of "salary plus sustained return."

** (c) Valuations have been implicitly pessimistic.** The market has already made considerable discounts on the downside of oil prices and energy transformation risks at the valuation level of less than 10 times the PE and nearly 5 per cent dividends.

# 4.2 Reasons not suitable for long-term holding

** (a) Unpredictable oil prices.** No one can accurately predict oil prices after 5-10 years. If you cannot believe in oil prices, it is difficult to believe in the long-term profitability of China and China oil.

** (b) "Final" is uncertain.** Unlike consumer companies, oil companies face a definite forward risk -- fossil energy will eventually be replaced. The difference is only in time. Do you believe that the return on investment will be sufficient before the end?

**(c) The ceiling for state-owned governance.** The incentives for corporate governance determine that management is unlikely to work as "to maximize shareholder benefits" in the way private firms do. There is always room for improvement in capital efficiency, but it may never be fully exploited.
