#CMOC: Business model analysis

> Analysis dimension: Duan Yongping's "good business" perspective | Date: May 16, 2026 | Currency: RMB (unless otherwise noted)

---

## 1. What kind of business is this?

The business essence of China Molybdenum Corporation (CMOC) is **global polymetal mining + bulk commodity trading**. The company has evolved from a molybdenum mining enterprise in Luanchuan, Henan Province when it was founded, to a global resource platform spanning seven major categories: copper, cobalt, molybdenum, tungsten, niobium, phosphorus and gold.

**Income Structure (2025)**:
- Mining business: approximately 26.5 billion yuan (copper, cobalt, molybdenum, tungsten, niobium, phosphorus, etc.)
- IXM trading business: approximately 180.1 billion yuan (top three basic metal traders in the world, headquartered in Geneva, Switzerland)
- Total revenue: 206.7 billion yuan

**Profit structure**: The mining business contributes the vast majority of profits, and the trading business has large revenue but thin profit margins. Copper and cobalt are the core profit sources, accounting for more than 70% of mining profits, almost all of which come from the TFM and KFM mines in the Democratic Republic of the Congo.

**Core Assets**:
- **TFM (Tenke Fungurume)**: Located in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, it has 5 production lines, with an annual copper production capacity of approximately 450,000 tons and an annual cobalt output of approximately 22,500 tons.
- **KFM (Kisanfu/Boss Mine)**: The world's largest cobalt mine, with an annual copper production capacity of approximately 150,000 tons and an annual cobalt output of approximately 25,500 tons.
- **Brazilian niobium phosphorus business**: the world's second largest niobium producer, with an annual output of approximately 10,000 tons of niobium
- **Brazilian Gold Mine**: The acquisition will be completed in January 2026, with an annual output of 6-8 tons of gold, and greater long-term potential.
- **IXM**: Metal trading network covering more than 80 countries

## 2. Duan Yongping’s “Good Business” Standard Inspection

### 2.1 Are user needs rigid? ★★★★★

Copper is the "blood" of human industrial civilization. Electric power transmission, new energy vehicles, AI data centers, defense equipment—almost every modern technological advancement requires more copper. S&P Global's January 2026 report predicts that global copper demand will grow from approximately 28 million tons currently to 42 million tons in 2040, an increase of 50%. This is one of the very few commodities where the gap between supply and demand has definitely expanded.

Cobalt is a key element in battery cathode materials (although ternary lithium batteries have a trend of decobaltization, high nickel and low cobalt still require cobalt). Niobium is used in high-strength steels. The safe-haven properties of gold need no elaboration. The company's product portfolio corresponds to the rigid needs of global industrialization and energy transformation.

** Score: The demand side is extremely strong, and copper’s long-term demand certainty ranks among the best among all commodities. **

### 2.2 Is the business model simple and easy to understand? ★★★☆☆

On the surface, the mining business is simple: dig for ore, process it, smelt it, and sell the metal. But the complexity of CMOC lies in:

- **Geographical dispersion**: core assets are in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Brazil, China, and Australia, and multi-national political relations need to be managed
- **Trading Business Overlay**: IXM’s metal trading is another business involving futures hedging, logistics, and credit risk
- **Wide Variety**: Each of the seven major metal varieties has different supply and demand cycles
- **Metal Price Fluctuations**: Profits are highly dependent on copper and cobalt prices and are beyond the control of management

Duan Yongping emphasized "do what you know." Mining is not a consumer product, there is no brand loyalty, the product is not differentiated and the company cannot set a price. This is not the type of "simple business" Duan Yongping likes.

**Rating: The business is understandable, but cyclical and geopolitical complexities increase cognitive difficulty. **

### 2.3 Is there sustained free cash flow? ★★★★☆

This is the biggest change of CMOC in the past two years.

- Operating net cash flow in the first half of 2025 is 12 billion yuan, +11% year-on-year
- Operating net cash flow in 2026Q1 was 11.3 billion yuan, a year-on-year increase of 762%
- The company expects capital expenditure to drop to less than 8 billion yuan in 2026
- If copper prices maintain current levels, free cash flow is expected to exceed 12 billion yuan in 2026

The characteristic of the mining industry is that once the mine is completed and put into production, the maintenance capital expenditure is much lower than during the construction period. TFM and KFM have been fully put into production, and the next 2-3 years will be the harvest period. The dividend ratio is about 40% (in 2024), and the willingness to return shareholders is clear.

**But be warned**: The sustainability of this cash flow is highly dependent on the price of copper. For every 10% drop in copper prices, profits may drop by 20-30%. In addition, the company is still conducting expansionary capital expenditures (KFM Phase II, TFM Phase III, and gold mines). Whether free cash flow can remain high depends on management's capital discipline.

**Rating: The current cash flow is excellent, but cyclicality is an endogenous feature and cannot be assumed to be sustainable. **

### 2.4 Is the management focused on the main business? ★★★★☆

CMOC’s expansion path is clear and focused:

- 2007: Molybdenum mining started and Hong Kong stock market listed
- 2012: A-share listing
- 2016: Acquired TFM (from Freeport) and entered the copper and cobalt field
- 2019: Acquisition of IXM trading platform
- 2020: Acquisition of KFM (the world's largest cobalt mine)
- 2025: Acquisition of the Cangrejos gold mine in Ecuador
- 2026: Acquire three gold mines in Brazil and establish "copper-gold bipolarity"

Every step follows the logic of "expansion of resource varieties + acquisition of low-cost assets" without crossing over into unrelated fields. From Duan Yongping's perspective, the management's focus on the main business is acceptable.

**But the hidden worry is**: Will continuous mergers and acquisitions create an "empire building" tendency? Is the timing of buying a Brazilian gold mine for US$1 billion reasonable (when gold prices are at historical highs)? These require continued observation.

** Score: The strategic direction is clear, but the frequency of mergers and acquisitions is high, and capital allocation discipline needs to be paid attention to. **

### 2.5 Does the product have differentiation or pricing power? ★★☆☆☆

This is a natural shortcoming of mining companies. Copper is copper, cobalt is cobalt - standardized commodities, no brand premium, no customer stickiness, the price is determined by global supply and demand and futures markets.

CMOC's "differentiation" is reflected in the **cost side** rather than the **revenue side**:
- TFM and KFM are located in the world-class copper and cobalt mineral belt, with high grade and large scale
- Copper and cobalt co-production reduces overall costs
- IXM trading network provides channel advantages

But in essence, this is a competitive advantage of "low-cost producers" rather than a competitive advantage of "differentiated pricing". During commodity cycle troughs, even low-cost producers face significant profit compression.

**Rating: Undifferentiation of bulk commodities is the fate of the industry. The low-cost advantage is valuable but does not equal pricing power. **

## 3. The core contradiction from Duan Yongping’s perspective

### Positive: Elements of "Good Business"

1. **Excellent track**: Copper is one of the commodities with the highest certainty, and the demand side (electrification + AI + defense) gives up to 15 years of growth visibility
2. **Outstanding asset quality**: TFM+KFM is a world-class mine, and the co-production of copper and cobalt has cost advantages
3. **Clear Growth**: 740,000 tons of copper in 2025 -> 800,000-1 million tons in 2028, with a clear path for production growth
4. **Cash flow improvement**: The peak of capital expenditure during the construction period has passed and the harvest period has entered

### Negative: Does not meet the elements of "good business"

1. **Strong cyclicality**: This is not a "guaranteed harvest during drought and flood" business. If the copper price falls from US$10,000/ton to US$6,000/ton (which has happened historically), the company's profits may be cut in half or even lose money.
2. **No pricing power**: The company is the recipient of prices, not the maker.
3. **Asset-heavy**: Mine development requires huge upfront investment, and the capital return cycle is long
4. **Geopolitical Risk**: The core asset is in the Democratic Republic of the Congo. This is the type of uncertainty that Duan Yongping dislikes the most - you cannot eliminate it through business efforts.

### Duan Yongping’s possible judgment

Duan Yongping will most likely not invest in China Molybdenum. His preference is businesses that are consumer brands, light assets, high in repurchases, and have pricing power (such as Apple, Moutai, and Tencent). Mining companies failed to meet his criteria for "good business" on almost every dimension.

But this does not mean that China Molybdenum has no investment value - it just means that it does not belong to the "ideal target" under Duan Yongping's framework.

## 5. Key tracking indicators

1. **Copper Price Trend**: LME copper price is the biggest variable in profits

4. **Policy Trends in the Democratic Republic of the Congo**: Mining Law Revisions, Tax and Fee Adjustments, and Export Policy Changes
5. **IXM trade profit margin**: Is the trading business with large revenue and small profit stable?
