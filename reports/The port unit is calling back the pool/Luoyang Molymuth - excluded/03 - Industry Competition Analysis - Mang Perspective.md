## 1. Industry structure analysis: Is copper mining a good industry?

### 1.1 Global Copper Market Structure

The annual output of global copper mines is about 22-23 million tons (mineralized copper), and the annual output of refined copper is about 28 million tons. Industry concentration is moderate:

| Ranking | Company | Copper production in 2025 (10,000 tons) | Global share |
|------|------|---------------------|----------|
| 1 | Codelco (Chile National Copper) | ~140 | ~6% |
| 2 | Freeport-McMoRan | ~120 | ~5% |
| 3 | BHP | ~110 | ~5% |
| 4 | Glencore | ~100 | ~4% |
| 5-10 | Zijin Mining/CMOC/Southern Copper, etc. | 60-100 | 3-4% each |

CMOC will produce 740,000 tons of copper in 2025, ranking among the top ten in the world. The guidance is 760,000-820,000 tons in 2026 and the target is 800,000-1 million tons in 2028. It is expected to enter the top five in the world.

### 1.2 Supply and demand structure: good for manufacturers in the long run

**This is the element that Munger values most when analyzing industry structure—the structural trend of supply and demand. **

**Deterministic growth on the demand side**:
- S&P Global January 2026 report: Global copper demand will reach 42 million tons in 2040, an increase of 50% from the current level
- New energy will contribute 87% of the global copper increase in 2025 (photovoltaics, wind power, electric vehicles)
- New demand for AI data centers: Global data center installed capacity will reach 550GW in 2040, more than five times that of 2022
- Defense spending doubles: Global military spending may reach US$6 trillion in 2040

**The supply side is severely restricted**:
- Annual demand growth requires 600,000-700,000 tons of new supply, but the average project approval in the past three years was less than 300,000 tons
- It takes an average of 15-20 years from discovery to production for a new copper mine
- Ore grades continue to decline and mining costs rise
- JPMorgan Chase estimates that the global refined copper deficit will be approximately 330,000 tons in 2026

**Munger's Judgment Framework**: In an industry where demand is certain to grow and supply is severely constrained, low-cost producers will enjoy lasting excess profits. This is exactly where China Molybdenum is.

### 1.3 Cobalt market: from surplus to rebalancing

The cobalt market has experienced a severe glut over the past two years (cobalt prices fell from US$30 per pound to below US$10 in 2023-2024), but structural changes are occurring:

- Congo (DRC) will implement a cobalt export quota system from October 2025
- The annual quota for 2026-2027 is 96,600 tons, which is only 40-50% of the production in 2024
- China Molybdenum received an export quota of 31,200 tons (accounting for about 32%), making it the enterprise with the largest quota.
- Although there is a trend of decobaltization of ternary lithium batteries, the absolute demand is still growing

The policy-driven supply contraction in the cobalt market is extremely beneficial to CMOC as the world's largest cobalt producer - the quota system is essentially a "supply-side reform."

## 2. Moat analysis of China Molybdenum

### 2.1 Cost advantage moat ★★★★☆

The core moat of a mining company is the position of the cost curve. CMOC’s cost advantage comes from:

**(1) World-class mining resource endowment**
- TFM is located in the Katanga Copper Belt of Congo (DRC), which is one of the highest grade copper and cobalt mineral belts in the world.
- KFM (Boss Mine) is the world’s largest cobalt mine and also produces copper as a by-product
- Copper-cobalt co-production model: Cobalt’s by-product income can significantly lower the net cash cost of copper

**(2) Scale effect**
- TFM has 5 production lines and an annual copper production capacity of 450,000 tons
- KFM has an annual copper production capacity of 150,000 tons
- The two mines have a combined production capacity of over 600,000 tons/year, which is among the top scale in a single mining area.

**(3) Cost location**
- The company's copper production is in the top 25% of the global cost curve (i.e., the low-cost quadrant)
- Copper and cobalt co-production enables comprehensive costs to remain competitive even if cobalt prices are low

**Evaluation**: Cost advantage is the most reliable moat for mining companies, but its durability depends on the decay rate of mine reserves and grades. TFM and KFM have long reserve lives (more than 20 years), and their cost advantages are solid in the short term.

### 2.2 Resource barriers (non-replicable assets) ★★★★★

**This is CMOC’s strongest moat. **

When analyzing a company, Munger emphasized "what competitors cannot copy." For CMOC:

- **TFM**: Originally owned by Freeport-McMoRan, China Molybdenum Corporation acquired a 56% interest in it for US$2.65 billion in 2016. This mine cannot be replicated - its geology is a natural endowment
- **KFM**: Acquired for US$550 million in 2020, it is known as "the world's largest untapped cobalt mine" and has now been put into production. There is no second similar cobalt mine in the world
- **Brazilian Niobium Phosphate**: The second largest niobium producer in the world, niobium ore resources are extremely scarce (90%+ of the world is concentrated in Brazil)

It takes 15-20 years for a world-class mine to go from exploration to production, and approval is becoming increasingly difficult. Even if new competitors have the funds, they will not be able to replicate China Molybdenum's resource portfolio in the short term.

**Evaluation**: Resource barriers are a unique type of moat for mining companies, similar to what Buffett calls a "franchise."

### 2.3 Scale and industrial chain integration ★★★★☆

**(1) Mining + trade integration**
IXM is one of the top three base metal traders in the world, covering more than 80 countries. The mining + trading combination offers:
- Diversify sales channels and reduce dependence on a single customer
- Better market information acquisition and pricing capabilities
- Professional capabilities in futures hedging and risk management

**(2)Vertical integration**
In the Democratic Republic of the Congo, China Molybdenum has formed a complete industrial chain from mining to mineral processing to smelting. At the same time, the self-built 200MW Heshima hydropower station solves the power bottleneck and further reduces operating costs.

**Evaluation**: Industrial chain integration enhances operational efficiency and risk resistance, but this is a "replicable" competitive advantage and is not as unique as resource barriers.

### 2.4 Government relations and licensing barriers ★★★☆☆

When operating a mine in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, the relationship with the local government is crucial. CMOC has been operating in the Democratic Republic of the Congo for more than 8 years and has accumulated deep government relations and community trust:

- Invested more than $325 million in the TFM community since launch
- MSCI ESG rating AA, ranking among the top 13% of the global nonferrous metals industry
- Obtained the largest proportion of cobalt export quotas issued by the Congolese government

But this "double-edged sword" is also a source of risk:
- Dispute with Congolese National Mining Company from 2021 to 2023, forced to pay US$800 million in settlement
- Congo’s policy requiring mining companies to transfer 10% of their equity to the government in 2025
-Government connections can help you or hurt you

**Comments**: Government relations are a moat in the mining industry, but they are also a source of vulnerability. This "moat" can turn into a "trap" overnight.

## 3. Competitive Landscape: Comparison with Major Rivals

### 3.1 Comparison with Zijin Mining

| Dimension | China Molybdenum | Zijin Mining |
|------|----------|----------|
| Market value | About HK$200 billion | About HK$400 billion |
| Core varieties | Copper, cobalt | Copper, gold |
| Copper production (2025) | 740,000 tons | ~1 million tons |
| PE（2025） | 11-12x | 14-16x |
| ROE（2025） | ~27% | ~22% |
| Asset Distribution | Mainly the Democratic Republic of the Congo | Global Dispersion |
| Risk characteristics | High concentration risk | Decentralized but difficult to manage |

Zijin Mining's assets are more diversified (China, Congo, Serbia, Tibet, etc.), and geopolitical risks are relatively dispersed. China Molybdenum has a higher ROE but greater concentration risk, so it is reasonable for the market to give it a lower PE.

### 3.2 Comparison with Freeport-McMoRan

Freeport is one of the world's largest copper miners (former original owner of TFM assets), with a PE of 20-25 times, much higher than China Molybdenum. The differences mainly come from:
- Freeport assets are mainly in the United States and Indonesia (geographic risk is lower than that of the Democratic Republic of the Congo)
- The valuation system of US stocks is generally higher than that of Hong Kong stocks
- Differences in brand awareness and investor awareness

This valuation gap reflects both reasonable risk premiums and potentially irrational market biases.

## 4. Key judgments from Munger’s perspective

### Positive: The moat exists

1. **Resource barriers are real moats**: World-class mines cannot be copied, and the construction cycle of 15-20 years constitutes extremely high barriers to entry.
2. **Favorable cost curve**: Copper and cobalt co-production + high-grade ore, the cost is at the forefront of the industry
3. **The supply and demand structure of the industry is favorable**: The long-term supply and demand gap for copper has created a lasting excess profit environment for low-cost producers.
4. **Benefits of the quota system**: The cobalt export quota system allows China Molybdenum Corporation, as the largest quota holder, to gain additional competitive advantages.

### Opposite side: The fragility of the moat

1. **Country risk can "confiscate" the moat**: The Congolese government can theoretically destroy shareholder value through nationalization, forced share conversion, excessive taxation, etc. The $800 million settlement is a lesson learned from the past
2. **Cyclic weakening of moat value**: If copper prices fall sharply, the value of low-cost advantages will be greatly reduced.
3. **Technological Substitution Risk (Cobalt)**: The decobaltization trend of ternary lithium batteries may weaken the demand for cobalt in the long term
4. **Concentration in a single region**: 70%+ profits come from Congo (DRC), lack of geographical dispersion

### Munger’s possible judgment

When Munger evaluates mining stocks, he will use the "thinking backwards" method: **Under what circumstances will this investment lose a lot of money? **

The answer is clear: serious political turmoil or drastic policy changes in the Democratic Republic of the Congo. This is a tail risk with low probability but serious consequences. Munger might say, "If you need the goodwill of others to protect your assets, that's not a real moat."
