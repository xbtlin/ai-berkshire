# II. Risk panorama

##2.1 Maximum risk: Democratic Republic of the Congo country risk (very high)

** This is the core variable that determines the fate of the investment in the Luoyang industry.**

** List of risk facts**:

1. ** Incidents of dispute 2021-2023**: The Congolese National Mining Corporation (Gecamines) initiated an investigation into TFM, which resulted in the forced payment of $800 million in settlement payments by the Luoyang Molympho industry. This is not a fine, but the nature of the "protection fee".

2. ** Requirement for compulsory transfer**: In January 2025, the Congolese Mining Authority requested 58 mining companies to transfer 10 per cent of their shares to the Congolese Government within 45 days, otherwise they would be at risk of revocation of mining rights.

3. ** Cobalt export quota**: Implementation in October 2025, although short-term benefits (limitation of exports from competitors) have also meant that the Congolese Government is taking greater control over mining.

4. ** Security environment**: The long-standing armed conflict in eastern Congo, although TFM and KFM are located in relatively stable Katanga Province, the political situation throughout the country is uncertain.

5. ** Large country games**: The competition of the United States and China in key mineral supply chains could make Chinese mining companies a bargaining chip for political games in the Congo.

** Worst scenario development**:
- The Government of the Congo has increased the mandatory transfer rate from 10 per cent to 20-30 per cent
- or higher royalties on copper and cobalt exports (from the current level of about 6 per cent to 10-15 per cent)
- In extreme cases, some or all mining rights are withdrawn or nationalized

** Probability assessment**: The probability of full nationalization is low (less than 5%), but the probability of continuous "skin-sheep wool" (tax, transfer, quota restrictions) is high (more than 50%). Each "seat-shell" can result in a loss of billions of yuan in value.

# Three, Lee Sze "Safe Margin" assessment

#3.1 How will Lee Jian think about this investment?

The core idea of Ree-Seong is:** buy in at prices that are significantly below intrinsic value, leaving sufficient security margins to deal with uncertainty.**

**Safe margin calculations**:

Inherent value in conservative assumptions:
- Assuming a period of 20-25 billion yuan net profit (copper price US$ 8500-9,000 per tonne)
- 10 times the PE (considering periodic and geo-risk discounts)
- Conservative valuation = RMB 2000-250 billion

The market value of the H share is currently about RMB 185 billion:
- Safety margin of relatively conservative valuation = 8%-35%
- Safety margin relative to baseline valuation (300 billion plus) = 60% +

** Possible judgement by Lee-soo**: Security margin exists but is not sufficient – only 8-35% discount in conservative scenarios, which is not wide enough for assets at risk to the state. Li-sang usually requires 40-50% security margin.

# 3.2 "10 Years Possession" test

If the purchase is forced to take possession for 10 years and cannot be sold, the investor faces a scenario:

** Optimal path (30% probability):
- Copper prices up to $12,000-15000 per ton over time
- The situation in the Congo is stable, with the company expanding smoothly to 1 million tons of copper
- 10 years later, the market value can be $500-800 billion, with an annualized return of 15-20 per cent

** Baseline path (40% probability)**:
- Copper price fluctuations at $80-10,000/tonne
- Congo has a friction but it's manageable.
- 10 years later, the market value is 30-500 billion yuan, with an annualized return of 8-12 per cent

** Pessimistic path (at 25 per cent probability)**:
- The copper price fell significantly to $6,000-$7,000/tonne
- The Congo continues to increase taxes and fees, which erode profits
- 150-200 billion yuan in 10 years ' worth, annualized return of 0-3%

** Extreme path (5% probability):
- The existence of a dispute over or nationalization of mining rights in the Congo
- Forced sale of assets or payment of substantial compensation by the company
- 10 years later, the market value is $50-100 billion, with a 50 per cent loss +

** Expected return**: about 9-13 per cent, with long-term returns expected after extreme risk deductions are not significant.
