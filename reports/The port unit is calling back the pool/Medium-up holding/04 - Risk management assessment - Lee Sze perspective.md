# I. Management assessment: stock buys are buyers

# Founder background

China ' s China Trade Union (CL) has been one of China ' s largest groups of luxury car dealers, which began in 1998 in Dalian with Huang Yi and Li Guqiang, and has grown over 28 years into one of China ' s largest fleet of luxury car dealers, and which was listed at Hong Kong Trade Office in 2010.

** Huang Yi** - Chairman and Executive Director, responsible for strategic management and the formulation of corporate goals.
** Li Guoqiang** - Deputy Chairman and Chief Executive Officer with more than 20 years of industry experience and responsible for day-to-day management of operations.

The two founders held approximately 48.92 per cent of the company ' s shares in the aggregate indirectly through Blue Natural Development Ltd. (30.48 per cent in Huang Ying and 18.44 per cent in Li Guoqiang).

# II. Risk List: From High to Low

# First ladder: structural risks (high impact, long duration)

**Risk 1: Disrupted dealer mode **

This is the greatest risk. If the mainstream car companies turn all over to the straight-up model in the future, the distributor loses its worth.

- Current status: Tesla, we're going straight; Biadey hybrid model; Huaiwan started working with dealers
- Probability assessment: the probability of total elimination over the next five years is low (less than 10 per cent), but the probability of the dealer ' s position in the industrial chain and profit share declining continuously (more than 70 per cent)
- Mitigation: the question community chooses to work with the distributors to indicate that the straight-line approach is not the only solution

**Risk 2: Revenue erosion after sale of electric vehicles **

The maintenance requirements for electric vehicles are much lower than for fuel vehicles – no oil, no fuel tanks, spark plugs, etc. As the capacity of electric vehicles increases, the revenue from after-sale services (currently the middle-slash profit base) may be structurally weakened.

- Current status: Māori is growing after sale because of the large fuel fuel vehicle base
- Time window: fuel vehicle holdings remain dominant over the next 3-5 years, but the turning point may arrive after five years
- Mitigation: electric vehicles, although simple, are more expensive to repair accidents (cell maintenance is expensive); smartness brings new post-sale demand such as software services, OTA upgrades, etc.

** Risk 3: Continuing price battles for luxury brands**

BBA has been forced to sell at a loss price to keep its market share down.

- Current status: there are signs of price increases in early 2026, but sustainability is uncertain
- Quantification of impact: the rate of Māori in new vehicles is equal to about $1.4 billion in profit improvement per 1 percentage point improvement
- Mitigation: brands are losing money and cannot cut prices indefinitely; BMW and Mercedes have started to shrink their net points

# Second ladder: operational risk (medium impact, manageable)

**Risk 4: The world of inquiry/new energy cooperation is not as good as expected**

The new energy transformation strategy in the medium- and medium-sized sector is hampered by questions about the decline in brand heat or changes in terms of cooperation.

- Current status: strong sales in 2025 and positive initial results with medium- and high-level cooperation
- Risk point: Cyrus/Wall is turning into a straight camp when it's possible to be large; increasing competition among brand names is asked
- Mitigation: promotion is simultaneously well-structured and not entirely dependent on a single brand

**Risk 5: Fitch continues to lose value **

If the industry continues to deteriorate, there may be a subsequent impairment.

- Impact: Non-cash losses that do not affect operating cash flows but that result in lower book profits
- Probability: Medium - Depending on whether industry improved in 2026

** Risk 6: Impairment of inventory **

In a price-fighting environment, the market value of vehicles in stock may continue to decline, resulting in a loss of inventory value.

- Mitigation: significant improvement in operating cash flows in 2025, up from mid- to medium-term, indicating an improvement in inventory management
- Monitoring indicator: days of inventory turnover

# 3rd Plateau: Macro and Policy Risk (External Uncontrollable)

# Three, security margin analysis

# Liquidation value (most pessimistic scenario)

How much would it be worth if we were to settle this case today?

- Cash: $20.4 billion
- Accounts receivable (80 per cent discount): estimated $8 billion-10 billion
- Inventory (70 per cent discount): estimated 15-20 billion yuan
- Fixed assets (50 per cent discount): estimated $6-8 billion
- Less total liabilities
- Crude liquidation value: $30-40 billion (approximately 12-17 HK$/share)

The current market value is about HK$50 billion, and the liquidation value provides a security margin of about 60-80% of the current stock price. This suggests that, even in the most extreme cases, the downside space is limited.

# # Business value (neutral scenario)

If the company goes on,
- After-sales services have been steadily generating profits of $5-6 billion per year
- New energy operations gradually contribute $1.52 billion
- Total normalized profits 4-6 billion yuan
- 10 times the PE, worth $40-60 billion (approximately HK$17-26/unit)

# Security margin assessment

The current stock price is about HK$21 in the middle of the liquidation value, the normal business value. The security margin is not sufficient — if the industry deteriorates further, the share price may converge towards the liquidation value; if the profit is restored, the upfront space is 25-70%.

# Four, Chinese identity factors

# Policy environment

- The Chinese Government has strong policy guidance for the automobile industry, and the decline in new energy subsidies, changes in acquisition taxes and so on have a direct impact on the industry
- For dealers, it's unlikely that the government will take the initiative to "protect" the traditional distribution model, but it's unlikely that it will allow the industry to crash in a disorderly fashion.
- Automobile consumption is an important tool for boosting domestic demand, and the general policy direction still encourages car consumption

# Port stock market characteristics

- Ports share a general valuation discount (AH premium) for Chinese companies
- Middle-level as a port-stock company, affected by both south-south and overseas funds
- Overseas investors may be more inclined to avoid valuations at times of high industry uncertainty, leading to further undervaluation

# The Chinese logic of industry integration

- China ' s automobile market remains the largest worldwide, with over 30 million vehicles sold annually.
- Over 300 million vehicles are in stock and the market for services is huge
- Head distributors are expected to gain a greater share of the market in industrial integration - a feature of the Chinese market, the "several strong."

# Seven, wrap up

The risk assessment of the MEUs can be summarized as a core contradiction:** Management is a top-notch, but they may face a shrinking market.**

Huang Yi and Li Guqiang are among the top entrepreneurs in China’s auto dealerships. Nearly 49% of the stockholdings, 28 years of industry experience, and cash flow management in adversity are all worthy of respect. In the industry’s big shuffling, if someone leads the business and survives, they are most likely to do so.

But investment is not a beauty contest. Even with management excellence, investment returns can be disappointing if the industry itself is shrinking and business models are being subverted.

** Proposal to investors**: If you decide to increase investment, it should be because you believe that (1) the distributor model is still valuable in the new energy age; (2) the industry gets a larger share of the business after clearing; and (3) the management transition is strong enough. If you are not convinced of any of these three conditions, it should not be bought simply because it is "favourable".

"Limit" is not like "safe margin" -- real security margins are derived from an understanding of the nature of business.
