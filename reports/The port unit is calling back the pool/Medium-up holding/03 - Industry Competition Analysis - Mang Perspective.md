# Zhongsheng Holdings (0881.HK) Industry and Competition Analysis

> Analysis dimensions: industry structure, competitive advantages and disadvantages, multiple thinking models, reverse thinking
> Core question: Who can survive the reshuffle in the dealer industry? Is Zhongsheng’s competitive position getting stronger or weaker?

---

## 1. The big picture of the industry: a structural reshuffle

China's auto dealer industry is experiencing the most violent shock since its birth. Use some data to describe this situation:

- **2020-2025**: More than 10,000 4S stores will withdraw from the network, and there will be more than 2,000 in 2024 alone
- **2025**: Dealers’ losses are as high as 58%, and only 23.5% are profitable.
- **China Grand Automobile**: China's once largest dealer group was delisted in August 2024. Its market value fell from a peak of over 100 billion yuan to 6.5 billion yuan at the time of delisting.
- **Average single store loss of dealers**: 1.78 million yuan/year

This is not a cyclical trough, but a structural reshaping. There are three driving forces:

### 1. Soaring penetration rate of new energy

The penetration rate of new energy vehicles will exceed 50% in 2025, which means that more than half of new car buyers choose electric vehicles or plug-in hybrids. Traditional dealers' brand portfolios (Mercedes-Benz, BMW, Audi, Toyota, etc.) are losing market share.

### 2. Price war intensifies

Throughout 2025, 177 models will have official price cuts, with the average price of new energy vehicles falling by 11% and that of fuel vehicles by 8.9%. The decline during the December peak season rose to 14.7%. In order to maintain market share, OEMs continue to lower prices, and losses are passed on to dealers.

### 3. Channel model changes

Tesla's direct sales, BYD's hybrid model (direct sales + franchise), Huawei's car selection model (online traffic + offline experience) - these new models are all challenging the business logic of traditional 4S stores' "brand authorization + regional monopoly".

**But looking at it the other way around**: the price war cannot go on forever. At the beginning of 2026, there are signs that car companies have begun to increase prices (the average price of domestic passenger cars from January to March increased by 15,000 yuan, 15,000 yuan, and 7,000 yuan respectively compared with the same period last year). The industry is shifting from a "price war" to a "value war", which may be good for leading dealers with quality service capabilities.

## 2. Competitive landscape: Who will live and who will die?

The car dealer industry is experiencing a natural selection. Companies that survive need to meet several conditions:

1. **Adequate cash reserves** - to survive the cold winter
2. **Flexible brand portfolio capability** - switching from declining brands to growing brands
3. **Powerful after-sales service system** - Maintain stable cash flow
4. **Prudent Asset and Liability Management** – Don’t get bogged down by over-leveraging

### "Last man standing" logic

Guanghui's delisting, Zhengtong's struggles, and the closure of a large number of small and medium-sized 4S stores - these are all reducing market supply. When industry supply shrinks to a certain extent, the profitability of those who remain will naturally recover. This is the logic of "last man standing wins everything".

Zhongsheng has the greatest probability of becoming the "last person standing":
- 20.4 billion cash is an "ammunition arsenal"
- 9.4 billion operating cash flow is the "supply line"
- After-sales gross profit of 11 billion is the "basic ration"

But it should be noted that this logic has a premise: the dealer model itself cannot be completely eliminated. ** If all car companies turn to direct sales in the future, then the "last dealer standing" will have no meaning.

## 3. New energy transformation: asking about the meaning of industry cooperation

### Why did Huawei/Sales choose Zhongsheng?

At the end of 2024, Thalys (the parent company of the Wenjie brand) signed a cooperation agreement with Zhongsheng, and Zhongsheng will open Wenjie 4S stores across the country. This cooperation itself sends several important signals:

1. **The limitations of the direct operation model are verified**: If direct operation is the optimal solution, Cyrus does not need to find a dealer. The reality is that quickly rolling out a nationwide sales network requires huge capital and manpower investment, and the dealer model has natural advantages in this regard.

2. **Zhongsheng’s channel value is recognized**: Zhongsheng has hundreds of stores in core cities across the country, a mature management system and a customer base. These are resources that it will take 3-5 years for Cyrus to accumulate by itself.

3. **Relatively healthy economic model**: Wenjie gives Zhongsheng a fixed rebate of about 4.5% for new cars, and dealers do not need to bear high inventory risks. This is much healthier than the traditional brand model of "stock first, sell later, and rely on rebates to make up for losses."

### Quantitative prediction of industry contribution

- Bank of America Securities predicts: The annual net profit of a single Wenjie store is about 20 million yuan
- Daiwa forecast: Wenjie will bring about 60,000 sales to Zhongsheng in 2026 (accounting for about 12% of the company's total sales)
- Zhongshan target: double the number of new energy stores by the end of 2026

If the number of Wenjie stores reaches 80-100 by the end of 2026, the annual profit contribution can reach 1.6-2 billion yuan. This is a substantial increase.

### Geely Cooperation

Zhongsheng is also expanding its strategic cooperation with Geely. Geely hits the target of 3.45 million vehicles in 2026 (including 2.22 million new energy vehicles), and Ji Krypton focuses on the market of more than 300,000 vehicles. If Zhongsheng can obtain the distribution rights of Ji Krypton and Lynk & Co, it will be another growth engine.

## 4. Analysis of multiple thinking models

### 1. Niche thinking

In the automobile industry chain, the "ecological niche" of dealers is shifting from "sales terminal" to "service platform". Just like commercial real estate has shifted from "selling shops" to "providing comprehensive services", the core value of a dealer is no longer "authorized to sell cars", but "the ability to provide full life cycle automotive services".

If Zhongsheng can complete this positioning transformation, its competition barriers will be deeper than before - because the integration of full life cycle services (after-sales + insurance + used cars + finance + charging, etc.) is more difficult and small dealers cannot do it.

### 2. Supply and demand reversal thinking

When an industry suffers serious losses and a large number of participants exit, the relationship between supply and demand will eventually reverse. More than 2,000 4S stores will withdraw from the network in 2024, and more are expected to be in 2025. When sales outlets are reduced to a certain extent, the bargaining power and customer flow of retained dealers will increase.

This is similar to the logic of coal and steel overcapacity reduction in 2015-2016 - when the industry is extremely pessimistic, it is precisely when leading companies have the most investment value.

### 3. Avoid the "Historical Analogy Trap"

But we should also be wary of a misunderstanding: comparing car dealers to "cyclical stocks that will recover." Coal and steel can recover after capacity reduction because downstream demand is still there (houses still need to be built and electricity needs to be used). Auto dealers face not only cyclical pressures, but also structural model changes.

If new energy vehicle companies ultimately prove that the direct sales + online model is a better solution, then the "reduction of overcapacity" in the dealer industry will not bring about a "reversal of supply and demand", but only a "slow demise."

### 4. Probabilistic thinking

Comprehensive judgment:
- The probability that the dealer model will be completely eliminated within 5 years: 5-10%
- The probability that the industry will continue to be depressed but the leader will survive: 40-50%
- The probability that the industry bottoms out and the leader benefits: 30-40%
- The probability of Zhongsheng successfully transforming into a comprehensive new energy service provider: 15-25%

The expected value calculation shows that the risk-to-return ratio of investing in the current valuation is neutral to positive, but it is far from a "certain opportunity."

## 5. Reverse thinking: Under what circumstances am I wrong?

### It’s wrong to be bullish

- The price war lasted for more than 3 years, and Zhongsheng’s cash reserves were exhausted
- New energy vehicle companies have fully shifted to direct sales, and the inter-industry cooperation model has been abandoned.
- The simplification of after-sales services for electric vehicles has led to a sharp decline in after-sales revenue
- Management made strategic mistakes and invested too much in the wrong brands

### If being short is wrong

- The industry price war will ease in 2026, and the gross profit margin of new cars will quickly return to positive levels.
- Wenjie/Geely cooperation exceeded expectations, and profits contributed by new energy sources grew rapidly
- The industry reshuffle is accelerating, and Zhongsheng’s market share has increased significantly.
- Policies impose restrictions on direct sales models to protect dealer channels

## 7. Summary

Summarize Zhongsheng Holdings’ position in the industry in one sentence: **It is the sturdiest house in a storm, but we are not sure whether there will be a need for houses on this land after the storm. **

The industry is experiencing a cruel survival of the fittest. Guanghui's delisting, Zhengtong's struggles, and the closure of tens of thousands of 4S stores - these all prove that "the weak are on the way out." Zhongsheng has a high probability of surviving with its abundant cash, strong after-sales base, and active new energy layout.

But "surviving" and "living well" are two different things. Whether Zhongsheng can re-find its growth engine and rebuild profitability in the new energy era depends on: (1) the effectiveness of cooperation with new energy brands such as Wenjie; (2) whether after-sales services can smoothly transition from fuel vehicles to electric vehicles; (3) when the industry price war will end.

**The decision investors need to make is not "whether Zhongsheng is a good company" (it is the best in the industry), but "whether this industry is worth treating." **
