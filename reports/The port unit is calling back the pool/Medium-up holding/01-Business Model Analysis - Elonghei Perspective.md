# Analysis of business model of Zhongsheng Holdings (0881.HK)

> Analysis dimensions: business essence, user value, moat, corporate culture
> Core question: Does the dealer model still have value in the new energy era? Is it a good business?

---

## 1. What is the essence of this business?

Car dealers are essentially a "middleman" business - connecting upstream OEMs and downstream consumers, providing one-stop services such as display, sales, delivery, after-sales, and finance. As one of the largest luxury car dealers in China, Zhongsheng Holdings will operate more than 400 stores and sell nearly 500,000 new cars in 2025.

From a business logic perspective, the dealer’s value chain has three layers:

1. **New car sales**: The essence is "moving bricks" - purchasing goods from OEMs and selling them to consumers at a markup or at a low price. Profit margins depend entirely on brand premium and supply and demand. The gross profit margin of new cars has dropped to -3% in 2025, indicating that this level is no longer making money or even making money.

2. **After-sales Service**: This is the dealer's "cash cow". Zhongsheng's after-sales gross profit in 2025 will be 11.05 billion yuan, a year-on-year increase of 8.2%. The after-sales gross profit alone can cover the company's entire operating costs. A car is a product that "you start spending money after you buy it". Maintenance, repairs and spare parts are continuous income.

3. **Value-added services**: including car finance commission, insurance agency, second-hand car replacement, etc. This part has high profit margins but is greatly affected by regulation. Financial commissions will plummet by 38.7% in 2025.

**Key Judgment**: If you only look at new car sales, this is not a good business - heavy on assets, low gross profit, and restricted by upstream. But if dealers are regarded as "comprehensive automobile service platforms", the stickiness and sustainability of after-sales services give them certain commercial value.

## 2. Where is the user value?

Buying a car doesn't end with the purchase. Users need more than just a car, they also need:

- **Purchasing experience**: one-stop test drive, options, financial plan, and registration
- **Continuous Service**: regular maintenance, warranty repairs, accident claims
- **Asset Disposal**: Used car evaluation, replacement, trade-in

Zhongsheng's core user group is luxury car owners - this group of people have requirements for service quality, are relatively less price sensitive, and value convenience and trust more. Zhongsheng luxury brand sales will account for 62.6% in 2025. This user base itself has a high lifetime value.

**But new energy is changing this logic. ** Tesla’s direct sales model, BYD’s low-price strategy, and Huawei’s online + offline ecosystem are all bypassing traditional dealers. New energy car owners are younger, more inclined to make decisions online, and less dependent on 4S stores. In terms of after-sales, the maintenance frequency and complexity of electric vehicles are much lower than that of fuel vehicles. Without conventional consumables such as engine oil, transmission oil, and spark plugs, after-sales revenue will naturally shrink.

**This is the biggest structural threat to the business model. **

## 3. Moat analysis

### Favorable factors

**1. Scale effect ★★★☆☆**

Zhongsheng is the industry leader, with revenue of 164.4 billion yuan in 2025, ranking among the top among Chinese auto dealers. But the car dealer market is highly fragmented—Zhongsheng’s market share is only about 2-3%. The purchasing advantage brought by scale is limited because the pricing power lies in the hands of the OEM. The real advantages of scale lie in: refined management of operational efficiency, talent training system, and cross-regional brand portfolio deployment capabilities.

**2. Brand relationship network ★★★☆☆**

Zhongsheng has long-term cooperative relationships with mainstream brands such as Mercedes-Benz, BMW, Audi, Lexus, and Toyota. Obtaining the qualifications for authorized stores is itself a barrier. But this barrier is "semi-passive" - ​​the authorization is in the hands of the brand, and the brand can increase or decrease the number of authorized outlets. BMW and Mercedes-Benz each reduced their business outlets by more than 100 in 2025, indicating that the brands are also shrinking.

**3. After-sales service stickiness ★★★★☆**

This is the most realistic moat. Car owners tend to have their cars maintained at authorized dealers during the warranty period, and Zhongsheng’s millions of retained customer base generates continuous after-sales revenue. After-sales gross profit margin will continue to increase in 2025, indicating that the pricing power of this business is still strong. However, in the long term, the increase in the penetration rate of electric vehicles will erode this advantage.

**4. Regional Density ★★★☆☆**

Zhongsheng has a high density of stores in key cities, forming a certain regional service network effect. When customers change cars, they tend to do so within the same dealer group, as evidenced by the annual sales of 220,000 used cars.

### Disadvantages

**1. Lack of pricing power**

Dealers are at a weak link in the industry chain. Upstream OEMs determine wholesale prices and rebate policies, and downstream consumers have full price comparison capabilities. The gross profit margin of new cars in 2025 will be -3%, indicating that dealers have become a "reservoir" for brands to digest inventory.

**2. Brand switching costs are high**

Moving from a BBA (Mercedes-Benz, BMW, Audi) to a new energy brand requires renovating stores, retraining employees, and adjusting the supply chain - all of which require real money investment. Zhongsheng has converted some Mercedes-Benz/BMW stores into Wenjie stores. The cost of each renovation is not low, and the profitability of the new brand has yet to be verified.

**3. Asset-heavy model**

Dealers need to hold a large amount of inventory (complete vehicles + spare parts), which takes up a lot of funds. Although Zhongsheng's cash balance of 20.4 billion yuan and operating cash flow of 9.4 billion yuan appear healthy, the risk of inventory depreciation continues to exist in the context of price wars.

## 4. Corporate culture and business philosophy

Zhongsheng was founded in Dalian in 1998 by Huang Yi and Li Guoqiang. It has developed from an independent 4S store into a national dealer group and was listed on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange in 2010. The two founders hold approximately 49% of the shares in total, and their interests are deeply tied to the company.

Judging from public information, Zhongsheng's business style is more "pragmatic and steady":

- Focused on the luxury brand track for a long time and did not blindly expand into the low-end market
- Decisively cooperate with Thalys to introduce industry boundaries by the end of 2024, reflecting strategic flexibility
- Although there will be a large impairment of 2.291 billion yuan in 2025, the proactive liquidation shows that the management does not avoid the problem.
- Operating cash flow improved significantly (+5.966 billion yuan), indicating that operational management capabilities are still solid

**But there are also points worthy of attention**: No dividends will be paid in 2025 (previously, dividends were paid continuously), indicating that management has insufficient confidence in short-term profit recovery and needs to retain cash to cope with the transformation.

## 5. Key question: Is the dealer model obsolete in the new energy era?

This is the core soul torture.

**Bearish logic**: New energy car companies are increasingly leaning towards direct sales (Tesla, NIO) or online + city showroom models (Xiaomi), and the existing value of the traditional 4S store model is declining. Simplifying the after-sales service of electric vehicles will erode dealers’ most profitable after-sales business. In the long run, dealers may be eliminated by the times like film developing shops.

**Bull logic**: The number of cars in China exceeds 300 million, and the after-sales demand for existing cars will not disappear overnight. New energy vehicle companies have also found that the pure direct sales model is too costly - Wenjie's choice to cooperate with Zhongsheng is itself a "return" to the dealer model. Huawei brands need to quickly expand their sales networks, and the channel capabilities of large dealer groups are scarce resources.

**Balance Judgment**: The dealer model will not "die", but it will "downsize" and "deform". From "selling new cars to earn the price difference" to "comprehensive service platform", from "dependence on brand authorization" to "flexible combination of multiple brands". Whether it can be converted successfully depends on execution and speed.

## 7. Summary

The business model of Zhongsheng Holdings is in a transitional period where the old logic has collapsed and the new logic has not yet been established. The old logic is "earn price difference by licensing luxury brands + after-sales service". This model has been broken in 2025 - new cars lose money and financial commissions are cut in half. The new logic is "multi-brand portfolio + new energy penetration + comprehensive services". Industry cooperation is a good start, but it is far from being large-scale.

As an investment target, the most critical question is not "whether this business is profitable now", but "whether this business will still exist and in what form five years from now." If the dealer model is proven to still have irreplaceable value in the new energy era, then the low valuation of the leading company will be a huge opportunity; if the dealer model is completely subverted, no matter how cheap the valuation is, it will be a value trap.

**Conclusion: The business model is at a structural inflection point and is not a "good business" in the traditional sense. However, the direction of transformation is clear and the execution ability remains to be seen. **
