# II. The source and sustainability of the moat

Manger said, "We need more than a moat, but we need a moat that is growing. " China’s safe moat has many sources, and we look at it one by one.

# Accompanied by River I: Full license combination of financial barriers #

** Description:** China has 13 core financial licences, which cover the entire spectrum of insurance (life, maternity, old-age, health), banking, securities, trust, management, and funds. This is one of the most fully licensed groups in China’s financial sector.

** Why is it difficult to copy?**
- Financial licence plates are subject to strict regulatory approval, new applications are extremely difficult
- Even with a license plate, synergies between operations need to be over 10 years old.
- Average number of contracts of 2.94 (1.8 in industry) with 99 per cent retention rate for customers of products of 3 or more - this cross-marketing capacity takes more than a decade to "breath"
- No second company can get the whole chain connected by insurance plus banks plus securities plus financials plus technology plus medicine.

The license plate is a scarce resource granted by regulation, and the capacity for integrated financial synergies needs to be built up over the long term. Unless regulatory policies change fundamentally (e.g., mandatory break-up of financial groups), the moat is largely insurmountable.

** Risk point:** Integrated finance also means "risk transmission" - if one section is problematic (e.g., a surge in bank non-performing loans), it may affect the others through intra-group linkages.

# Accompaniment II: The size of the insurance industry and brand barriers #

** Description:** China’s São Paulo brand is worth $29.6 billion, with China’s insurance industry leading for 11 years. The size of 251 million individual clients means a huge data advantage and a capacity to share costs.

** Why is it important?**
- Insurance is a "trust selling" business. Brand is a trust carrier.
- Cost advantages of scale: the same IT input, actuarial team, compliance costs, more insurance policies, lower unit costs
- Data advantages support more precise pricing and wind control

** Sustainability assessment: ** High. Branding requires long-term input and accumulation, which cannot be built overnight. But branding can also be destroyed by scandal.

# Three of the moats: medically old-age ecology closed #

** Description:** Safe cumulative investment of over RMB 50 billion in the construction of a health management model for HMO+ family doctors + O2O. Safe doctors, home-based old-age, high-end old-age communities form the ecological isolation of "insurance + services".

** Why the moat?**
- 93 per cent retention rate of clients using medical old-age services, significantly higher than the rate of unserved clients
- 18.3 million people have been using health services for life-risk clients
- These services increase the differentiation of insurance products - clients buy more than just a policy, but a package of health management programmes
- Tekang is doing a similar layout, but the scale of the peace and the combined financial advantages make it more ecologically complete.

** Sustainability assessment: ** Medium and high. Medical ageing is a long-slow track with clear pre-eminence. But this area is a huge investment, a long-term return cycle and faces policy uncertainties.

# The moat of the river: technology and data barriers #

** Description:** Continuously invested in artificial intelligence, big data, cloud computing, etc., with more than 40,000 patents.

** Objective assessment:**
- Technology inputs have indeed increased operational efficiency (e.g. smart claims have reduced processing time from days to minutes level)
But technology does not constitute a lasting moat -- technology will be pursued, algorithms will be mimicned.
- The real barrier is not technology itself, but the combination of technology plus data plus scenes -- the cumulative data of 251 million clients is hard to replicate.

** Sustainability assessment: ** Medium. Technology is a accelerator, not a moat itself, and requires sustained investment to stay ahead.

# Five for the moat: "Deduction efficiency" reforms from proxy channels #

** Description:** Safely spearheaded the most painful proxy reform in the industry — from 1.2 million to about 350,000 people, with a significant increase in per capita productive capacity.

** Why the moat?**
- The competition is still in the reform. Peace is out.
- 350,000 "Elegant Agent" with a 29.9% increase in the value of new business per capita
- Experience gained in the reform process (training system, appraisal system, technology enabling system) constitutes an organizational barrier

** Sustainability assessment: ** Medium and high. The reform results will be pursued, but the early advantage of peace will remain at least two to three years.

# IV. Trends and changes in industry

# Trends I: Long-termization of the low interest rate environment

** Impact:**** This is the biggest structural challenge facing the insurance sector. Low interest rates reduce the rate of return on investment, which may lead to a loss of profit risk.

** Peaceful response:**
- Downscaling of the long-term investment return assumption to 4.5 per cent (more prudent)
- Increase in the allocation of high-equity assets and long-term bonds
- Promote a shift in product from traditional to red (transfer of investment risk to clients)
- Management clearly says "no profit margin."

** Impact of the moat: ** Neutral bias. ** Low interest rates are not good for the industry, but they are better able to cope with them than small and medium-sized enterprises - the larger the size, the more professional the investment team can handle them.

# Trends II: Accelerating population ageing

** Impact:** China has over 15 per cent of the population aged 65 and over, and the need for old-age and health insurance continues to grow.

** Peaceful response:**
- Early and high-intensity medical and old-age ecological isolation (RM$50 billion+)
- The "insurance plus old age" model is becoming competitively differentiated.
- Home-based and institutional ageing

** Impact of the moat: ** Positive. Ageing is a structural benefit for the insurance sector, and the early release of safe health care for old age places it in a favourable position in this wave.

# Trend three: the silver insurance pipeline rises

** Impact:** The NBV channel, the safe and secure silver channel, increased by 138 per cent in 2025, becoming a new growth engine.

** Peaceful response:**
- Safe Banks is naturally the biggest silver bank channel - a unique advantage of integrated finance.
- Other risk companies rely on third-party banking channels, with high commission costs and uncontrollable

** Affect of the moat: ** Positive. Own banking channels are the only structural advantage of safety.

# Trend IV: Technology Re-engineering Industry

** Impact:** AI's application in the Nuclear Insurance, Compensation, Services, Investment and Other components is changing the operating model of the insurance industry.

** Peaceful response:**
- As the largest industrial technology investment, safe ahead of intelligence.
- AI enabling agents to upgrade per capita capacity
- But the marginality of technology is diminishing, and the competition is catching up.

**Caveland impact:** Short-term positive, medium-term. Technology's advantage is pursued, and the key is whether or not to stay ahead.

# Trends V: Rigorous and normative regulation

** Impact:** Regulatory policies such as lower scheduled interest rates, consolidated reporting lines and new solvency regulations continue to tighten.

** Peaceful response:**
- Large companies are naturally superior to small and medium-sized companies in terms of compliance and capital adequacy
- The tightening of regulation is essentially helping head companies consolidate their market position - "compatibilities costs" become a barrier to access.

** Impact of the moat:** Positive. The tightening of regulations has contributed to increased concentration in the industry, benefiting leading companies.
