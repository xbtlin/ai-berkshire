# Ping An of China: Business Model Analysis - "Good Business" Perspective

> Core question: Is Ping An a good business? Can you understand it? Can it be held for a long time?

---

## 1. Is this a good business?

### Conclusion: It’s a good business, but not the best one

Duan Yongping repeatedly emphasized the criteria for a "good business": the business model is simple and easy to understand, you have pricing power, your customers can't live without you, and you have good cash flow. Using this ruler to measure China's Ping An, the answer is "above the standard", but it is not a perfect score.

**Positive arguments:**

1. **The essence of insurance is "collect money first and then provide services". ** Premiums are collected in advance and claims are deferred, which naturally creates a huge amount of float. As of the end of 2025, Ping An's insurance capital investment scale reached 6.49 trillion yuan - this is an almost zero-cost long-term capital pool used for investment to obtain income. Buffett used Berkshire to prove the power of this model, and Ping An is the company closest to this model in China.

2. **Integrated finance creates a flywheel effect. ** 251 million individual customers, with an average customer holding 2.94 contracts (the industry average is 1.8), and the retention rate of customers holding 4 or more contracts is 98%. This means that customers who have sold insurance will also buy bank financing, credit cards, securities, and trust products. With each additional product, customers become more sticky and the marginal cost of acquiring customers is lower.

3. **The brand value has ranked first in China’s insurance industry for 11 consecutive years. ** Brand value in 2025 reaches US$29.6 billion. In an industry that "sells trust" like insurance, brand is the biggest intangible asset.

**Negative Arguments:**

1. **Insurance is not a "win-win" business. ** Different from Moutai's model of "it's over once the product is sold", insurance companies need continuous actuarial management, investment management, and claims management. Any error in any link may erode profits. China Fortune Land Development's RMB 54 billion impairment is a bloody lesson.

2. **Profit is highly sensitive to investment income. ** In the first quarter of 2026, net profit attributable to the parent company fell 7.4% year-on-year only because the comprehensive investment rate of return fell by 1.1 percentage points year-on-year. This illustrates that profits are much more volatile than for consumer products companies.

3. **Regulatory risks always exist. ** The insurance industry is strictly supervised by the China Banking and Insurance Regulatory Commission, and policy changes such as scheduled interest rate adjustments, agent management regulations, and solvency requirements may have a substantial impact on the profit model.

## 2. Is the business model easy to understand?

### Conclusion: The core is understandable, but the complexity is high

**Understandable part:**

Ping An's core business logic can be summarized in one sentence: **Sell insurance to collect premiums (liability side) + Use premiums to invest and make money (asset side) + Cross-sell other financial products (comprehensive finance)**.

Specifically, the group's business is divided into five major sectors:

| Business Segment | Main Indicators in 2025 | Proportion of Group Profit |
|---------|--------------|-------------|
| Life and health insurance | NBV 36.9 billion yuan, +29.3% | Core profit sources |
| Property insurance | Premium income of RMB 343.2 billion, combined ratio 96.8% | Stable contributor |
| Ping An Bank | Net profit 42.6 billion yuan, -4.2% | Important but under pressure |
| Asset Management | Loss narrowed | Still in the adjustment period |
| Technology business | Lufax, OneConnect, etc. | Strategic layout period |

**The difficult part to understand:**

1. **The profit calculation of insurance companies is extremely complicated. ** Under the new IFRS 17 standard, the revenue recognition method has undergone fundamental changes, and even professional investors will need time to adapt. The difference between operating profit and net profit, and the adjustment of actuarial assumptions in embedded value, all add to the difficulty of understanding.

2. **Portfolio complexity. ** The RMB 6.49 trillion investment portfolio covers multiple asset classes such as bonds, stocks, real estate, and infrastructure, each with its own risk-return characteristics. It is difficult for outside investors to accurately assess the true quality of a portfolio.

3. **Cross-subsidy of comprehensive finance. ** The synergies and cross-subsidies between the various segments within the group make it difficult to accurately separate the true profitability of a single segment.

**What would Duan Yongping think? ** He will most likely say: "I can understand the general logic of insurance, but it is too difficult to figure out all the details. This is not the best business to understand within my circle of competence." This is one of the reasons why Duan Yongping has never invested heavily in insurance stocks.

## 3. What is the quality of free cash flow?

### Conclusion: Cash flow is abundant but needs to be differentiated

**The "free cash flow" of insurance companies requires special understanding. ** The traditional concept of free cash flow (FCF = operating cash flow - capital expenditures) does not fully apply to insurance companies, since an insurance company's "inventory" is policy liabilities and "capital expenditures" is mainly solvency capital.

**Positive indicators:**

1. **The ability to pay dividends is the best proof of cash flow quality. ** Cash dividends in 2025 will be RMB 48.9 billion, increasing for 14 consecutive years, and dividends will account for 36.4% of the operating profit attributable to the parent company. A company that can increase dividends for 14 consecutive years will not have poor cash flow quality.

2. **The stability of operating profit is better than net profit. ** Operating profit attributable to parent companies in 2025 is RMB 134.4 billion, a year-on-year increase of 10.3% - operating profit excludes short-term investment fluctuations and one-time items, and can better reflect the cash creation ability of ongoing operations.

3. **Predictability of premium income. ** The high proportion of renewal premiums and the customer retention rate of 99% (holding more than 3 types of products) mean that there is a large amount of predictable cash inflow every year.

**Negative Indicators:**

1. ** China Fortune Land Development has swallowed up a lot of resources. ** The cumulative risk exposure is approximately RMB 54 billion, of which approximately RMB 43.2 billion has been impaired, and the remaining exposure is approximately RMB 10.8 billion. The failure of this investment directly eroded free cash flow over the past few years.

2. ** Solvency requirements lock in some capital. **Insurers must maintain statutory solvency ratios, which means not all profits can be freely distributed to shareholders.

3. **Bank subsidiaries are capital consumers. ** Ping An Bank needs to continue to replenish capital to meet regulatory requirements. Net profit will still decline (-4.2%) in 2025, which is a net consumption rather than a net contribution to the group.

## 4. Customer stickiness and switching costs

### Conclusion: Customer stickiness is very strong, and switching costs are relatively high in the financial industry.

**Sticky evidence (data speaks):**

| Indicators | Data | Description |
|------|------|------|
| Number of individual customers | 251 million | 1 out of every 6 Chinese people is a Ping An customer |
| Number of contracts per customer | 2.94 | Far exceeding the industry average of 1.8 |
|Customer retention rate for products of more than 3 categories | 99% | Almost no churn |
| Customer retention rate for products in category 4 or above | 98% | Extremely high stickiness |
| Medical and elderly care service customer retention rate | 93% | Service ecology enhances stickiness |

**Switching cost source:**

1. **Natural lock-in of long-term insurance policies. ** The term of life insurance policies is usually 20-30 years, and surrendering the policy midway will result in large losses (the cash value is much lower than the premiums paid). This is an institutional switching cost.

2. **Multi-product bundling of comprehensive finance. ** When a customer holds Ping An’s life insurance, auto insurance, bank cards, and wealth management products at the same time, the cost of migrating to another company is huge—not only the cost of money, but also the cost of time and information reconstruction costs.

3. **The service dependence of the medical and elderly care ecosystem. ** Ping An has invested more than RMB 50 billion in total to build the "HMO + family doctor + O2O" medical and health management model. Once customers become accustomed to Ping An Good Doctor's consultations, Ping An Health Insurance's claims settlement, and home care services, it will be difficult to switch to competitors that do not have these facilities.

**Limitations of stickiness:**

1. **Price-sensitive product lines have low stickiness. ** For standardized products such as auto insurance, the cost for customers to switch to PICC and CPIC is very low, and competition mainly depends on price.

2. **Banking business is less sticky than insurance. ** It is very convenient to move deposits and change credit cards in China. The customer stickiness of Ping An Bank is obviously weaker than that of the insurance business.

3. The **tech ecosystem has not yet developed strong stickiness. ** Lufax has a high customer churn rate, Autohome faces competition, and the customer stickiness of the technology sector still needs to be verified.

## 5. Business model summary score

| Dimensions | Rating | Description |
|------|------|------|
| Business quality | ★★★★ | The insurance float model is a good business, but profits fluctuate greatly |
| Comprehensibility | ★★★ | The general logic is clear, but the financial details are complicated |
| Cash flow quality | ★★★★ | Increased dividends for 14 consecutive years, operating profits are stable |
| Customer stickiness | ★★★★★ | 251 million customers, 99% retention rate, top in the industry |
| **Overall Rating** | **★★★★** | **Good business, but not the easiest to understand good business** |

**One sentence summary:** Ping An's business model is essentially a "cash collection + capital allocation" machine. The core engine is insurance float, comprehensive finance is the amplifier, and medical and elderly care is the moat deepener. If you believe that China's economy will be improving in the long term and residents' insurance density will continue to increase, this is a good business. But it's not a "forget it" kind of business - it requires good management to continually navigate a complex balance sheet.
