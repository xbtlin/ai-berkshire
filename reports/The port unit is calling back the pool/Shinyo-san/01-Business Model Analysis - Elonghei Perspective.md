# Xinyi Solar (0968.HK) Business Model Analysis

> Analysis dimensions: business model quality, moat depth, user value, long-term competitiveness
> Study date: May 16, 2026

---

## 1. The nature of business: What kind of business is this?

### 1.1 Business model description

Xinyi Solar's core business is the manufacturing and sales of photovoltaic glass, accounting for approximately 85% of total revenue. Photovoltaic glass is the key packaging material for photovoltaic modules and is located in the auxiliary material link of the photovoltaic industry chain. The company also operates a small amount of photovoltaic power station business (through its holding subsidiary Xinyi Energy).

This is essentially an asset-heavy manufacturing business. The investment for a 1,000 tons/day photovoltaic glass production line is about 1.0-1.5 billion yuan, the construction period is 18-24 months, and the kiln life is 8-10 years. Once ignited, it cannot be stopped and production must be continuous 24 hours a day.

### 1.2 Product Features

The degree of product differentiation of photovoltaic glass is low and it is essentially a bulk industrial product. Customers (photovoltaic module manufacturers) are mainly concerned about:
- Product quality stability (light transmittance, intensity)
- Supply capability and delivery reliability
- price

This is not a "differentiation" business, but a "cost leadership" business.

### 1.3 The core question from Duan Yongping’s perspective: Can this business be “winnable”?

**Frankly, no. ** This is not a business where you can "lay down and make money" like Maotai and Apple. It has no brand premium, no network effects, and no high switching costs.

But within the scope of manufacturing, this is a **relatively good manufacturing business** because:
- The entry barrier is not low (capital intensive + process barriers + production capacity approval control)
- The industry has formed a stable duopoly structure
- The cost advantage of leading companies comes from structural sources

## 2. Moat analysis: sources and sustainability of competitive advantage

### 2.1 Economies of scale moat ★★★★☆

The core moat of Xinyi Solar is the cost advantage brought by economies of scale**. The specific performance is:

**Kiln scale advantage:** The average scale of a single kiln of the company is about 1,009 tons/day, and 1,000-ton kilns account for about 91%. The average single kiln size in the industry is only 200-300 tons/day. The larger the kiln, the lower the unit energy consumption - the natural gas consumption of a 900-ton production line and a 500-ton production line is not much different, but the output gap is huge.

**Raw material procurement advantages:** The combined raw material procurement scale of Xinyi Solar and parent company Xinyi Glass far exceeds that of any competitor, and they have significant bargaining power on bulk raw materials such as soda ash and quartz sand.

**Energy cost advantage:** The company uses pipelines to directly supply natural gas, eliminating the need for intermediate price increases. Fuel costs account for 25-30% of photovoltaic glass production costs, and this advantage has a significant impact.

**Logistics cost advantage:** The Wuhu factory is close to the Yangtze River and has its own dock. It transports soda ash and quartz sand by water, which saves a lot of transportation costs compared with land transportation. The Beihai factory in Guangxi is close to the source of low-iron sand resources.

### 2.2 Barriers to production capacity approval ★★★☆☆

In 2025, six departments issued the "Work Plan for Stable Growth of the Building Materials Industry", which clearly proposed to strengthen the management and control of photovoltaic rolled glass production capacity. Approval of new production lines has become more difficult, which provides a policy moat for existing capacity holders.

However, it should be noted that the sustainability of this moat depends on the direction of policy and there is uncertainty.

### 2.3 Customer stickiness ★★☆☆☆

Photovoltaic module manufacturers have low supplier switching costs. Photovoltaic glass is a standardized product, and the certification cycle is about 3-6 months. Large customers usually maintain 2-3 suppliers in parallel. Xinyi Solar's customer stickiness mainly comes from the supply reliability brought by its production capacity scale, rather than product differentiation.

### 2.4 Comprehensive assessment of moat ★★★☆☆

Xinyi Solar's moat is weak compared to the brand moats of consumer goods companies. But it is at an upper-middle level in the manufacturing industry. The key point is: the source of cost advantage is structural (kiln size, location, resources) and cannot be easily copied quickly.

## 3. User value: Why do downstream customers choose Xinyi?

### 3.1 Supply reliability

The production lines of photovoltaic module manufacturers cannot run out of materials, and Xinyi Solar's 23,200 tons/day production capacity provides the most reliable supply guarantee. During periods of tight supply in the industry (such as 2020-2021), large customers are more likely to lock in long-term orders with leading players.

### 3.2 Quality consistency

The process stability of large kiln production lines is better than that of small kilns. Xinyi Lighting’s decades of experience in glass manufacturing (born out of Xinyi Glass, established in 1988) has accumulated in-depth process knowledge.

### 3.3 Global supply capabilities

With the layout of production capacity in Malaysia and Indonesia, Xinyi Solar can provide localized supplies to overseas component manufacturers and avoid trade barriers. This is of great value in the current anti-globalization trend.

---

## 4. Weaknesses of the business model

### 4.1 Strong cyclicality

Photovoltaic glass prices fluctuate violently. The high point in 2021 is about 40 yuan/square meter, and the low point in 2024 is about 20 yuan/square meter. This cyclical nature means that company profits fluctuate wildly, and even leading companies cannot completely avoid them.

### 4.2 Lack of pricing power

Photovoltaic glass is essentially a bulk product. Although Xinyi Solar is the leader, it does not have real pricing power. Prices are determined by industry supply and demand, and companies can only maintain profits by driving down costs.

### 4.3 Capital intensive

Continued expansion requires significant capital expenditures. If the production line encounters an industry downturn (such as the current one) after it is put into operation, the investment return period will be extended and fixed assets will face the risk of impairment. The company's provision for fixed asset impairment of 1.55 billion yuan in 2025 is proof of this.

### 4.4 Risk of technology substitution

New cell technologies such as perovskites may change the characteristics of demand for photovoltaic glass. If perovskite commercialization accelerates, existing production lines may require large-scale modifications.

## 5. "Right" business vs. "wrong" business

Duan Yongping emphasized doing the "right" thing. Is Xinyi Solar's business "right" or "wrong"?

**Front view:**
- Photovoltaics is a deterministic long-term growth track, and the demand for photovoltaic glass will increase in the long term.
- The industry structure has become stable and the leading position is not easily overturned.
- The company focuses on its main business and does not blindly diversify
- Management has decades of experience in the industry

**Negative view:**
- This is not an "easy" business and requires continuous and efficient operations
- Cyclicality makes it difficult to "hold for the long term"
- The moat is not deep enough to completely isolate competition

## 7. Core Conclusions

Xinyi Solar is an asset-heavy manufacturing business that is on a good track, run by experienced people, and has structural cost advantages.

It is not the kind of business that you can "forget" after buying it - you need to track industry supply and demand, the pace of production capacity deployment, and changes in raw material prices. But at the bottom of the current cycle, its cost advantage means that it is the "last one to fall" - when industry profits are squeezed to the extreme, small companies exit with losses, but the leader can still maintain positive cash flow.

From a business model perspective, this is an investment object that is most suitable for buying at the bottom of the cycle and selling at the high point of the cycle, rather than a "buy, hold and never sell" target.
