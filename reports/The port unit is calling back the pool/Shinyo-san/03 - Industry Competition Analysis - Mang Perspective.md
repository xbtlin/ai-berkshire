# I. Business landscape: where are the glazing glass?

#1.1 Industrial chain positioning

Simplified structure of the photovoltaic chain:

```
Silicon, silicon, silicon, battery, battery, assembly, power station.
                           ↑
Photovoltaic glass (encapable accessories)
```

Photovoltaic glass is the core material of the component envelope, which protects the battery plate and the light power generation. Each component must use a single photovoltaic glass on the front side, and a double-sided component needs to be added to the back. Photovoltaic glass represents about 6-8% of the total cost of the component.

##1.2 Demand Driver

The demand for photovoltaic glass depends directly on the volume of the photovoltaic component. The long-term growth trend in the global volume of new photovoltaics is certain - one of the few sectors that are not affected by short-term economic fluctuations.

Global photovoltaics installed in 2025 are estimated to be about 550-600 GW and are expected to reach 800-1,000 GW by 2030. Assuming an average annual growth of 10-15%, demand for photovoltaic glass will continue to grow.

** Key variables: bipolar component penetration.** Two photovoltaic glass (upside + back) per piece of dual-sided power generation component is required, with penetration rising from about 30% in 2020 to about 50% in 2025. This means that the demand for photovoltaic glass is increasing faster than the load.

##1.3 Supply end pattern

This is the core of the analysis. The light glass industry has undergone a shift from "unsequenced expansion" to "manufacturing control."

**2020-2022: surge in production.** The sharp rise in the price of photovoltaic glass in 2020 (a once over 50 yuan/m2) triggered a large number of new entrants to the production line.

**2023-2025: Overcapacity and price falls.** New capacity is concentrated, with excess capacity in the 2024 photovoltaic main chain (silicon, batteries) resulting in a price pressure on component manufacturers, and a price of approximately $20 per square metre per photovoltage glass.

**2025: Production control has become more stringent and clears have accelerated.** Six departments have issued policies on production control and approval of new lines of production have been tightened. Two or three small businesses have lost their output. Shinyo-san also stopped production in Malaysia and proposed a large reduction.

#2: Structural causes of oligarchic formation

** First, kiln scale economy.** There are significant scale effects in optical glass kilns: the unit cost of a thousand-ton kiln is well below three hundred and five hundred tons of small kilns. The KMTs represent 91% of the 91%, while the industry averages much less. The economies of scale constitute a natural entry barrier.

** Second, learning curve effects.** The ignition, commissioning, and steady operation of the kilns requires a rich accumulation of technology. The rates of excellence and energy efficiency of new entrants are significantly lower than those of old players at the beginning of the process.

** Third, financial barriers.** One kiloton of production line is invested in $1 -15 billion, and it takes two years to get to work. In the current period of industry recession, the cost of financing and the willingness of new entrants has declined dramatically.

** Fourth, policy barriers.** The policy and regulatory controls for the replacement of photovoltaic glass production have further increased the threshold for new production capacity. This is beneficial for the leader.

#2.3 Key judgement of the Mang perspective: Will the double-oligarchy pattern be broken?

** Short term (1-3 years): unlikely.** Industry is in a period of overcapacity and low profitability, with no firm having the incentive to build new capacity on a large scale. Conversely, the clean-up continues.

** Mid-term (3-5 years): The pattern is tending to strengthen.** The sector’s low-growth is mainly in the second- or third-line small enterprises, whose share of the taps is also increasing passively without expansion. Historically, industry concentration has increased after each cycle.

** Long term (5 years+): Attention needs to be given to technological variables.** If new technological routes such as the mine are commercialized successfully, the demand characteristics of the glazing glass may change fundamentally. But even if they do, the current tap is the best able and resource to adapt.

# Four, the industrial chain playing

##4.1 Upstream: raw materials supply

The main raw materials for photovoltaic glass are pure alkali (~15%), quartz (~15%) and natural gas (~25-30%).

- **Pull:** Big chemical, well supplied, and leading large-scale procurement with bargaining advantages
- **Praisin (low iron sand):** High-quality low-iron sand resources are concentrated in specific areas (Warn north-west sea, etc.), and altruism is close to the resource area on the production-capability layout
- ** Gas:** Pipeline supply is a key advantage, avoiding broker price increases and supply instability

Shining Light is more competitive with upstream bargaining.

## 4.2 Downstream: Component Vendor

The concentration of the photovoltaic component industry is also increasing (Lonki, Crystal, Sky, Crystal Australia, etc.), and large clients have not been weak in bargaining. But the provision of photovoltaic glass is highly concentrated (50% oligarchs plus), and the switching supplier has certification costs and supply risks, so the bargaining relationship is relatively balanced.

In cases of excess industrial capacity, downstream price pressure is high; in cases of supply stress (e.g. 2020-2021), upstream pricing is clear. This cyclical bargaining power shift is a central cause of industrial volatility.

# V. Trends in key sectors

#5.1 Overseas market premiums - beneficiaries of counter-globalization

The ratio of IKW overseas sales rose from about 25% to 33.5% in 2025, and the volume of overseas glass sales grew by about 36%.

- Countries promote localization of photovoltaic supply chains and supply of overseas capacity.
- Trade barriers have led to higher export costs for China ' s photovoltaic glass production and local chemical capacity premium
- The U.S., Indian, etc. market light-voltage machines are accelerating.

Shinrikyo can make production performances in Malaysia and Indonesia the beneficiaries of the anti-globalization trend. This may be the most important source of growth in the next 3-5 years.

#5.2 Performance control policy - catalyst at the bottom of the industry

The construction materials industry programme, published in 2025 by the six Chinese sectors, clearly strengthens the control of photovoltaic glass production capacity. This means:
- New production capacity is harder to get.
- Stock lags speed up exit.
- To promote a higher market share for the leader

#5.3 Technical overlap - dual-sided components and thinner

The light glass is moving in a thinner, larger, and more radiant direction. This places higher demands on manufacturing processes and further widens the gap between the head and the small business.

#5.4 <unk> -foresee variables

The high containment requirements for thorium cells (anti-hydrooxysine penetration) may require TCO conductor glass to replace traditional photovoltaic glass. If the thorium is commercialized on a large scale within 5-10 years, the existing production line may need to be modified.

** But an objective assessment is needed:** The stubble is still in the middle of a trial phase, possibly 5-10 years from large-scale commercialization. And technology is often incremental, with the leading firms having the most resources to adapt.

# Eight, core conclusion

In Manger's "backward thinking" — what circumstances would the competitive position of Shining Light significantly deteriorate?

** A company has broken through the technology of the knitton kiln and has made large-scale production** — low probability, technical barriers and clearance restrictions make this difficult.
** Fundamental changes in the technical route of the photovoltaic component to remove the need for photovoltaic glass** — the mine may change the nature of the demand, but it is unlikely that complete replacement of the glass will be possible for the foreseeable future
** The vicious price war between the oligarchs** — which would harm both sides, never in history, and the cynicism of the two is the cornerstone of industry stability.
** Strong local competitors in overseas markets** — Short-term lack of technology and accumulation of scale, but long-term concern

In sum, the trade of the faith-based light is solid and growing. The core variable is not whether the competition will defeat it, but when the industry cycle will warm.
