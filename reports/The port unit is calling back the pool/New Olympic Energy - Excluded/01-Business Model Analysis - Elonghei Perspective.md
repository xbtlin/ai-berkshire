# ENN Energy (2688.HK) business model analysis

> Analysis dimensions: business essence, user value, moat durability
> Core question: Is this business good? Can users live without it? Can you continue to make money?

---

## 1. What kind of business is this?

ENN Energy's core business is natural gas distribution - purchasing natural gas from upstream and delivering it to thousands of households and industrial and commercial users through self-built pipeline networks. In essence, it is a "pipeline toll" business, similar to a highway: heavy assets are invested in building a pipeline network in the early stage, and the price difference is earned through stable gas flow in the later stage.

The company's turnover in 2025 will be 111.9 billion yuan, and its business can be divided into three major segments:

**1. Natural gas retail (accounting for approximately 54% of revenue)**
Selling gas to residents and industrial and commercial users, the retail gas volume in 2025 will be 26.6 billion cubic meters, +1.5% year-on-year. This is the basic market, characterized by stable volume, thin profits, and is greatly affected by government pricing. The proportion of residents complying with the price has reached 71.6%, but nearly 30% are still not fully complying with the price, which means that the company has to bear part of the cost when upstream prices increase.

**2. Natural gas wholesale and platform trading (accounting for about 30% of revenue)**
The wholesale gas volume was 10.187 billion cubic meters (+36.7%), and the platform trading gas volume was 5.425 billion cubic meters (-1.6%). The impressive growth rate of wholesale business reflects the company’s advantage in acquiring upstream resources - it has signed a long-term agreement for 10 million tons of LNG per year, and will begin to implement a 900,000-ton/year agreement with Cheniere in 2026 (linked to the HH price index). This part of the profit elasticity is greater, but it also bears the risk of international gas price fluctuations.

**3. Engineering installation and value-added services (accounting for approximately 16% of revenue)**
The engineering installation business is under downward pressure from real estate, with revenue and gross profit falling by 17.9% and 12.3% respectively in 2025. Value-added services (ubiquitous energy business, integrated energy) are the second curve for which the company has high hopes, but its current contribution is limited.

## 2. User value: Do users need it?

**Positive review ★★★★☆**

Natural gas is a necessity. There are no substitutes for cooking, heating and industrial energy in the short term. China's natural gas consumption is expected to grow by 4%-6% annually, reaching 550-600 billion cubic meters in 2030. ENN covers more than 250 cities across the country and serves tens of millions of users. Once the pipeline network is laid, it is almost impossible for users to switch to another gas company - this is a natural regional monopoly.

The reason why users cannot live without ENN is simple: the gas pipeline in your community is ENN, so you can only use ENN gas. This lock-in effect does not rely on brand or technology, but on the physical pipeline network.

**Reverse view**

But users do not "like" ENN. The gas company is similar to the water company in the eyes of users - they don't think about it when they use it, and they scold it when something goes wrong. Users have no loyalty, they just have no choice. This means that ENN has no pricing power, and prices are controlled by the government. What it earns is "reasonable profits under control" rather than "the premium that users are willing to pay."

## 3. Moat analysis

### 3.1 Regional Monopoly Franchise ★★★★☆

City gas implements a franchise system, and usually only one enterprise is authorized in an area. The franchise rights obtained by ENN cover more than 250 cities, and the duration of these rights is generally 30 years. This is the core moat - if competitors want to come in, they must wait for the franchise to expire, and after expiration, existing operators usually have priority to renew the contract.

**But this moat is not monolithic. ** Franchise rights are granted by local governments, and policy changes (such as giving priority to state-owned enterprises, anti-monopoly) may erode the status of private enterprises. In recent years, Kunlun Energy (a central enterprise) has increasingly obvious advantages in bidding for new projects.

### 3.2 Pipe network asset barriers ★★★★☆

Laying out a network of pipes requires huge capital expenditures and a construction period of several years. ENN's existing pipeline network is the product of sunk costs, and latecomers will need to reinvest even if they obtain the concession. This is a typical "first-mover advantage + heavy asset barriers".

### 3.3 Upstream resource acquisition capability ★★★☆☆

ENN has signed a long-term agreement for 10 million tons of LNG per year and owns the Zhoushan LNG receiving terminal. It is one of the few private companies with the ability to acquire upstream resources. This gives ENN an advantage over purely downstream urban gas companies in terms of gas source costs.

**But there is a significant gap compared with central enterprises. ** Kunlun Energy is backed by PetroChina and has natural advantages in wholesale and retail integration. Xinao's upstream resources are "bought with money", while Kunlun's upstream resources are "own".

### 3.4 Value-added Services/Ubiquitous Energy Business ★★☆☆☆

Wang Yusuo’s vision is that the revenue from digital business and natural gas business will be 1:1 in 2029. However, so far, the contribution of ubiquitous energy and integrated energy services is still limited, and the business model has not yet been fully developed. This is more of a "story" than a proven moat. As an investor, you need to focus on actual progress rather than management's forward promises.

## 4. The "good" and "bad" aspects of business models

### Good things

**1. Cash flow is highly predictable. ** Natural gas distribution is a "water selling" business, with stable downstream demand and continued positive operating cash flow. In 2024, the operating cash flow will be 10.3 billion yuan, the net profit to cash ratio is 1.72, and the profit content is high.

**2. Growth certainty is high. ** There is still room for improvement in China's natural gas penetration rate. The "coal-to-gas" policy continues to advance, and the industry's annual growth rate of 4%-6% is highly certain. As a leading company, ENN’s growth rate is likely to outperform the industry.

**3. The economies of scale are significant. ** The wider the pipeline network coverage, the lower the unit gas transmission cost. The coverage network in more than 250 cities brings ENN’s procurement bargaining power and operational efficiency advantages.

### Bad aspects

**1. No pricing power. ** This is the most fundamental problem. Prices are controlled by the government, and if you don't comply with the price in a timely manner, you will have to bear the cost increase. In 2025, the proportion of residents following the price will be 71.6%, which means that there is still nearly 30% loss risk exposure.

**2. Asset-heavy, low profit margin. ** The gross profit margin is only 11.9%, and the net profit margin is about 5.3%. A large amount of capital is deposited in pipeline network assets. ROE 15% looks good, but it partially relies on financial leverage.

**3. Policy risks are uncontrollable. ** As a private enterprise, in the general environment of "the state advances and the private sector retreats", ENN faces uncertainty in its franchise renewal and new project bidding. Kunlun Energy’s rapid rise in recent years has proven this.

**4. The shrinkage of the engineering and installation business is irreversible. ** The downturn in real estate has led to a decrease in new connection users, and the shrinkage of this high-margin business will continue to drag down overall profitability.

## 5. Duan Yongping’s questioning: Is this business worth holding for a long time?

When Duan Yongping looks at business, he looks at two core points: **whether the user value is real** and **whether the business model is sustainable**.

**User Value**: Real, but "low-level". Natural gas is a necessity, but there is no brand premium. Users use you not because they like it, but because they have no choice. What this kind of business earns is "monopoly rent", not "return on value creation".

**Sustainability**: Better, but has a ceiling. The demand for natural gas distribution will continue to grow for 10-15 years, but in the long term, electrification and new energy may gradually replace natural gas. What ENN faces is not the risk of "die tomorrow", but the risk of "slowly being replaced in ten years."

**Summary: This is a "profitable but not sexy enough" business. ** Stable, predictable, and with a moat, but lacking exciting room for growth. It's more like a "bond-like" stock - you buy certainty and earn dividends and reasonable valuations.
