## 1. The nature of the natural gas distribution industry

### 1.1 Industry operation logic

The operation of China's natural gas distribution industry can be simplified into three steps: **Buy gas -> Gas transmission -> Sell gas**.

**Upstream**: Three barrels of oil (PetroChina, Sinopec, and CNOOC) control more than 90% of domestic natural gas resources. Overseas LNG imports are participated by large central enterprises and a few private enterprises.

**Midstream**: The long-distance pipeline network (National Pipeline Network Group) is responsible for national backbone pipeline transportation, and the urban medium and low-pressure pipeline network is built and operated by urban gas companies.

**Downstream**: Urban fuel companies monopolize retail distribution in their respective regions through franchise rights.

The core contradiction of the ** industry: upstream resources are highly concentrated, downstream prices are controlled by the government, and urban gas companies in the middle are squeezed from both ends. ** This determines that the profit margin of this business is naturally low, and also determines that "who can get cheaper gas and more concessions" is the key to competition.

### 1.2 Industry scale and growth

China's natural gas consumption will be approximately 430-450 billion cubic meters in 2025, and is expected to reach 550-600 billion cubic meters in 2030, with a compound annual growth rate of 4%-6%. Growth drivers come from three aspects:

1. **Coal to Gas**: The policy of "replacing coal with gas" in the industrial and heating fields continues to advance
2. **Urbanization**: Residential gas demand brought by new urban population
3. **Natural gas power generation**: The construction of peaking power plants promotes the growth of gas consumption

**Rising dependence on foreign countries** is another important trend - the growth rate of domestic natural gas production cannot keep up with the growth rate of demand, import dependence is expected to further increase from the current level of about 40%, and the strategic importance of LNG imports is increasing.

### 1.3 Munger's question: Will natural gas still be a "good business" in ten years?

This is an issue that must be faced squarely. Natural gas is positioned as a "transition energy source" - the bridge between coal and renewable energy. In the next 10-15 years, natural gas demand is likely to grow, but what about after that?

The combination of **PV + energy storage + electrification** is eroding the market space of natural gas. Industrial heating, residential heating, and even cooking all have the potential to be fully electrified. If this trend accelerates, natural gas could hit peak demand around 2040.

**Conclusion: Natural gas distribution is a good business with "limited certainty" - good for now, but not sure how long it will last. ** Investors need to choose between certain 10-year returns and uncertain forward prospects.

### 2.2 Evolution of competitive situation

**The biggest change in the past five years is the "rise of central enterprises". **

Kunlun Energy has jumped from third place to first in the industry, surpassing ENN and China Resources in 2022 to become the gas sales champion. Its resource advantage backed by PetroChina is especially obvious during periods of upstream gas price fluctuations - it has its own gas fields and does not need to compete for gas at high prices in the market.

**What does this mean for ENN? ** In bidding in incremental markets (new urban areas, industrial parks), the resource commitments and government relationships of central enterprises put private enterprises at a disadvantage. ENN's response strategy is to seek profits from the existing market (improve services and realize value-added) and seek resources from overseas (sign long-term LNG agreements), rather than compete head-on with central enterprises for new projects.

### 2.3 ENN’s differentiated positioning

ENN has several unique advantages over state-owned gas companies:

**1. Highest operating efficiency ★★★★☆**
The management efficiency and cost control of private enterprises are generally better than those of central enterprises. ENN's ROE (15%) is significantly higher than Kunlun Energy (9%) and China Gas (6%), indicating that each dollar of shareholder equity creates more profits.

**2. LNG long-term cooperative layout takes the lead ★★★★☆**
A total of 10 million tons/year long-term LNG agreements have been signed. The Chenier 900,000 tons/year long-term agreement will be implemented in 2026 (HH link). In 2025, a 1 million tons/year, 15-year long-term agreement will be signed with Abu Dhabi. Among private enterprises, this upstream resource locking ability is unique.

With the right to use the Zhoushan LNG receiving station, it can flexibly allocate gas sources and have certain room for negotiation on the pricing of imported LNG.

**3. First-mover exploration of integrated energy services ★★☆☆☆**
Ubiquitous energy business, carbon consulting, digital intelligence platform - ENN has gone ahead in these directions earlier than its peers, but the business model has not yet been fully verified. If it can be passed, this will be a real differentiation barrier; if it cannot be passed, it will be a waste of resources.

### 2.4 ENN’s Structural Weaknesses

**1. Private enterprise status ★★★☆☆ (important risk)**
In China's public utility sector, the situation of private enterprises is becoming increasingly delicate. The granting and renewal of franchise rights, bidding for large-scale projects, and policy formulation—in these links, both central and state-owned enterprises have natural advantages. Wang Yusuo's political and business relationships are indispensable for ENN's success today, but this "relationship barrier" is fragile and cannot be inherited.

**2. Upstream resources still need to be outsourced ★★★☆☆**
Although there are 10 million tons of LNG long-term contracts, the prices of these long-term contracts are also volatile (Chenie is pegged to HH, influenced by the US natural gas market). Compared with Kunlun Energy's "self-produced gas", ENN's resource acquisition is essentially "bought". Under extreme market conditions (such as global LNG prices soaring), cost pressure will be greater.

**3. Not the largest in scale ★★☆☆☆**
Kunlun Energy’s gas sales volume is 54.1 billion cubic meters, and ENN’s gas sales volume is approximately 35 billion cubic meters. In an industry with significant economies of scale, greater scale means lower unit costs and greater bargaining power.

## 3. Key variables of the industry

### 3.1 Improvement progress of the price matching mechanism

Adaptive pricing (when upstream gas prices rise, downstream retail prices are adjusted simultaneously) is the lifeline of gas companies. Since 2023, various regions have accelerated the establishment of linkage mechanisms. In 2025, the proportion of residents in Xinao will be 71.6%. If it can reach more than 90%, it will significantly improve profit stability.

**Positive:** The policy direction is clear and the linkage mechanism is gradually established.
**Converse:** Raising gas prices for residents is very politically sensitive, and the speed of advancement is limited by the will and ability of local governments.

### 3.2 Impact of National Pipeline Network Reform

The National Pipeline Network Group, established in 2019, took over the long-distance pipeline assets of three barrels of oil, theoretically achieving "controlling the middle and releasing both ends." The impact on Xinao is **two-sided**:

**Benefits:** After the pipeline network is independent, gas source selection will be more market-oriented. ENN can purchase from multiple upstream suppliers, reducing dependence on a single gas source.
**Risk:** The independence of the pipeline network also lowers the entry barrier, making it easier for potential competitors to obtain gas sources.

### 3.3 Market-oriented reform of natural gas pricing

China's natural gas pricing is transitioning from "government pricing" to "market pricing". The trading volume of the Shanghai Oil and Gas Trading Center has increased year by year, and ENN has also participated in the cooperation with the trading center. Market-based pricing will benefit high-efficiency companies (such as ENN) in the long run, but it will increase price volatility in the short term.

### 3.4 Long-term threats of electrified substitution

Heat pumps, induction cookers, industrial electric furnaces—these technologies are gradually replacing natural gas usage scenarios. Although the substitution rate is limited in the short term (especially in the field of industrial high-temperature heat), the long-term trend is clear. This is the "Sword of Damocles" faced by all participants in the natural gas industry.

## 4. Munger’s reverse thinking: Under what circumstances will ENN lose?

Munger likes to "think the other way around." Under what circumstances will the New Olympic Games completely fail?

**Scenario 1: The franchise is withdrawn. ** If the policy changes to require that public utilities must be operated by state-owned enterprises, ENN's fundamentals will collapse. The probability is low but the impact is huge.

**Scenario 2: The long-term LNG agreement becomes "poison". ** If a large number of long-term agreements linked to HH are signed, and international gas prices remain low for a long time and domestic pipeline gas prices are even lower, ENN will be forced to buy gas at a high price and sell gas at a low price. This has happened to some gas companies in Europe.

**Scenario 3: Electrification accelerates substitution. ** If technological breakthroughs allow electricity to completely replace natural gas in all fields, the industry's long-term demand logic will be subverted. The time window may be 10-20 years.

**Scenario 4: Integration fails after privatization. ** The debt ratio of the consolidated entity has soared to 67%. If it encounters an operating trough, high leverage may trigger a liquidity crisis.
