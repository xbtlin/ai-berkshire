# New Olympic Energy (2688.HK) risk management assessment

> Analytical dimensions: risk list, management integrity and capacity, governance structure
> Core issue: What can make this investment fail? Is management trustworthy?

---

# I. Risk panorama

The risks to investing in new energy sources can be classified into three levels of "lethal":

#1st level: Fatal risk (which could result in permanent capital loss)

**1. Risk of privatization failure **

The new Olympics are privatized with 80 HK$/shares (24.5 HK$) and 2.94 HK$/shares H shares, with a total trade of 59.9 billion HK$. By early 2026, two of the four preconditions (foreign investment foreign exchange registration, independent shareholder approval) had been met and two were pending (approved by the Hong Kong joint stock exchange board, and filed by the China Securities Commission).

On 29 April 2026, the New Olympics Share announcement extended the resolution on major asset restructuring for 12 months, implying that the transaction was slower than expected.

** What if privatization fails?**
- Stock prices may fall back to HK$50-55 (pre-privatization valuation level)
- Market confidence in corporate governance and shareholder returns will be undermined
- The ability of Wang Yu to operate capital will be questioned.

** What happens if privatization succeeds but integration does not work out?**
- The combined liability rate may rise to 67 per cent and financial risk will increase significantly
- Increased complexity of the management of the dual A+H listing structure
- Synergy of commitments (lower procurement costs, increased management efficiency) may not be realized

**2. Policy and institutional risks**

While the rate of the favourable price is rising (71.6%), there are still nearly 30% risk exposures. In extreme cases, firms may face a gradual loss if upstream gas prices rise significantly and downstream prices cannot be adjusted in parallel.

The deeper systemic risk is ** "Nationalization and Civil Recession"**. Utilities are a national domain, and the policy landscape can change at any time. If future policies require a stronger state-owned public utility, the extension of concessions and new project bids by private companies may be affected. This is not a question of "no" but of "how much".

**3. Leveraging and liquidity risk **

The firm’s financial resilience would be significantly reduced if it were to add to the debt after privatization. In an environment of high interest rate hikes or tight credit, a combination of high asset-plus liabilities would be dangerous.

The share-level liability rate at the New Olympics has reached 53.92%, and may rise to 67% after privatization. While the cash flow of public utility enterprises can support higher liabilities, 67% is close to the maximum in comfort areas.

## Second tier: significant risk (which may result in lower than expected returns in a few years)

**4. Risk of international gas price fluctuations**

The company has signed 10 million tonnes/year LNG, and started implementing 900,000 tonnes/year in 2026. If the international LNG price deviates significantly from the price of the contract (whether rising or falling), it will affect profits:

- ** Prices rise:** Prices targeted by the Association have been given advantages, but if additional purchases are needed in the spot market, costs will soar
- ** Prices dropped:** The JCJ could become a burden of "high price locking," especially with Chenier's HH-link contract being influenced by US gas prices.

The essence of this risk is that ** the New Olympics have attempted to lock upstream costs through the Association, but the Association itself is an irreversible commitment.**

**5. Continuing impact of downline real estate**

The engineering installation operation (new user access) is a high-mori business block in New Olympic, but it relies heavily on new house delivery. In 2025, the revenue of the plate and Māori fell by 17.9 per cent and 12.3 per cent, respectively, without any indication of short-term improvement.

This not only affects current profits, but also relates to future user growth: the fewer new houses, the slower the future retail gas growth.

**6. Realistic "one voice" risk**

Wang Yuk's indirect control of the energy sector through the New Olympic Consort is typical of the "family-controlled enterprise." This structure has an advantage in its implementation (quick decision-making, strategic coherence), but there are governance risks:

- Frequent and complex associated transactions (between New Olympics, New Olympics, New Olympic Holdings)
- Are the interests of small shareholders fully protected?
- Is privatization fair for HK$80? The question has been brought to the attention of the submission.

## Level 3: Long-term risk (which may affect the fate of the enterprise after 5-10 years)

**7. Energy transformation/electricization replacement **

The positioning of natural gas as a "transitional energy" means that it is not the ultimate solution. The combination of photovoltaic-plus storage-heat pumps is gradually replacing the natural gas use scene. This process may take 10-20 years, but it is not negligible for long-term investors.

**8. Risk of technological subversion **

New technologies such as hydrogen, distributed energy, if broken, could change the underlying logic of energy distribution. But the risk is still far away.

# II. Management assessment

#2.1 King Jade Lock: Founder and Real Control

** Background:** Hebei's richest, founded in 1989 by the New Olympics Group, which began selling liquid gas cans and developed it into a 100 billion-grade gas empire in 35 years.

** Positive evaluation **

- ** Strategic long-term vision:** Urban gas is installed before the industry breaks out, seizing the historic opportunity of "coal gas conversion". Early signing of the LNG LNG Chairship targeted upstream resources and created a unique opportunity among the private sector.
- ** Strong implementation:** The development of gas dealers from a small county town to a national company covering 250+ cities requires a very strong enforcement and political relations capacity.
- ** Resolutely resigning as Chairman of the New Olympics in March 2025 and leaving it to the career manager, Jiang Hoon, to take over, showing a pattern of unretroactive rights.

** Reverse review **

- ** Capital is often and complex:** The new Olympic system’s capital architecture (New Olympics, New Olympics, New Olympics, and various related companies) is confusing. Privatization, listing, asset restructuring – do these operations really create value for small and medium-sized shareholders, or are they mainly capital layouts for real-controlled people? The queries sent reflect this market suspicion.
- ** ** Is the vision too ambitious:** The goal of 1 % of the 2029 digital and gas operations seems too radical. The business model of the general-energy business has not yet been operational, and if a massive investment in the "second curve" is not realized, it will be wasted.
- ** "Continued anxiety":** Prince Wang Yuk's son, Prince Wang, who was in the New Olympic System (New Olympic Energy Executive Director, New Olympic Holdings Director, etc.) but was not elected successor. Wang Yuk's choice of a career manager, Jiang Seung-hung, is a good sign of governance, but also raises another question: Can New Olympics remain competitive in their political and commercial relations without the founder's Halo?

** Positive evaluation **

** Uncertainty**

- Can you manage the complex operations of hundreds of billions of firms with less than nine years of experience in the New Olympic system?

- Implementation is extremely difficult to take over during the critical period of privatization

##2.3 Governance structure

**ESG rating:** MSCI ESG rating AAA for two years in a row, is at the top of the global gas utility list. This is a positive sign of good corporate performance in ESG disclosure and governance system building.

** The ESG rating and actual governance quality cannot be equated.** The following issues require sustained attention:

1. ** Fairness of associated transactions:** Is there a large number of related transactions between New Olympic energy and New Olympic shares and New Olympic holdings that are fair to small and medium-sized shareholders in each case?
2. ** Information asymmetry:** Does the real control have more information than small and medium-sized shareholders, particularly in the privatization process, and does the pricing adequately reflect the intrinsic value of the company?
3. ** Independence of independent directors:** Can independent directors really play a checks and balances role in a controlled company?

# 3.4 Privatization "implicit conflict of interest"

There is a delicate but important conflict of interest: Wang Yu's chain is also the real owner of the New Olympic energy and the New Olympic shares. Privatization is essentially "left-hand buy-right" - a company that uses A shares (the market gives a higher value) to buy a company that has a market (the market gives a lower value) and from which it receives ** valuation arbitrage**.

Is the exchange price fair for HK$80? The submission of the inquiry has raised concerns about the large difference between the Sino-Ou energy acquisition price and the Sino-Ok A share price? There is no simple answer to this question — the value of the 2.94 shares of the Sino-Ok share H share included in the price is dependent on the market price of the future H-stock, and there is considerable uncertainty.

# Four, Lee's security margins

Lee Szell stresses the first principle of investment as "no loss." For New Olympic energy, one needs to ask: Under what circumstances can investors face permanent capital losses?

** Scenario I (probability 15%): ** Privatization failure + market mood weakening <unk> stock price down to HK$45-50, with investors facing a 20-30% loss. If the company’s fundamentals do not deteriorate, this is a temporary rather than a permanent loss.

** Scenario II (feasibility 5%):** Successful privatization but failed integration + Debt crisis <unk> Over-indebtedness of consolidated entities is too high and subject to liquidity pressure in the lower economic period.

** Scenario III (at 2% probability):** Policy mutation, with concessions withdrawn or substantially restricted ** company fundamentals broken down.

** Comprehensive assessment:** The main risk facing New Olympic energy is that it "does not earn the expected return" rather than "permanent loss". Companies have a stable operating cash flow ($10.2 billion/year), a real asset base (management network, LNG contracts) and a continuing user demand.
