##1.2 Risk factor depth analysis

# # Risk I: IBI363 Global III Clinical Failure (Master Risk)

** Why is this the biggest risk?**

IBI363 is not just a pipe line for Sinda -- it's a key support for Sinda's leap from "China Innovation Medicines" to "Global Innovation Medicines". Of the $11.4 billion in collaboration with Wuda, IBI363 is at the core. If three fail:

- Direct financial loss: the confirmed BD income is not affected, but all subsequent milestone payments (which may exceed $5 billion) will be lost
- Valuation re-engineering: the market will make a substantial discount on the "global narrative" of Sinda, with a possible 30-50% return on stock prices
- Confidence shock: other BD partnerships will be significantly weakened in their bargaining chips

** Objective probability of events**: The historical failure rate of three-stage oncology is about 50-60%. IBI363, though the data are bright, is not uncommon in the field of tumors from two to three. Adding that the global three-stage start has been delayed by more than six months, there is uncertainty at the implementation level.

** buffer**: Even if IBI363 fails, the Nada still has $11.9 billion in product income base, other pipes such as IBI343 (CLDN 18.2 ADC), and a platform-level collaboration with courtesy. Companies do not fall into a survival crisis because of a single pipe failure.

## Risk II: Core product collection (high probability risk)

**The risk of collection of beverage single resistance**:
- 14 enterprises have been approved to meet the conditions for collection
- The Angkor Health Service will lead the National Biopharmaceuticals Alliance.
- If the collection land is reduced by 60-70 per cent, the annual loss of income may be $8-12 billion

**The health care renewal pressure of the Sindeli Singles**:
- Prices are downgraded per round of health insurance negotiations for the PD-1 species
- 5 domestic PD-1s have been introduced into health insurance and price competition continues to increase
- But because of the extent of the ICTD's anti-addiction and the prescriptions, they won't be "out" in the short term.

** Management response**: Price erosion of older products by accelerating the roll-out of new products. 3-4 new products are expected to be marketed in 2025-2026, and the increase in new products is expected to cover stock losses from harvesting.

Risk three: the commercialization of the GLP-1 track is lost

Although the data are excellent, commercialization faces multiple challenges:

- **Time window**: approval later than partial competition, prescriptions taking time to build
- **Competition **: 13 NDAs accepted, 35 in three, track is about to enter the Red Sea
- ** Channel restrictions**: Currently only prescription drug channels are limited and OTC (unprescripted) markets are not covered
- **Extrenchment**: Nord and Nord have accumulated in the GLP-1 market in China, with brand recognition leading

If Mato’s sales in 2026 fall short of the expected 1.8 billion yuan (e.g., only 800 to 1 billion yuan), market confidence in the Sinda “second growth curve” will be undermined.

Risk four: core brain drain

Liu Yongjun, the President of Sinda Biogens, left and joined the Stone Pharmacy Group in November 2024. Liu Yongjun is a world-renowned immunology and oncology scientist with core responsibilities in the global research and development, pipeline strategy, and so forth in Sinda. His departure raised market concerns about Sinda’s leadership in research and development.

The core competitiveness of innovation companies lies in people, especially those at the top who can judge target directions, design clinical programmes, and communicate with global regulators. Sinda needs to prove that its research and development capacity is "organised platform capacity" rather than "dependent on individual talent."

# Risk five: Geopolitics and uncertainty of going out

The volatility of Central American relations may affect:
- Implementation in cooperation with partners such as Takeda (Japan), Relai (United States) and others
- IBI363 approval and commercialization in the US market
- The speed of advance in overseas clinical trials

Currently, the drug sector is relatively less geographically affected (as opposed to chips, AI, etc.), but the long-term risks cannot be ignored.

** Rating: <unk> (3.5/5)**
The risk is high, with the core being the uncertainty of the pipeline. But companies have built up enough "safe mats" (24.3 billion cash, 11.9 billion product revenues) to avoid survival crises in the worst cases.

# III. Corporate culture and governance

#3.1 Enterprise culture

The business culture of Sindha bears the obvious "scientific start-up" mark:

- ** Mission Drive**: "Developing high-quality biopharmaceuticals that people can afford." - This mission helped to attract a group of scientists and managers who agreed to the idea of "Drug for People" in the early days.
- **Scientific orientation**: "Scientific Gardens" - emphasis on the sense of achievement and participation of R & D personnel
- ** Higher than market remuneration**: wage treatments of more than 25 per cent above market prices, resulting in brain-snapting effects
- **Genesis**: Over 60 sea returns in the early team, bringing international standards of research and development and management concepts

But when companies enter into the 10 billion-dollar phase, they need to move from "scientific culture" to "scientist plus business." How 4,000 people in commercialization teams work together effectively with research and development teams is the next challenge to culture.

#3.2 Corporate governance

** Positive signal:**
- Listing on the main board of the port of entry and subject to strict information disclosure regulations
- Establish cooperation with a number of top pharmaceutical companies around the world, and partner due diligence is itself an external certification of corporate governance
- Financial statements audited by four major accounting firms

** Subject of concern:**
- Founder's shareholding and voting power structure - Yude's control over the company
- Control of associated transactions and conflicts of interest - especially in the context of multiple partners
- Timeliness and transparency of information disclosure - adequacy of disclosure of progress in the pipeline

#3.3 Capital market communication

Sinda's investor relations work is at a better level among 18A enterprises:
- Regular performance sessions and investor days
- Progress of pipelines and disclosure of BD transactions is timely
- The introduction of management objectives (e.g. $20 billion targets) reflects the initiative to communicate with markets

But the 20 billion-dollar target is a double-edged sword in itself. If it is achieved, it will greatly enhance market confidence; if it is not achieved, it will damage management’s credibility.

** Rating: <unk> (3.7/5)**
Corporate culture and governance are at a higher level in the industry, but the transition from "scientists culture" to "globalized corporate culture" is still under way.

# IV. Key issues from a long-term investment perspective

# 4.1 is this a business that can accompany ten years?

From a long-term perspective, the following questions need to be answered:

** Q1: What will Sinda be in ten years?**

Optimistic scenarios: IBI363 became the global IO-based treatment, with Mahdipoon occupying the top three markets in China ' s GLP-1 and with companies earning more than $50 billion annually, steadily making profits and becoming the global Top 30 biopharmaceutical enterprise.

Pessimistic scenarios: IBI363 failed, core products were collected, new products were lost in competition, companies maintained annual earnings of 15-20 billion yuan and profit margins of 10-15 per cent, a good but not particularly good Chinese medium-sized pharmaceutical company.

** Q2: Is management capable of managing the 10-year growth?**

Yude has demonstrated leadership from zero to ten billion. But from 10 billion to 50 billion requires a very different organizational capacity – global operations, multi-business lines, and thousands of people working together.

**Q3: Is the industry environment allowing long-term value accumulation?**

China’s pharmaceutical industry has generally been in a policy environment that favours innovative pharmaceutical enterprises (which have been increasingly supported by health insurance), but the lack of pricing rights means that firms must sustain growth through sustained innovation. This is fundamentally different from the business model in which consumer goods companies maintain pricing rights through brand power.

# 4.2 The least visible risk

**(1) "BD Dependence"**

The current high valuation of Sinda is largely supported by BD transactions. If the global BD market is characterized by a decline in the climate (e.g., the multinational drug firm shrinks its cooperation budget), the valuation of Sinda may face systemic compression.

**(2) "Innovator's dilemma"**

Sindal built his advantage by "scaling up" in the PD-1 era, but in the IBI363 it was necessary to "lead innovation." The bottom logic of the two capabilities -- the former requires enforcement and the latter requires unknown judgement. The ability of firms to do this is a deep challenge.

**(3) Ports 'Equal Values'**

As a listed company, the HQ faces both liquidity and geopolitical discounts. Innovative pharmaceutical firms of equal quality usually value higher in A or US shares. This is not a problem that the company can solve itself.

# Core judgment

From a risk perspective, Sindha is a company characterized by "high risk, high return." Its greatest risk is not bad business or financial crisis, but rather the scientific uncertainty inherent in the development of innovative medicines — a good management team cannot guarantee that a molecule will succeed in three clinical phases.

From a management perspective, Yu De-Hong is one of the best founders of China’s innovative pharmaceutical industry. His deep-seated and entrepreneurial profile, with scientists, has made many strategic decisions over the past 15 years that have been proven to be correct.

For long-term investors, the central question is not "Sinda's good" (the answer is yes -- it is one of China's best innovation companies), but "Is the current price a full reflection of its risk?" 41 times PE means that the market's probability of success for IBI363 is set at 60-70% — if you think the probability of success is higher than that, the current price is attractive; if you think it is below that level, you need to wait for lower prices.
