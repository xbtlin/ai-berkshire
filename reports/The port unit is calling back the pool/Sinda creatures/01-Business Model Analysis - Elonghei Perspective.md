# Analysis of business model of Innovent Biologics (1801.HK)

> Analysis Dimension: Business Nature and Moat
> Core question: What is the nature of this business? Can you make money in the long term? Are user needs real? Is the business model sustainable?
> Study date: May 16, 2026

---

## 1. The essence of this business

### 1.1 What business is Cinda doing?

Innovent's core business is: ** research and development of innovative biological drugs, sell them in China and the global market, and at the same time realize the value of the pipeline through external licensing (BD)**.

Looking at the revenue structure, the total revenue in 2025 is 13.042 billion yuan:
- **Product sales revenue**: 11.896 billion yuan, accounting for 91.2%, which is the core source of income
- **BD licensing and other income**: approximately 1.146 billion yuan, accounting for 8.8%

In product sales, oncology drugs are still the absolute main force. Sintilimab (PD-1) will contribute approximately 3.8 billion yuan in 2024, accounting for 46% of product revenue. However, non-oncology products (metabolism, autoimmune, ophthalmology) are rapidly increasing in volume, reaching approximately 540 million yuan in 2024 and expected to reach 3.27 billion yuan in 2026, with a compound annual growth rate of 145.8%.

**Judgment of the nature of the business**: This is a "R&D-driven" business. A large amount of investment is made in R&D in the early stage. Once the product is approved and launched into the medical insurance market, the marginal cost is extremely low (the gross profit margin of biopharmaceuticals is 87.2%), forming huge operating leverage. But the risks are also clear - R&D failure means huge sunk costs.

### 1.2 Are user needs real?

The demand for innovative drugs is undoubtedly real and rigid. Cancer, diabetes, and obesity are all serious diseases, and patients’ willingness to pay is extremely strong. There are approximately 4.8 million new cancer patients in China every year, more than 140 million type 2 diabetes patients, and more than 600 million obese/overweight people - these are huge markets.

However, it is necessary to distinguish between "real demand" and "whether Cinda's products are irreplaceable". In the field of PD-1, more than 10 similar products have been launched in China, and sintilimab faces serious homogeneous competition. What truly have differentiated demand are innovative products such as IBI363 (the world's first PD-1/IL-2 dual antibody) and Masdutide (GCG/GLP-1 dual receptor agonist).

**Rating: ★★★★☆ (4/5)**
The demand fundamentals are extremely strong, but some core products (PD-1, bevacizumab) are facing homogeneity and do not have "unique" demand lock-in.

## 2. Dismantling of the business model

### 2.1 "Two-wheel drive" mode

Innovent's business model can be summarized as the two-wheel drive of "domestic commercialization + global BD licensing":

**Round 1: Commercialization of Domestic Products**
- 16 listed products constitute the basic revenue base
- Rapidly expand the volume through medical insurance access, covering hospitals at all levels across the country
- Oncology lines (sintilimab, etc.) + metabolic lines (mastotide, etc.) go hand in hand
- Sales team of approximately 4,000 people, covering approximately 5,000 hospitals

**Round 2: Global BD going overseas**
- License early or mid-stage pipelines to multinational pharmaceutical companies
- Get down payment + milestone payment + sales share
- From 2025 to 2026, a total of over US$22 billion in BD transactions will be reached

The subtlety of this model is that domestic commercialization provides stable cash flow and supports continued R&D investment; BD’s overseas expansion not only realizes the value of pipelines in advance, but also serves as an external verification of innovation capabilities.

### 2.2 The evolution from "selling elements" to "joint management"

Cinda’s BD model has undergone three stages of evolution:

**Phase 1 (2015-2023)**: Traditional authorization model. Licensing early-stage molecules with lower down payments is a typical example of the early collaboration with Eli Lilly.

**Phase 2 (Takeda cooperation in 2025)**: Co-Co model (joint development, joint commercialization). Cinda has reached a 40/60 joint operation model with Takeda for IBI363, with Cinda retaining 40% of global profit rights. This means that Cinda is no longer just "selling young people", but is participating in global value distribution as a partner.

**Phase 3 (Li Lilly Cooperation in 2026)**: Early platform-level cooperation. Eli Lilly paid a down payment of US$350 million to Innovent before it even made a stake in the deal, for a total of US$8.85 billion. This shows that what multinational pharmaceutical companies are buying is not just a molecule, but Innovent’s innovation platform capabilities.

**Rating: ★★★★☆ (4/5)**
The business model is evolving rapidly, shifting from single product sales to a compound model of "independent commercialization + global profit sharing". However, domestic commercialization is still highly dependent on the medical insurance system, and pricing power is limited.

## 3. Moat analysis

### 3.1 What moats does Cinda have?

**(1) Innovation R&D platform—the core moat**

Innovent has established a research and development platform covering multiple technical routes such as monoclonal antibodies, double antibodies, ADCs, and fusion proteins, and has 34 pipelines under development. As a first-in-class molecule in the world, IBI363 has obtained three breakthrough therapy designations from China's NMPA and two fast-track qualifications from the U.S. FDA. This innovation capability is the most solid barrier.

Eli Lilly was willing to pay US$8.85 billion to cooperate before the molecule was born, essentially paying for the "future innovation capabilities" of this platform.

**(2) BD negotiation ability and trust assets of multinational pharmaceutical companies**

It has cooperated with Eli Lilly seven times since 2015, and has established in-depth cooperative relationships with the world's top 20 pharmaceutical companies such as Takeda and Roche. The trust accumulated in this "repeated game" is difficult for new entrants to replicate in the short term.

**(3) Commercial network**

A sales team of 4,000 people covers 5,000 hospitals, and 16 products are sold through the same channel to form a network effect. The marginal promotion cost of each new approved product is significantly lower than the cost of competitors building it from scratch.

**(4) First-mover advantage and medical insurance lock-in**

Sintilimab is the first PD-1 product to enter the national medical insurance, establishing brand recognition and prescription habits in the grassroots market. Although competing products continue to enter, the channel advantage in the sinking market is difficult to be subverted in the short term.

### 3.2 The fragility of the moat

**(1) Patent expiration and centralized procurement risks**

Centralized procurement of biosimilars is advancing. 14 companies have been approved for bevacizumab, and the possibility of being included in the centralized procurement is extremely high. Once centralized purchasing is implemented, prices will drop significantly.

**(2) Uncertainty of technical route**

Although the current data of IBI363 is impressive, it has not yet completed global phase III clinical trials. If the third-phase data falls short of expectations, the value of this core pipeline will shrink significantly. The launch of the global third phase has been delayed by more than 6 months from the original plan.

**(3) Talent dependence risk**

Innovative pharmaceutical companies are highly dependent on core R&D talents. The departure of former president Liu Yongjun (who later joined CSPC) illustrates that the mobility of top talent is a real risk.

**Rating: ★★★☆☆ (3.5/5)**
The innovation platform and BD relationship network are valuable moats, but the moat at the individual product level is weak and faces continued erosion from centralized procurement and homogeneous competition.

## 4. Sustainability of business model

### 4.1 Can you make money in the long term?

Answering this question requires looking at two levels:

**Product level**: The life cycle of a single product is limited. The PD-1 track has entered a mature stage, and the high-growth era of sintilimab may be over. Bevacizumab faces centralized procurement. However, Innovent's strategy is to "continue to launch new products to replace expired products" - Masdutide takes over the metabolic field, and IBI363 targets the global IO market.

**Platform level**: If Innovent can continue to produce competitive innovative molecules, then the flywheel of "R&D-licensing-commercialization" can continue to rotate. Eli Lilly's platform-level cooperation is an important signal - multinational pharmaceutical companies believe that Innovent's innovation capabilities are sustainable.

The gross profit margin of 87.2% means that once the sales scale increases, the profit elasticity is extremely high. The sales expense ratio has dropped from 50.9% to 48.0% in 2025. As the scale effect continues to be released, there is still room for improvement in profit margins.

### 4.2 Core risks of business model

**The biggest concern: whether the leap from "following innovation" to "original innovation" can be successful. **

Innovent's past commercial success was largely based on the model of "fast follow + large volume in the Chinese market" - sintilimab, bevacizumab, etc. are all follow-up innovations on overseas proven targets. IBI363 is Innovent's first truly First-in-Class molecule. The success or failure of its global Phase III clinical trials will determine whether Innovent can complete this leap.

If the third phase of IBI363 is successful, Innovent will join the first echelon of global innovative drugs, and its valuation logic will switch from "China's leading innovative drug" to "a global innovative pharmaceutical company."

If the third phase of IBI363 fails or the data is mediocre, Innovent will still be an excellent Chinese Biopharma, but the valuation ceiling will be significantly lowered.

**Rating: ★★★★☆ (4/5)**
The business model is moving from "feasible" to "excellent", but the key variables (IBI363 Phase III, Masdutide volume expansion) have not yet been fully verified.

## 5. Comprehensive assessment

### Overall business model rating: ★★★★☆ (3.9/5)

| Dimensions | Rating | Description |
|------|------|------|
| Authenticity of user needs | ★★★★☆ | Disease needs are rigid, but some products lack differentiation |
| Business model maturity | ★★★★☆ | The two-wheel drive model has been verified, and the Co-Co model is an important evolution |
| Moat strength | ★★★☆☆ | The platform-level moat is beginning to appear, but the single-product moat is weak |
| Sustainable profitability | ★★★★☆ | 87.2% gross profit margin + scale effect, but relies on continuous innovation |
| Business model evolution speed | ★★★★★ | From imitation to follow-up to originality, the speed of evolution is leading among domestic pharmaceutical companies |

### Key Observations

Innovent Biologics' business model is in a critical "upgrading period." In the past ten years, the accumulation from 0 to 10 billion has been achieved through "fast follow-up + China's medical insurance expansion". In the next ten years, it will be achieved from "original innovation + global commercialization" to achieve a leap from 10 billion to 50 billion. These two models have completely different requirements for organizational capabilities, R&D strategies, and global operations.

On the bright side, the sky-high price cooperation between Takeda and Eli Lilly shows that the world's top pharmaceutical companies recognize Innovent's innovation capabilities. The bad side is that more than 90% of these cooperation amounts are milestone payments, and whether they can be realized depends on clinical results-this is a fundamental uncertainty that cannot be eliminated by business model design.
