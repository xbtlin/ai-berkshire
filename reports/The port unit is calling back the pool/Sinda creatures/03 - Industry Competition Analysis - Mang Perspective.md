# I. The macro-scenario of the innovative pharmaceutical industry in China

# 1.1 What stage is the industry at?

The Chinese innovation industry is going through a historic turn from "Modified Pharmaceuticals for the Most." Several key signs:

- **The top $100 billion in revenue of the company listing the innovative medicine in 2025** marks a critical mass for the innovative pharmaceutical industry in China
- **China ' s innovation drug BD went out to the sea in 2025 for $135,6 billion**, crushing the United States and becoming the world ' s largest authorized exporter of innovation drugs for the first time
- ** Five companies have more than 10 billion dollars in innovative medicines: Chimju State, Heng Swe Medicine, Chinese Biopharmaceuticals, Hanson Pharmaceuticals, Sindha Biology

The industry has gone through the "Talking Story, Burning Money" phase, and head businesses have started to make real profits and self-build. That means the industry is switching from "Risk Investment Logic" to "Investment Logic".

##1.2 Core drivers of industry

** (1) Policy end: a double-edged sword for health care reform**

The NHS negotiations have accelerated access to and availability of innovative medicines, but have also severely restricted pricing. The price of health care payments for the Sindili policy has been revised downwards from about $100,000 per year to about $30,000 per year. The "price-for-value" logic of health insurance imposes a very high demand on the ability of firms to scale – only firms that can make a profit in a low-cost environment.

**(2) Technological End: From Following Innovation to Source Innovation**

China’s innovation medicine, which used to be dominated by me-too (following innovation), has in recent years emerged a group of molecules with global potential for first-in-Class. IBI363 of Sindha is typical of this. This technological upgrading is changing China’s bargaining position with multinational drug companies – from selling green seed seedlings to sharing profits.

**(3) Capital end: a return to reason after cold winter**

After the break-up of the 2021-2022 biotech bubble, a large number of mid- and small-sized Biotech financing difficulties and pipelines were stranded. This was good for head businesses like Sinda – talent and pipeline assets were flowing at a lower cost, and industry concentration accelerated.

** Rating: <unk> (4/5)**
Industry is at an early stage of rapid growth, with clear structural opportunities (innovative alternatives to generics, Chinese medicine, and seaboard), but competition is increasing and policies are suppressing pricing rights.

# Four: Can Sindha's competitive advantage be sustained?

##4.1 Continuous analysis

** Strength and continuity advantages:**
- Multi-technology platform capacity (uni-resistant, bi-resistant, ADC, integrated protein) - this takes more than 10 years to accumulate and new entrants are difficult to replicate
- Deep partnership with multinational drug companies - trust assets need to be built up over time.
- The scale effects of commercial networks - 4,000 sales teams + 16 product channels

** Strength of weak continuity:**
- IBI363 data lead - three issues are not out and the competition is fast-tracking.
- PD-1 market share - Red Sealing the track, competing for a zero-sum share
- High-scene BD trade - influenced by global capital cycle and drug enterprise strategy adjustments

# 4.2 "Reverse Thinking": What are the circumstances under which Sinda will fail?

Using a reverse mindset, the greatest risk of failure for Sinda is:

(1) **IBI363 series failed** - the most deadly single risk event. If it happens, Sintar loses the valuation narrative of the Global Innovative Medicines Corporation and returns to the valuation framework of the China Innovative Medicines Corporation.
(2) ** Core product collection price reduction is higher than expected** - if collected and reduced by more than 70%, annual revenue loss may be over $1 billion
(3) ** GLP-1 track failure** — if the commercialization of Mastau Pin is significantly lower than expected, the company will lose the second growth curve
(4)** Strategic failure of management** — if there is a significant deviation in the allocation of resources between "globalization" and "deep farming in the country"

# 4.3 The finals of the industry

The final outcome of the Chinese innovation medicine industry could be:

- ** Head concentration**: 3-5 firms account for over 60 per cent of the domestic market, similar to the pattern of the United States market Pfizer/Merck/J&J/Lilly/Abbvie
- ** Globalization **: The state of Chihuahua and Sinda are expected to become globally commercialized enterprises, most others are able to compete only in the domestic market
- ** Classification**: Oncology, metabolism, self-exclusion, and separate breakdowns of treatment areas such as CNS

The most likely location for Sinda in this final round is "China Top 3 Innovative Medicines + Global Innovative Medicines Second Echelon." More depends on the global clinical results of core tubes like IBI363.

# Core judgment

Sindha is in a position of "take-and-take" in the Chinese innovation industry — with the basic product base of 10 billion-grade income as the foundation of "take-home" and high-potential tubes like IBI363 and Masidopus as the sharp instrument of "take-off".

The industry landscape is generally favourable to Sinda: policies that promote innovative alternatives to generics, increased head concentration, and an acceleration of Chinese pharmaceutical companies' sea trip — trends that are favourable to the Nada.

But competition is real. On the PD-1 track, Sinda faces a close body struggle with hundreds, Heung-seo, and Gun-sui; on the GLP-1, a global run-off against Noor and Nord, a global run-off from Lei and a dozen more national firms; and on the new generation of IO, the pace of follow-up by the big global factories, although temporarily leading.

To win this competition, Sinda needs not only good molecules, but also excellent enforcement – clinical speed, commercial efficiency, and global ability to operate. These are not the organizational capabilities that can be built up by technological breakthroughs, but rather by the organizational capacity accumulated in "the day."
