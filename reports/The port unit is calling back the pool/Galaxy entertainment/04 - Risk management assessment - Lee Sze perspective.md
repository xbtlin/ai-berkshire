# II. Key risk identification

##2.1 Policy risk - the greatest uncontrollable factor

** Risk description:** Macao Gambling Industry is fully influenced by the policies of the Chinese and Macao SAR governments.

** Historical lessons:**
- The campaign against corruption in 2014 caused a 40 per cent drop in the income of the VIP hall +
- The 2022 Gambling Act was amended to cause a panic in the industry and stock prices fell.
- Customs clearance policies directly determine the life and death of the industry during the epidemic.

** Current policy environment:**
- The new Gambling Act of 2022 has been laid down and the regulatory framework is stable
- The government encourages "non-comb diversity." The Galaxy investment is in line with policy.
- License valid until 2032, short-term risk of non-renewal
- The policy tone has shifted from "restriction" to "normative development."

** Probability assessment:** Major adverse policy changes (e.g., significant reduction of tables, limited tags, etc.) are low but not entirely excluded.

##2.2 Macroeconomic risks

** Risk description:** Gambling is optional and highly dependent on the will of the Chinese economy and high net population to consume.

**Exact performance:**
- China ' s economic slowdown may inhibit the number of gamblers and per capita consumption
- The devaluation of the renminbi will reduce the purchasing power of mainland tourists in Macau.
- The low real estate market may affect the consumption confidence of the wealthy
- Rising unemployment affects the general population ' s middle-class flow

** Pulse factor:**
- The appreciation of the renminbi against the dollar in 2025 is good for the gamblers.
- The number of passengers has been innovative, indicating that the will to consume remains strong
- The Galaxy masters are in the middle and upper end of the market, with relatively little influence on the general economy.

** Probability assessment:** China has a moderate probability of a continuing recession.

#2.3 Competition increases risk

** Risk description:** Six major firms are expanding their capacity, and increased overall supply of industry can lead to increased competition.

**Exact performance:**
- All the houses are in non-cocam facilities, and the space for differentiation may be reduced.
- Additional room supply may suppress hotel occupancy and house prices
- Competition for marketing costs may erode profit margins

** Galaxy response:**
- Maximum land reserves provide continuous differentiational capacity
- Zero liability means there's a longer "magnity" in the price war.
- Gabriel is super-high-level, competing with the market.

** Probability assessment:** Increased competition is determined but the probability of a malignant competition is low.

#2.4 Capital expenditure risk

** Risk description:** Phase IV projects are heavily invested and will drag ROE if the return on post-partum delivery falls short of expectations.

**Exact performance:**
- Total investment in phase IV is HK$10 billion, focusing on non-comotive facilities
- Non-cocam projects are usually less profitable than lottery
- If the macro-environment is not good when it comes to production in 2027, it may be "input or excess."

** Deductive factors:**
- The galaxy has its own capital. No borrowing.
- Non-chamber facilities meet policy requirements and facilitate renewal negotiations
- Phase 4 includes a gambling facility that is not entirely dependent on non-comotive income

** Probability assessment:** Low risk of project completion but moderate rate of return.

# 2.5 Geopolitical risks

** Risk description:** Sino-American tensions may affect US-owned ventures in Macao and indirectly affect industry patterns.

** Impact on Galaxy:**
- Galaxy is a local Chinese-owned business, which is less affected by China-United States relations.
- But if US-owned companies (Kinsa, Yongli, US-Gorme) are restricted, the industry environment may change
- In extreme cases, if the United States dollar is forced to withdraw, the Milky Way may be the most beneficial beneficiary.

** Probability assessment:** U.S. funds are subject to very low probability of forced exit, but geo-stressed.

#2.6 The risks of the Yokohama project

** Risk description:** The Yokohama project invested about RMB 10 billion, but progress was slow and the schedule of returns was uncertain.

** Specific issues:**

- The harp does not allow for play, it is for leisure, and the profit rate may be much lower than for the Macau project
- A lot of money is deposited in a project that doesn't pay off.
