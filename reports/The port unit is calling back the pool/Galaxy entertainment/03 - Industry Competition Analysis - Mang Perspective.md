#Galaxy Entertainment (0027.HK) industry competition analysis

> Analysis dimensions: industry structure, competition landscape, long-term evolution trends, multiple thinking models

## 1. Industry structure analysis

### 1.1 The nature of Macau’s gaming industry

Use Munger's "think the other way around" method: under what circumstances will this industry get worse?

1. China opens up more areas to legal gambling → Macau’s monopoly disintegrates
2. The government significantly tightens policies → industry space is compressed
3. Legalization of online gambling → Offline casinos lose their meaning
4. China’s long-term economic recession → Declining spending power of gamblers

At present, among these four risks: (1) The probability is extremely low in the short term, and gambling is still prohibited in mainland China; (2) It has already experienced a round of regulatory tightening in 2022, and the industry will become healthier after adapting; (3) China severely cracks down on online gambling; (4) There is a certain possibility but it is not a structural risk.

**Conclusion**: The industry structure of Macau's gaming industry will remain a "government-chartered oligopoly" in the foreseeable future.

### 1.2 Industry scale and ceiling

| Indicators | 2019 (pre-pandemic peak) | 2025 | 2026 forecast |
|------|-------------------|--------|-----------|
| Macau GGR | MOP 292.4 billion | HKD 240.2 billion | ~HKD 260 billion |
| Visitors to Macau | 39.4 million | 40.1 million (new high) | ~42 million |

The number of passengers has exceeded the peak in 2019, but there is still a gap of about 10-15% in GGR. The core reasons are:
- Significant shrinkage of VIP room business (exit of gaming promoters)
- Per capita consumption in the midfield has not yet fully recovered
- Changes in visitor structure (more leisure travelers vs. pure gamblers)

The ceiling of the industry depends on: the growth rate of China’s high-net-worth population × the proportion of them choosing Macau for consumption. In the long term, the growth of China's middle-class population from 400 million to 600-800 million will continue to provide Macau with incremental tourists.

### 1.3 Industry Economics

Core Economics of the Gambling Industry:
- **Extremely high gross profit margin**: The gross profit margin of table games is about 25-30% (Baccarat house advantage)
- **High fixed costs**: resort depreciation and labor are the major contributors
- **Extremely high marginal profit margin**: for each additional gambler, the incremental cost is extremely low
- **Strongly cyclical**: Revenue fluctuates significantly with economic cycles and policy cycles

This means: when the industry is good, profit elasticity is extremely high, and when the industry is bad, losses may also be serious. Galaxy's choice of a zero-debt strategy is a hedge against this high volatility.

## 2. Competitive Landscape—Six Nations Struggle for Hegemony

### 2.1 Positioning of the six major gaming companies

| Company | Market Share (2025) | Positioning | Core Strengths |
|------|---------------|------|----------|
| Sands China | ~25% | Integrated resort + MICE | Largest convention and exhibition facility, most guest rooms |
| Galaxy Entertainment | ~21.7% | Mid- to high-end comprehensive | Zero debt, largest land bank |
| Wynn Macau | ~12.5% | High-end boutique | Brand high-end positioning |
| MGM China | ~16% | High-end comprehensive | Art and culture differentiation |
| Melco Crown | ~14% | Young and high-end | City of Dreams brand |
| SJM Holdings | ~11% | Traditional local | The oldest and most stores |

### 2.2 Competitive Trends

**Market share is concentrating towards the top. ** CLSA pointed out that Galaxy Entertainment and Sands China will continue to gain share in 2025-2026, mainly from the transfer of Melco Resorts and SJM Holdings.

Galaxy's share increased from about 20% at the beginning of 2025 to 21.7% at the end of Q4, an increase of about 1.6 percentage points. CLSA further raised Galaxy's share assumption from 20% to 20.5% in 2026.

**Factors driving share changes:**
1. Hardware quality: new facilities attract more high-end customers
2. Non-gaming experience: Integrated resorts are more attractive to family travelers than pure casinos
3. Financial strength: Companies with money can invest more in marketing and customer service
4. Management execution: differences in operating efficiency

### 2.3 Comparison with Sands China (most direct competitor)

| Dimension | Galaxy Entertainment | Sands China |
|------|----------|----------|
| Market share | 21.7% | ~25% |
| Financial status | Net cash 35 billion | Net debt ~40 billion |
| Land bank | Largest (the fourth one is to be built) | Basically developed |
| Non-gaming | Strong, but not as big as Sands | Strongest (convention + retail) |
| Shareholder structure | Family holding ~50% | LVS holding ~70% |
| Valuation | PE ~13x | PE ~14x |

**Key Difference**: Galaxy relies on financial security and land reserves to win in the future, while Sands relies on the current largest size and most complete facilities to win today.

## 3. Sustainability of competitive advantage

### 3.1 "Ecological niche" analysis

Use Munger’s ecological thinking: every gaming company occupies an ecological niche.

Galaxy's ecological niche is: **The most financially secure mid- to high-end integrated resort operator with the greatest expansion potential**.

The sustainability of this ecological niche depends on:
- Can zero debt be maintained? → Can be maintained as long as management maintains financial discipline
- What happens when the land bank is exhausted? → There is really no room for large-scale expansion after the fourth phase
- Can the mid-to-high-end positioning be maintained? → Depends on hardware updates and service quality

### 3.2 Threat of substitutes

| Substitutes | Threat level | Analysis |
|--------|----------|------|
| Southeast Asian casinos (Philippines, Cambodia, etc.) | ★★☆☆☆ | Small scale, low quality, difficult to replace Macau |
| Singapore Casino | ★★☆☆☆ | Different positioning and only 2 licenses |
| Japan IR (Integrated Resort) | ★★★☆☆ | Osaka will open in 2030, which may divert some high-end customers |
| Online Gambling | ★☆☆☆☆ | China cracks down hard and the experience is completely different |
| Domestic consumption upgrade and substitution | ★★★☆☆ | Hainan duty-free, theme parks, etc. divert leisure demand |

**Overall judgment**: The threat of substitutes will not constitute a substantial impact in the foreseeable 5-10 years. Japan's IR is the most noteworthy medium- and long-term variable.

### 3.3 Industry cyclicality

The gaming industry has obvious overlapping policy cycles and economic cycles:

| Phase | Period | Triggers | Galactic Impact |
|------|------|----------|-------------|
| Boom period | 2010-2013 | China’s high economic growth + VIP room expansion | Stock price rose from 1 yuan to 90 yuan |
| Recession period | 2014-2016 | Anti-corruption + economic slowdown | Profits cut in half |
| Recovery period | 2017-2019 | Midfield transformation successful | Performance restored to high levels |
| Crisis period | 2020-2022 | Epidemic + regulatory tightening | Huge losses |
| New recovery period | 2023-2025 | Customs clearance + tourism recovery | Record high performance |

**Where are you currently? ** Late recovery/maturation period. Revenue is close to 2019 levels and growth is slowing. The industry may enter a period of medium to low growth (annualized at 5-8%) until the next policy or economic cycle triggers new changes.

## 4. Industry Final Deduction

### 4.1 Macau Gaming Industry in 10 Years

**High probability scenario:**
- The structure of the six gaming companies remains unchanged (licenses will be valid until 2032, with a high probability of renewal thereafter)
- GGR reaches HK$300-350 billion (annualized growth of 4-6%)
- The proportion of mass market increased to 75-80%, and VIP rooms further shrank.
- Increase the proportion of non-gaming revenue to 30-40%
- The top three (Sands, Galaxy, MGM/Wynn) have a combined share of over 60%

**Variables with low probability but large impact:**
- Opening up gambling in a certain place in China → Reshaping the industry landscape
- Successful operation of Japanese IR → diverting high-end customers
- Technological changes (VR gambling, etc.) → changing consumption patterns
- Sino-U.S. relations deteriorate → U.S.-owned gaming companies are forced to withdraw

### 4.2 The final position of the galaxy

Under a high probability scenario, Galaxy may become the first or second largest gaming company in Macau:
- After the fourth phase is put into operation, the total floor area of Galaxy will be the largest in Macau
- If share gains continue, it may reach 23-25% by 2030
- Financial advantages enable M&A capabilities during the next industry downturn

Galaxy holds the second-best position in a good industry (after the size of Sands) and has the best balance sheet and the most room for growth. If Munger were to evaluate, he might say: This is a company with "a good position in a good industry." The only question is whether the price you pay for this position is reasonable.
