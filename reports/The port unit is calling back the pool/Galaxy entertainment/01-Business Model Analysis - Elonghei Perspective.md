# Galaxy Entertainment (0027.HK) Business Model Analysis

> Analysis dimensions: business nature, user value, moat depth, whether management is "doing the right thing"

## 1. What kind of business is this?

### 1.1 Nature of business

Galaxy Entertainment's core business is to operate integrated resorts in Macau, with revenue sources including gaming (mass, VIP) and non-gaming (hotels, restaurants, retail, entertainment).

In essence, this is a business of ** license monopoly + real estate + consumption **:

- **License Monopoly**: Macau only has 6 gaming licenses, which will be valid until 2032 after renewal in 2022. It is almost impossible for new entrants to obtain a license, which is the toughest barrier to entry.
- **Real Estate Attributes**: Resorts are heavy assets. Once built, they will be long-term operating assets. Galaxy has the industry's largest reserve land bank in Cotai (the total development area will reach 2 million square meters).
- **Consumption attributes**: Gambling and tourism consumption have optional consumption characteristics and are greatly affected by economic cycles and policies.

### 1.2 Evolution of income structure

Macau’s gaming industry has undergone profound structural transformation:

| Period | Proportion of VIP business | Proportion of mass business | Driving force |
|------|-------------|-------------|--------|
| 2010 | 78% | 22% | Gambling junket traffic |
| 2019 | 38% | 62% | Organic midfield growth |
| 2025 | <15% | >65% | Intermediary exit + policy guidance |

Galaxy Entertainment's non-gaming revenue will account for close to 20% in 2025, and policy requirements will further increase in the future. This means that the company is transforming from a "casino" to an "integrated resort".

### 1.3 Profit model

The mathematical advantage of gambling ensures that the banker will definitely make profits in the long run (the banker advantage in Baccarat is about 1.06-1.24%). The key variables are:
- **Passenger flow** (number of tourists visiting Macao)
- **Per capita consumption** (gaming table yield, non-gaming consumption)
- **Operating Efficiency** (EBITDA Margin)

The number of tourists visiting Macau in 2025 will reach a record high of 40.1 million, and Galaxy’s adjusted EBITDA profit margin will be approximately 29.5%.

## 2. Moat analysis

### 2.1 License Barrier ★★★★★

This is the core moat. Only a few regions around the world allow legal gambling, and Macau is the only legal gambling place in China. Six licenses means an oligopoly - you can't get a seventh license by "working harder".

**Positive**: Barriers are extremely high and new entrants are completely excluded.
**Converse**: Licenses are granted by the government, and policy risks always exist. It needs to be renewed after 10 years, and the government can impose conditions.

### 2.2 Land Bank ★★★★☆

Galaxy owns Macau's largest undeveloped land bank in Cotai. The third phase (Capella, opening in 2026) and the fourth phase (expected to be completed in 2027) will bring the total area to 2 million square meters, far exceeding its peers.

**Positive**: Maximum room for organic growth, no need to bid for new land.
**Conside**: Large capex can suppress short-term free cash flow.

### 2.3 Brand and customer stickiness ★★★☆☆

The Galaxy brand has a certain degree of recognition in the mid-to-high-end mass market, but the overall brand loyalty of gaming consumers is not as good as that of fast-moving consumer goods. Gamblers value the environment, service and "luck" more than the brand itself.

**Positive**: Galaxy World’s hardware facilities are the latest in Macau, and Capella is positioned as ultra-high-end.
**Cons**: Customer switching costs are low and gamblers can move freely between casinos.

### 2.4 Economies of Scale ★★★★☆

The larger the resort and the more guests there are, the lower the unit operating costs will be. Galaxy Integrated Resort City is one of the largest complexes in Macau, with obvious scale effect.

## 3. "Doing the right thing" vs. "doing things right"

### 3.1 Strategic direction (doing the right things)

**What you did right:**
- It laid out large tracts of land in Cotai in the early days. While competitors were still grabbing the Macau peninsula, Galaxy bet on the Cotai Strip.
- Adhere to a business model that is not dependent on gaming junkets, and suffered minimal damage when the "anti-corruption" attack on VIP rooms in 2015
- Maintaining a zero-debt balance sheet has demonstrated its survival advantages in the three years of the epidemic.
- Continuous investment in non-gaming businesses is in line with policy directions

**Debatable:**
- The investment in the Hengqin project is about 10 billion yuan, but progress is slow and it is uncertain when returns will be generated.
- The investment in the fourth phase of the project is huge, and peak capital expenditure is expected in 2026, which may affect the pace of shareholder returns.
- The feasibility and risk-benefit ratio of overseas expansion (such as the Thai market) are still unclear

### 3.2 Execution (doing things right)

Galaxy’s operating efficiency is at the upper-middle level in the industry:
- EBITDA will grow by 19% in 2025, exceeding the overall industry growth rate
- Market share increased from approximately 20% at the beginning of the year to 21.7% at the end of Q4
- The Capella project has a reasonable pace from trial operation to official opening

However, the EBITDA profit margin (approximately 29.5%) is still lower than the pre-epidemic level (approximately 32-33%), and the main reason is the increase in the cost of attracting customers during the transformation.

## 4. User value creation

Who does Galaxy Entertainment create value for?

1. **Gamblers**: Provide legal, safe and high-quality gambling experience
2. **Tourists**: Integrated resorts provide a "one-stop" leisure experience
3. **Macau Economy**: Contribute to tax revenue, employment, and promote economic diversification
4. **Shareholders**: Steady growth in profits and dividends

From the perspective of "doing the right thing", Galaxy's user value proposition is clear: **Provide world-class comprehensive entertainment and vacation experiences for China's high-net-worth individuals**. This positioning is in line with the trend of consumption upgrading and policy requirements.

## 5. Business model score

| Dimensions | Rating | Description |
|------|------|------|
| Business simplicity | ★★★★☆ | The core logic is simple (license + passenger flow + mathematical advantage), but it is greatly affected by policies |
| Moat Depth | ★★★★☆ | License monopoly is extremely strong, but policies can change the rules of the game |
| Free cash flow quality | ★★★☆☆ | Large capital expenditures in the expansion stage, good cash flow in the mature stage |
| Growth certainty | ★★★☆☆ | There is a ceiling for passenger growth, and the effect of non-gaming transformation needs to be verified |
| "Do the right thing" | ★★★★☆ | The strategic direction is correct and the execution is steady |

**Overall Rating: ★★★★☆ (3.6/5)**

This is a good business with strong barriers, but it is not a "perfect" business. Its weaknesses are: (1) Highly dependent on a single region (Macau); (2) Largely affected by policy risks; (3) Consumption attributes lead to performance fluctuations. For long-term investors, the core question is: Do you believe that Macau's status as the only legal gambling destination in China will not change in the next 10-20 years?
