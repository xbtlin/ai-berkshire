# 1.3 "Golden" of profit

In 2025, there was a significant accounting adjustment effect: one-time accounting adjustments related to new energy subsidies have held back the profits of new energy sources. The loss of these sources would have been less significant, but the impact of lower prices and dumping is real.

The large increase in the profits from fire power (+64.7%) is more cyclical — the profits from the coal were compressed when the price of coal was high in 2022-2023, and the profit elasticity of the fallback in the price of coal released in 2024-2025. This "parity regression"-type growth should not be pushed out.

# II. Balance sheet analysis

##2.1 Asset structure

By the end of 2023 (the most recent complete disclosure of data), the total assets were approximately HK$32 billion, with fixed assets and construction in progress accounting for the majority. The total assets increased to HK$346.8 billion in mid-2025.

Asset-end core focus:

** Quality of new energy assets**: In the first half of 2025, the new energy capital expenditure was approximately HK$15.8 billion, with an estimated HK$42 billion for the year. Will these assets yield sufficient returns in an environment where electricity prices continue to fall? If future prices fall further by 10-15%, IRRs in some projects may fall short of the cost of funding.

** Receivables from renewable energy subsidies**: This is a "deep thunder" in the electricity sector. Although data on subsidies arrears in Huayun are not separately disclosed, the problem of subsidies arrears in the entire industry is long overdue and is also being asked about in the IPO audit.

#2.3 Capital efficiency

For example, a wind-fire project invests $1 billion, generates 150 million per year, earns about $50 million net after deduction of peacekeeping interest, and has a simple recovery period of about 20 years. This means that companies need to invest new capital constantly to sustain growth, and the return on old projects may be eroded in the context of below-priced electricity.

This "runner effect" is a common disease in the heavy asset industry: you cannot stop because depreciation is consuming the value of the stock; but you run faster and the capital demand is increasing.

# III. Cash flow analysis

#3.1 Operating cash flow

The cash flow from the operation of the Hua Yu electricity is relatively robust, with EBITDA running approximately HK$ 40 billion and approximately HK$ 25-30 billion between the districts (after deduction of operating funds and taxes).

# 3.2 Free cash flow

This is the most important indicator of Buffett's concern - free cash flows = operating cash flows — capital expenditures.

In 2025, for example:
- Operating cash flows: approximately HK$ 25-30 billion
- Capital expenditure: approximately HK$56.8 billion
- **Free cash flow: approximately HK$27 billion to $32 billion**

Yes, Hwa Yu is currently ** negative for free cash flow. The company does not make enough money to make up for the gap. This is a typical dilemma for "growing utilities" — a huge amount of money is being burned for future loading.

** What does this mean for the valuation?**

If evaluated using the DCF (cash flow) model, the free cash flow of HC-YE could remain negative or very low over the next five to eight years. The company's value is more dependent on "cash flow released after capital spending slows in the future". This makes the DCF valuation extremely sensitive to assumptions and uncertainty.

#3.3 Equities sustainability

In 2025, the dividends were paid by a "lending bonus" in essence, with a rate of HK$ 1.127 per dividends, 40.2 per cent and 5.7 per cent, and in a situation of negative free cash flows. This was not unsustainable — so long as companies could finance them, they could continue to receive the dividends — but it depended on the continued support of capital markets and the maintenance of credit ratings.

## 4.4 Security margin assessment

Under Buffett's "safe margin" framework:

** EXCHANGE** (30% probability): The price of coal continues to be low, electricity is stable, new energy is being consumed, with profits growing by 15-20% in 2026, corresponding to six times PE, and with the catalytic fragmentation, there is room for 30-40% increase in equity prices.

** Baseline scenario** (50% probability): Coal prices recovered slightly, electricity prices were moderate and down, profits from new energy were flat, profits were flat or small in 2026, stock prices fluctuated near current levels, and dividends earned 5-6%.

** Pessimistic scenario** (20% probability): coal prices rebounded significantly, electricity prices fell faster, the drop in the wind was exacerbated, profits fell by 15-20% in 2026 and shares may fall to HK$15-16.

In summary, the current valuation provides a "limited margin of security" – not particularly cheap, but not expensive. Downside space is about 10-15%, upward space is about 20-30%, and risk returns are comparable.

# Six, summing up

The financial picture of Hua Yu power is a "compromise": the profit sheet looks good (14.5 billion profit, 12 per cent ROE), but the cash flow statement reveals problems (failful free cash flow, large capital expenditure). The balance sheet is in a manageable but not comfortable position (67 per cent debt ratio).

From Buffett’s perspective, Huayun is not a “good” business – it earns almost all (or even more) its business back, and shareholders receive far less real returns than implied by book profits. But, under the seven-fold PE and 5.7% dividends valuation, it may be an opportunity to “bought reasonable businesses at reasonable prices” if investors have sufficient confidence in the long-term growth of demand in the electricity sector and in the efficiency of Huayun’s operations.
