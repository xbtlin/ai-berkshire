## 2. Investment thesis

### 2.1 One sentence summary

China Resources Power is a central power enterprise in a critical period of green transformation. It has an inexpensive valuation and acceptable dividends. However, it faces the dual challenges of falling electricity prices and excessive capital expenditures. It is an opportunity to "buy a reasonable company at a reasonable price" rather than "buy a good company at a good price."

### 2.2 Core facts

**Company level**:
- The managed grid-connected installed capacity exceeded 100 million kilowatts, with renewable energy accounting for over 50%
- Net profit attributable to parent companies in 2025 is HK$14.519 billion, a year-on-year increase of only 0.9%
- Thermal power profit +64.7% and new energy profit -17.6% form a "seesaw"
- China Resources New Energy's 24.5 billion A-share IPO was registered by the China Securities Regulatory Commission in May 2026
- Annual capital expenditure of HK$56.8 billion and negative free cash flow

**Valuation level**:
- PE 7.1 times, PB about 1 times
- Dividend yield 5.7%, dividend payout rate 40.2%
- Analysts’ average target price is HK$21.3 (currently HK$18.26)

**Industry Level**:
- China's non-fossil energy installed capacity accounted for more than 60%
- Green electricity prices continue to fall (wind power fell by 22% in two years, photovoltaic fell by 24% in two years)
- The wind and light abandonment rate increased rapidly (the light abandonment rate increased from 1.5% to 12.7%)
- The capacity price recovery ratio will be increased to 50% in 2026, and the "guaranteed income" of thermal power will be enhanced.
- Document No. 136 requires new energy to fully participate in market transactions

## 3. Debate between pros and cons

### 3.1 Look at the long argument

**Argument 1: Valuation safety margin is sufficient**
PE is 7 times, PB is 1 times, and the dividend rate is 5.7%. Even if profits do not grow, you can get good returns from dividends alone. Compared with the average dividend rate of 3-4% in the Hong Kong stock market and the 2.3% yield of 10-year government bonds, the dividend rate of 5.7% is attractive.

> Refutation: When free cash flow is negative, the high dividend rate is maintained by "borrowing money to distribute dividends", and its sustainability is questionable. Once financing conditions tighten, dividends may be forced to be cut.

**Thesis 2: The spin-off of China Resources New Energy releases value**
The A-share market usually values new energy assets at 20-30 times PE, which is much higher than the 7 times of Hong Kong stock parent companies. After the spin-off and listing, the value of undervalued new energy assets is expected to be released, driving the revaluation of the parent company.

>Rebuttal: After the spin-off, the remaining assets of the parent company are mainly thermal power, and may face a valuation discount due to "good assets being taken away". Moreover, China Resources New Energy's net profit will fall by 23.3% year-on-year in 2025, and there is uncertainty about whether the valuation of A-shares can remain high after listing.

**Argument 3: The value of thermal power is re-recognized**
The capacity price mechanism has been raised from 30% to 50%, and thermal power has changed from "generating electricity to earn the price difference" to "standby and receiving a guaranteed minimum". The proportion of deterministic revenue from thermal power assets has increased, profit volatility has decreased, and valuations are expected to return from "cyclical stocks" to "public utilities".

> Refutation: The implementation details of the capacity price policy and the implementation situation in different places may vary greatly. And if coal prices rebound, the buffer in capacity prices may not be enough to fully offset rising costs.

**Argument 4: AI data centers bring new demand for electricity**
The AI revolution has brought huge increases in electricity demand. The power consumption of a single large data center is equivalent to that of a small city, which provides a new growth pole for the power industry.

> Refutation: The growth in demand for data centers takes time to materialize, and data centers usually have high requirements for power quality and are more inclined to build their own distributed energy resources or sign long-term PPAs. The "cake" that large power generation companies can get may be limited.

### 3.2 Bearish argument

**Argument 1: The downward trend in electricity prices has not yet bottomed out**
The growth rate of new energy installed capacity (20-35%) far exceeds the growth rate of electricity demand (5%), resulting in serious oversupply. After Document No. 136 requires new energy to fully participate in market-based transactions, the price of green electricity may accelerate its decline. For every 5% drop in electricity prices, new energy profits may drop by 10-15%.

>Rebuttal: Electricity prices will not fall indefinitely. When prices fall below project costs, new construction will slow and supply and demand will eventually rebalance. The construction of capacity electricity prices and ancillary service markets is also providing "bottom support" for electricity prices.

**Argument 2: Abandoning wind and light is a structural problem**
The light abandonment rate soared from 1.5% to 12.7%, reflecting the fundamental mismatch between the power grid’s consumption capacity and the installed capacity of new energy. This problem requires huge power grid investment and energy storage configuration to solve, and it cannot be completed in one or two years.

> Refutation: The country has listed new energy consumption as a key task. In 2025, the National Development and Reform Commission issued a notice on the weight of responsibilities for renewable energy consumption, mandating that provinces increase their consumption proportions. Policies are driving problem solving.

**Argument 3: Large-scale capital expenditure "destroys value"**
The annual capital expenditure of 56.8 billion corresponds to a profit of 14.5 billion, and the capital input-output ratio is extremely low. In an environment of downward electricity prices, the IRR of newly added assets may fall below the cost of capital, leading to a situation where "the more you invest, the more you lose."

>Rebuttal: The current high capital expenditure is phased, mainly to complete the installation target of the "14th Five-Year Plan". During the "15th Five-Year Plan" period, the growth rate of installed capacity may slow down, capital expenditures are expected to converge, and there is room for improvement in free cash flow.

**Thesis 4: The governance of central enterprises is naturally not conducive to small shareholders**
Management incentives are not entirely aligned with shareholder interests, and the urge to "get bigger" may outweigh the drive to "increase returns." Under the central enterprise system, small shareholders have almost no influence on company decisions.

>Rebuttal: In recent years, the State-owned Assets Supervision and Administration Commission has promoted "market value management" and "improvement of quality and efficiency" of central enterprises, and has clear assessment requirements for the ROE and dividend rate of listed companies. The governance of central enterprises is improving, albeit slowly.

## 4. Key Assumptions and Scenario Analysis

### 4.1 Optimistic scenario (probability 25%)

**Hypothesis**: Coal prices run at low levels, green electricity prices stabilize, wind and solar power abandonment improves, and China Resources New Energy’s A-share listing achieves high valuations

**Expectation**: Profit will increase by 15-20% to HK$16.5-17.5 billion in 2026, corresponding to a PE of 5.5-6 times. Superimposed on the expectations of spin-off catalysis and market value management of central enterprises, the stock price target is HK$22-25 (+20% to +37%).

### 4.2 Baseline scenario (probability 50%)

**Assumptions**: Coal prices rebound slightly, green electricity prices fall moderately, wind and solar power abandonment rates remain high, and new energy listings proceed as planned.

**Expectation**: Profit will remain flat or slightly increase to HK$14.5-15.5 billion in 2026, with PE maintained at 6.5-7.5 times. The stock price fluctuates in the range of 17-20 Hong Kong dollars, earning 5-6% dividend income. The total return is approximately 5-15%.

### 4.3 Pessimistic scenario (probability 25%)

**Hypothesis**: Coal prices rebound sharply, green electricity prices accelerate their decline, consumption problems worsen, and subsidies are impaired

**Expectation**: Profit in 2026 will fall by 15-25% to HK$11.0-12.5 billion, corresponding to a PE of 7.5-8.5 times. The stock price may fall back to HK$14-16 (-13% to -23%). But the 5.7% dividend yield provides some cushion.

### 4.4 Calculation of expected return

Weighted expected return = 25% x (+28%) + 50% x (+10%) + 25% x (-18%) = **+7.5%**

Combined with the 5.7% dividend yield, the total expected return is approximately **+13%**. This is a "decent but not exciting" expected return.

## 5. Investment Conclusion

### 5.1 Qualitative judgment

China Resources Power is an investment target with "medium quality and reasonable valuation". It is not the kind of opportunity that makes people "beat the thigh" - there is no extremely low valuation, no explosive growth, and no irreplaceable competitive advantage. But it is not a trap - the business fundamentals are solid, cash inflows are predictable, and the status of a central enterprise provides a safety cushion.

To use an analogy: China Resources Power is a "3% government bond + some equity fluctuations". You don't buy it to double, but to obtain relatively stable returns in an uncertain market.

### 5.2 What kind of investors is it suitable for?

**Fits**:
- Income investors seeking stable dividend income
- Investors who are optimistic about the long-term demand growth trend of China's power industry
- Portfolio managers who want to allocate defensive sectors in Hong Kong stocks

**Not suitable**:
- Growth investors who pursue high growth and high flexibility
- Value investors who have strict requirements on free cash flow
- Investors who have fundamental concerns about the governance of central enterprises

## 6. One sentence summary

> ** China Resources Power is a state-owned power enterprise in transformation, with reasonable valuation, good dividends and controllable risks, but it lacks exciting sources of "excess returns". It can be considered for allocation as a defensive income target, but it is not recommended as a core position in a portfolio. It is worth putting it in the observation pool and waiting for electricity prices to stabilize or the stock price to fall below HK$15 before actively getting involved. **
