##1.3 Policy environment: market-based reforms into deep water

The period 2025-2026 was a crucial period for the reform of China ' s electricity market, and several major policy changes are noteworthy:

**136**: marks the official entry of new energy generation from "safe acquisitions" into "full participation in market trading." This means that the new energy policy umbrella is shrinking and will be competing in the market for real guns in the future.

** Capacity price adjustment**: Since 2026, the share of electricity recovered from the power capacity of the fire has increased from 30% to no less than 50%. The underlying meaning of this policy is that the government accepts the "spill" and "safe supply" functions of the power, and is willing to pay for it. The power of the fire has changed from "bringing off the power" to "bringing the money."

** Downward trend in electricity prices**: The overall trend in the price of electricity on the Internet for all types of power sources was downward in 2025. The price of electricity on fire was adjusted downwards with coal prices, while the price of green electricity was accelerated by oversupply.

# Three, multi-thinking model analysis

#3.1 Economy of scale model

The electricity sector has economies of scale, but not "winner-take" economies. Large power producers have advantages in financing costs, equipment procurement, and talent attraction, but electricity is a regional market and national size does not translate directly into regional competitive advantages.

The 100 million kilowatts of Chinese electricity for management is a "big enough but not crushing" scale — large enough to enjoy financial facilities and policy concerns in the country, but not sufficient to create a monopoly in the industry.

#3.2 Supply and demand models

The core contradiction of the current electricity sector is** that the supply is growing at a significantly faster rate than the demand. Especially in the new energy sector, wind and light engines are growing at an annual rate of 20-35%, while electricity demand is increasing at only 5%.

The paradox is unlikely to be resolved in the short term — the country's "carbon peak carbon neutral" goal determines that the growth of new energy installed will not slow, and demand growth is subject to macroeconomic constraints.

#3.3 Model of incentive mechanisms

Mange attaches great importance to incentives. In China’s electricity sector, the incentives of the management of the company are not fully consistent with shareholders’ interests:
- Management ' s assessment indicators include installed growth, safe production, social responsibility, etc., with profit being only one dimension
- The impulse to do mass could be more than the drive to raise the rate of return.
- The completion rate of the new energy loading indicator is a political task, and even the project ' s return may be advanced if it is not ideal

This explains why China Yu power is accelerating loading in the face of rising drop-out rates - incentives-driven behaviour may not necessarily be compatible with maximizing shareholder interests.

#3.4 Physics model: energy for persistence

Thinking about the electricity sector from a physics perspective: solar and wind energy are "free" energy, but transforming them into stable electricity requires huge system costs - storage, grid modification, peak power, etc. The current downward trend in electricity prices reflects, to some extent, the new energy "negative cost zero" feature that is lowering the average price of the system, but the "system integration cost" is not fully accounted for.

This means that the current green electricity price is not sustainable — either by making the cost of the system visible through mechanisms such as capacity prices, the market for auxiliary services, or by regressing the price of electricity to cover the entire system. In the long run, the price of electricity may have a "bottom" that does not fall indefinitely.

# Four: Reverse thinking: Under what circumstances will this investment fail?

Manger used to say, "Tell me where it's gonna be, I'm not going there."

# 4.1 The biggest risk scenario

** Scene one: electricity prices continue to accelerate down **
If green electricity access prices fall by another 20-30% over the next three years, IRRs for new energy projects will fall into the cost of financing, and investment in production will be at risk of impairment. Given China’s hundreds of billions of new energy assets, the potential impact of impairment is enormous.

** Scene II: Coal price refraction plus electricity price down **
The restoration of the profits from the fire is based on lower coal prices. If the price of coal is substantially higher as a result of geopolitical or supply contractions, and the price of electricity falls because of marketized transactions, the electricity may fall back into a loss.

** Scenario III: worsening of the problem of disinfection**
The drop rate has risen from 1.52% to 12.73% as a dangerous signal. If grid capacity does not keep pace with the build-up of the installed machines, a large number of new wind projects may "sell the power out of the market" and the return on investment is much reduced.

** Scenario IV: Refund of benefits less than expected**
The new energy subsidy receivables are a "dark thunder." If policy changes make part of the subsidy unpayable, they may require significant impairment.

# Six, summing up

In the light of the Mange's thinking framework:

Power is a permanent need, new energy is an age-old one. But "good industry" is not "good investment" -- overloading is underpressing industry returns.

** Does this company have a competitive advantage?** Yes, but not prominent. Huayun has a comparative advantage in balanced allocation, operational efficiency and credit, but in a highly homogenous market of electricity, it is difficult for any single firm to create lasting barriers to competition.

** Is the current price good?** In comparison with industry, Chinese electricity is valued at medium in the peer, not particularly cheap. Seven times the PE corresponds to a "no or low growth" expectation, and the current price is attractive if the company can achieve a steady 5-10% profit growth; if profits stagnate or fall, seven times the PE does not provide sufficient security margins.

Ultimately, the central bet for investing in Chinese electricity is:** After the current "value-up" pain, can China's electricity sector rebalance supply and demand through policy adjustments and market clean-up, and reverse the price and profit?** The answer to this question will determine whether China's electricity is a "value trap" or a "value-deep spot".
