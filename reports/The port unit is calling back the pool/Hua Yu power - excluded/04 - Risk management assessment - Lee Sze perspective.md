# One, risk panorama scan

The investment framework of Lee-soo emphasizes "risk first, opportunity later." For Chinese electricity, the risk can be divided into three levels: lethal, significant and manageable.

# 1.1 Fatal risk: Is there anything that would make the company "dead"?

**Answer: No.**

The Chinese electricity industry, as a large power company with a holdings in China, has virtually no risk of bankruptcy.
- Electricity is an infrastructure for national security and economic operations and cannot be allowed to collapse.
- The total assets of the Huayun Group are over HK$ 2 trillion, supported by sufficient resources
- AAA credit rating, financing channels open.
- Electricity assets have a base value in kind

But "not dead" does not mean "not losing money." Companies can stay closed, but shares can be depressed for a long time.

##1.2 Significant risk: factors that may lead to a significant decline in profits

** Risk I: Green electricity prices continue to fall [level of impact: <unk> ]**

This is the most central risk to China's electricity.
- Average wind and wind: from 0.45 - > 0.44 - > 0.35 per kilowatt hour (22 per cent reduction for two years)
- Average photovoltaic price: from 0.37 - > 0.32 - > 0.28 kWh (24 per cent reduction for two years)

Text 136 requires that new energy sources become fully involved in marketized transactions, and the green power price "protective umbrella" officially disappears. In a context where new energy installed far more rapidly than demand increases, the pressure on lower prices is structural, not cyclical.

The profits from the new energy core of China Yu electricity in 2025 have already fallen by 17.6 per cent over the same period, and if electricity prices fall by another 15-20 per cent, the profits from the new energy business may be cut off.

**Risk II: Increased problem of dumping [level of impact: <unk> ]**

The data on the rate of dumping of new energy sources in China is worrying:
- Wind-out rate: 2.80% - > 4.16% - > 5.26% (annual increase)
- Dropout rate: 1.52% - > 6.59% - > 12.73% (several deterioration)

The rate of discards jumped from 1.52 to 12.73 per cent in two years, meaning that nearly 13 degrees of photovoltaic power per 100 degrees were wasted. This directly reduced the real rate of return on photovoltaic projects.

The root cause of the problem is that the grid is lagging behind the new energy installed. Addressing this will require massive grid investment and storage, which is not a unilateral solution for China-Yu power.

**Risk three: Coal price rebound risk [level of impact: <unk> ]**

In 2025, the profits from the fire and electricity increased by 64.7%, mainly from the lower coal prices. In 2026, the industry pre-empted the power coal price center for a small upturn. The profits from the coal business are extremely sensitive to the price of the coal – a 50-ton increase in coal prices could eat up billions of dollars.

However, the adjustment of the capacity price policy (to 50% recovery) provides a cushion for the profits from the fire power. Even if coal prices rise, the large losses from 2021-2022 are unlikely to occur again.

** Risk 4: impairment of new energy subsidies [impact level: <unk> ]**

The regulatory authorities have focused on the issue in the context of the review of the energy IPO. If some projects are not eventually included in the subsidy list or if the subsidy policy changes further, companies are at risk of recognizing a part of their income impairment or refunding the subsidy.

The specific amount is not fully disclosed, but the potential impact may reach billions of levels, given the scale of new energy sources in China.

** Risk five: Capital expenditure is out of control [level of impact: <unk> ]**

In 2025, the capital expenditure was HK$56.8 billion, of which 42 billion was for new energy. Is it rational to spend large amounts of capital in an environment of electricity prices and dissipation?

This raises the fundamental question of whether Huayun is "reasonable investment" or "delivering"? The installation of the engine is a political task attribute, and management may still have to move the project forward, knowing that the rate of return has fallen.

#1.3 Controllable risk

** Interest rate risk**: highly leveraged enterprises are naturally sensitive to interest rates. But China is currently in a interest-reduction cycle with low short-term interest rate risk.

** Risk of policy change**: The electricity sector policy is frequently reoriented, but the overall direction (marketization + cleaning) is clear. Policy risks are more about "pacing" and "strength" than about a directional reversal.

** Exchange risk**: Hong Kong dollar-denominated assets, renminbi income, with a certain exchange rate mismatch. However, China Yu is home to most of its assets and liabilities, with limited exchange rate impact.

# II. Management assessment

##2.1 Management structure

The management structure of Hua Yu power is the "group holding + career manager" model:
- ** Holdings**: Hua Yun (Group) Limited, State Council, Direct Management of China Corporation
- ** Chairman of the Group**: Wang Xiangming
- ** Managing Director, Hua Yui Power**: Wang Bo (appointed in December 2024)

Wang Bo, as the new Managing Director, presented the 15th Five-Year Plan orientation at the March 2025 workshop, focusing on three main components: clean and efficient power generation, integrated energy services, and energy science, technology and innovation.

#2.2 Speciality of Management of the United States

From a Lee Siu's perspective, management assesses that there is a need to pay particular attention to "the consistency of management and shareholder interests".

**Enaps**:
- ** High level of professionalism**: The executives of the company usually have a rich professional experience and a professional background
- ** Long-term perspective**: Unlike the founder of a private enterprise, who may have a passion, the management of the company is looking at the long-term sustainable development
- ** Strong resource mobilization capacity**: access to quality resources through group and government relations

** Disadvantage**:
- **Incentive deficiency**: The remuneration of the executive is low compared to marketed enterprises, equity incentives are limited and there is no strong incentive to make "big cakes".
- ** Rotation system**: The executive director of the company usually rotates once for 3-5 years, and the "management of the company's running water with a bar" leads to uncertainty about the continuity and depth of the strategy
- ** Multi-target constraints**: Management needs to meet both the National Finance Committee (profits, security, social responsibility), political requirements (loading targets, carbon peaks) and market expectations (equity prices, dividends), possible conflict between multiple objectives
- ** Efficiency in decision-making**: Major investment decisions require a layer of approval and response is not as fast as private enterprises

#2.3 Strategic judgement of management

Several key strategic decisions in the past few years by Hua Yuon power are worth reviewing:

** Do the right thing **
1. **New energy sources pre-positioned**: winding speeded up in 2019, with clear first-hand advantages and greater access to high-quality wind resources
2. ** Disclosing new energy listing**: good timing for IPOs in the high valuation window period for new energy assets in the A share market
3. **Retention of fire-beds**: No new "all in" energy, as some companies do, maintaining a balanced configuration of fire-power plus new energy sources

** It's debatable:
1. ** ** Is the loading speed too fast**: is there a "growth for growth" tendency to plan for the addition of 10 GW wind-loaders in 2025 against the backdrop of a rapid increase in the rate of abandonment of wind?
2. ** Capital expenditure management**: 56.8 billion annual capital expenditure corresponds to 14.5 billion profits, with low output ratio of capital inputs
3. ** Price risk hedge**: Companies appear to lack effective risk hedging in the face of persistent downs in green electricity prices

#2.4 Capital allocation capacity

This is one of Li Su's most focused managerial competencies: how can we spend it when the company makes money?

The capital allocation priorities for HPR are broadly:
1. **New energy investments** (approximately 42 billion/year): largest financial destination
2. **Score** (approximately HK$5.8 billion): distribution rate 40.2%
3. ** Fire and electric transmission peacekeeping upgrade** (approximately 10-15 billion/year)
4. ** Debt service**: net liability is increasing in real terms

The question is: Is it the best option to invest more than 70 per cent of capital expenditure on new energy sources in the downward trend in the rate of return on new energy sources? Should there be a higher share of the dividends, a buy-back or a slower pace of investment?

The answer is often "This is a national strategy, and it has to be voted on." But for small shareholders, "national interest" and "shareholder interest" are not always the same.

# III. Evaluation of governance structures

#3.1 Equity structure

China Rough (Ggroup) Ltd. holds an equity interest of about 62.95% of China Rough Power and is in absolute positions. This highly concentrated equity structure has advantages and disadvantages:

**Lire**: The interests of major shareholders are highly tied to the interests of companies, there is no risk of "barbargue" invasion, and the strategic direction is clear.

** Negative**: Small shareholders have limited voice and large shareholders may influence the interests of listed companies through related transactions, resource allocation, etc.

#3.2 Associated transaction risk

As a listed platform under the Group’s banner, there are a large number of related transactions between China’s electricity and the Group (coal purchases, asset injections, management fees, etc.). Continued attention is needed to the fairness of the associated transactions.

#3.3 Quality of information disclosure

The general regulation of information disclosure for Hua Yu power is that, as a company listed as a port stock, it is subject to the rules of disclosure at the port office. However, the port unit has insufficient frequency and detail of quarterly data disclosure compared to A share company.

#3.4 Risks associated with IPOs for new energy sources in China

In the IPO audit of the new energy source in Hua Yun, two rounds of queries were presented, with the following concerns:
- 200+ administrative penalties
- Compliance with renewable energy subsidies
- Fairness of associated transactions
- Profit sustainability (23.27% to $6.102 billion in 2025 net profit on a year-on-year basis)

These inquiries revealed a number of risk points that had previously been less transparent in the Hong Kong stock statements and that deserved the attention of investors.

# Four, "Stocks are buyers" -- a comprehensive assessment

Lee Szee stressed that "buying shares is buying people", but for the company, the "person" factor is important and limited:

** Important**: Strategic direction of management, efficiency in implementation and capital allocation capacity
** Limited **: The exercise of individual capacity in the institutional framework is limited

The management of Hua Yu power has made no major mistake in its strategic direction – pre-displaced new energy sources, pushed for market segmentation, and kept balanced. But, in terms of "creating value for shareholders," the management of the company naturally lacks sufficient incentives to maximize shareholder returns.

For such a firm power as Hua Yun, investors should not expect management to have the same passion and energy as "entrepreneurs." The legitimate expectation is that management can (1) avoid major strategic failures, (2) maintain operational efficiency at the forefront of the industry, and (3) maintain a reasonable level of redency. These three points are largely met in the light of current performance.

# Six, summing up

From the investment philosophy of Lee Su, the core question for investing in Hwa Yu power is:** is the risk fully compensated at this price?**

** Risk end**: Downside electricity prices, disbursing difficulties, excessive capital spending are three real threats that could lead to lower than expected profit growth or even a decline over the next 2-3 years. Incentives for management in the firm also mean that "maximizing shareholder benefits" is not the first priority.

** End of compensation**: 7 times the PE and 5.7% dividends provide a buffer. More importantly, China Yu power does not "dead" – the worst is a 20-30% profit decline, and the corresponding PE increases from 7 to 9-10, which is still within reasonable limits.

** Conclusion**: Hua Yu power is a target for "risk limited and return limited". It is suitable for investors who pursue stable returns (euro plus moderate growth), but not well suited for high-growth, flexible investors. The key decision variable is your judgement on the price of electricity in China's electricity sector and your tolerance for "Golding of China's Governance".
