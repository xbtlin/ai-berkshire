# Analysis of Chow Tai Fook (1929.HK) business model

> Analysis dimensions: user value, business essence, differentiated positioning, long-term competitiveness
> Core question: Is this company doing "the right thing"? Do users really need it?

---

## 1. What business is the company doing?

Chow Tai Fook is Asia's largest jewelry retailer, and its core business is the sale of gold jewelry**. Business model breakdown:

1. **Gold Jewelry** (Majority): Including plain gold jewelry priced by gram weight and fixed price (fixed price) gold jewelry
2. **Jewelry inlaid jewelry**: diamonds, gemstones and other inlaid products
3. **Platinum/K gold jewelry**: relatively small proportion
4. **Retail Network**: Covering 5,663 retail points in mainland China and 151 retail points in Hong Kong and Macao

In essence, what Chow Tai Fook is doing is a retail business with "brand premium + channel coverage" - reaching consumers through brand trust and a wide range of channels, and obtaining premiums in the standard/semi-standard product market of gold and jewelry.

## 2. User value analysis: Why do consumers buy Chow Tai Fook?

### Consumer Portraits and Purchasing Motives

1. **Rigid necessities for weddings (core)**: The "three golds" (gold necklace, gold ring, gold bracelet) for Chinese weddings are traditional necessities. Chow Tai Fook is one of the preferred brands for wedding gold jewellery.

2. **Investment/Value Preservation Demand**: Against the background of the continued rise in gold prices, some consumers regard gold jewelry as a "wearable asset."

3. **Daily jewelry consumption**: Young consumers use gold jewelry as daily accessories.

4. **Gift-Giving Scenarios**: Demand for gold gifts in birthdays, festivals, newborns, etc.

### Reasons why consumers choose Chow Tai Fook

1. **Brand Trust**: 90 years of history, brand endorsement of "real gold and real diamonds". In gold and jewelry, a field with extremely high "information asymmetry", brand is the biggest guarantee for consumers.
2. **Convenient channels**: 5,800+ retail points, which can be found in shopping malls in most cities.
3. **Product value preservation**: Chow Tai Fook’s pure gold products have good buyback and trade-in services.
4. **Design improvement**: In recent years, product lines such as "Forbidden City Co-branded" have improved their design sense.

### Key Questions

1. **Is gold jewelry a "good business"? **Essentially, this is a "porter" business - buying gold from upstream, processing it and retailing it to consumers. The added value comes from the brand and channel, not the originality of the product itself.

2. **Gold price dependence**: When the gold price rises, consumers "buy up" to promote sales; when the gold price falls, consumers wait and see. The company's fortunes are largely controlled by the price of gold.

3. Are **channels an advantage or a burden? ** Among the 5,663 retail points, a large number are located in sinking markets. Against the background of high gold prices and declining unit quantities, are the sales per square meter of these stores increasing or decreasing?

## 3. "Right" and "Wrong" business models

### Do the right thing ★★★☆☆

**Right:**

1. **The value of brands in gold jewelry is extremely high**: What consumers fear most when buying gold jewelry is "fake goods". The trust premium provided by the brand is more important than any other consumer product in this industry. Chow Tai Fook's 90-year brand accumulation is a real asset.

2. **Product structure transformation**: From "priced by the gram" to "fixed price/fixed gold" products. In fiscal year 2025, the contribution of priced gold products will increase, and the gross profit margin will significantly increase from 24% to 29.5%. This is the correct transformation from selling gold to selling design.

3. **Trade-in ecology**: The "trade-in" service for gold jewelry creates customer stickiness - consumers bring old gold to the store to exchange for new models, forming a continuous flow and consumption cycle.

4. **High Dividend**: The company maintains a high dividend ratio (dividend rate 5-6%) and is investor-friendly.

**What’s wrong:**

1. **Severe reliance on gold prices**: This is not a truly "autonomous pricing" business. Profits improve when gold prices rise and worsen when gold prices fall. The company has no control over raw material prices.

2. **A large number of store closures**: 611 stores were closed in the mainland (in the first half of fiscal year 2026), indicating that the past expansion strategy was too aggressive and many stores were not profitable.

3. **High leverage and low profits**: Jewelry retail is essentially a "high inventory turnover" business, and gold inventory takes up a lot of funds. The revenue is nearly 90 billion but the net profit is only 5.5 billion (net profit margin is about 6%), which shows that this business is "big but not profitable".

4. **Channel homogeneity**: Chow Tai Fook, Luk Fook, Chow Sang Sang, Lao Feng Xiang... These brands are often located next to each other in shopping malls, with many choices for consumers and extremely high price transparency. There is limited differentiation between brands.

## 4. In-depth questioning of the nature of business model

### "Is this a good business?"

**Duan Yongping's standards**: A good business should be one where "users can't live without you", "competitors can't learn from you", and "making money is effortless".

Use this standard to examine Chow Tai Fook:
- **Can users leave you? ** able. Consumers can go to Luk Fook, Lao Feng Xiang, or even gold stores at any time. Gold is the standard product, and the cost of brand switching is very low.
- **Can competitors learn? ** Can learn. The threshold for opening a jewelry store is not high, and building a brand takes time but is not impossible.
- **Is it difficult to make money? ** Effortless. Thousands of stores need to be maintained, huge gold inventories need to be managed, and gold prices fluctuate. The net interest rate is only 6%.

**Conclusion: This is not a "great business", but an "average business that can make money." **

### "What is the essence of gold retail?"

In essence, it is a "trust intermediary" - consumers trust Chow Tai Fook's brand, so they are willing to buy gold here (rather than in unknown gold stores). But the value of a trusted intermediary depends on:
1. Information asymmetry - if consumers can easily identify the authenticity of gold, the brand value will be reduced
2. Number of competing brands – too many alternative brands will dilute the value of a single brand
3. Alternative channels – the rise of online gold sales may weaken the importance of offline stores

### "What will happen to Chow Tai Fook in ten years?"

- People will still buy gold jewelry ten years from now ✓ (Chinese people have deep-rooted gold complex)
- The Chow Tai Fook brand will still exist ten years later ✓ (high probability)
- But the channel form may change (fewer offline stores, more online)
- The direction of gold prices is unpredictable and directly affects company performance
- Whether profit margins can continue to improve is a key variable

## 5. Differentiated positioning and moat assessment

### Moat Analysis ★★★☆☆

| Moat Types | Assessment |
|-----------|------|
| Brand value | ★★★★☆ 90-year-old brand, leader in the gold industry |
| Channel Network | ★★★★☆ 5800+ retail points with extremely wide coverage |
| Scale effect | ★★★☆☆ Procurement has certain advantages but not significant |
| Switching cost | ★★☆☆☆ Extremely low, consumers can switch brands at any time |
| Product differentiation | ★★☆☆☆ Gold is a standard product with limited differences |

### Core moat: brand trust + channel density

In China's third- and fourth-tier cities, when consumers want to buy gold jewelry, they have limited choices of trustworthy brands. The three words "Chow Tai Fook" are the best quality assurance. This brand barrier is stronger in sinking markets than in first- and second-tier cities.

## 6. Comprehensive assessment

| Dimensions | Rating | Description |
|------|------|------|
| User value | ★★★★☆ | Gold jewelry is a real demand, and brands provide trust |
| Business logic | ★★★☆☆ | High leverage, low profit, greatly affected by gold price |
| Positioning differentiation | ★★★☆☆ | Well-known brand but limited differentiation among peers |
| Moat depth | ★★★☆☆ | The brand is valuable but consumers have low switching costs |
| Long-term certainty | ★★★☆☆ | Unpredictability of gold prices makes performance fluctuate |

**Overall Business Model Rating: ★★★☆☆ (3/5)**

Chow Tai Fook is a "passable but not outstanding" business - the brand is valuable and demand is certain, but it has high leverage and low profits, relies heavily on gold prices, and has low consumer switching costs. It is more like a "gold industry channel provider" rather than a true "branded consumer goods company." It performs well during rising gold price cycles, but may come under pressure when gold prices fall.
