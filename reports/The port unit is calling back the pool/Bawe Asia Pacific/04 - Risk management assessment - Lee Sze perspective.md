# Budweiser Asia Pacific (1876.HK) Risk and Management Assessment

> Analysis dimensions: management quality, governance structure, long-term value creation capabilities, factors with Chinese characteristics

## 1. Assessment of management and governance structure

### Core question: Who does management serve?

There is a fundamental problem with Anheuser-Busch Asia Pacific's governance structure: ** Management is essentially an agent of parent company AB InBev, not a trustee for minority shareholders. **

Specific performance:
1. The CEO is appointed by the parent company (the new CEO will take office in 2025)
2. The strategic direction is subject to the group’s global layout
3. Prioritize capital allocation to meet the parent company’s dividend needs
4. Brand strategy needs to coordinate with global brand unified management

This means that no matter how capable the management of Budweiser Asia Pacific is, their primary KPI is to meet the needs of the parent company, rather than maximizing the value of minority shareholders of the listed company.

### The "blood drawing" mechanism of parent company AB InBev

AB InBev holds approximately 87% of Anheuser-Busch Asia Pacific. Its methods of extracting value from Budweiser Asia Pacific include:

1. **Direct dividend**: US$750 million in 2025, dividend payout rate 153%
2. **Management fee/brand licensing fee**: The specific amount is not transparent, but it must exist
3. **Raw material purchasing arrangements**: Possible benefits through internal transfer pricing
4. **Technical/system service fee**: Use of the group’s IT system, brewing technology, etc.

### Impact of the new CEO

Budweiser Asia Pacific will have a new CEO in 2025. This is both a risk and an opportunity:
- **Opportunity**: New leadership may bring about a more aggressive localization strategy
- **Risk**: New appointments usually require 1-2 years of adaptation, and their power is still restricted by the parent company

## 3. Long-term value creation ability

### Li Lu Framework: Is the company accumulating long-term competitiveness?

**More negative evidence**:

1. **Brand value is consuming rather than accumulating**: Declining market share = The brand’s position in consumers’ minds is weakening
2. **Excess dividends consume the balance sheet**: If this money is used for brand building and channel investment, long-term competitiveness will be stronger
3. **No breakthrough innovation**: There are no disruptive new products in the product line and no revolutionary changes in channels.
4. **Localization is not deep enough**: Compared with China Resources and Qingdao, Budweiser is slower to gain insight into and respond to Chinese consumers.

**Positive Evidence**:

1. **The Indian market layout is far-sighted**: The high-end + ultra-high-end product portfolio has grown by 20%+, cultivating new engines for long-term growth
2. **Multi-brand matrix still has advantages**: Budweiser, Corona, Fujia and other brands still have appeal in their respective market segments
3. **Global R&D/Marketing Resource Sharing**: The global capabilities from the parent company are difficult to match for local brands

## 4. Special considerations for the Chinese market

### The deep impact of the "Consumption Nation Trend" trend

Li Lu paid special attention to the long-term trends of social change in China. In the field of consumer goods, "national trends" are not short-term fashion, but a manifestation of deep cultural confidence:

- Young consumers no longer need to prove their taste by consuming foreign brands
- The quality of domestic high-end brands has reached or even surpassed foreign brands
- The positive narrative of "Light of Domestic Products" makes consumers feel proud when choosing domestic products

For Budweiser, this means that its "foreign brand premium" is structurally shrinking. In the past, choosing Budweiser meant "I can afford high-end products"; now, choosing China Resources/Qingdao high-end line means "I have taste and support domestic products".

### Competitive disadvantages of channel depth

Competition in China's consumer goods market often comes down to channel depth. China Resources Beer has the most extensive distribution network in China and can reach a large number of county and township markets. Budweiser's channel advantages are mainly in nightclubs and high-end restaurants in first- and second-tier cities, and these channels are shrinking.

## 5. Investor structure and market positioning

### The "shareholder positioning" issue of Budweiser Asia Pacific

When the company was listed, it was positioned as an "Asia-Pacific high-growth consumer goods target" and attracted a large number of foreign funds pursuing growth. But as the growth story falls apart:
- Foreign capital continues to reduce its positions
- Valuation center moves downward
- Shrinking transaction volume
- May enter a "valuation trap" - looks cheap but continues to be cheap

## 7. Core Conclusions

Starting from Li Lu's investment philosophy, Budweiser Asia Pacific has two "fatal flaws":

1. **The governance structure is not friendly to minority shareholders**: Excess dividends and capital allocation decisions under the control of the parent company prevent minority shareholders from truly sharing the company's long-term value creation. Even if the company's operations improve, the additional value created may be extracted by the parent company.

2. **Being on the "headwind side" of China's consumption trends**: The rise of national trends, downgrading of consumption, changes in channels - these structural trends are all unfavorable to foreign high-end beer brands.

**Li Lu's possible judgment**: This is not an object that a long-term investor should hold. The governance structure determines that minority shareholders cannot receive a fair distribution of returns, and industry trends determine that even if short-term valuations appear reasonable, long-term returns may be disappointing.

The only investment logic is "transactional": if China's economic recovery exceeds expectations and brings about a rebound in high-end consumption, Budweiser may have short-term valuation repairs. But this is not the "long-term holding of high-quality companies" investment approach pursued by Li Lu.
