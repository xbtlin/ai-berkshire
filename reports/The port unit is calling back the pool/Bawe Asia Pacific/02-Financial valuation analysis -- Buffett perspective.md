# Budweiser Asia Pacific (1876.HK) Financial Valuation Analysis

> Analysis dimensions: earnings quality, capital return, free cash flow, safety margin

## 1. Profitability analysis

### Revenue and Profit Trends

| Year | Revenue (USD 100 million) | Year-on-year | EBITDA (USD 100 million) | Net profit attributable to parent companies (USD 100 million) |
|------|---------------|------|-----------------|-------------------|
| 2022 | 65.5 | +4% | 20.0 | 9.6 |
| 2023 | 67.2 | +2.6% | 19.6 | 9.2 |
| 2024 | 61.4 | -8.6% | 17.6 | 7.3 |
| 2025 | 57.6 | -6.1% | 15.9 | 4.9 |
| 2026E | 58.3-62.5 | +1%-8% | 16.5-17.5 | 6.3-8.6 |

**Key Findings**: Budweiser Asia Pacific’s revenue and profits have declined for two consecutive years. Net profit will drop from US$960 million in 2022 to US$490 million in 2025, halved in two years. EBITDA fell 21% during the same period.

### Earnings Quality Assessment

**WORRYING SIGNALS**:
1. **Double kill in revenue in China**: sales volume dropped by 3.9% + ton price dropped by 7.7% = revenue dropped by approximately 11.6%
2. **EBITDA profit margin compression**: EBITDA in China plummeted 42.3% year-on-year
3. **Profit fell faster than revenue**: Net profit attributable to parent companies fell by 32.6%, far exceeding the revenue decline of 6.1%, indicating that operating leverage amplified the downward impact.

**Positive Signs**:
1. **India/Korea Growth Hedge**: The Indian market continued double-digit growth in Q4, and the high-end product portfolio grew by 20%+
2. **Cost control space**: There is room for optimization of cost per ton
3. **Analysts expect growth to resume in 2026**

## 2. Capital return analysis

### ROE and ROIC

Budweiser Asia Pacific's ROE and ROIC were once bright spots (15-20% range), but as profits continue to decline, return on capital is deteriorating:

- ROE in 2022: approximately 18%
- ROE in 2023: approximately 16%
- ROE in 2024: approximately 13%
- ROE in 2025: approximately 9% (estimate)

ROE dropped from 18% to 9%, which reflects not only cyclical fluctuations, but also a decline in structural competitiveness.

### Capital allocation: distortions dominated by parent companies

Budweiser Asia Pacific’s capital allocation shows unusual characteristics:

1. **Dividend payout ratio exceeds 100%**: Dividend payment in 2025 is US$750 million, net profit is only US$490 million, and dividend payout ratio is 153%
2. **Meaning**: The company is draining its balance sheet to maintain dividend levels
3. **Reason**: Parent company AB InBev needs stable cash flow to pay off the huge debt incurred in acquiring SABMiller

**Warning from Buffett's perspective**: If a company needs to pay dividends that exceed profits to meet the needs of controlling shareholders, this is essentially a form of capital exploitation that is unfriendly to minority shareholders.

## 3. Free cash flow analysis

### Cash flow quality

Operating cash flow in the beer industry is generally of higher quality:
- Fast turnover of accounts receivable
- Moderate inventory turnover
-Capital expenditure demand is stable

But Budweiser Asia Pacific's free cash flow was also affected by the decline in profits:
- Operating cash flow in 2025 is estimated to be approximately US$1.2-1.4 billion
-Capital expenditure of approximately US$400-500 million
- Free cash flow of approximately US$700-900 million

Based on the current market capitalization of approximately US$13 billion, FCF Yield is approximately 5.5-7%, which seems reasonable, but please note:
- This comes after profits had already fallen sharply
- If China continues to deteriorate, FCF may shrink further

## 4. Valuation analysis

### Current valuation

| Indicators | Values | Evaluation |
|------|------|------|
| PE (2025 actual) | ~27 times | High |
| PE (2026 forecast) | ~18-20x | Medium |
| EV/EBITDA | ~8-9 times | Reasonable range for consumer goods |
| Dividend yield | ~5.5% | High but unsustainable |
| PB | ~2.5x | Moderate |

### The "trap" of valuation

On the surface, Budweiser Asia Pacific's expected PE of 18-20 times seems reasonable (global consumer goods leaders usually have 20-25 times). But it needs to be questioned:

1. **Are analyst forecasts too optimistic? ** 2026 profit forecasts vary widely ($630 million-$860 million), with high uncertainty
2. **When will China stabilize? ** After two consecutive years of decline, there are no clear signs of stabilization.
3. **Risk of continued loss of high-end market share**: If the share continues to drop from 40% to 35%, the profit impact will be greater

### Historical valuation comparison

- When listed in 2019: PE 35-40 times
- Peak in 2020-2021: PE 30-35 times
- 2022-2023: PE 25-30 times
- Current: PE 18-27 times (depending on which year's profits are used)

Valuations continue to be revised downwards, reflecting the market's downward revision of the company's long-term growth expectations.

## 5. Safety Margin Assessment

### Conservative valuation (DCF idea)

Assumptions:
- FCF averages US$600 million/year in the next five years (considering recovery in China but limited growth)
- 2% sustainable growth thereafter
- Discount rate 8%

Intrinsic value ≈ 6/(8%-2%) = US$10 billion ≈ HK$78 billion

The current market value is about HK$105 billion, with **no margin of safety** and may even be overvalued by about 35%.

### Optimistic hypothesis

If it is assumed that FCF returns to US$800 million (corresponding to China returning to 2023 levels):
Intrinsic value ≈ 8/(8%-2%) = US$13.3 billion ≈ HK$104 billion

Basically the same as the current market value, there is still no margin of safety.

## 6. Core issues of Buffett’s framework

### 1. Does it have a lasting competitive advantage?

Yes, but it is weakening. The global influence of the Budweiser brand remains strong, but it is an indisputable fact that it continues to lose share in the Chinese market - the company's largest single market.

### 2. Is management honest and competent?

The ability is acceptable, but "honesty" needs to be questioned. A payout ratio of 153% is not a decision that normal management would make - it is imposed by the parent company.

### 3. Will this company be stronger or weaker in ten years?

**Probably weaker**. The total beer market in China is shrinking (population decline + changes in drinking concepts), and the high-end space is being eroded by domestic brands. The growth of the Indian market may partially offset the decline, but it is difficult to fully compensate for the decline in China.

### 4. Does the current price provide a margin of safety?

**Not available**. 18-20 times PE is not a sufficient margin of safety for a company whose profits continue to decline, share continues to lose, and capital allocation is controlled by the parent company.

## 8. Core Conclusions

The current financial situation of Budweiser Asia Pacific presents the triple dilemma of "high valuation + declining profits + capital extraction". From the perspective of Buffett’s value investing:

1. **Valuation is not cheap enough**: 18-20 times PE does not provide a safety cushion for the risk of continued profit decline
2. **Harmful capital allocation**: Excess dividends under the control of the parent company are consuming the company’s competitiveness
3. **Unfavorable trend**: China’s high-end beer market share loss + consumption downgrade = low visibility of profit recovery

**Buffett won't buy this stock** - not because business is bad, but because the price is not cheap enough, the governance structure is unfavorable to minority shareholders, and the competitive trends are not on the company's side.
