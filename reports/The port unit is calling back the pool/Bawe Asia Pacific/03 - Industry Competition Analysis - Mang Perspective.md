# Budweiser Asia Pacific (1876.HK) industry competition analysis

> Analysis dimensions: industry ecological niche, competitive dynamics, reverse thinking, multidisciplinary model

## 1. Industry Panorama: Structural Changes in China’s Beer Market

### The total amount peaks, and the stock game

China's beer industry has entered a stage of stock or even shrinkage:
- Total beer production in 2024 will be 35.21 million liters, -0.6% year-on-year
- Per capita beer consumption continues to decline from its peak in 2014
- The driving force shifts from "quantity" to "price" (high-end)

### High-end: From Blue Ocean to Red Ocean

The premiumization of Chinese beer began around 2015, with Budweiser Asia Pacific being the earliest and largest beneficiary. But today, 10 years later, high-endization has changed from "blue ocean opportunity" to "red ocean competition":

- CR5 (China Resources, Qingdao, Budweiser, Chongqing, Yanjing) total share 76%+
- All leading companies are "moving up" - high-end is no longer Budweiser's unique advantage
- Growth in the high-end market has slowed down (consumption downgrade + base effect)

## 2. In-depth dismantling of the competitive landscape

### Comparison of ton prices of various parties (2024 data)

| Enterprise | Ton price (yuan) | High-end proportion | Trend |
|------|-----------|----------|------|
| Budweiser Asia Pacific | ~5300 | High | Decline |
| Chongqing Beer | ~5200 | High | Flat |

| China Resources Beer | ~3300 | Medium | Rapid rise |
| Yanjing Beer | ~3100 | Medium to low | Slowly rising |

Budweiser Asia Pacific has the highest ton price, but this also means that the "upward" price increase space is limited, while the "downward" competitive pressure is increasing.

### Budweiser’s share loss trajectory

- 2015: China’s high-end beer market share ~50%
- 2020: ~42%
- 2025: ~40%

It has lost 10 percentage points of share in 10 years, and the trend has not yet bottomed out. Mainly cannibalized by China Resources (Heineken) and Qingdao (Augute/White Beer).

### Competitor’s offensive strategy

**China Resources Beer**:
- Quickly enter the high-end market with the help of channel synergy from the acquisition of Heineken China
- In 2024, mid-to-high-end beer sales will account for more than 50% for the first time
- The growth rate of Lao Xuehua and Hong Jue doubled.
- National channel network is its greatest advantage

**Tsingtao Beer**:
- High-end lines such as Augute, White Beer, and IPA continue to develop
- The brand rejuvenation strategy has obvious effects
- Craft beer business layout

**Chongqing Beer (Carlsberg China)**:
- Wusu Beer’s national expansion
- Carlsberg Brand Matrix (1664, Greenburg, etc.)
- The western base is stable

## 3. Munger's "reverse thinking": What will make Budweiser reverse?

### Thinking "Under what circumstances would I be wrong?"

If you are bearish on Budweiser, you need to think about the possibility of a reversal:

1. **China’s economic recovery exceeds expectations**: High-end consumption picks up, nightclub channels recover, and Budweiser is the first to benefit
2. **Domestic high-end products have reached the ceiling**: It is difficult for China Resources and Qingdao’s high-end products to make further breakthroughs in terms of quality/image.
3. **New CEO brings changes**: The new CEO of Budweiser Asia Pacific will take office in 2025, which may bring new strategies
4. **Indian market explosion**: Young population structure + consumption upgrade = long-term high growth market

### Reversal probability evaluation

Frankly speaking, among the above reversal conditions, China's economic recovery is the most likely trigger. But even if the economy recovers, there are still doubts whether Budweiser can regain its lost share-domestic brands have formed structural advantages in quality and channels.

## 4. Multidisciplinary model analysis

### Physics Model: Inertia and Friction

The decline in Budweiser Asia Pacific's market share has "inertia" - once consumers form a new brand preference (China Resources/Qingdao), they need to overcome huge "frictions" (brand switching costs, channel laying, price competition) to return to Budweiser.

**Key Insight**: Market share loss is often irreversible unless disruptive product or channel innovation occurs. Budweiser is not currently demonstrating this kind of innovation.

### Ecological Model: Invasive vs. Native Species

Budweiser is an "invasive alien species" in the Chinese beer market - it quickly occupied the ecological niche in the early days by relying on brand differentiation (high-end foreign brands). However, as native species (China Resources, Qingdao) evolved to be equally competitive, the advantages of exotic species gradually disappeared.

Local brands have natural advantages:
- Better understand Chinese consumers’ taste preferences
- Channels are deeper and wider (sinking into the market)
- More flexible decision-making (no need to report to global headquarters)
- Brand narrative is more likely to resonate with national trends

### Psychological model: The fading of the endowment effect

In the early years, consumers chose Budweiser, partly due to the psychological endowment of "foreign brands = high-end". But a new generation of consumers:
- There is no "low-end" stereotype of domestic brands
- Pay more attention to the quality of the product itself rather than the nationality of the brand
- Have positive feelings towards "national tide"
- Information symmetry is improved and is no longer easily affected by brand premium

## 5. Long-term industry trends

### Structural Trends Against Budweiser

1. **Population Decline**: The size of the core beer consumer group (men aged 20-45) is shrinking
2. **Increased health awareness**: The growth of low-alcohol/non-alcoholic drinks squeezes beer consumption
3. **Changes in consumption scenarios**: Stay-at-home economy + changes in social interaction → Nightclub channels continue to shrink
4. **National Trend**: Young people’s favorability towards domestic brands increases

### Trends good for Budweiser

1. **Indian market dividends**: Young population, huge room for consumption upgrade
2. **Craft beer opportunity**: There is still growth in the high-end segment
3. **Expansion of non-ready-to-drink channels**: New growth in O2O, convenience stores, and e-commerce channels

## 7. Munger’s conclusion

### Core judgment

Munger will focus on "whether the company is in a structurally favorable competitive position." Budweiser Asia Pacific's answer is: **It used to be, but it's changing**.

The high-end beer market used to be Budweiser's "exclusive territory" (50% share), but now it has become a battlefield of multi-party melee (40% share and declining). This deterioration in the competitive landscape is structural and unlikely to be reversed by a short-term economic recovery.

### Munger’s possible judgment

"I don't like investing in a company that's losing market share, even if it's still No. 1 in its industry. Share loss means a competitor is doing something right and you're doing something wrong -- or worse, you're doing nothing wrong but times have changed and your advantage is no longer an advantage."

Budweiser Asia-Pacific may belong to the latter - it is not that the company has done anything wrong, but the rise of China's local brands and changes in consumer psychology have caused the ecological niche of "foreign high-end brands" to shrink.
