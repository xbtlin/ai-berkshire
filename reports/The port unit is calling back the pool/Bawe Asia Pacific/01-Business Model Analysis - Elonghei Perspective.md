#Business model analysis of Budweiser Asia Pacific (1876.HK)

> Analysis dimensions: user value, business nature, moat depth, management level of "duty"

## 1. Business essence: "user value" of high-end beer

### The essence of beer consumption

Beer is a "social lubricant" and a "mild relaxation tool." On this basis, high-end beer adds the added value of "identity mark" and "taste expression". Consumers buy Budweiser and Corona not only for the alcohol itself, but also for the lifestyle signals represented by these brands.

From the perspective of Duan Yongping's "user value": the user value of high-end beer is real - the brand premium stems from consumers' need to be "seen" in social scenes. This need is rooted in human nature and will not disappear.

### But the problem is: user value is being diluted

China's high-end beer market is undergoing fundamental changes:
1. **Consumption downgrade trend**: In the context of economic slowdown, some consumers return from high-end to mid-range
2. **Accelerating domestic substitution**: The quality and image of domestic brands such as China Resources (Heineken) and Qingdao (Augute) have rapidly improved in the high-end market.
3. **Channel changes**: Traditional nightclub/catering channels are shrinking, and non-ready-to-drink channels (retail, e-commerce) are less supportive of brand premiums than ready-to-drink channels.

## 2. Moat analysis

### Brand moat ★★★★☆

The Budweiser brand has a global history of more than 100 years, and has long ranked first in brand recognition in China's high-end beer market. Brands such as Corona and Fujia also have strong differentiated positioning.

**Front**:
- "Budweiser" is equivalent to "high-end beer" in the minds of Chinese consumers
-Multi-brand matrix covering different scenarios and price ranges
- Global marketing resource support (sporting event sponsorship, etc.)

**Backside**:
- The market share of high-end beer will drop from nearly 50% in 2015 to about 40% in 2025, and brand power is declining.
- Young consumers’ brand stickiness to Budweiser is lower than that of the previous generation
- Domestic high-end brands (China Resources Snow, Qingdao Augute) are competing for minds

### Channel moat ★★★☆☆

Budweiser Asia Pacific has established a distribution network covering millions of terminals in China, especially in the nightclub channel, which has traditional advantages.

**But the channel advantage is weakening**:
- The nightclub channel has shrunk due to the economic downturn and changes in consumption habits
- China Resources quickly penetrates the high-end market with the help of its national channel network
- O2O and e-commerce channels eliminate traditional distribution advantages

### Scale effect ★★★★☆

As the largest beer company in Asia-Pacific, Budweiser Asia-Pacific has significant scale advantages in procurement, production and logistics. However, the scale effect is mainly reflected on the cost side. When sales decline, fixed cost amortization becomes a disadvantage.

### Pricing power ★★★☆☆

Budweiser Asia Pacific's high-end positioning theoretically gives it strong pricing power. But the reality is: the ton price in China will drop by 7.7% year-on-year in 2025, indicating that in an environment of intensified competition, companies are being forced to maintain sales through price cuts/promotions, and their pricing power is weakening.

## 3. "Simplicity" assessment of business model

The beer business itself is a "simply good business":
- High degree of product standardization
- High consumption frequency and high repurchase rate
- Longer shelf life (compared to fresh food)
- Huge room for brand premium

But the specifics of Budweiser Asia Pacific add complexity:
- Parent company AB InBev’s strategic priorities may not align with its Asia-Pacific business
- The competitive landscape of China’s market is changing rapidly
- High-end strategy encounters headwinds from consumption downgrade

## 4. Assessment of the degree of "duty"

### Parent company's "blood draw" problem

This is the most alarming point in Budweiser Asia Pacific’s business model. As the controlling parent company of Budweiser Asia Pacific, AB InBev has huge global debt pressure (leftover after the acquisition of SABMiller), and has a strong incentive to "draw blood" from its Asia Pacific subsidiaries:

- Dividends of US$750 million in 2025, with a dividend payout rate of 153% (exceeding net profit!)
- The parent company extracts value through management fees, brand licensing fees, raw material procurement, etc.
-Capital expenditures in Asia Pacific are under the unified control of the parent company

**This seriously violates Duan Yongping's principle of "duty": the company should not sacrifice its long-term competitiveness for the benefit of the parent company. A dividend payout ratio of over 100% means that the company is consuming its own accumulation to meet the needs of the parent company.

### Management independence

Although Budweiser Asia Pacific is independently listed, its management is essentially delegated and controlled by AB InBev. This means:
- Prioritize group interests in strategic decisions
- Profit distribution is not entirely oriented to Asia-Pacific business development
- The interests of minority shareholders may be systematically ignored

## 6. Core Conclusions

Budweiser Asia Pacific has first-class brand equity and a leading position in the Asia-Pacific market, and the business model of the beer business itself is "good business." But from Duan Yongping's perspective, two key issues make it less than qualified as a "good company":

1. **The moat is narrowing**: China’s high-end beer market share dropped from 50% to 40%, and the trend continues
2. **Parent company draws blood**: The dividend payout ratio of 153% and the priority of capital allocation under the control of the parent company have caused the interests of minority shareholders to be diluted in the long term.

Duan Yongping might say: This is a good business, but it is not a good shareholder governance structure. For minority shareholders, the money the company makes doesn't really belong to you.
