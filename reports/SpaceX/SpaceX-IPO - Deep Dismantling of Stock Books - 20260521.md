In three years, ARPU dropped from $99 to $66, a 33 per cent decrease. The reason is simple: the new users come mainly from low-cost markets in developing countries. Users are rising, but the income per user is shrinking.

The growth story of the chain of stars will take two legs to build: the number of users running at low prices, the quality of the company and the airline. The company's customers are 30 to 300 times consumers, and the airline market is just beginning to have a huge potential. If the ratio of business and aviation does not rise rapidly, the "no gain" trap is ahead.

** Competition pattern**: There is no real threat among satellite peers — Kuiper of the Amazon has only launched two test satellites so far, OneWeb is slow to move forward, and the GW Constellation and G60 chains of China are still in the planning phase. The pre-eminence of the star chain is at least three to five years ahead. But more alarming competition comes from the ground: the 4G/5 G-based stations in Africa and South-East Asia are being laid quickly, and the core value of the chain, "unreachable places" , will be gradually narrowed as ground infrastructure improves. Satellite direct-line mobile phone operations — allowing ordinary smart phones to directly connect to satellites — may be key to opening up the new amount. SpaceX has signed a cooperation agreement with T-Mobile, and the FCC has approved the spectrum framework.

# Three, Eagle 9: A banknote printing machine. Starship: A bankroll shredder.

The story of the space plate is also torn apart.

** Eagle 9** is the most successful commercial rocket in space history. 170 launches were completed in 2025, with a success rate of over 99%, and foreign offers of about $74 million. Over 540 repeaters, turning "reusable" from concept to routine. They accounted for 95% of American orbit launches, 70% of global launches -- a de facto monopoly.

** Starship** is another story. The largest rocket in human history has experienced multiple test failures in 2025, including the break-up of atmospheric re-entry. In 2025, the development of starships invested about $3 billion, directly dragging space plates from profit to loss.

But the significance of a starship is not today, but what it wants to do: to reduce the cost of in-orbiting per kilogram from less than $1,000 to $100, or even less. If it does, the orbital data centre, the moon base, and the large-scale satellite deployment will become economically viable. If it fails, all of the narratives will lose their physical foundation.

** Starships are the cornerstone of the entire SpaceX valuation system.** Without the success of a starship, the deployment of second-generation satellites in the chain would have slowed, the orbital computing centre would have been impossible, and the NASA Mooning Contract would have been impossible to implement.

# Four. Artificial intelligence: $250 billion for a lost engine

In February 2026, SpaceX acquired xAI under the Mask flag (valued at $250 billion) on a stock-wide basis, placing all the Grok large models, X platforms (former Twitter) and related AI assets on the public domain. This merger changed the nature of SpaceX — no longer just a space company, but a complex of space, communications and artificial intelligence.

** Key data**:
- X platform, 550 million. Grok, 117 million.
- $3.2 billion in corporate smart plate revenue in 2025 and $6.4 billion in operating losses
- $12.7 billion in artificial intelligence capital expenditure in 2025, and $7.7 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (approximately $30.8 billion annually), with the rate of burning doubling
- Consolidation only 3 months, 50+ fellows and engineers separated, and all 11 xAI co-founders left

** Three key transactions revealed the true face of the AI plate**:

**The amount of the power lease with Anthropic**: Anthropic pays SpaceX $1.25 billion per month (annually 15 billion) to rent the XAI data centre, which lasts until 2029. This figure is worth thinking about — Anthony accumulated financing of about $15 billion by 2025, with annualized computing rent of $15 billion, which means that it pays SpaceX almost all the money it has in its way within one year. This is both indicative of the madness of the need for artificial intelligence and implies that the contract is highly dependent on the sustainability of Anthropic itself for its continued large financial flow. More importantly, the contract is accompanied by a 90-day termination clause, which can be withdrawn by either party within 90 days.

2. **According to Cursor’s options**: an offer of $60 billion, and a fee of $10 billion for break-up if Cursor chooses not to sell. This may be the most expensive "competing ban" agreement ever.

3. ** Spectrum transaction with EchoStar**: acquisition of satellite spectrum assets for EchoStar for $19.6 billion to pave the way for satellite direct cellular operations.

Each of these three transactions is in the $10 billion range and each is subject to significant uncertainty.

The AI plate is now a purely fee-burning machine. Business losses of $6.4 billion throughout 2025 and a single-quarter loss of $2.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026 – the rate of burning money is accelerating. Its existence is for two purposes: adding the label "Associative Smart" to the SpaceX narrative to sustain higher valuations, and providing technical reserves for forward orbital computing centres.

To reduce reliance on British Weidelda GPU, SpaceX also launched the Terafab semiconductor manufacturing project (an initial investment of $55 billion) jointly with Tesla and Intel, with the goal of moving its own chip away from dependence on British Weibo. But the project, "no defined financial terms, no schedule for the allocation or development of intellectual property" is still in its intended phase.

# Five, one's empire: 85.1% voting rights mean something

Disclosures in the books of equity on corporate governance are the most appropriate part to read.

** Equity structure**: Three types of shares - 1 vote in A, 10 votes in B, zero votes in C. Mask holds 93.6% of B shares and controls 85.1% of voting rights after IPO.

** What does that mean?** Means that investors in the open market have little influence on governance. The offer even explicitly states that SpaceX is a controlled company, and quotes the Nasdak rule exempts most independent directors.

** More details to be noted**:
- **Forced arbitration**: waiver of jury and class action rights by shareholders
- **Texas Register**: Registered from Delaware to Texas
- **One-way valve for type B units**: The transfer of the B shares automatically downgraded to category A (lost 10 times the voting right), but Mask is not restricted to new category B shares. Voting rights only flow to and from Mask.

Three pension institutions that manage trillions of dollars in assets — CalPERS, the New York State Joint Retirement Fund, and the Auditor General of the City of New York — have jointly sent a letter to SpaceX, calling it “the most favourable governance arrangement ever for management in the public distribution of this scale”.

# Nine, IPO structural design: every step of a calculated scale

The structure of the IPO itself deserves to be studied:

**Super-small floating ratio**: Only about 4% of shares are sold (75 billion/1.75 trillion). Usually IPOs sell 15-25%, and NASDAQ also requires at least 10% of shares of listed companies to be publicly circulating. However, NASDAQ reportedly exempted this requirement specifically for SpaceX.

** Unprecedented distribution**: up to 30% of the share is allocated to the bulk, purchased through the Robinhood, Fieldity and Charles Schwab platforms. The $22.5 billion bulk is about three times the standard allocation.

** Fragmentation**: Not traditional 180 days a cut. The first 20% is unlocked after the second quarter of the year, and then 7% every 15 days until they are released.

** Index integration**: The new regulation of NASDAQ 100 allows for the inclusion of only 15 transactions after listing (provided the market value is 40). At 1.75 trillion market value, SpaceX will be ranked 6th to 8th. It is expected to be included in July, when the demand for compulsory purchases from passive funds is estimated at $22 to $60 billion.

Four percent of the floating ratio is faced with tens of billions of passive purchasing demand, with a huge imbalance between supply and demand. This is a carefully designed, scarce equation.

# Ten, government depends: 20% of the cards, and the dark ones

The offer reveals that about one fifth of the revenue comes from the United States federal government. But this is only a figure that is explicit.

** NASA contract**: cumulatively approximately $15 billion (US$ 2.6 billion in manned space, US$ 700 million in freight, US$ 4.04 billion in landers, etc.).

** Defense contract**: National Security Space Launch (NSSL) Phase 3 received $5.92 billion in orders (28 missions). NRO's confidential spy satellite contract is $1.8 billion. NASA $739 million.

**Starshield**: The military version of the Star Chain is the largest black hole in information. At least 183 satellites have been launched, the contract ceiling has been expanded from 900 million to $13 billion, and SpaceX has received approximately 97% of task orders. But investors have little visibility for their earnings and profitability as classified items.

The real share of government revenue, with classified items, could reach 30-35%. And this dependence is in two directions – the US government's reliance on SpaceX may be deeper than SpaceX's reliance on government, because no other company can replace SpaceX's launch capability for the foreseeable future.

** Political risk**: The role of the DoGE (Government Efficiency) in Mask and its close relationship with the Trump government raise serious conflict of interest questions.

# Twelve, conclusion: What kind of decision are you making?

The price of $1.75 trillion requires you to believe in all of the following:

- The chain can continue to grow at a high rate as ARPU drops.
- Starships can be reusable reliably by 2027.
- A.I. plates can shift from a quarter loss of 2.5 billion to a profit.
- The Anthony contract is sustainable at $1.25 billion a month.
- Orbital computing is more than a concept.
- Musk's attention is enough.

** It's a price "everything has to go smoothly."**

The total median value of the segment plus the total was approximately $1.25 trillion (FutureSearch), with fair value between $1.1 and $1.7 trillion (morning star). 1.75 trillion was in the upper range of the most optimistic.

** For long-term investors**: SpaceX has real and rare assets – the only profitable broadband satellite Internet worldwide, monopoly launch capability, and non-repeatable space infrastructure. These are core values worth long-standing possession. But buying at 94 times market sales, you need to accept a significant retreat that may occur in the medium term.

** For short-term traders: ** 4% floating ratio plus tens of billions of passive index buying demand, short-term supply and demand are seriously imbalanced. But a stratification mechanism means that every 15 days from the 70th day after being listed, there are internals.

** Mars is the soul of SpaceX, but not its business. ** The buyers of SpaceX buy cash streams from the starring chain, multi-purpose potential from the starship, and the risk of artificial intelligence infrastructure. Mars dream is to make all this seem much more ambitious. But remember, if the Mask pay scheme were to be triggered all the time, about 1.3 billion new B shares would dilute the existing shareholders by about 15 to 20% – the Mars Colony might not come, but the pressure of dilution of equity is written in black and white.

** Three signals worth continuous observation**:

1. ** Progress in the reuse of Starships**: If the first full reuse cannot be achieved in 2027, the technical premise of the entire valuation system will be shaken.
2. **Standards of customers**: if the three-quarters report in 2026 contained a ratio of income of more than 25 per cent for business and aviation customers, the downward trend in ARPU is expected to reverse.
3. ** Development of contract renewal for Anthropic**: Any sign of deterioration of the relationship in contracts with a monthly payment of $1.25 billion is a major warning.

If all three signals are moving in the negative direction over the next 12 months, the $1.7 trillion valuation will be under severe pressure. Conversely, if the starship succeeds, the business customers are released, and the computing contract is solid, SpaceX will prove itself worthy of that price.
