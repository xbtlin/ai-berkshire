# How big model companies make money: from MiniMax, Ideas to OpenAI, Anthropic

** Date: 2026-07-02**

The stock is bought for companies, and the future cash flow is bought for companies. Using this ruler to model companies is the most honest and cruel way to measure. This paper puts two model companies (MiniMax, Ideas) that are already listed in the country under the same framework and two leading companies (OpenAI, Anthropic) in the United States, answering the question: ** How does model companies make money?**

Let's start with the conclusion:

1. Future cash flows from domestic model companies are currently a matter of faith and not of measurement;
2. The cash flow between the two head United States is "unable" and not "non-existent" - the difference in pricing rights;
3. Determinability sorting: a model-level start-up company that sells shovels > large plants with eco-distribution > > existing good business with AI landings > application-level companies > > model-level companies.

Structural dilemma: not enforcement, business

** First, token may be the fastest-depreciated commodity in history.** Six rounds of API price battle in the first half of 2026, DeepSeek pushed down the base price to one dollar per thousand token, at a price of more than 90% lower than at the beginning of 2025. 90% for every 18 months of product, while cost ends (chips, algorithms, researchers) were rigid - they had pricing rights upstream, they didn't. This structure is like fibres in 2000, like the airline industry:** industry changed the world but shareholders did not earn money.**

** Second, open source commercializes model capabilities.** After DeepSeek, the popular source of the Great Episode, "good model" is no longer a moat, and the cost of client switching is almost zero. The moat can only be built outside the model – distribution, user relations, data wheels, ecology – which are bytes, Ali, and the field of the evangel. Large factories can use model as a central cost centre, and put money on cloud and application; start-up companies can play price wars with large factories, and they can play with their own lives.

** Third, look at the shape of their "future cash flow":**

- ** Brain spectrum**: Detach AI's coat, increasingly like a political corporation IT integrator - big projects, customization, receivables, bidding. This is a traditional IT-integrator economic model that has never produced a free cash flow of high quality in history. The industry's self-help direction is "from selling API to selling results," but the result is essentially a labour-intensive service, not a profit margin for software.
- **MiniMax**: C-side purchase and advertising is a proven way of making cash, closer to traditional Internet cash flow. But product moats - there are many competitions for emotional accompaniment, user loyalty is based on "good chatting," and modeling is commercialized. It's more like a sea-going application, not a "big model."

# Valuation

The intellectual spectrum is about 30 billion dollars in value for 700 billion dollars, with a market sales rate of about 400 times. To make this price reasonable, it needs to be believed that income doubles by more than five to ten years a year, with a software-grade profit margin at the end -- two assumptions that are barely created by themselves, and that are almost miracles.

** Companies that do not understand future cash flows are companies that cannot buy.** Not that they will not succeed -- there may be one that eventually run out. The problem is that the winners cannot be identified in advance; even if the industry wins, the structure of the price war determines that shareholders do not necessarily win. This stage of buying is a venture, a second-tier market price, and a double risk.

# II, OpenAI and Anthropic: Two paths that are almost opposite

# Income structure: mirror relations

** OpenAI is a consumer company.** Annualized income in 2026 is about $20-25 billion, mostly from ChatGPT subscriptions - about 20 million of 700 million users per week paid for, and more than 95% of users paid no. The business model essence: first, free products to take over the "access to the AI era" and now to be left behind (subscription, advertising, division of electricity, business services). This is Google's play.

**Anthropic is a corporate company.** Approximately 80-85% of the revenue is from the API paid by firms and developers on token, with annualized revenues amounting to $30 billion in April 2026, exceeding for the first time OpenAI (note: analysis indicates that part of the income is reported as a total cloud channel, with the headlines being discounted). The brightest single item is Claude Code: went online in May 2025, and reached $2.5 billion in February 2026, with clients including Netflix, Salesforce, etc. It sells not "talk" but "work" - directly from the enterprise's software and human budget.

# Why are they better off than the brain spectrum, MiniMax?

** Pricing rights**. Domestic DeepSeek hits token to a point, US front-line model pricing is largely stable - only two or three of the most capable (especially programming and smart body) are available, and customers pay unsensitively for the best model to work. The difference between selling cabbage and selling a dumpster is not at cost, and there are no alternatives for customers.
** Customer's wallet**. Anthropic cuts the software and manpower budgets of American enterprises, the thickest dollar in the world; and the intellectual tacks of domestic corporations' IT budgets — thin, slow and expensive.
3. ** The scale is beyond the threshold**. The annual income of over $20 billion means that the large fixed cost of model development is distributed between the east and the west; the income of 700 million yuan is in the balance.

# But the DCF doesn't count on them

It's not like you can be a value-worthy company because you're better than your domestic counterparts. The real problem is:

- The rapid depreciation of model assets — a model of 10 billion trainings this year, with a zero-regime value after 18 months;
- The unprecedented scale of capital expenditure and the suspicion of revolving financing (Vida, Microsoft, cloud producers investing in each other ' s procurement);
- Google and open-source models can close capacity gaps at any time;
- The forecast of Anthropic's "70 billion revenue in 2028, 17 billion positive cash flow" is still essentially a map that needs to be trusted before it can be understood.

# Three, Conclusion: Who's future cash flow in AI can see

Sort by certainty:

** shovels** (calculation, electricity, advanced sealing) - demand is the sum of all players, without betting on who wins;
** Large plants with distribution and ecology** - models are their costs, not their revenues;
** Existing good business with AI real efficiency reduction** - AI is a magnifying agent of profit margins;
** Applied Company** - close to the customer's wallet, far from price wars, but individual success or failure is difficult to judge;
** Model Level Entrepreneurs** - Cash flow is either "unable" (OpenAI, Anthropic) or "not exist" (current intellectual spectrum, MiniMax).

If you have to distinguish between the model layer: Anthropic's business model is more like a business that can understand -- who the customer is, why the money is paid, how the money becomes profit, the chain is clear, and the rest is a question of enforcement and competition; OpenAI is a bigger lottery. Neither is cheap. And the intellectual spectrum and MiniMax, now contain a very optimistic picture, and the trend is just to get the market ahead of it.
