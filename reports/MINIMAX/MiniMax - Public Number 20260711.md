# MiniMax: Unpaid boss, pretending to be a big loss

On July 9, MiniMax (St.T., Port Unit 00100.HK) first released on the market, with 153 million shares pouring into the market, accounting for 48.9 per cent of the circulation, and stock prices fell by about 18 per cent on the same day.

The following day, the founder, Joon-jae, sent an open letter in his own internal capacity: he would not receive any remuneration until AGI was realized; he would give his own share of about 4% in the next four years to motivate the team and take about 1% to set up the Open Source Fund.

A non-wage boss. A book loss of $1.87 billion, and a real burning of $250 million. These two counterintuitive things happen to be the best two entry points to this company.

The article does one thing: to use this letter and the company's equity structure as two keys to open up a question — what is MiniMax doing in the business, what it will be in three, five, ten years.

Put the conclusions on the table before you get halfway lost. This is a card game. The cards are first-rate, the bets are on real silver, but the people sitting at the table are not easy, and the chips (equity prices) that enter the arena are expensive for a long time.

It's a stake, not a compound share. It's a good payoff, a fair win, and a zero tail.

We're going to follow this table, one card to the other.

# One, inside letter: Cardman put his chips on the table

Look at the card player himself. This guy, Junjie, the "buyer" logic is basically based on the idea.

He came out of Henan County, where the Faculty of Mathematics of the Southeast University, a doctor from the Automated Institute of Middle School, had over a hundred essays, quoted thousands, and then worked seven years as vice president in the trade soup. This is a classic curriculum vitae of the first-class technology founder — not packaged, survived.

So when he says, "Unpaid before AGI is realized," you can at least believe that he's not acting. A man who's already financially free, cuts his salary and his own 4% shares out of the team, and it's really pushing his chips to the center of the table. The position of the interest-singling is real.

But the point in this letter is worth thinking about.

It didn't come in the company as it went well, but it happened to be at the fire relief point, when the company was free of the fall, and the stock price was higher. The day before it fell 18%, the founder came out the next day, saying, "I don't want money" – and on the same day, the company announced a new round of financing of about HK$16 billion.

I don't think that's a contradiction between the two. Good faith and motivation can exist simultaneously. A true and seasoned founder can often combine "I really believe in this" with "I need to say it now." You do not have to choose between standing in the line, but rather see: this letter tells you "the man and the bet" -- the cardman is willing to press himself, the blood is still flowing on the table.

It doesn't, and it can't, tell you whether this card itself can win. The determination of the player can't change the number of cards he holds.

One more layer: MiniMax is a Cayman VIE structure, which is covered by both Chinese and American regulations. There are two rules that can be changed at any time on top of the head, and the cards themselves can be stopped by someone outside the table.

Internal letters are exposed to "people and bets," and the equity structure is exposed to "control and structural risk." Two keys open to the same door: a strong card player and a dangerous table.

# Three, put two cards together: what kind of business is this?

MiniMax has three things to make: Talkie and its domestic version, A.A., an A.P.A., who accompanys users and virtual characters, who talk, interacts with voice, and pays for company; conch, an AI video-generation tool, which enters text or pictures to generate video, and sells the foreign-based Sora; and a model, API, which sells the capabilities of the larger model to businesses.

Look at this business, you have to take down one of the most horrifying numbers.

The financial statement says that a net loss of $1.870 billion in 2025 sounds like bloodshed. But about $1.6 billion is in it, a non-cash accounting item like "change in fair value of priority shares".

What is the extent of this intuition? The higher the company’s valuation, the more the books are lost. The principle is not clear: early investors hold priority shares, the higher the company’s value, the higher the book value of the priority shares, which are added to the statement as a “debt” to ordinary shareholders.

The point is that the account was largely written off at the moment the company was listed -- the priority shares were converted into ordinary shares, and the scary figure disappeared. It was not meaningless (the equivalent of the implicit cost of dilution of ordinary shareholders), but it was not "the company burned down 1.870 billion dollars in cash."

If you want to see how much it burns, it depends on "adjusted net loss" — only $250 million. And it's almost equal to the year when the annual income increases about 1.6 times. Income more than doubled, real loss does not expand, which means that the scale effect is beginning to appear for every dollar that comes in excess, no more than a dollar. (A reminder: the adjusted caliber is a company's own defined caliber, and the trend is not to be considered an audit finding.)

Look at the blood. Cash on the books is about US$ 1.50 billion, plus IPO money, and the cash runway is five to six years old. There's no cash crisis in the recent days -- the chips in the hands of the card player, enough to take a long time to beat this game.

So what does the business look like? The whole year of 2025, $7.904 million, a 159% increase, and the Maori rate, which rises from 12% to 25.4%, is small, but the pace of growth and improvement is right.

The most unique part of it is going out to sea. Overseas revenue is 73%, a rare sample of China's AI companies that actually close the door. Others are telling stories about the sea, and it's already received money abroad.

The three legs of income are split as follows: AI is a cash cow now, with a C-end head; conch videos are expected to be a second-growth curve; and model API is sold to businesses.

There's a place where it's easy to see: year-round, C ends are about 67%, API is about 33%. But API is the fastest, the fastest, Maori 69%, the healthiest, and by the latest single season, it's nearly 50% -- that is, the new income is almost entirely based on this leg. It's the best one in three legs.

One sentence summed up the bone of this business: accompanying the money for today, API bets tomorrow's money.

Here, the deck is clear: the card is first-hand, blood is good, the card is good, the report is fake, the business is improved. If the story ends, it's a good hand.

# Four, three pitfalls: deciding how to end this game

The real decisions of three, five and ten years are the following three issues.

First, cash bulls are really crisp.

Talkie and Hoshino earn money as companions, and they are on the red line of supervision. The temporary AI Humanized Interactive Service Management Scheme, which came into force on July 15, explicitly prohibits the provision of virtual intimacy to minors - a knife that cuts directly to Hoshino. Over there, Talkie has been set up twice, once in America in December 2024, and once in Google Play in April 2026.

The company's 73% of its income is built on a base that can be "one-size-fits-all" at any time. Cash cows are not milking, but locking the bulls in the hands of others.

Secondly, the moat is thin.

Three tracks, none of which is first. Model layers are being commercialized by open-source models like DeepSeek -- when equal-capable models are available free of charge, why would your API sell at a high price?

The more subtle thing is the repetition of technical narratives. MiniMax’s most loud label is "linear attention," a structure called “fine-minded.” But it has itself gone down the road for two years: the M1 model used it, the M2 official admitted that the architecture was premature in its complex reasoning and temporarily returned to the traditional focus, the M3 bypassed another less costly dilution; and the head of the structure who had pushed it was then relieved of his duties.

It needs to be clear: the official tone is "time is not right, hold it for now," not "the road is not working," and M3 is back in the direction of saving money. For a company that uses "structure innovation" as a sign, the sign is removed and replaced by its own repeated calls, which in itself weakens the story of "I have a single protector of the river."

Its only relatively strong moat is the extraordinary capital efficiency: the M1 model was developed without the 1% input of OpenAI, about $530,000 in training costs (excluding R & D manpower and prior test errors). In a giant race that burns billions of dollars, "doing things with small money" is a capacity. The question is whether this capacity can sustain a sufficiently wide moat, and it has not yet been proven.

Third, the price was a mountain passer-by.

Stock prices fell from a peak of about HK$ 980 to about HK$ 260 to HK$ 297 today, and retreated from the six-month period of 70 to 80%. The market market sales rate (the market value of which is divided by income) was once hundreds of times higher — which, for a company with an annual income of less than $100 million, would have pushed its stock prices forward for many years. Even now, even if so much falls, the valuation is not cheap.

Three questions come together: the business of making money is on the edge of the regulatory cliff, the moat is so shallow that it can be filled up at any moment, and prices are not falling to the point where they are. That is why, with a good hand, the winning side is still fair.

Let's be clear, these next probabilities are my subjective simulation based on current information, not actuarial results, and they change with the cards. You can disagree with the numbers, but you can revise your judgment against this framework.

Three years later, around 2029. Probably alive, even past the profit/loss balance line. But the acceleration will drop, and the snails and API will take over the Talkie stick, becoming the main income force. The A.I. Six Dragons' shuffling is almost over, and MiniMax is one of the survivors -- notice, survivors, not winners.

Five years later, around 2031. Models are completely commercialized, and anyone can use them. By then, MiniMax's best chance of being a semi-integrated hip-specific company, bound deep into Ali's ecology, doing what Ali does best for living resources. The company is still, but it's not its own chip. This is the end of the scenario that I give the highest probability, about 45%.

Ten years later, in 2036, this is the real open option, with a huge difference. The probability of being in the head is about 5 to 10%; remaining in the second ladder is about 25 to 35%; becoming a fairy share or being acquired is about 35 to 45%; and the probability of being completely zero is 20 to 30%.

In 10 years, people will argue whether MiniMax is the "preliminary or the first of the efficiency course." But the line that determines its fate is not technically correct -- right, history will judge. The real line is whether it survives to the day when that path is proven.

Technology can be pioneered, but companies cannot. Last-smile, it's often not the best, but the ones who are not cleared in the middle.
