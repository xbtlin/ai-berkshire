## 4. Core Findings

### 1. The storage industry's "low P/E trap" is common

The storage industry is currently at the top of an AI-driven supercycle, and almost every company is showing extreme earnings elasticity. In this industry, a low P/E - especially a low forward P/E - **does not mean undervaluation**. More often, it reflects the market rationally pricing in "peak earnings are not sustainable."

### 2. DRAM > NAND > HDD (business quality ranking)

- **DRAM / HBM**: a three-player oligopoly, HBM is more differentiated, and pricing power is strongest
- **NAND**: five or six competitors plus a state-backed YMTC, so differentiation is weaker
- **HDD**: a duopoly that is actually showing "good business" characteristics in the AI era (capacity sold out, strong pricing power)

### 3. The three low-valuation names are all cyclical stocks

Samsung, Nanya, and Winbond are cheap **not because they are truly undervalued**, but because the market is pricing the storage cycle rationally. Value investors should wait for the cycle to cool off and buy at "normalized earnings" valuations, rather than being attracted by low forward P/Es at the top of the cycle.

### 4. If forced to pick one

Samsung Electronics is the one worth tracking most closely:
- HBM4 being validated by Nvidia's top rating shows its catch-up ability
- Diversification provides downside protection
- South Korea's governance reform direction is positive
- **But the stock should pull back 20%-30% before it is worth building a position**
