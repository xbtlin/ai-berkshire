# I, core conclusion (first answer)

1. ** China has entered a low interest rate era, which is not a forecast, but a fait accompli.** The 10-year rate of return on public debt fell by 2 per cent in December 2024, with historical lows of 1.60 ~ 1.90 per cent in 2025 and currently (early July 2026) of 1.73 ~ 1.75 per cent.
2. ** The next three years: low-level shocks, limited downside space.** The policy tone remains "moderately loose", with markets generally expecting 10-20 basis points to fall in 2026, but mainstream institutions have judged that the 10-year state debt runs between 1.6 per cent and 2.1 per cent, with interest rates flat or modestly rising - i.e., "low interest rates plus high volatility", rather than continuing to fall unilaterally.
3. ** The next 5-10 years: Structural factors support the continuation of low interest rates.** Population ageing, potential downwards growth, continues to have a negative downward pull on natural interest rates, and current real interest rates (loans ' interest rates minus core CPI) remain high, with a potential decline in nominal interest rates.
4. ** Meaning of the low interest rate for the poo, the troupe: the low rate is a smooth wind of valuation, but not a single-way security cushion.** The lower discount rate systematically raises the reasonable valuation of high-quality assets, and long-line funds (insurance, social security) are configured to "bondize" high-value, stable cash flow assets; however, the experience of Japan shows that the lower interest rate may fall in the pre-period stock market (which is still falling during the 1991-1995 interest-rate period), and that the post-adaptation trend is determined by the fundamentals.
