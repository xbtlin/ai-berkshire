# Risk five: Australian product recall event

At the end of the year, the Australian Competition and Consumer Commission (ACCC) issued a secure recall notice for SigenStor single-separate 8/10/12kW energy controllers (source: ACC Project Safety, SolarQuotes).

** Problem:** AC plugs may be overheated, melted and exposed to fire. Some **100 sets** equipment affected.

** Corporate response:** Provisional solution (reduced full output power) through remote fixture update and free replacement of AC plugs for all affected products. Sig attributed the problem to ** inadequate installation ** not product design deficiencies.

** Objective assessment:**
- Limited impact (approximately 100 units) and no loss of life, injury or property
- Company response speed, remote fixture update + free replacement
- But it exposed the short-boards of the fast-expanding period - only two years from research and development to large-scale delivery, and the maturity of the quality control system is worth asking.
- Long-term impact on brands depends on whether similar incidents occur in the future.

** Reverse argument:** Almost all new brands face quality problems in the early stages of rapid expansion, and Tesla experienced a lot of recall early. The key is whether the company can learn from them and improve its processes. 100 sets of players have relatively manageable reach.

# V. Summing up -- end with a question and answer set

** Ask: Is Sig's new energy a good company?**

Sig was truly excellent in terms of product strength, enforcement, and founder capacity. SigenStor's five-in-one design addressed real pain, 3 years of coverage for 172 distributors in 85 countries at an amazing rate, 50% of which were in the industry.

** Ask: Is Sig's new energy a good investment?**

Not sure at the moment. Good companies and good investments are two things: good companies turn bad investments at the wrong price. The market has already priced much good news for a company that has only a 1-year profit history, 93 per cent one-species dependence, and faces litigation and increased competition.

** Asked: When could it become a good investment?**

When at least 3 conditions are met:
- The valuation is returned to reasonable areas (under 25 times PE, corresponding share price ~ 400 HK$)
- at least 2 quarters of the year after listing, to validate the sustainability of growth
- The evidence that the Māori rate is stable at 40% plus
- SiganStack income ratio was 15% +
- The patent dispute is settled.

** Ask: What if only one thing is remembered?**

** The biggest uncertainty about Sig's new energy is not whether it can grow -- 60% of the race speed, and the short-term growth is very certain. The biggest uncertainty is "Can we hold it when it grows" -- 50%. How long can the Māori rate last? How much will China shrink when it comes in? ** When is the vulnerability of monolithic dependence exposed?**

The answer to these questions takes time. And time, it's exactly what a company with a four-year history lacks.
