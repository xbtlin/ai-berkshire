### 2.2 Huawei: The elephant in the room

**Munger will first focus on the biggest threat-Huawei. **

Xu Yingtong resigned from Huawei in 2022 and founded Sige New Energy. Huawei FusionSolar ranks first in global distributed photovoltaic inverter shipments, accounting for 18% of the distributed photovoltaic storage market (vs. SiGe’s 1.3% in wide-scale).

**The relationship between Huawei and SIG is extremely delicate:**
- Xu Yingtong took away the core knowledge and channel resources of Huawei’s photovoltaic business
- In the short term, Huawei may "turn a blind eye" - after all, the market segments are different.
- But once the stackable optical-storage all-in-one market becomes large enough, **Huawei will inevitably fight back**
- Huawei's technology, brand, channels, and funds crush Sigurd in all aspects

**Munger will ask:** "If Huawei launches a five-in-one product tomorrow, how long can Sig's moat last?"

## 3. 50% gross profit margin - Munger's suspicion

### 3.1 Why does SIG’s gross profit margin far exceed that of its peers?

| Peers | Gross profit margin (2025) | Gap |
|------|:---------------:|:----:|
| SIG New Energy | 50.1% | — |
| Enphase | ~45% | 5pct |
| GoodWe | ~30% | 20pct |
| Jinlang Technology | ~30% | 20pct |
| SolarEdge | ~23% | 27pct |
| Huawei FusionSolar | ~25% (estimate) | 25pct |

### 3.2 Reasonable explanations and negative arguments for high gross profit

**Reasonable explanation (Bull Case):**
1. Five-in-one integration reduces the cost of intermediate links, but the selling price remains at the total price of the split model.
2. High-end market positioning (Europe, America and Australia), users are not price sensitive
3. Installation cost savings make channel providers willing to push premium products
4. Software (SigenOS) creates additional value

**Bear Case:**
1. Insufficient market competition in the early stage resulted in "first-mover monopoly profits"
2. The "overseas price involution" of China's optical and storage companies has begun - the price war will spread from domestic to overseas in 2026
3. After Huawei and Goodwe launch similar five-in-one products, the price premium will narrow rapidly
4. **50% gross profit margin may fall back to 35-40% in 2-3 years, which is still excellent, but the valuation needs to be revalued**

## 4. Competition on technical routes

### 4.1 Stackable vs integrated vs split

| Route | Representation | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|------|------|------|------|
| Stackable and integrated | SigenStor | Flexible expansion, simple installation | High initial cost |
| All-in-one (fixed capacity) | Tesla Powerwall | Strong brand, transparent price | Not expandable |
| Split type | Huawei/Goodwe/Jinlang | Optional components, controllable costs | Complex installation, multi-brand compatibility issues |

**Munger's judgment:** Stackable all-in-one is a better user experience, but it is not the only solution. Tesla Powerwall 3 has done a great job in the unibody direction. The technical route will not be "winner takes all", but will coexist for a long time.

### 4.2 AI Empowerment: Real Barrier or Marketing Gimmick?

Sigen emphasizes SigenOS’s AI energy management capabilities. **But a calm assessment is required:**
- AI optimization of household energy usage is indeed valuable (peak and valley arbitrage, weather prediction, electricity consumption optimization)
- But this is not unique to SIG - Huawei, Tesla, and Enphase all have similar functions
- The real AI barrier lies in the amount of data - whoever has a larger installed base will have more accurate AI optimization
- At present, SiGe's installed capacity is much smaller than that of Huawei and Tesla. **Data barriers are actually a disadvantage**

## 5. General comments from Munger’s perspective

### Rating: ★★★★ 3.8/5

**Track advantage:**
- Global distributed optical storage is a deterministic long-term track
- The stackable all-in-one machine segment is growing much faster than the overall market
- Energy transformation is irreversible and policies will continue to support it

**Competitive Disadvantage:**
- The wide-diameter market share is only 1.3% - "Segment No. 1" is misleading
- Huawei is the Sword of Damocles hanging over its head
- 50% gross profit margin will inevitably attract more competitors
- The overseas price war among Chinese optical and storage companies has begun
- North American market is difficult to enter due to tariff barriers

**Munger's possible conclusion:** "The track is a good track, but this company is still a small player in the broader competitive landscape. A 'segment share' of 28.6% sounds bluffing, but it is only 1.3% of the entire distributed optical storage market. Huawei owns 18% - if Huawei is serious about making stackable products, SIG's life will be difficult. I am more inclined to invest in companies with lasting competitive advantages in the track, rather than a new player that may be crushed by the previous owner."
