| **Industry Experience** | Joined Huawei in 1999, 23 years of experience in Huawei, served as President of Huawei Smart Photovoltaics from 2010 to 2022, and led Huawei's photovoltaic inverters to rank first in global shipments | ★★★★★ |
| **Entrepreneurial ability** | From 0 to 9 billion in revenue in 3 years, with 172 distributors in 85 countries around the world, and the products have ranked first in the world in terms of segmentation | ★★★★★ |

| **Business Judgment** | Accurately enter the blue ocean market of stackable optical and storage devices, avoiding Huawei's main battlefield (traditional inverters) | ★★★★ |

| **Salary Reasonability** | Xu Yingqi’s salary is about 70 million RMB (disclosed in the prospectus), which is relatively high for an IPO company | ★★★ |

**Xu Yingtong’s assessment conclusion:** There is no doubt that he is the most suitable person to do this. Huawei's genetics + world-leading experience in photovoltaic inverters + a deep understanding of distributed energy. This is an entrepreneur who "has the right capabilities at the right time and has chosen the right track."

**But Li Lu will ask:**
- "Why did he leave Huawei? Was it passive or active?"
- "Can he independently build a lasting brand without the endorsement of the Huawei brand?"
- "Is an annual salary of 70 million reasonable when the company is only 3 years old? Are the founders' incentives aligned with long-term value?"

### 1.2 Core Team

The core team of SIG is "extremely rich" - many core management and technical leaders are from Huawei.

**Advantages:** Huawei's team has strong execution capabilities, top-notch engineering capabilities and channel capabilities.
**Risk:** Huawei-based entrepreneurship has a typical problem - success in Huawei relies heavily on Huawei's brand, resources and platform. Whether we can replicate the same success after leaving Huawei will take time to verify.

## 2. Core risk list

### Risk 1: It has only been established for 3 years, and the sustainability of performance is highly doubtful ★★★★★

| Indicators | Facts | Concerns |
|------|------|------|
| Date of establishment | May 2022 | Less than 4 years of history |
| Profit history | Profit for the full year will not be until 2025 | Profit data for only 1 year |
| Business cycle | Has not experienced a complete industry down cycle | Unknown resilience |
| Customer stickiness | Unknown | No data on repurchase rate and replacement rate |

**Li Lu's Judgment Criteria:** "I usually need to see a company go through at least one complete industry cycle (usually 5-7 years) before I can assess the durability of its competitive advantage. SiGe has not experienced even one cycle."

### Risk 2: Single product dependence exceeds 90% ★★★★★

SigenStor accounts for 93% of revenue. This means:
- Any technology iteration, product quality issues, and supply chain disruptions are fatal
- No product mix to spread risk
- The "second growth curve" is not yet clear

**Contrary argument:** Apple started with the iPhone, SigenStor may also become a platform-level product and gradually expand the surrounding ecosystem. But Apple has 10+ years to build an ecosystem, while SIG has only 3 years.

### Risk Three: Geopolitics and Tariff Barriers ★★★★

| Market | Risk Level | Specific Threats |
|------|:-------:|---------|
| United States | Extremely high | Chinese optical and storage products face high tariffs (possibly >50%) and are almost inaccessible |
| Europe | Medium-High | Currently no targeted tariffs, but the risk of countervailing investigations rises |
| Australia | Medium Low | Currently friendly, but policies may change with China-Australia relations |
| Southeast Asia/Middle East | Low | Currently open for growth opportunities |

**The global trade environment will continue to deteriorate in 2026:**
- The Trump administration’s trend of imposing additional tariffs on Chinese products remains unchanged
- The EU is evaluating countervailing measures against Chinese battery and energy storage products
- The "safety first" restructuring trend of the global supply chain is not conducive to Chinese brands

**SiGe's response:** The company currently mainly manufactures in China (Nantong factory) and has no overseas production base. If major markets impose additional tariffs, gross profit margins will be directly impacted.

### Risk 4: Huawei’s counterattack risk ★★★★

**This is the most subtle and critical risk:**

Xu Yingtong left Huawei to start a business. Huawei may "tacitly approve" it in the short term for the following reasons:
-Xu Yingtong has connections and goodwill in Huawei
- Stackable optical-storage all-in-one machines are a niche market and do not completely overlap with Huawei’s main business (inverters)
- Huawei faces its own geopolitical issues (US sanctions)

**But in the long run, Huawei’s counterattack is almost inevitable:**
- If the stackable all-in-one machine market is big enough (>10 billion U.S. dollars), Huawei will definitely enter
- Huawei has all-round advantages in technology, brand, channels, and funds
- Huawei FusionSolar accounts for 18% of the distributed optical storage market, 14 times that of SIG
- It is not uncommon for Huawei startups to be "crushed" by their old employers.

### Risk 5: Intensified competition leads to decline in gross profit margin ★★★★

- The "overseas price involution" of Chinese optical and storage companies has begun
- Goodwe, Ginlang, etc. have already competed head-on with SIG in the Australian and European markets
- 50% gross profit margin is extremely rare among Chinese manufacturing companies and will inevitably attract more entrants
- **Prediction: Gross profit margin may drop from 50% to 35-40% within 2-3 years**

### Risk 6: Over-concentration in overseas markets ★★★

Almost 100% of SIG's revenue comes from overseas, and the domestic market is basically zero.

**This is both an advantage (avoiding domestic rolls) and a risk (all eggs in one basket):**
- Unable to take advantage of mainland China, the world's largest energy storage market
- Overseas market policy changes (tariff and subsidy adjustments) affect the overall situation
- If it is necessary to withdraw from the domestic market, it will face crushing competition from Huawei, BYD and others

## 4. General comments from Li Lu’s perspective

### Rating: ★★★ 3.0/5

**Management Advantages:**
- Xu Yingtong is an extremely excellent entrepreneur, and Huawei’s genes are its core competitiveness
- The team has first-class execution capabilities, with revenue from 0 to 9 billion in 3 years
- Strong capital operation ability

**Core Risks:**
- The company is too young (3 years) and has not experienced any industry downturn
- Single product dependence exceeds 90%, risk concentration is extremely high
- Geopolitics + tariffs are structural headwinds
- Huawei’s counterattack is the Sword of Damocles that cannot be ignored
- The 50% gross profit margin will most likely be lowered by competition

**Li Lu's possible conclusion:** "Xu Yingtong is an excellent entrepreneur, but investment cannot only be based on the ability of the founder. I need to see this company go through at least a complete industry cycle to prove the resilience of its business model. 3 years of data is too short to distinguish between 'the company is excellent' and 'it just caught up with the good times'. I will put it on the watch list and wait 1-2 years after listing before making a decision."
