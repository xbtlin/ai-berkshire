## 1. Company overview and business breakdown

Cheung Kong Holdings is the core real estate flagship of the Li Ka-shing family. It was independently listed in 2015 from the reorganization of Cheung Kong Holdings. Its business has transformed from a pure real estate developer to a diversified asset holding platform across the world.

**Business segment revenue/profit contribution (full year 2025):**

| Segment | Revenue (HKD 100 million) | Profit contribution (HKD 100 million) | Profit rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Property sales | 204.5 (+105%) | 27.3 (+24%) | 13.4% (significant compression) |
| Property leasing | 60.2 (-1.9%) | 46.1 (-2.2%) | 76.6% |
| Hotels and serviced apartments | 46.5 | 16.6 | About 36% |
| Greene King (UK Pub) | 262.3 (+7.4%) | 3.1 (-42% after impairment) | Underlying profit increased by 9% |
| Infrastructure joint ventures (including Changjian/Electric Energy) | 275.9 (+7.1%) | 86.6 (+3.6%) | 31.4% |

**Key structural features: Recurring revenue accounts for 76% of total revenue and contributes 85% of profits. ** This means that Cheung Kong's profit base is much more stable than that of a pure property developer, with infrastructure and leasing businesses providing utility-like cash flow.

**Geographical Profit Distribution:**
- Overseas (mainly UK): 58% (UK 5.79 billion + other international 4.92 billion)
- Hong Kong: 31% (5.71 billion)
- Mainland: 11% (1.93 billion)

**Essentially, it is an international holding company with British infrastructure assets as its profit pillar**, rather than a Hong Kong real estate stock in the traditional sense.

**Asset Sale Updates:**
- **UKPN (British Power Grid)**: Signed a sale agreement with Engie in February 2026, with a total consideration of approximately 10.5 billion pounds (Cheung Kong holds 20% of the equity, corresponding to approximately HK$22.2 billion). It is expected to recognize revenue of approximately HK$8.4 billion, and is expected to be completed in mid-2026
- **Eversholt UK Rails**: The sale was completed in January 2026 for a consideration of approximately GBP 1.1 billion and a recognized gain of approximately HKD 617 million.

## 2. In-depth financial analysis

**Revenue and Profit Trends:**
- Consolidated revenue in 2025 is HK$85.85 billion (+19.9%), but revenue has averaged an annual decline of approximately 12.5% in the past five years
- Net profit attributable to shareholders in 2025 is HK$10.85 billion (-20.3%), mainly dragged down by the revaluation loss of investment properties of HK$1.1 billion.
- **Core profit after revaluation is HK$11.96 billion (+2.7%)**, actual operating performance is stable
- **ROE is only about 3.4%**, which has continued to decline in the past five years. This is the biggest financial worry

**Balance Sheet (Extremely Conservative):**
- Net assets per share (NAV): HK$113.28
- Bank deposits and cash: HK$41.7 billion
- Total bank loans: HK$51.4 billion
- **Net debt is only HK$9.7 billion, with a net debt ratio of 2.3%**
- Credit rating: Moody's and S&P are both A (stable outlook)

**Dividends and buybacks:**
- The full-year dividend for 2025 is HK$1.78 per share (+2.3%), with a dividend rate of approximately 3.9%
- Dividend payout ratio is approximately 57% (based on core profit)
- Initiate a 10% share repurchase authorization in 2024, with the repurchase price set at HK$51/share

## 3. Moat assessment

**Pros:**
1. **Extremely conservative financial discipline**: The net debt ratio is only 2.3%, which is almost unimpacted in the high interest rate environment
2. **Solid foundation for recurring income**: 85% of profits come from recurring businesses, with utility-level cash flow
3. **Defensive nature of global layout**: Sources of income are scattered in markets with perfect rule of law such as the United Kingdom, Australia, and Canada.
4. **Asset Quality**: Properties in Hong Kong’s core locations and overseas infrastructure assets are scarce

**Disadvantages:**
1. **ROE continues to be sluggish (3.4%)**: The returns generated by a large number of net assets are far lower than the cost of capital, and the efficiency of capital allocation is low.
2. **It is difficult to eliminate the discount of comprehensive enterprises**: the business spans many unrelated fields such as real estate, infrastructure, bars, etc.
3. **Doubtful transparency of management governance**: Li Zeju was absent from the 2025 interim results conference for the first time

## 4. Valuation analysis

**NAV Discount:**
- The current stock price is approximately HK$46 vs. NAV per share of HK$113.28, a **discount of approximately 59%**
- The average discount in the past 10 years is 55%. The current discount is close to the historical average, which is not abnormally cheap.

**Morningstar’s reasonableness assessment of HK$99:**
Morningstar's estimate of HK$99 represents a discount to NAV of only about 12.6% (versus the historical average of 55%). The implicit assumption is that the market will narrow conglomerate discounts significantly. **This assumption is overly optimistic. ** Unless Cheung Kong carries out a major asset spin-off or privatization, a NAV discount of around 55% is a structural norm.

**My fair value estimate: HK$55-65**
- Conservative scenario (maintaining 55% discount): approximately HK$51
- Baseline scenario (45% discount, asset sale releases value): approximately HK$62
- Optimistic scenario (35% discount, catalyst trigger required): approximately HK$74

## 5. Risks and Catalysts

**Core Risks:**
1. **Value trap risk - medium to high**: ROE is only 3.4% and continues to decline. Low ROE is the most fundamental value trap signal
2. **Discounts for conglomerates are structural**: Unless they are spun off and listed, the market’s continued discounts are a reasonable efficiency penalty.
3. **Hong Kong/Mainland Real Estate Risk**: Property sales are based on price for volume, and profit margins are compressed from 22% to 13%
4. **Management Uncertainty**: Li Zeju’s business style is defensive and lacks the willingness to radically release value.

**Potential Catalysts:**
1. **UKPN sale completed (mid-2026)**: approximately HK$22.2 billion in cash obtained and revenue of HK$8.4 billion confirmed
2. **Special dividend/large-scale buyback**: Cash reserves may exceed HK$60 billion after sale
3. **Hong Kong property market picks up**: Signs of Indian Spring are already appearing in 2026
4. **Sales revenue locked to be confirmed**: HK$20.7 billion signed but not recorded

---

## 6. Investment Conclusion

**Cautiously optimistic, but not a strong buy. **

Cheung Kong is a company with extremely conservative finances and reliable asset quality, but its long-term sluggish ROE (3.4%) and conglomerate discount are real structural problems.

- **Fair value estimate**: HK$55-65
- **Morningstar 99 Hong Kong Dollar Assessment**: Too optimistic and not recommended as a basis for investment decisions
- **Downside**: About 10-15% (to HK$40, the deepest discount in history)
- **Upside**: About 20-40% (to a reasonable range of HK$55-65)
- **Risk to reward ratio**: about 2:1 to 3:1, acceptable but not very attractive

**Key observation window**: UKPN’s capital usage decision after delivery in mid-2026 - whether it is willing to pay special dividends or large-scale repurchases will determine whether the valuation can be repaired.
