# The terrible part of Tesla

** First, manufacturing power is not the same.** Ji-won delivered 5,100, Tesla 2026, and 5-15 million, all year 2025. Even Tesla has only completed half the target limit (2.5 million), it is 5 times the size of the brain. Tesla has done something like: produce an electric car from 0 to over **1.8 million per year. Its manufacturing system, supply chain management and automation experience is beyond the reach of any robot start-up company.

** Second, cost advantage may be the level of crushing.** When 1 million units were produced in the year, Tesla estimated the cost of the single table **2-25 million United States dollars (approximately RMB 145-18 million). The price of the IJM A2 is **168,000-62 million. **, Insight X1 sales ** 98,000. ** If Tesla could actually make the full-sized human robots 20 million dollars, Chinese firms would face the dilemma of "either losing the price or losing the market."

** Third, the data wheels may eventually run.** Tesla has a global **millions of ** electric vehicles running on the road, and the FSD system has accumulated billions of miles of driving data. Although the driver data and robotics operations data are not identical, the bottom-level capabilities of visual perception, motion planning, and end-to-end decision-making are connected. Once Tesla moves the FSD experience to Optimus, the data gap will be fatal.

# But Tesla's weakness is equally obvious

** First, the Mask timetable never worked.** Looking back: five years have passed since the FSD "Achievable Next Year" was said; two years have passed since Cybertrak; Robotaxi has not been traded on a large scale to date. Optimus's target of 5-15 million in 2026, and a real 3-5 fold is a reasonable expectation.

** Second, China’s market is unlikely to cover it in the short term.** Tesla Optimus was initially deployed in his own factory, with out-growers at least until 2027-2028.

** Third, interoperability has not been verified.** Tesla Optimus is currently doing simple tasks like battery sorting at his own factory, and has not entered the third-party client factory. There is a huge engineering and commercial divide between "showing his own factory" and "scenario deployment."

** Combined judgement**: Tesla is the largest variable in the human robotic race -- if its mass production and cost controls move forward as scheduled, all Chinese enterprises will be significantly reduced in their survival space. But the uncertainty behind the word "if" is extremely high.

The Go2 and B2-foot robots of Woo-tree have formed a mature product line, with a single price of ** thousands to tens of thousands**, with a large volume of shipments (about **18,000** before 2025, four-foot shipments in nine months), and a Maori rate of **~60%**.

** The advantage of a human robot over a specialized automation device is "generality" - a human robot can theoretically perform a wide range of tasks, whereas a mechanical arm can only do one.

** But universalization is "false advantage" ** -- because the human robot is currently very limited in its range of tasks that can be performed in a stable manner. A 200,000 human robot, if it can only do one thing, is four times more expensive than a 50,000-dollar AGV, but less efficient.

** The "generalized premium" is only established when human robots can perform many different tasks within the same class.** When will this day come? No one can give a credible timetable.

** The counter-view**: The human working environment is designed for human beings - stairs, door handles, handles of tools. The human robot has the advantage not only of universality, but also of its seamless integration into the human physical environment, without the need to transform the plant. This is particularly important in the context of service scenes (in hotels, retail, old people).
