# Series Guide for "Understanding AgiBot"

> 4 main articles that break down a humanoid-robot company founded only 3 years ago, valued at RMB 15 billion, and not yet profitable.
>
> Estimated reading time: about 50 minutes; recommended to read in order.

---

## Series Positioning

AgiBot is not a company that "lacks attention" - it is probably one of the most **talked-about and most divisive** companies in China's hard-tech startup scene from 2023 to 2026.

- The bulls say it has the world's No. 1 shipment volume, has shipped 10,000 units, Tencent / Hillhouse / Sequoia all invested, and Zhu Ji is the strongest hardware founder in China
- The bears say there is volume but no profit, related-party orders are frothy, a shell-listing path is ugly, and 140 competitors are trapped in brutal competition

**Both sides are looking at the same company and drawing completely opposite conclusions.** The harder part is that AgiBot is unlisted, unaudited, and missing all core financial data, so both sides are arguing from claims that are "reasonable but impossible to verify."

This series aims to **separate the known facts and data from the blind spots** - so that you can decide whether this company is worth watching.

## Writing Principles

This series follows a few hard rules:

1. **Start with the data, then the logic, and only then the conclusion** - do not pre-judge bullish or bearish
2. **Attach a counterargument to every major conclusion** - let readers weigh both sides
3. **Delete unreliable data rather than inventing it** - AgiBot's core financial data (net profit, gross margin, cash flow) has not been publicly disclosed, so this series only uses the most reliable sources
4. **Do not give investment advice** - every article ends with a disclaimer
5. **Separate "facts" from "speculation"** - inferred data is clearly labeled
