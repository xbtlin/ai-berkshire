# The bottom logic of this business

In Italy, a 30-room independent hotel. Half-time off, the boss can't afford to do global marketing and cannot afford to have a multi-language customer service.

Booking.com. Here comes the 30 rooms to 200 million people worldwide who travel on a living basis, and the owner does nothing, just fix prices and house patterns in the system, and orders come in automatically. Cost: commission **15-18%** per pen.

Is it expensive? Of course it is. Is there a choice in this hotel? There are millions of such small and medium hotels around the world.

** This is the core of the Booking business model: it does not own a single house, but it is indispensable for millions of hotels around the world.**

Booking’s commission rate is at a medium-high level in the industry. Hotels certainly want to pay less – a large chain of thousands of dollars, Hilton, etc. – to continue to drive the "scaling movement" and to use membership credits and best prices to ensure that users are able to bypass the OTA.

** But the key fact is that large chains of hotels account for only about 30% of the global supply of accommodation, while the remaining 70% are independent hotels and alternative accommodation.** These small and medium-sized suppliers have no branding influence, no membership system, no global distribution capacity - they have **30-50%** orders from Booking.com.

For 70 percent of the supply side, Booking is not an "optional marketing channel", but a "vital necessity." That's the root cause of the **15-18 percent ** commission rate.

** Reverse view:** Commission rates are not a piece of paper. The EU has discussed capping OTA commissions. If major market legislation limits the commission rate to 10%, Booking’s income will be directly affected.

# From agency to business: Booking The most important strategic transformation

Understanding the business model of Booking requires understanding of two key words:** agent model** and** business model**.

# # Agent mode

The user selects the hotel on Booking and pays for it at the front desk.

- Advantages: high user trust (paying at shop)
- Weakness: Booking can't get payment data, can't control user experience, and has no floating cash.

# # Business mode (Mercant)

New practice: Users pay directly on Booking. Booking collects money and settles with hotels.

- Advantages: (1) data on payments are available to lay the foundation for personalization recommendations; (2) "floating cash" is obtained - users' money goes first to Booking; (3) users experience better (flexible cancellation, locking in exchange rates, etc.)
- Disadvantages: more exposure to payment and refund treatment

# Network effects: Booking Core moat

# The logic of the bilateral network effect

```
More hotels online <unk> Users choose more <unk> More users get more orders <unk> Hotels get more orders
♪ More hotels on the line ♪ (flying wheels constantly spinning)
```

The strength of this ship depends on a key question: ** Will users and hotels "more belong" -- use multiple platforms at the same time?**

The answer is:** yes, but to a limited extent.**

- **end: ** Travelers do compete between Booking, Expedia, Airbnb. But Booking has the advantage of: (1) maximum coverage; (2) optimal evaluation system (billions of evaluations accumulated over decades); (3) user preference for data to be personalized and more appropriate. This means that while users "may" value, ** inertia and data locking** will allow most people to default on Booking
- ** Supply end:** Independent hotels can theoretically be located on multiple platforms, but managing multiple platforms has high housing, prices, and evaluation costs. Many small and medium hotels actually use Booking as the "main channel" -- **30-50% of orders from Booking**, a dependency that means high conversion costs.

** The counter-view:** The tourism industry’s network effects are less exclusive than social networks (micro-mail, Facebook). Users do not use Booking because friends are all on Booking – travel decisions are based on prices and supplies rather than social relations. This means that the moat is wide, but not the Great Wall.

# Evaluation systems: non-reproductive time barriers

Booking.com has accumulated ** Billions of user evaluations** — not just data, but trust.

Would you book a hotel abroad that you've never been to, on a platform with only five evaluations? No, probably not. You'll book a platform with 2,000 ratings, 8.7 ratings?

** The value of evaluation is that it is a function of time - not to buy it, not to catch it up quickly.** Even Google has unlimited funds and flows, it cannot replicate the evaluation system accumulated in the two decades of Booking in a short time.

**87.4% Māori rate means that Booking has $100 per income, only $12.6 million is cost** - this cost is mainly to cover processing costs and small infrastructure costs. The remaining $87.4 is all gross profits.

Why so high? Because Booking doesn't own any real assets -- it doesn't build hotels, doesn't maintain a fleet, doesn't manage a room clean. All heavy stuff is on the supplier, Booking just does "connect" and "package."

** ROIC 43.7% means that for every dollar of capital invested, profits are close to 44 cents.** The median industry is only 3.6%. This is the result of the triple advantage of "Little assets plus bilateral network effects plus economies of scale".

** This model is very similar to that of insurance companies - collecting money and then providing services.** This means that Booking has a stronger real cash-creation capacity than the profit scale shows.

# A word of business

**Booking's business essence is: to match global travellers and accommodation providers efficiently with algorithms and data, drawing 15-18% of commissions per transaction. It does not own any physical assets, but millions of hotels around the globe depend on it for their guests. Bilateral network effects and decades of accumulated evaluation systems constitute a vast but non-monopolistic moat**

Where is the business? Light assets, high profits, strong cash flows, network effects are increasing.

What's the risk of this business? The moat is wide, but not exclusive -- users can compete, hotels can hang more. More importantly, if the search entrance is recreated by AI, the value of Booking as the "information polymer meso level" may be reduced.
