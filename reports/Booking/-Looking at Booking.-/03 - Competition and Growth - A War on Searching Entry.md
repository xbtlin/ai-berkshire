The problem with Expedia is "branding, inefficient." There's too many brands under the flag, users don't know which to use, and internal resources are fighting. Booking just needs the world to remember a name: Booking.com.

** Booking leads the Expedia in almost all markets outside North America.** Expedia's advantage is limited to the United States Resort Leasing Market (Vrbo).

** Conclusion: Expedia is not a real threat to Booking. It's more like a follower whose market share will continue to be eroded**

# Three against: Take the ride - Asian Fortress

The process is almost monopolistic in China, but it is actively expanding to international markets:

- Increased international income share to about 40% in 2025**
- In South-East Asia and the Korean market, Trip.com competes positively with Booking (Agoda)
- ~12x's Forward PE reflects the stock discount

With China’s return to the border, the distance to China, with its large base of users and strong supply chain integration, could challenge Booking’s position in Asia-Pacific markets. Especially in China’s hot destinations, such as Japan and Thailand, the share of the market to carry the journey is growing rapidly.

** Conclusion: The couriers are unshakeable rulers in the Chinese market, and the Booking is the rival that needs to be taken seriously in the Asia-Pacific market. But, globally, the threat of the couriers is concentrated in the Chinese diaspora and does not shake the roots of Booking in Europe.**

# Four against Google -- elephants in the room

** This is the most interesting threat to be discussed in depth.**

The other competitors that Booking faces are "comparable competitions" -- everybody runs in the OTA track. But Google is different -- it stands at the entrance to the track, controlling "who can be seen by users."

A set of key data:

- 2025, the global OTA spent **5.2 billion dollars in the second quarter alone** marketing costs, most of which went to Google
- Booking's paid-for-marketing expenditure as a percentage of revenue **20% or more**, mostly to Google
- November 2025, Google announced the development of a "agent" travel booking tool based on Gemini AI, when Booking and Expedia stock prices fell **4-7%**

Data source: Skift, HBR

**Google threat is two-tier:**

** First layer: AI search diversion.** When the user says to Google "Get me a hotel for five days of Italian travel," AI directs out recommendations and prices - users may no longer need to open Booking.com. According to Bernstein Analysts, up to **40%** travellers are willing to book directly via the AI chat interface.

** Second floor: ultimate subversion of proxy AI.** If AI proxy can automatically compete, book, re-sign, refund, OTA downgrades from "the user's directly interactive platform" to "ALI proxy call backend API" - profit margins will be significantly reduced.

** The counter-view - Google won't get off easily OTA:**

** Conflict of interest:** OTA is one of Google’s largest advertising clientele. If Google competes directly with OTA, it loses this huge advertising revenue.
** Historical precedent:** Google failed to attempt vertical tourism on several occasions in the last decade — Google Trips closed and Google Travel used much less than expected
** Short-capacity:** OTA requires a strong vendor relationship management and customer service capability (refunds, resigns, dispute resolution), which is not the core advantage of Google
4. **Booking Response:**Booking is investing in "agent AI" with the goal of creating barriers between users and AI. 12,335 million night transaction data and hundreds of millions of evaluations are unique assets for training AI for tourism

** Conclusion: Google is the biggest uncertainty facing Booking -- not because Google will have to subvert OTA, but because that possibility cannot be ruled out. The outcome of this game will become clear within the next 3-5 years.**

# Structural drivers of growth: Global tourism is far from the top

And, despite the competition, Booking has a smooth wind -- the global penetration rate is still rising.

# Three structural growth engines

** First, the Asia-Pacific online penetration rate has increased.**

The Asia-Pacific region currently has an online travel reservation of about **50%**, which is expected to reach **75%** by 2029. The rise of the middle class in China, India, and South-East Asia means hundreds of millions of new online travel consumers. Booking through Agoda is in the Asia-Pacific layout, but faces intense competition for portering.

Data source: Grand View Research

The market will also be nearly doubled in five years, using a more conservative CAGR (9-10%).

** Third, experience economic rise.**

In the post-epidemiological era, consumers value experience rather than in-kind consumption. Tourism is the most typical "exercise consumption" — a trend that is irreversible worldwide.

# Risks of counter-trend

There is no room for optimism. There are several factors that could interrupt growth:

- ** Global economic recession:** Tourism is an optional consumption, with the economy being cut first. Tourism expenditure fell by about **15% during the 2008 financial crisis**
- **Geopolitical conflict:** Middle East, Eastern Europe situation affecting tourism demand in specific regions
- ** "Anti-Tourism":** Barcelona, Amsterdam, etc., have introduced restrictions.
- **Climate change:** Possible changes in the pattern of global tourism destinations

** Inverse:** Long-term growth trends in the tourism sector (30 years CAGR - ~ 7%) are very robust, but do not represent a smooth five-year cycle. Booking in 2026 gave itself a year-round guide to only "low- and medium-digit growth" — a growth rate that is indeed slowing in the short term. Investors need to distinguish between "long-term structural growth" and "short-term cycle fluctuations".

# A summary of the competition pattern

** Booking is a lot like Google in 2015 in the search engine industry - industry number one, moat river wide, and very profitable. But, just as Google himself faced the challenge of the AI age search entrance, Booking is facing the biggest risk from Google**.

This is not a binary question about "will not be subverted." The more accurate one is:

1. **Google AI search how much of the OTA traffic will be split?** - The most pessimistic estimate is currently 20-40%, and the most optimistic estimate is "small impact."
** Booking's own AI transformation successful?** ** — If it's possible to turn Booking into the "first entrance to users", AI is good.
** What is the time frame?** ** This change will not happen tomorrow, but it will become clear within 3-5 years.

The answer to these three questions determines the future valuation hub of Booking.
