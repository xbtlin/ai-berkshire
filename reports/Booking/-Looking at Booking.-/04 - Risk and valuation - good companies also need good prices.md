# Let's get the risk straight

Booking is great when the first three are finished. This is going to be the opposite -- just tell us what might cost the investment, then talk about prices.

Good companies are not good investments. The price is wrong, it's bad money. Let's look at the risk before talking about valuation.

** Key judgement:** Google has a low probability of doing OTA on a full scale ( conflict of interest is too large), but "part of the stream" is almost certain. The question is whether it will be a diversion **10%** or **30%** -- a gap that has a huge impact on the valuation of Booking.

** Reverse view:** AI threat to OTA may be overestimated. Travel booking is not a purely information-based scene -- it involves a complex link of payment, refunds, re-signing, customer service, etc. AI search can help users find hotels, but "complete trade plus after-sale service" still requires OTA infrastructure. Booking is more likely to end not by "replace" but by "replace" -- profit margins may fall, but not by volume.

# Risk IV: Valuation compression of slower growth (in progress)

Booking 2025 income growth ** 13.4%**, Q1 2026 growth ** 16%**, but year-round guidance only "low and medium" numbers. If the market determines that Booking is moving from "growth" to "maturity," the valuation hub may move from 20-25x PE down to 15-18x PE.

** Inverse:** Even if the acceleration drops to 5-8%, Booking remains a cash flow machine. Free cash flow rate 34%, plus 4-5% of annual buyback shares – even if the top line does not grow – naturally increases at 4-5%. For long-term holders, the slowdown is not a disaster, but whether profit margins and capital allocation discipline can be maintained.

**Repurchases in 2025 amounted to $6.4 billion, or about 70 per cent of free cash flows in that year.** With dividends,** almost all free cash flows were returned to shareholders**.

# Security margin assessment

** Current market value ~137 billion dollars, based on baseline scenario of $170-$19 billion, has approximately 22-39 per cent up-front space**

** Even in conservative scenarios, bottom valuation ~ $105 billion means the largest downswing of about 24%** - a controlled downswing for a top platform enterprise with ROIC above 40% and free cash flow at 34%.

More importantly, the "security mat" offered by the buyback: **The liquidity stock is down 4-5% per year, which is equivalent to the management's continuing "siloing" ** even if the stock price is not rising, the value of each share inside is growing at 4-5%.

** Inverse:** The core assumption of the three scenario valuation – that free cash flow rates remain at 30% + — may be overly optimistic. If Google AI really subverts the search entrance, the cost of booking's visitor is likely to increase significantly, and free cash flow may fall to 20-25%. In this scenario, even if income continues to grow, the reduction in profit rates will cause the valuation to shrink considerably. Moreover, the terminal multiplier of 15-25 x FCF assumes that Booking remains a "high-quality platform enterprise" — and if it is downgraded to "AI back-end supplier", it may have a reasonable multiplier of 10-12x.

# All series of summaries: What kind of investment is Booking?

Four articles were removed, and Booking's portrait should be clear:

** Good side:**

- The largest travel-match platform in the world, with strong bilateral network effects
- Māori **87.4%, ROIC **43.7%, free cash flow **33.8% ** financial quality top global
- Free cash flow per year **9.1 billion United States dollars, of which 70 per cent + returned to shareholders through buy-back
- 10 years of reduction **40% + ** circulation unit with excellent management capital allocation discipline
- Global tourism penetration continues to rise, providing a 10-year-long structural growth runway
- Forward PE **14.7x** in the lowest of the last five years

** Bad side:**

- Google AI is the biggest uncertainty -- possibly re-reflectioning industry traffic distribution within 3-5 years.
- 2026, the year-by-year acceleration is slowing down to "low and medium."
- The moat is wide, but not exclusive. - Users can compete, hotels can hang more.
Conneced Trip's strategic direction is correct, but the profit contribution is not yet fully realized
- Tourism is optional, and it fluctuates during the recession.

** The final judgement depends on your answer to a core question:**

> ** Do you believe that Booking can still remain irreplaceable in the era of AI re-engineering the search entrance?**

If your answer is "probably yes" -- then the Forward PE 14.7x, FCF rate of return 6.6%, plus a 4-5% buyback gain per year, provides a good starting point for long-term returns.

If your answer is "unsure" -- it is also a perfectly reasonable choice to wait for more evidence to make a decision.

** Investments are not about choosing between "buy" and "no buy" -- but about balancing "what price buy" with "what evidence is needed".**
