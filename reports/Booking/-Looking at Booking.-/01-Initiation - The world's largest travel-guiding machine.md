## A set of numbers that let you re-understand Booking

There is a company that sells 3.38 million hotel rooms every day without owning a single bed. It has higher gross margins than Moutai, a return on capital that exceeds 95% of the world's listed companies, generates $9.1 billion in free cash flow annually, and then spends 70% of that on buying back its own stock -- quietly, year after year.

It's called Booking Holdings. Many Chinese investors are unfamiliar with it, but in the global travel market, it is an out-and-out dominant player.

Core numbers:

| Indicators | Numerical values | Magnitude judgment |
|------|------|---------|
| Total revenue in 2025 | **USD 26.9 billion** (approximately RMB 194 billion) | 3.3 times Ctrip (USD 8.2 billion) |
| Total bookings in 2025 | **186.1 billion USD** | No. 1 in the global OTA industry |
| Room night bookings in 2025 | **1.235 billion room nights** | An average of 3.38 million room nights globally are sold through Booking every day |
| Free Cash Flow 2025 | **$9.1 billion** | Free Cash Flow Rate **33.8%** |
| Gross profit margin | **87.4%** | Higher than Moutai |
| ROIC | **43.7%** | Industry median is only **3.6%** |
| Market capitalization (May 2026) | **Approximately US$130-137 billion** | Ranked No. 1 in global OTA market capitalization |
| Share price (post-split) | **Approximately $151.6** | Completed 25:1 stock split in April 2026 |

Data sources: Booking Holdings 10-K Annual Report, Q1 2026 Financial Report, GuruFocus, MacroTrends

Put the numbers together - **A company with 87% gross margins, over 40% ROIC, and $9.1 billion in free cash flow per year, doesn't own a hotel room, doesn't own a plane**.

Every penny it makes comes from "connections" - connecting millions of hotels and hundreds of millions of travelers around the world, charging a commission of **15-18%** on each transaction.

## Who is Booking?

Booking Holdings has six brands:

| Brand | Core Business | Market Positioning |
|------|---------|---------|
| **Booking.com** | Accommodation booking | Europe's No. 1 and the world's largest hotel booking platform |
| **Priceline** | Air tickets + hotels | In the US market, the "Name Your Own Price" model started |
| **Agoda** | Accommodation Reservation | Southeast Asia + East Asia Market |
| **KAYAK** | Metasearch Price Comparison | Global Flight/Hotel Price Comparison Engine |
| **OpenTable** | Restaurant Reservations | The largest restaurant reservation platform in the United States |
| **Rentalcars.com** | Car Rental | Global Car Rental Price Comparison |

Among them, **Booking.com contributes the majority of revenue**. In Europe, "Booking.com" is almost synonymous with "booking hotels online" - just as natural as the Chinese saying "go to Ctrip".

But Booking’s ambitions don’t stop there. The **Connected Trip** strategy proposed by CEO Glenn Fogel aims to transform Booking from a "booking tool" into a "travel operating system" - completing all bookings of accommodation, air tickets, car rentals, and attraction tickets on one platform. In 2025, the total booking volume of the air ticket business will reach **16.8 billion**, a year-on-year increase of **37%**; the attraction ticket business will increase by approximately **80%** year-on-year.

## Three common misconceptions about Booking

### Misunderstanding 1: Booking is a "booking website"

This is the most superficial understanding.

Opening the 2025 annual report, Booking’s revenue structure is as follows:

| Type of income | Amount | Proportion | Nature |
|---------|------|------|------|
| Merchant revenue (Merchant) | **17.76 billion US dollars** | **66%** | The platform processes payments and collects commissions + payment service fees |
| Agency revenue (Agency) | **7.97 billion USD** | **30%** | Traditional commission model, hotel front desk payment |
| Advertising & Others | **$1.19 billion** | **4%** | KAYAK/OpenTable Advertising + Restaurant Reservations |

Data source: Booking Holdings 10-K Annual Report

**Merchant revenue share jumps from 60% in 2024 to 66% in 2025** - This means that Booking is upgrading from an "information intermediary" to a "transaction infrastructure". Users pay Booking first, and Booking then settles with the hotel. The significance of this change is: (1) Booking has obtained the payment data; (2) It has gained the "float" advantage - users pay first, the hotel takes the money later, and the intermediate funds are deposited in Booking's hands.

**The essence of this business is not "helping you book a room", but "an algorithmic platform that matches the supply and demand ends of global travel" - making money through commissions and payment of service fees, with a gross profit margin of 87%, which is higher than most software companies. **

### Misunderstanding 2: Booking and Ctrip are the same company

On the surface, it does look like it - they are all OTAs (online travel agencies) and they all make money on commissions. But the difference is huge:

| Dimension | Booking | Ctrip |
|------|---------|------|
| Core Markets | Europe + Global | China + Asia Pacific |
| 2025 Revenue | $26.9 billion | $8.2 billion |
| Gross profit margin | 87.4% | ~80% |
| ROIC | 43.7% | ~15-20% |
| Market Cap | ~$137 billion | ~$34.2 billion |
| Valuation (Forward PE) | 14.7x | ~12x |

Data source: Financial reports of each company, GuruFocus

**Booking’s scale is more than 3 times that of Ctrip, and its capital efficiency (ROIC) is 2-3 times that of Ctrip**. More importantly, Booking’s supply-side barriers are higher – there are a large number of independent small and medium-sized hotels in Europe, and 30-50%** of their bookings come from Booking.com, with few alternatives. The Chinese hotel market is more concentrated (such as Home Inn, Huazhu and other chains account for a large proportion), and Ctrip faces more intense competition.

**Opposite view:** Ctrip’s valuation discount (Forward PE 12x vs Booking 14.7x) partly reflects the discount of Chinese concept stocks, rather than the fundamental gap. Ctrip’s international business is growing rapidly, with international revenue accounting for approximately 40% in 2025. In the Asia-Pacific market, Ctrip competes head-on with Booking's Agoda through the Trip.com brand, and the long-term pattern has yet to be finalized.

### Misunderstanding 3: Booking is the "old economy" and has no growth story

Many investors classify Booking as a "mature company"—large and stable, but with no room for growth.

The fact is:

- **Revenue in 2025 is US$26.9 billion, +13.4% year-on-year** - For a company with a market capitalization of US$100 billion, this growth rate is not low
- **Q1 2026 Revenue $5.53 billion, +16% YoY** - Accelerated
- **Q1 2026 net profit is US$1.1 billion, a year-on-year increase of 225%** (2025 Q1 base is low)
- The global online travel market will be approximately **975 billion US dollars** in 2025 and is expected to reach **2.19 trillion US dollars** in 2030 - the market will double

Data source: Booking Q1 2026 financial report, Grand View Research

**Conside:** Full-year guidance for 2026 is only "low to mid-single digit growth," reflecting geopolitical and currency pressures. If the global economy declines, travel as an optional consumption will bear the brunt. The premise of the growth story is "long-term upward trend in global tourism" - this has been true in the past 50 years, but it does not mean that there will not be structural breaks in the next 5 years.

## To understand Booking, you need to answer four questions

If we were to define Booking in one sentence, the closest version might be:

> **Booking = the world’s largest travel matching algorithm platform (extremely strong bilateral network effects) + a free cash flow money printing machine ($9.1 billion per year) + the biggest uncertainty facing the AI era (will Google end up grabbing jobs?) **

These three things add up to determine the investment value of Booking. To understand it, you must answer at least the following four questions:
