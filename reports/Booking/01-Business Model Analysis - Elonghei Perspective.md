# Booking Holdings (BKNG) Business Model Analysis

> Analysis lens: business essence and user value
> Date: 2026-05-16

---

## One-Sentence Definition of the Business

**Booking is a transaction-matching platform between global travelers and accommodation suppliers - the more users it has, the more supply it attracts; the more supply it has, the more users it attracts. It does not own a single hotel room, yet it takes a commission from every booking.**

The core of this business is connection. Booking.com connects millions of hotels, homestays, airlines, and car-rental suppliers with hundreds of millions of travelers, and it collects a 15-18% commission on every transaction. Because the platform does not carry inventory risk, this is a classic asset-light, high-leverage model.

## Revenue Structure

According to Booking Holdings' 2025 annual report (10-K), revenue for the year was USD 26.9 billion, with three major sources:

| Revenue type | 2025 amount | Share | YoY growth | Explanation |
|--------------|-------------|-------|-----------|-------------|
| Merchant revenue | USD 17.76 billion | 66% | +25.5% | The platform acts as a payment intermediary, processing transactions and taking a commission plus payment service fees |
| Agency revenue | USD 7.97 billion | 30% | -6.5% | Traditional commission model: users pay at the hotel front desk and Booking collects a commission from the hotel |
| Advertising and other | USD 1.19 billion | 4% | +11.3% | Advertising revenue from KAYAK and OpenTable, plus restaurant reservation services |

**Key trend:** Merchant revenue rose from 60% of revenue in 2024 to 66% in 2025, showing that Booking is accelerating the shift of transactions into its own payment system. This improves user experience (prepaid locked-in pricing and flexible cancellation), while also giving the platform more control over transaction data and laying the groundwork for cross-selling and the Connected Trip strategy.

## Network Effects and Platform Stickiness

### Supply-Side Barriers
- **Largest global lodging inventory:** Booking.com covers lodging suppliers in more than 220 countries and regions, including hotels, apartments, and homestays
- **Long-tail supply advantage:** Small and mid-sized independent hotels in Europe rely heavily on Booking.com for customer acquisition, and many get 30-50% of their bookings from the platform, making switching costs extremely high
- **Agoda coverage in Asia-Pacific:** Its Agoda brand has deep supplier relationships across Southeast and East Asia

### Demand-Side Flywheel
- **1.235 billion room nights booked in 2025** (source: FY2025 annual report), creating massive amounts of user reviews and behavior data
- **Review moat:** Decades of accumulated user reviews form a trust asset that later entrants can hardly replicate
- **Brand awareness:** In Europe, Booking.com is almost synonymous with "booking hotels online"

### Data Flywheel
Every transaction adds user preference data - price sensitivity, destination preferences, and booking lead times - and feeds it back into the search-ranking algorithm, improving matching efficiency and conversion rates. The rise in merchant revenue means Booking now captures more complete payment and behavioral data, which supports personalized recommendations and dynamic pricing.

## Connected Trip Strategy: From a Booking Tool to a Travel Operating System

CEO Glenn Fogel's Connected Trip strategy is a key initiative that expands Booking from a single-purpose lodging platform into a full-trip service platform:

- **2025 results:** Cross-category bookings, such as hotel plus flight in the same itinerary, grew more than 20% year over year; flight bookings grew 37% to USD 16.8 billion in gross bookings; attraction-ticket bookings grew about 80% year over year
- **AI enablement:** Generative AI trip-planning tools have launched on mobile in more than 10 countries, and Connected Trip transactions grew 30% year over year in Q2 2025 thanks to AI integration
- **Strategic meaning:** It raises user spend and usage frequency on a single platform and increases switching costs

Data sources: [Booking Connected Trip strategy analysis](https://www.ainvest.com/news/booking-holdings-ai-driven-connected-trip-execution-sparks-moat-widening-catalyst-2604/); [Travel Weekly](https://www.travelweekly.com/Travel-News/Travel-Technology/Booking-Holdings-q4-call-connected-trip)

## What Makes This Business So Good?

**First, it is asset-light and highly profitable.** Gross margin in 2025 was 87.4%, operating margin was about 32.8%, and free cash flow was USD 9.1 billion. It does not need to build hotels or buy planes; it only needs to maintain the platform and the algorithms.

**Second, the two-sided network effect creates a natural moat.** More supply brings more users; more users attract more supply. Once the flywheel gets going, it is extremely hard for newcomers to catch up. The accumulation of reviews is also an irreversible time barrier.

**Third, demand has natural growth drivers.** Global travel penetration is still increasing, especially in Asia-Pacific, where online booking rates are moving from below 50% toward 75%, creating a structural growth runway of a decade or more.

**Fourth, cash conversion is extremely efficient.** The merchant model, where users pay first and hotels settle later, creates a huge float advantage - similar to an insurance model, where the company gets the money first and provides the service later.

**Fifth, management knows how to create value through buybacks.** Since 2014, the share count has fallen by more than 40%, and Booking bought back USD 6.4 billion of stock in 2025, returning almost all free cash flow to shareholders.

**In one Duan Yongping-style sentence: this is the "right" kind of business - users need it, merchants cannot live without it, and competitors find it hard to replace. The longer it runs, the higher the barrier becomes.**
