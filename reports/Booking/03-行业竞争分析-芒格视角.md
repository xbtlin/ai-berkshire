# Booking Holdings (BKNG) Industry Competition Analysis

> Perspective: competitive landscape and moat assessment
> Date: 2026-05-16

## 1. OTA Industry Landscape

### Global Duopoly Plus Regional Challengers

The online travel agency (OTA) industry has a clear "duopoly plus regional players" structure. Booking Holdings and Expedia Group together account for more than 50% of global online travel bookings.

| Company | 2025 revenue | Market cap | Core market | Differentiation |
|------|---------|------|---------|-----------|
| **Booking Holdings** | USD 26.9 billion | USD 137 billion | Europe, global | Accommodation-first end-to-end travel platform |
| **Expedia Group** | USD 13.7 billion | USD 32.5 billion | North America | Multi-brand portfolio (Hotels.com / Vrbo) |
| **Airbnb** | USD 11.9 billion | USD 78.5 billion | Global | Alternative accommodation and experiential travel |
| **Trip.com (Ctrip)** | USD 8.2 billion | USD 34.2 billion | China, Asia-Pacific | Leader in Chinese outbound travel |
| **Google** | - | - | Global | Traffic gateway / metasearch |

Data sources: public company filings; [Mize OTA market share](https://mize.tech/blog/online-travel-agencies-market-share-across-the-world/); [Business of Apps](https://www.businessofapps.com/data/travel-app-market/)

### In-Depth View of Each Competitor

**Expedia: the eternal No. 2**  
Expedia has a business model similar to Booking's, but execution has consistently lagged. Revenue is only about half of Booking's, net margin is about 8% (Booking is 20.1%), and market cap is just 24% of Booking's. Expedia has some advantage in North America, but it trails Booking in globalization, profitability, and technology investment. Its multi-brand strategy (Hotels.com, Vrbo, Orbitz, etc.) gives it broad coverage but also dilutes brand recognition and lowers operating efficiency.

**Airbnb: a friend in a different lane**  
Airbnb created the homestay / short-term-rental category. It competes with traditional OTAs, but is also somewhat complementary. Booking.com also has a large amount of apartment and alternative-accommodation supply, so it competes directly with Airbnb in that segment. But Airbnb's positioning is more about "experience" and "living like a local," so its user base is different. Airbnb's 34x P/E reflects the market's belief in its brand premium, but its revenue scale and margins are still below Booking's.

**Trip.com (Ctrip): the Asian fortress**  
Ctrip is near-monopolistic in China, and its 8x P/E reflects the valuation discount applied to Chinese ADRs. But Ctrip is actively expanding abroad, with international revenue reaching about 40% in 2025. In Southeast Asia and Japan/Korea, Ctrip competes directly with Booking through Agoda.

**Google: the elephant in the room**  
This is the most important force to watch. Google controls the global search traffic gateway. In 2025, major OTAs spent USD 5.2 billion on marketing in the second quarter alone, and most of that went to Google. In November 2025, Google announced it was developing an agentic AI travel-booking tool based on Gemini, and Booking and Expedia shares fell 4%-7% that day.

Although Google has publicly said it has "no intention of becoming an OTA," it owns Maps, Flights, Hotels search, and a powerful AI stack that could theoretically bypass OTAs and connect users directly to suppliers. According to a Bernstein analyst report, if an AI chat interface integrates live pricing and payments, up to 40% of travelers might be willing to book directly through AI.

Data sources: [Skift - Google clarifies AI booking plans](https://skift.com/2025/11/20/google-agentic-ai-travel-booking-no-intention-become-ota/); [HBR - Gen AI threatening online travel](https://hbr.org/2026/01/gen-ai-is-threatening-the-platforms-that-dominate-online-travel)

## 2. Moat Assessment

### Type and Strength of the Moat

| Moat type | Strength | Explanation |
|-----------|------|------|
| **Network effects** | Extremely strong | The classic two-sided-platform flywheel: 1.235 billion room nights per year is nearly impossible to replicate |
| **Scale economics** | Strong | USD 26.9 billion of revenue spreads technology and operating costs; 87.4% gross margin proves it |
| **Brand recognition** | Strong | In Europe, "book a hotel" often means Booking.com in the consumer's mind |
| **Switching costs** | Moderately strong | Supply side: small hotels rely on Booking for customer acquisition, with 30%-50% of bookings coming from the platform; demand side: accumulated reviews and preference data create stickiness |
| **Data moat** | Strong | Decades of accumulated reviews, user preferences, and pricing data are not replicable |
| **Regulatory moat** | Weak | Global payment licensing and compliance requirements raise the bar for new entrants |

### Weak Points in the Moat

Network effects in travel are not as exclusive as they are in social networks. Users can compare prices across Booking, Expedia, and Airbnb, and multi-homing is common. That means the moat is wide but not a Great Wall - more like a wide river that can still be crossed.

## 3. "Invert": What Could Destroy the Business?

### Scenario 1: Google really enters OTA

If Google uses AI search to integrate live pricing and one-click booking, users may no longer need to open Booking.com. That would fundamentally cut off Booking's traffic source - currently, Booking's paid search spend is more than 20% of revenue, and most of that goes to Google. If Google moves from "traffic seller" to "transaction intermediary," Booking's customer-acquisition cost structure would be upended.

**Counterpoint:** Google has tried and failed repeatedly in travel verticalization over the past decade (Google Trips was shut down). OTA requires deep supplier-management and customer-service capability, which is not Google's core strength. And competing directly with OTAs would mean losing massive ad revenue from those same OTAs, creating a conflict of interest.

### Scenario 2: Direct hotel booking keeps rising

Large hotel chains such as Marriott and Hilton have pushed "book direct" campaigns, using loyalty points and best-rate guarantees to steer customers away from OTAs. If large chains can lift direct bookings from about 30% today to 50%+, Booking's addressable market would shrink materially.

**Counterpoint:** Large chains account for only about 30% of global lodging supply; the remaining 70% of independent hotels and alternative accommodations depend heavily on OTAs for demand. Booking's strength in Europe exists precisely because Europe has a high share of independent hotels.

### Scenario 3: A deep global recession crushes travel demand

During the 2008 financial crisis, travel spending fell about 15%. A recession of that scale would hit Booking's revenue and profit hard. But Booking proved its resilience during the 2020 pandemic, when travel almost stopped entirely - its asset-light model allowed it to recover quickly.

### Scenario 4: Regulatory risk

The EU is already discussing caps on OTA commissions charged to hotels. If major markets adopt similar rules (for example, a 10% commission cap), Booking's revenue would be directly affected.

### Probability Assessment of the Biggest Risks

| Risk | Probability | Impact | Timeframe |
|------|------|---------|---------|
| Google AI disruption | Medium (20%-30%) | High | 3-5 years |
| Direct-booking erosion | Low (10%-15%) | Medium | Gradual |
| Recession | Medium (25%-30%) | Medium-high | Cyclical |
| Regulatory restrictions | Low (10%) | Medium | Uncertain |

## 4. Final Competitive Judgment

**Booking Holdings occupies a "good business, but not perfect" competitive position.** It has the widest moat in OTA, but that moat faces new AI-era challenges.

The most important point is this: **Booking has to beat not only Expedia and Airbnb, but also prove that it remains indispensable in a world where Google AI reshapes how people search and book travel.** The Connected Trip strategy and its own AI investment are the right direction. If Booking can turn itself from a "booking tool that gets traffic from search engines" into a "travel super-app users open directly," its moat may actually widen in the AI era.

The outcome of this competition will become much clearer over the next 3-5 years.
