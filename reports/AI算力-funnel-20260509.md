## Layer 3: Deep-dive analysis (9 names, 300-500 words each)

### 3.1 NVIDIA (NVDA.O)

**Business model:** AI accelerator GPUs + CUDA software ecosystem + NVLink high-speed interconnect + DGX/HGX reference server designs. FY2026 revenue was $215.9B (YoY +65%), datacenter revenue was $194B (YoY +68%). FY2026 Q4 alone was $68.1B (YoY +73%). The product cadence has shortened from two years per generation to one year (H100 -> B100 -> GB200 -> Rubin).

**Financial quality:** FY2026 ROE of 76.3%, FCF of $96.7B, and long-term gross margin above 70%. Asset-liability ratio around 30%. FCF / net profit above 100% (including equity-compensation reversals), so cash-flow quality is exceptional.

**Moat:** CUDA ecosystem (developer lock-in for 15+ years, millions of developers), NVLink proprietary interconnect protocol, Mellanox networking, DGX/HGX reference designs, and priority access to TSMC advanced-process capacity. Ten years out, CUDA's software moat will likely still be there, but hardware moats will be partly eroded by ASICs (Google TPU / Broadcom XPU) and AMD's MI series.

**Main risks:** (1) Inference gets heavily diverted to custom ASICs (already happening: Google / Meta / AWS are all building in-house); (2) China policy swings back and forth (H200 easing / tightening); (3) capex bubble bursts - the turning point for the four big cloud providers.

**Valuation quick take:** FY27 P/E 38 (consensus). PEG ~0.6. Among the Mag 7, the P/E is not cheap, but the $4.82 trillion market cap is the largest in history; getting back to P/S < 10 would require another doubling of revenue. **Valuation is fair-to-high, depending on whether you believe AI capex can double again.**

### 3.2 TSMC (TSM.N / 2330.TW)

**Business model:** Advanced-process foundry. 3nm/5nm/7nm contribute 70%+ of revenue, and almost all AI accelerators (NVIDIA / AMD / Broadcom / Apple) rely on it. 2025 revenue was $122.9B (YoY +31.6%), and 2026 consensus is still +30%.

**Financial quality:** 2025 gross margin of 59.9% (up 3.8pp from 2024), ROE of 35.4% (+5.1pp), FCF of $31.7B (+15%), D/E 0.18, debt ratio 12.5%. Every metric is textbook quality.

**Moat:** Advanced-process exclusivity (besides Samsung, no other company dares do 3nm at scale), a 5+ year EUV learning curve, highly concentrated and irreplaceable customers (NVIDIA / Apple switching costs are extreme), and a scale moat built on $40B+/year of capex. Ten years out the moat is likely even deeper unless geopolitics intervene.

**Main risks:** (1) Taiwan Strait geopolitics - this is the Damocles sword hanging over the valuation; (2) a down-cycle in AI capex; (3) new fabs in the U.S. / Japan / Germany underperform on yield, dragging down capital returns.

**Valuation quick take:** P/E ~25, PEG < 1. In a business with this much certainty and this much ROE, P/E 25 is exactly Buffett's "buy a great company at a fair price." **Valuation is fair-to-cheap, with the discount mainly coming from Taiwan risk.**

### 3.3 Broadcom (AVGO.O)

**Business model:** Custom ASICs (XPU, main customers: Google TPU, Meta MTIA, ByteDance) + networking chips (Tomahawk switch chips, including CPO) + software (VMware). FY2025 FCF was $26.9B (+38.6%). FY2026 Q1 FCF was $8.01B. AI semiconductor revenue has grown fast for three straight quarters.

**Financial quality:** ROE 33.37%, P/E 72 (high), but PEG ~1. Debt ratio climbed to ~50% because of the VMware acquisition (still deleveraging quickly post-VMware). FCF is extremely strong.

**Moat:** (1) Deep ties with hyperscale customers (joint ASIC development cycles of 18-24 months, so switching costs are huge); (2) 70%+ share in network-switch chips; (3) VMware software provides cash flow that outlives cycles. Ten years out, the ASIC business moat is questionable (cloud players may work even more directly with TSMC), but networking chips and software are likely still there.

**Main risks:** (1) Google / Meta internalize ASIC design (Google is already trying); (2) VMware integration disappoints; (3) P/E 72 embeds extremely high expectations, so if AI ASIC growth slows, the stock could correct sharply.

**Valuation quick take:** P/E 72, FCF yield around 2%. **Expensive.** You only buy it if you believe AI ASICs are a certain five-year megatrend.

### 3.4 SK Hynix (000660.KS)

**Business model:** No. 1 in global HBM (62% share), deeply tied to NVIDIA for HBM3E. Integrated DRAM / NAND memory. FY2026 Q1 revenue was KRW 52.58 trillion (YoY +198%), operating profit KRW 37.61 trillion (YoY +405%), net margin ~77%.

**Financial quality:** ROE 63%, D/E 0.12 (net cash), cash balance KRW 34.9 trillion. HBM capacity is effectively booked through 2026.

**Moat:** (1) HBM3E process is half a generation ahead of Samsung; (2) first-mover relationship with NVIDIA; (3) 5+ years of capex and process accumulation. Ten years out, memory is a cyclical business - that is the biggest variable. The HBM up-cycle may peak within 2-3 years.

**Main risks:** (1) Cyclicality (memory is the classic cyclical stock; it lost money in 2022); (2) probability Samsung HBM4 catches up; (3) a turning point in AI training demand.

**Valuation quick take:** P/E ~10 (est.), P/B ~3, valuation at the top of the cycle. **Cheap - if you think AI memory demand is bigger than one super-cycle. Dangerous - if you value it like a cyclical stock.**

### 3.5 ASML (ASML.O)

**Business model:** The world's sole supplier of EUV lithography machines, and the No. 1 DUV supplier. Mandatory equipment for every advanced node below 7nm.

**Financial quality:** ROE ~50% (est.), strong FCF, low debt ratio. Order backlog exceeds two years.

**Moat:** Decades of accumulation in physics, optics, and precision mechanics; the only company in the world that can mass-produce EUV. Ten years out it is very likely still there - this is a civilization-level technology barrier. **One of the deepest moats on earth.**

**Main risks:** (1) U.S. export restrictions to China expand further (Low-NA EUV already banned, High-NA still undecided); (2) capex volatility in the semiconductor cycle; (3) customer acceptance of High-NA EUV.

**Valuation quick take:** P/E ~30. **Reasonable valuation.**

### 3.6 Arista Networks (ANET.N)

**Business model:** High-speed Ethernet switches for datacenters, already ahead of Cisco in datacenter switching. 2025 revenue was $9B (YoY +29%), and the 2026 AI-network revenue target was raised from $2.75B to $3.25B.

**Financial quality:** ROE 30%+ (est.), strong FCF, zero debt.

**Moat:** (1) EOS software platform is highly modular; (2) deep co-development with hyperscalers; (3) leading iteration speed in high-speed Ethernet. The ten-year moat is only medium-strength - networking switches are always under standardization pressure, and NVIDIA's InfiniBand / Spectrum-X creates competition.

**Main risks:** (1) NVIDIA networking solutions take share; (2) Broadcom Tomahawk + white-box switch price competition; (3) a down-cycle in AI datacenter buildout.

**Valuation quick take:** P/E ~50 (est.). A bit expensive, but growth can absorb it.

### 3.7 InnoLight Technology (300308.SZ)

**Business model:** Global optical-module leader, with 1.6T silicon photonics scaling rapidly. 2026 Q1 revenue was ¥19.5B (YoY +192%), net profit ¥5.735B (YoY +262%). 2025 annual report: net profit ¥9.8B-¥11.8B (YoY +89% to +128%).

**Financial quality:** ROE 27.03%, asset-liability ratio 29.7%. **Operating cash flow of ¥3.165B / net profit of about ¥11B ≈ 30% - this is the key weakness**; working-capital expansion is eating cash flow. The spike in prepayments reflects upstream tightness, and future cash-flow improvement still needs to be watched.

**Moat:** (1) Deep ties with NVIDIA, Google, and Meta, plus an execution edge in 1.6T silicon photonics; (2) scale effects; (3) leading process yields. The ten-year moat is medium-strength - optical modules can become commoditized, and CPO disruption risk is real.

**Main risks:** (1) **CPO (co-packaged optics) substitution risk** - once top customers switch to CPO, the value of DSP + pluggable optical modules shrinks sharply; (2) insufficient optical-chip self-sufficiency, constrained by U.S. suppliers / Coherent / Lumentum; (3) high customer concentration (top four North American customers >70%).

**Valuation quick take:** Based on 2025 net profit, P/E is 77x, but 2026 growth can compress it to around 35x, with PEG < 1. **Valuation is reasonably expensive; upside is large, but downside risk is also large.**

### 3.8 Hudian Co. (002463.SZ)

**Business model:** High-speed PCB for datacenter switches + AI servers. 2025 revenue was ¥18.9B (YoY +42%), and net profit attributable to shareholders was ¥3.82B (YoY +48%).

**Financial quality:** ROE 28.57% (weighted), operating cash flow ¥3.872B, roughly equal to net profit (~100%, excellent). Asset-liability ratio 46.5%. Every line clears the threshold.

**Moat:** (1) High-end process capability for 800G / 1.6T switch PCBs; (2) deep cooperation with Broadcom and Arista; (3) relatively early capex (>¥10B). However, the PCB industry faces an oversupply risk (industry-wide new capacity >¥40B). The ten-year moat is medium-to-weak.

**Main risks:** (1) High-end PCB capacity could be oversupplied in 2027-2028; (2) high customer concentration; (3) slow substitution of downstream customers.

**Valuation quick take:** P/E ~30 and ROE ~28%. **Valuation is reasonably cheap; financial quality is one of the cleanest among the second-layer names.**

### 3.9 Shenghong Technology (300476.SZ)

**Business model:** No. 1 in global AI-compute PCB, with a 13.8% global share. 2025 net profit was ¥4.312B (YoY +273.52%), and it is the world's only mass-producer of 8-layer / 28-layer HDI. 2026 Q1 net profit was ¥1.288B, and operating cash flow was ¥2.117B (YoY +399%).

**Financial quality:** Operating cash flow of ¥4.603B / net profit of ¥4.312B ≈ 107%. **Financial quality is good.** Asset-liability ratio is reasonable.

**Moat:** (1) The world's only 8-layer / 28-layer HDI mass production; (2) deep ties with NVIDIA / Anduril and other top GPU vendors; (3) scale effects + yield leadership. The ten-year moat is medium-strength - PCB is still manufacturing, and the process can be caught up.

**Main risks:** (1) Very high customer concentration on NVIDIA; (2) industry oversupply risk; (3) process catch-up by competitors (Avary's ¥23.3B expansion, Shennan Circuits, etc.).

**Valuation quick take:** P/E ~50 (est.), PEG < 1. **Valuation is reasonably expensive, but growth is the strongest.**

## Final selection logic

### Final 1: TSMC

#### Duan Yongping lens: business essence

Duan Yongping has repeatedly said that he understands TSMC's business - it is the "foundry power grid" of the chip industry.

**Business essence: selling the core production line for chips** - not a model company, not a cloud company, but the "electricity plant" for semiconductors. Every model, every accelerator, every AI stack depends on it.

Revenue structure (FY2025):
- Foundry: majority of revenue
- Advanced process: 3nm / 5nm / 7nm concentrated
- AI accelerators: key customers across the ecosystem

**Is this a good business? The answer is yes, and one of the best.**

Positive:
- ROE 35%+
- Gross margin ~60%
- FCF $31.7B

Negative:
- Taiwan Strait risk
- Huge capex requirement
- End-market concentration in AI

#### Buffett lens: moat

#### Duan Yongping lens: business essence

Duan Yongping has publicly said he does not understand NVIDIA ("if you don't understand it, don't touch it"), but he does acknowledge CUDA as a real moat.

**Business essence: selling the "compute generator" of the AI era** - but not an ordinary generator. It is a generator with an operating system (CUDA), and that operating system has locked in 90% of the world's AI developers.

Revenue structure (FY2025):

- Gaming: ~$15B
- Automotive / Pro Viz: ~$7B

**Is this a good business? The answer: top-tier right now, with the long-term outcome depending on whether CUDA can withstand the counterattack from open ecosystems (PyTorch, JAX, ROCm).**

Negative:

#### Buffett lens: moat

**Will the moat still be there 10 years from now?** The software ecosystem moat (CUDA) likely will be, but the hardware layer will be chipped away by ASICs. Ten years out NVIDIA will probably still be the global No. 1 in AI training, but training share could fall from 90% to 50-60%, and inference share from 60% to 30-40%. Absolute revenue could still be higher because the pie is larger.

#### Munger lens: risk and failure modes

2. **CUDA moat breaks under open ecosystems**: PyTorch + compilers (like Triton) + ASIC backends keep evolving; developers may stop writing CUDA directly, lowering migration costs.
3. **Customer internalization accelerates**: Google TPU can already handle all Gemini training; Meta MTIA and ByteDance ASICs are moving fast.
4. **China goes to zero**: repeated H200 restrictions and faster domestic substitution.
5. **Inventory risk**: GB200 already experienced yield issues and inventory write-down risk.

Historical lesson: GE's market cap rose many times from 1900 to 1929, then spent a century in low growth. NVIDIA today looks more like GE in 1925 - already at a cyclical peak.

### Final 3: SK Hynix

#### Duan Yongping lens: business essence

Duan Yongping is always cautious on cyclical stocks - "cyclicals are best avoided; if you buy them, do it only at the bottom."

**Business essence: HBM / DRAM oligopoly** - a business combining cyclicality with oligopoly dynamics. It is currently in an AI-driven "super-cycle," with gross margins having swung from losses in 2022 to a 77% net margin in Q1 2026.

**Is this a good business? The answer: yes, but not the best one, because it is cyclical.**

Compared with the other two (NVIDIA / TSMC), it has one unique advantage right now: **it is very cheap (P/E ~10)**. Its AI exposure is also more pure - HBM as a share of DRAM revenue is rapidly rising, reaching 40%+ in 2026.

#### Buffett lens: moat

| Moat | Strength | Evidence |
|--------|------|------|
| Brand / pricing power | ★★★★ | HBM3E prices are 4-5x ordinary DRAM |
| Switching costs | ★★★ | NVIDIA's HBM qualification takes 6+ months |
| Network effects | ★★ | — |
| Scale | ★★★★ | The DRAM trio (SK / Samsung / Micron) |
| Technology / license | ★★★★ | HBM4 process barrier |

**Will the moat still be there 10 years from now?** Not necessarily. DRAM history has seen 50+ players eventually shrink to 3. Over the next 5-10 years HBM should still be a three-horse race, but shares will move: Samsung may rebound on HBM4 and Micron may win NVIDIA orders. SK Hynix's current 62% share could fall to 45-50% by 2028.

Buffett has historically bought memory stocks only rarely (aside from a small Micron position in 1995), because the business is so cyclical.

#### Munger lens: risk and failure modes

**How could it fail?**

1. **Memory down-cycle**: DRAM has historically cycled every 4-5 years. The current up-cycle began in 2023 and is likely to peak in 2027-2028. ROE can fall from 60% at the top to -10% at the bottom.
2. **Samsung comeback**: Samsung HBM4 could mass-produce in Q4 2026 to 2027 and win NVIDIA orders.
3. **AI training demand inflection**: 80% of HBM demand comes from AI training; once the four big cloud providers' capex turns, a 30%+ price decline in HBM is quite plausible.

**Worst-case value?** If memory returns to the 2022 cycle bottom (net profit -$1 billion), P/E is not meaningful and P/B could be 0.5 - market cap roughly $200 billion, around -70%.

**Why might smart people not buy it?** Cyclicality. "At the top, a 10x P/E looks cheap, but it is actually 10x peak-cycle EPS - bottom-cycle EPS can go to zero or negative."

#### Li Lu lens: civilization-scale trend placement

**HBM is the "must-have path" for AI.** Every GPU currently needs HBM - this is an ecosystem-level necessity. Will that necessity still hold ten years from now?

Possible substitutes: (1) CXL memory pooling partially replaces HBM; (2) DRAM-on-logic changes the HBM form factor; (3) optical interconnect lowers HBM dependence.

But within ten years (before 2030), HBM remains the bottleneck that cannot be replaced. So SK Hynix's AI positioning is real, but its moat depth is less than TSMC's EUV process moat.

### Historical analogy

The closest historical analogies are **Cisco + EMC + Sun during the 2000 internet bubble** or **Microsoft + Intel in the 1990s**:

- **Cisco analogy:** In 1999, P/E was 100+, market cap was $550B (then the largest). As a company "selling the picks and shovels for networking," its stock fell 80% after the bubble burst in 2001. Only in 2024 did its market cap come close again to the 1999 level. **Lesson: even a real moat can take 10 years to recover if the P/E is too high.**
- **Microsoft + Intel analogy:** monopoly-style moats, survived the bubble and later reached new highs (but it took 10 years). **Lesson: CUDA may be like Windows, and it may ultimately withstand the cycle.**

**Did most investors make money or lose money?** In the 1999-2002 internet bubble, most retail investors lost money (buying the top), while 50% of institutions lost money and 30% broke even. **The key is timing.**

### Civilization-scale trend judgment

AI is probably a **"electricity-scale paradigm shift"**:

- The electricity revolution spread to all industries over 1880-1930. AI is likely faster, perhaps 20-30 years.
- AI is not just "the internet"; it is a "general-purpose infrastructure."
- The "winner-take-most" parts of the compute chain are: **EUV lithography (ASML) -> advanced foundry (TSMC) -> AI chip design (NVIDIA) -> HBM (SK Hynix / Samsung)**
