# Core judgement

PayPal is a large pay-up of $33.2 billion a year, $5.6 billion in free cash flows and $15 billion in net cash**, but the market only gives it a market value of $36.4 billion ** — almost equal to one year's income. The 8.7-fold market surplus is 42 times the historical median ** one fifth**.

This is either a gross underestimation or a price that the market is pricing for the future of a “slow recession”.

** Conclusion: PayPal's current valuation is in the blurring zone of "value trap" and "historical underestimation".** The core variable is whether Branded Checkout can stop falling — if so, a buy-in opportunity of 10 years; if not, the low market rate is not a security cushion but a recession alert.

# Two, the moat's analysis

# 1. Bilateral network effects # (Middle strong, but weakened)

PayPal has the largest bilateral network of payment industries - 439 million active accounts + 35 million businesses. The more consumers, the more willing the business owners are to access the PayPal button; the more the business owners, the more the consumer is used.

** But the network effect is being eroded:**
- Active accounts dropped from a peak of 435 million in 2022 to 388 million (except after active calibres)
- The monthly balance of brands is about 142 million, which is only one third of the total active account.
- A lot of users are "Zosterious accounts" -- account numbers, but not.

** Inverse:** The decline in active accounts is mainly due to the natural loss of low-quality users acquired during the epidemic, and core high-frequency users remain highly sticky.

#2. Brand trust #

PayPal is the most trusted way for consumers around the world to pay on strange websites -- they are willing to pay when they see the PayPal button. This trust premium has been established for over 20 years, including:
- Buyer protection policy (payment problems can be refunded)
- No need to expose the bank card to the merchants.
- Global awareness

** Key challenges:** Apple pays double-click on sidebars plus face ID to complete the payment without the need to jump, log in or write down passwords. For young users, this first-born experience is far better than PayPal. In 2026, US Apple pays an estimated 90.5 million, PayPal 92.1 million users — the gap has become almost flat.

#3 Data assets and wind control #

- Real-time risk assessment using over **500 data points per transaction**
- AI blocked approximately US$ **500 million per quarter** fraudulent transactions
- Machine learning models of transaction data accumulated over 20 years, which can detect patterns that are not visible in other wind control systems
- It's real for the merchants - lower rejection rates, higher pass rates.

##4. Venmo <unk> (with great potential, but still early in the realization)

- More than 100 million people live a month, and the brand is very well known among young Americans.
- Revenue of approximately $1.7 billion (+20 per cent) in 2025, with double-digit growth for five consecutive quarters
- 60% increase in debit cards and 34% increase in "pay with Venmo" transactions
- Transitioning from a loss P2P tool to a real-estate business platform

** But ** 1.7 billion dollars of income is only 5 per cent of the total PayPal and is not enough to change the overall picture.

##5. Pricing rights # (weak, falling)

The rate of take-rate of the PayPal continues to drop:
- Brand closings were drawn at about 3.5 per cent (high, but stagnant rate)
- Braintree's got about 2.9%.
- 4 basis points to 1.73 per cent drop in overall output by the same year

** Core paradox: The fastest growing business (Brintree) has the lowest profit margin, and the most profitable business (brand closure) has the slowest growth.** This is a structural "growth dilution of profits" dilemma.

# Nine, investment conclusions

# Core contradictions

PayPal is a company with a strong earning capacity but uncertain growth prospects. It generates 5.6 billion free cash flows, 15 billion net cash in hand each year, and 20 billion repurchases of mandates -- all hard power.

# A price to buy

You believe brand closure is steady.
2. Would you like to wait two to three years for the buy-back to drive the gains per share?
3. You treat Venmo and advertising as options for free gift.
4. You can accept the implementation risks of the new CEO

# Not worth buying

1. Do you think payments are being commodified and PayPal ' s pricing rights will continue to decline?
2. You think Apple will replace most online PayPal in 5-10 years?
You have no confidence in the corporate governance of two and a half years of CEO.
4. You think 8.7 times PE is not undervalued, but market is in a long-term price-depreciating recession.

# One word

> ** PayPal is like a leaking dam - the dam itself (33.2 billion revenue, 5.6 billion cash flow) is still strong, but the cracks (brand closings, management turbulence, apple competition) are growing. 8.7 times the price of the market price for the repair: if the cracks were blocked, it would be extremely cheap; if it was blocked, the dam would eventually be eroded.** Michael Burry’s 49-dollar warehouse would suggest that he bet the former, but the market would fall to $41 that most people bet the latter.
