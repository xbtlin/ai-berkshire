# PayPal vs. Ant Group: Similarities and Differences Between Two Payment Giants

> Analysis date: 2026-06-08
> This article does not constitute investment advice.

---

## Similarities

| Dimension | Shared characteristic |
|------|---------|
| Origin | Both started in third-party payments (PayPal -> the eBay ecosystem, Alipay -> the Taobao ecosystem) |
| Core function | Both solved the "buyer does not trust the seller" problem by holding funds in escrow |
| Scale | Both are payment leaders in their respective markets (PayPal 43% of global online payments, Alipay 36% in China) |
| Super-app ambition | Both tried to expand from payments into finance (credit, wealth management, insurance) plus lifestyle services |
| Regulatory pressure | Both have faced heavy regulatory action (Ant's IPO was halted; PayPal was investigated by the UK FCA over antitrust concerns) |
| Valuation compression | Both have seen dramatic declines from peak valuations (Ant from RMB 2 trillion to RMB 600 billion, PayPal from USD 300 billion to USD 36.4 billion market cap) |

---

## Core Differences

| Dimension | PayPal | Ant Group |
|------|--------|---------|
| **Market** | Global (200+ countries), core in the U.S. / Europe | Primarily China, with internationalization just getting started |
| **Underlying architecture** | Built on Visa / Mastercard rails - a **"sub-landlord"** | Connects directly to bank accounts and bypasses card networks - the **"landlord"** |
| **Take rate structure** | Charges 3.49% but shares about half with card networks and issuing banks, retaining about 1.5% | Lower fee rate (about 0.6%), but direct bank connectivity means no card-network toll |
| **Source of user stickiness** | Brand trust + buyer protection (functional layer) | Social relationships + everyday use cases + mini-program ecosystem (ecosystem layer) |
| **Biggest threat** | Apple Pay (OS-level substitution) | WeChat Pay (social-scenario erosion; share has already recovered to 60%) |
| **Financial services** | Light involvement (BNPL, credit), actively shrinking | Deep involvement (Huabei, Jiebei, Yu'e Bao, insurance), forced to contract by regulators |
| **Profitability** | Net margin about 16%, free cash flow USD 5.6 billion | 2025 net income of RMB 15.3 billion (down 60% YoY), R&D spending hit a record RMB 29 billion |
| **Listing status** | Nasdaq-listed, market cap USD 36.4 billion | Not listed; IPO remains on hold |
| **AI strategy** | Risk control + ads + Fastlane checkout (application layer) | Ant Fu + Bailing model (infrastructure layer) |

## Revenue Funnel Comparison

### PayPal

```
Total payment volume: USD 1.79 trillion
    │ × take rate 1.73%
Revenue: USD 33.2 billion
    │ - card network / issuer / fraud costs: USD 17.7 billion
Transaction profit: USD 15.5 billion
    │ - operating expenses: USD 9.4 billion
Operating profit: USD 6.1 billion
    │ - taxes
Net income: USD 5.2 billion -> free cash flow USD 5.6 billion
```

Conversion rate: free cash flow / total payment volume = **0.31%**

### Ant Group (estimate, limited data)

```
Total payment volume: about RMB 118 trillion (estimated from 2023 Alipay data)
    │ × take rate about 0.06% (far below PayPal)
Revenue: about RMB 190 billion (2023 estimate)
    │ - operating costs
Net income: RMB 15.3 billion (2025, sharply lower because of restructuring and AI investment)
```

Conversion rate: net income / total payment volume = **about 0.001%**

### Key Difference

**PayPal's take rate (1.73%) is almost 30x Ant's** - not because PayPal is inherently better at making money, but because the underlying architectures are completely different:

- PayPal sits on Visa / Mastercard rails, and card-network fees are already high (U.S. credit-card interchange is about 2%); PayPal adds another layer on top
- Ant connects directly to bank accounts, with no card-network middleman, so the whole payment chain is cheaper
- The China payments market has been trained by WeChat and Alipay to expect an almost free user experience, which leaves very limited room for take rate extraction

---

## The Most Fundamental Difference

**PayPal is an "add-on layer" in the card-network ecosystem, while Ant is an "alternative layer" to the banking system.**

Every PayPal transaction must travel on Visa / Mastercard rails and pay a "toll." It earns a premium for adding trust and convenience on top of the card networks. The problem is that when Apple Pay provides the same trust and even better convenience through native OS capabilities, that premium gets squeezed.

Ant (Alipay) connects directly to users' bank accounts and does not depend on card networks - China simply does not have that Visa / Mastercard middle layer. Its business model is more like "building its own railway" than providing services on someone else's railway. But that also means it directly touches the core interests of banks and regulators, which is why the restructuring has been much harsher.

**PayPal's ceiling is determined by how much the card ecosystem lets it earn; Ant's ceiling is determined by how much regulators let it do.**

## Investment Implications

| Dimension | PayPal | Ant |
|------|--------|------|
| Investability | Investable (Nasdaq-listed, 8.7x earnings) | Not investable yet (unlisted, unclear exit path) |
| Valuation | At historical lows, potentially deeply undervalued | Valuation fell from RMB 2 trillion to RMB 600 billion, but there is no public market price |
| Core bet | Can branded checkout stabilize? | When will regulation ease and when will an IPO happen? |
| Suitable investor | A value investor willing to bet that payments will not become a commodity | A primary-market investor comfortable with long lockups and policy uncertainty |
