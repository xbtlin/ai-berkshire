# How deep the moat is: five dimensions per test

Many investors see "a 20-year-long bonus" and "Roe 17" as a good company. But the key to evaluating a business is not "how much money did it go in the past," but "how much will it go on in the future"?

# 1. Brand moat: almost none

What Young-shin does is B2B business. You don't turn over the chips and watch "The Packing Bag is provided by the Never-news." The terminal consumer has no idea of the new existence, and the brand does not bring the premium.

** Rating: extremely weak**

#2. Conversion costs: This is the most important moat of Everlasting

The replacement of the packaging supplier is not a simple matter:
- Recertification required (food exposure material safety standards, internal vendor review)
- Need to debug production parameters (print colour differential, compound strength, heat cover temperature)
- Test-cycle ** 3-6 months**
- Big branders usually maintain two to three qualified suppliers at the same time, and they don't just phase out.

This means that relationships are sticky once you get into the list of suppliers for large clients.

** But the counter-opportunity is:** conversion costs are effective for large clients and low for small and medium-sized customers. And conversion costs are protected by "not kicking out," not protecting "not getting out of prices" -- customers are not leaving, but they are not accepting price increases.

** Rating: medium**

##3. Network effects: do not exist

Packaging manufacturing is a traditional manufacturing industry, and there is no network effect of "the more products the better."

** Rating: none**

##4. Scale effect: limited

In the year 2025, the harvest was **37.23 billion**, ranking ahead of the paint-coloured complex. Size gives a certain bargaining power and productivity advantage.

But industry CR5 **6%** shows that scale effects are not obvious – and if scale is strong, industry is already concentrated. Anmko collects 20 times more than Eternity in China than it does in a crushing cost advantage.

** Rating: Weak**

##5. Technical/Pictician Barriers: Medium

- **Linse composite packaging**: technological maturity, limited differentiation, barriers mainly in process experience and client certification
- ** Vacuum plating aluminium**: large equipment input, high process requirements, relatively few participants and high barriers
- ** Plastic soft film**: 30,000 tons of dual stretch multi-purpose film project under construction, commissioned in 2026

Technical barriers to aluminium plating and functional thin film are higher than normal colour packaging but smaller in the eternally new income structure (aluminium plating packaging is only 1.7%).

** Rating: medium**

# Pricing authority test: 10% increase, will the client run?

This is the most immediate issue in assessing the quality of business.

The answer depends on the product line:
- ** Normal colour wrapping** (68% of income): presumably running.
- ** High-end aluminium plating/functional film** (income ~ 24%): some clients hesitate but are not irreplaceable

** The data for 2025 provide hard evidence:** Growth in revenue 5.61% and a decline in net profits 6.33% - which means that neither the eternal nor the rising costs can be passed on to the customers, let alone the positive price increases.

Further looking at raw material sensitivity:

# Dubon analysis: 17% of ROE behind

ROE is the most central measure of profitability. For a five-year continuum, ROE > 15%, looks excellent. But by looking at it (dubon analysis), the structure is changing:

<unk> ROE net interest rate <unk>
|------|-----|-------|-----------|---------|
| 2021 | 15.90% | 10.62% | ~1.08 | 1.39 |
| 2022 | 17.01% | 11.16% | ~0.99 | 1.54 |
| 2023 | 17.72% | 12.26% | ~0.93 | 1.56 |
| 2024 | **19.18%** | **13.37%** | ~0.87 | 1.64 |
| 2025 | **17.34%** | **11.87%** | ~0.85 | **1.71** |

*Source: Annual report of Young-Sin

Two trends are alarming:

1. **The asset turnover rate has been declining steadily** (from 1.08 to 0.85) — operational efficiency is deteriorating
** Equity multipliers are rising ** (from 1.39 to 1.71) — leveraging is increasing

In other words, ROE’s maintenance depends partly on leverage rather than increased profitability. The net interest rate fell from 13.37% to 11.87% in 2025, a core variable that supports ROE is weakening. If net interest rates continue to fall and leverage cannot continue to increase, ROE will fall further.

** Inverse:** Asset-liability ratio increased from 27.85% to 41.68%, and absolute levels remain healthy. For manufacturing, the debt ratio is about 40% perfectly normal, far from dangerous areas.
