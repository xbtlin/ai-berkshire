# VIII. Summary - Core judgements from the investment perspective

# Know the core of the driver's contradictions

The driver is a powerful, yet independent business. It shows a remarkable growth rate with the byte algorithms and traffic, but with the stripping of the coat, there is doubt about its viability.

# The biggest risk is not competition, but "mother dependency."

In a metaphor: do you know that the driver is married to a branch of a shivering tree, and the flowering results are beautiful, but if one day you want to "stand independently and tree" the roots are deep enough?

# The key "no" test

1. ** Is the moat deep enough?** Not enough. Flows are from tremors, technology without unique barriers, branding less than car homes.
2. ** Has the business model been fully validated?** - Not validated. The CPS model is still being rolled out, the offline transactions are just beginning and financial data are not publicly available
3. ** Has management been tested?** - Not fully tested.
4. ** How certain is ten years later?** -- low. Autopilot + shared travel may fundamentally change the logic of car consumption

# And conclusions

** From the point of view of deep value investment, it is understood that the Autobots are not currently an attractive investment target.** The reason is not that the business itself is bad, but that:
- Insufficient independence to create value independently
- Core risks (flow dependence, credibility) do not have clear solutions
- Unknown profitability, valued at $3 billion
- Lack of transparency and channels of exit as non-listed companies

** Time window for attention:** When the 3-4 quarter financial statements after IPO were released, real financial data, independently operated traffic performance and management capacity to face capital markets could be seen.
