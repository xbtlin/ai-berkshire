# Five, four business blocks depth analysis

#5.1 Thread operations (42% income, core cash cow)

** Business model:** Annual fee charged to distributors (CPT model) for providing a road-to-road user trail.

** Current dilemma:**
- The total number of industry leads has increased 66 times over 10 years (from 10 million to 660 million), but the volume of traffic sales has increased only 1.4 times.
- The quality of the clues is heavily watered, the cost of the dealers gaining visitors increased by 20 per cent over four years and the rate of transformation decreased by 14 per cent
- The number of pay-as-you-go dealers dropped by about 20 per cent in 2025
- Understanding the CPS mode as a de-concentration to the CPT mode (at delivery fees)

** Trends in judgment: Threading is a sunset pattern. CPT fees cannot prove results, and distributors are voting with their feet**

#5.2 Media services (accelerated contraction, 18 per cent income)

** Business model:** Sale of display slots, content marketing services to auto branders.

** Current dilemma:**
- The fuel truck brand budget continues to shrink.
- New energy brands prefer social platforms like tremor, little red books, etc.
- A cumulative decline of 38 per cent over the two years, from 26 per cent to 18 per cent
- Content patterns (the main figure) are behind the short video age

** Trends in judgement: traditional brand advertising will continue to shrink in vertical platforms in cars, a structural trend**

#5.3 Online marketing and others (40% income, only pole growth)

** Includes operations:**
- New retail: offline space station plus satellite store, providing one-stop service for car sightings, test driving, trading
- Second-hand car: day-to-day platform, two-way service.
- Data products: data analysis and decision support tools for car enterprises
- Transaction commissions: service charges to facilitate the trading of new and used vehicles

** Growth drivers:** Growth for the second consecutive year was about 8-9 per cent, but not fast, and the new retail business heavy asset model lowered the Māori ratio.

#5.4 AI and data products

Five major digital product lines were launched in 2025:
AI Marketing Brain - Support for Smart Decision Making
AI Accessed Pioneer - automated content production
AI Lead Master — Precision User Touch
AI Crownized Oracle - Sales Empowerment
AI Cars Specialist — Vehicle Profile Assessment

**Assessment:** AI productization is in the right direction, but currently the revenue contribution is limited, relying on the large external model (DeepSeek) and its own technical barriers are not deep.

# # Summary of key differences

1. **The essential differences in the source of the traffic**: Automotive House is an "silo-like" flow, and users need to download App or access the website on their own initiative; the flow pool of 600 million + DAU, which is understood to be the owner 's back of the car, "the car content consumes 500 million times a day" and the cost of traffic acquisition is almost zero.

2. ** Intergenerational differences in content production methods**: Automotive homes rely on editorial teams and a small number of contracted creators (2,500+) to produce standardized graphic content; understand the UGC ecology of the Automobile with 7.5 million creators, with an average of 2 million authors per day, with a wide disparity in content supply levels.

3. ** Evolutional differences in business models**: CPT models for car homes are "rent-taking" logic (which is charged regardless of effect), with low vendor satisfaction; CPS models for drivers are "share" logic (by delivery fee) and are more closely tied to the interests of distributors. 65.9% of dealers found that they know better the quality of the driver's trail, and 60% indicated a significant increase in the conversion rate after the use of the CPS.

4. ** Intergenerational differences in technical capabilities**: the bottom of the technology at the Automotive House is the search + classification information system in the Web 2.0 era; the driver has inherited the byte-dancing recommended algorithm engine, which enables the distribution of content and clues for the "thousands of people".

# Nine, Investment Decision Analysis: Buying Automotive Home or Waiting for The Autobot IPO?

# 9.1 Investment logic of the car house

** See multi-purpose:**
- Net cash exceeds market value, theoretically "free" to get business.
- PE 10.9x, PB 0.65x, valuation at the bottom of history
- $200 million buybacks offer a certain security margin.
- Helming may bring a new story about "car interconnection."
- Even if operations continued to contract, large cash reserves provided a window of time

** Reason for viewing:**
- Two consecutive years of income and profit decline and accelerated decline
- Core business (thread + media) is facing a structural decline
- New retailing has not grown fast enough and has delayed the Maori rate
- There is great uncertainty about the cross-border integration of Haile.
- The tendency to be informed of the emperor's share is hard to reverse.
- Payers cut by 20%, basics are falling apart.
- The founding team has long left.

# 9.2 Knows the driver's investment logic

** See multi-purpose:**
- Byte beat/twitch flow ecology with extremely low cost of access
- User growth was strong, DAU outpaced 10 million and still growing
- The CPS model represents the direction of industry evolution.
- The UGC ecology of 7.5 million creators is difficult to replicate
- Structural beneficiaries of the new energy track
- If IPO values $3-5 billion, corresponding to about 5-8x PS, there is still reasonable room

** Reason for viewing:**
- Financial data not made public, questionable profitability
- Potential downward pressure on valuation after IPO (current market-to-market low valuation of the Central Unit)
- Over-reliance on byte beat/shaking flow
- The profitability of the CPS model needs to be validated (for the distributor but possibly for the platform less)
- The vertical track itself faces the erosion of a combination of platforms like tremors, small red books, etc.

#9.3 Investment proposals

** Core judgement: If you want to invest in the car Internet track, you should wait for the driver to know IPO instead of buying a car home.**

The reasons are as follows:

1. ** Trends are more important than valuation**: Automotive homes are in a continuous downward path, although they are cheap to value. Value investments are not about buying cheap goods, but about buying good companies. The current dilemma of auto homes is more like Koda or Nokia — not cyclical downswings, but about the collapse of business models by new paradigms.

2. ** But do not rush to buy the driver**: understanding the driver IPO requires observing the public financial data for several quarters and validating its profit model. The $3 billion equivalent PS multiplier requires income data to be made public before reasonableness can be assessed.

3. ** The "net cash" of the Automotive House appears to be safe, but it is a trap**: unless management is willing to split or privatize on a large scale (Haer is likely to do so), the cash on the account is of no practical significance to small shareholders. In the light of the past days of peace, large amounts of cash have not been translated into shareholder returns.

4. **The best strategy is to wait**:
- 2 to 3 quarterly financial data published after IPO knowledge of the driver
- Observing their income size, growth rates, profitability
- If it is determined that the profit model is sustainable, build a warehouse at the time of reasonable valuation
- Automotive homes can be used as a view of the hedges, but not as a proposal for a warehouse.

** Deeper question**: Is the vertical platform of a car a good track? How deep is the river of the vertical platform of an independent car when the combined platform of tremors, small red books, etc. are cutting into car content and transactions? This question applies to the car house and to the owner.
