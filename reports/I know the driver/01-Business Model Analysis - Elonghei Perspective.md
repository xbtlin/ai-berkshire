# III. A waterfront analysis (the Eternity standard)

Good business standards that are repeatedly emphasized by Eterning: differentiation, pricing, long-term moats. We evaluate each case.

#3.1 Brand moat -- medium weakness

** Status**:
- There's a certain level of awareness in the younger user community.
- Large-scale IP events like winter and summer have created a professional evaluation.
- But branding is highly dependent on the tremor ecology.

**The Eternity Assessment**: The core of brand power is "User takes the initiative instead of the contest." The users who know the Autolord are more pushed by the shivering algorithm than by the acceptance of the Autolord's brand to download App. This is not the real brand moat.

** Rating: 3/10**

#3.2 Network effects - embryonic but not solid

** Flying wheel logic**:
```
More creators produce content * More users view * More dealers are stationed
* More deals generate more commission revenue * more input into content ecology
```

** Status**:
- 7.5 million car creators (largest industry)
- DAU 10 million plus (industry first)
- 28,500 dealers ' cooperation (high coverage)
- 57% of businesses are trading.

** But the wheel has a fatal weakness**: the creator of content is mostly active in the voice of the vibrating rather than in the app of the car's owner. The "content" gear of the wheel is actually borrowed. If the creator can publish the content simultaneously at the vibrating, fast hand, and station B, it is weak to understand the owner's monopoly at the content end.

** Rating: 4/10**

##3.3 Conversion costs - low

**Cendant**:
- The cost of downloading another app is almost zero.
- Low cost of migration of user accumulated browsing history, collection records, etc.
- Purchase of vehicles is a low-frequency decision (on average, three to five years) and user loyalty is naturally low

**B end distributor**:
- Under CPS mode, the distributor can switch platforms at any time.
- Multiplate is normal, not exclusive.
- The "Trote" SaaS tool is definitely sticky, but it's not irreplaceable.
- More than 50% of the distributors are in a loss situation and are very sensitive to platform costs

** Rating: 2/10**

##3.4 Data barriers -- medium

** Unique data assets**:
- Structured data over 50 million.
- 76,000 vehicles covered by the garage.
- The database covers 4,000 + vehicle types, with data exceeding 1 million
- The real quotes of millions of active car mates.
- User behavioral data (browsing, comparison, request for quotations)

**Assessment** Data volume is significant, but the key to data barriers is not about the amount, but about the exclusivity and incompetence. Vehicle parameters data are open, other platforms can do them, and the real value is user behavioral data and dealer transaction data. But the value of these data is highly dependent on the size of the user, and if the user loses it, the data is devalued.

** Rating: 5/10**

#3.5 Technical barriers - medium-high (but from the parent body)

** Technological advantage**:
- The recommended algorithm for byte beats is the best in the world.
- The AI model is used in car selection, car comparison, valuation, etc.
- Short video content distribution and understanding techniques
- The Big Bang combines the dim-wilding with the smart-inputing and business tools of the big guy.

** But key questions**: How many of these technical capabilities are self-researched by the Autobot? How many are provided by the bytes? Can these technical capabilities be supported by by by bytes on a continuous basis when they are marketed?

** Rating: 6/10 (if bytes are held) / 3/10 (if technology is only available)**

# 3.6 # The Eongping core three questions

Question 1: Is this a good business?

** Not entirely.** The good business in this long-term business has several characteristics: stable demand, manageable competition, high profitability, light assets. Knows that the driver meets the "stable demand" (people are constantly in need of information) but there are other obvious weaknesses:

- Heated competition: not only in the traditional car house, but also in the fast-hand cars, in the red-book cars, in the channel of the twitching car itself.
- The business model is not "light": the CPS model means that revenues are tied to car turnover, while the Chinese car market is slowing down, prices are fierce, and dealers are losing a lot of money
- Doubts about profitability: there is a need for continuous input into content ecology (500 million cash + 150 billion traffic to support creators), while the CPS model has a ceiling on commission rates

** Question II: Is there a difference?**

** Yes, but the difference is not from understanding the emperor himself, but from byte beat.** The difference in understanding the driver is "byte flow plus algorithm recommended," which is not really available at the car house or car. But it also means that the difference in understanding the driver is "lending" and not being created by himself.

Yongping used to say, "Do nothing you don't know," "Do what the moat is." The moat of the Autobots is more like, "Living in someone else's pool."

** Question III: Do you have pricing rights?**

** There is a certain price right, but it is weakened.** Automotives once called "Ace of A" have a voice in silence with dealers. But the following trends are eroding pricing:

- The company started to build its own direct marketing system (Haw-Hong Mun-Wan has suspended its collaboration with the three main agents)
- Many new energy cars use a straight-line model, and do not need traditional leads.
- More than 50 per cent loss by distributors and reduced affordability of the platform
- The CPS model essentially gives the pricing power to the market (commission rate influenced by transaction prices and competition)

# Five, combined judgment

# 5.1 What does Yongping think of this business?

If you use the text system of the language of the Pongping, you can see that:

** "This business is not good enough."**

The reasons are as follows:

1. ** Not "doing the right thing"**: understanding the core competitiveness of the Autotron is not his own, but the parent. The paragraph of Senghei always emphasizes "doing what you can control," and understanding that the fate of the Autolord depends to a large extent on the byte strategic decision.

2. ** There is no real pricing authority**: The CPS model seems to be advanced (pay-for-effects) but also means that revenue fluctuates with the market.

3. ** The moat is "rented"** If the byte support is removed, the remaining moats of the Autobots -- databases, App users, brand awareness -- do not have an overwhelming advantage over the car house.

4. ** Industrial structure is unfriendly**: Automotives are essentially an "information broker" business, and the value of information intermediaries may be significantly reduced in the AI era. When users can ask AI directly "What car I should buy" and receive personalized advice, the value of the intermediate platform will be diminished.

** Low-frequency consumption + high competition**: Buying a car is a three- to five-year low-frequency consumption, with no loyalty on the part of the user. There are many competitors (car homes, car-free cars, fast-handedness, small red books, individual cars with their own channels) and no pattern of winner-takes-all.

#5.2 Valuation and investment attraction

- Current valuation: approximately $3 billion (2024 financing valuation)
- IPO target: $1 billion to $15 billion, implying a market value of $5-8 billion
- Reference to the logo: car house with a market value of approximately $2.1 billion (United States dollar shares) and a double profit margin

** Risk point**:
- If IPO valuation is set at over $5 billion, compared to the projection of RMB 3-4 billion, P/S is over 10 times more expensive for an information broker
- Byte segmentation may be motivated by capital operating needs rather than by the value of the business itself as independent.
- The downside of the car house (13% net profit in 2024 and double profit in 2025) may be a microcosm of the industry.

# 5.3 Summing up of one sentence

** The rider is a business that's "good rider riding other people's good horses." ** The rider is really good, the horse is strong, but the horse is not his own.

For investors, the core bet that knows the driver is really one: a byte beat will continue to give the driver the flow and commercialization of the car's adrenaline rights, rather than repossessing it to the sound of the tremor. The bet is won by the byte's strategic will, not by the byte's ability.
