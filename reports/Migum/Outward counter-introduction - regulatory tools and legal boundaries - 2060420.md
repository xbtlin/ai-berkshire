#3. Tool-by-tool assessment

Antimonopoly law -- from "choice one" to "market competition investigation."

** Traditional route (abuse of dominance) no longer applicable: US Order 2021 34.42 billion tickets based on the "one-way-one" exclusive agreement (abuse of a single market dominance on a platform for the sale of goods). Three (Americans, treasure-snuggling/hunting, Kyoto) currently compete with each other.** None of them have a single dominant position** - US Order share is about 50%, Ali 42%, Kinton 8% (2026Q1 data) and "abuse of dominance" in antimonopoly laws is difficult to invoke.

** New routes (competition survey + Monopoly agreement) have been launched**: 9 January 2026,** the Office of the State Council Antimonopoly Anti-Aggression Commission conducts a survey and evaluation of the state of competition in the foreign selling platform services sector, in accordance with the Antimonopoly Act.** This is an additional tool for amending the Antimonopoly Act in 2022, which allows for data retrieval, transmission of regulatory pressure, and proposals for disposal measures without having to be found illegal.

** Assessment**: Survey itself is a stress tool and three have begun to cool down.

#B. Anti-Improper Competition Law 2025 Revised (Key Arms but Border Limit)

**Adopted by the Standing Committee of the National People's Congress on 27 June 2025, with effect from 15 October 2025**.

- ** Article 14**: Platform operators** may not impose or disguisedly compel operators within the platform** to sell goods at prices below cost in accordance with their pricing rules, thus disrupting the competitive order of the market. 50.000 - 500,000 fines; aggravating circumstances 500,000 - 2 million.
- ** Article 13**: The use of data, algorithms, platform rules, etc. for fraudulent transactions, malicious return of goods, etc., is prohibited.

** Key limitations**: Article 14 is directed at "Pilot for compulsorily low prices", but the large subsidies in the outlet war are **Pilot for itself to pay consumers** (non-compulsory traders), with limited legal precision. Only when it is ascertained that the Platform requires a share of subsidies for the business. However, the Basic Requirements for the Management of Services in Outer Space Platforms (Advancement Draft), published in September 2025, makes it clear that "**The costs of price promotion for the Platform should not be disguised or directly shared by the business owners**" - this is the law enforcement catch on Article 14.

** Assessment**: The law is in place but the finding of "compulsory assessment of the disguised" requires investigation; 2 million fine caps to trillion revenue platforms** are not sufficiently deterrent**.
