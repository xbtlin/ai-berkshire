# 7.3 What price would Buffett buy?

"The price is what you pay, the value is what you get."

** Barfitt probably won't buy the concierge for the following reasons:**

1. ** The moat is not deep enough.** The heavy losses of 2025 prove that the American Regiment's "net effect" is vulnerable to determined competitors (Kingdong). Buffett wants the business of "making money even if management is mediocrity," which is not.

2. ** The profit predictability is poor.** From the annual earnings of $35.8 billion to the annual losses of $23.4 billion, it took only one year, and this volatility has rendered any DCF model meaningless.

** There is no pricing authority.** Outsourcing is essentially homogenous services - low user loyalty to brands and almost zero switching costs. The Corps relies on enforcement and size, not irreplaceable brands/products.

** If you have to give a price:**

- Buffett-style purchase price = regularized profit × conservative PE x security margin
- = HKD 6.66 EPS x 15x PE x 0.7 discount = **HKD 70**
- That is, to drop about 20% from the current price.

But more likely, Barfithe would say, "I don't know where the moat of this business is, so I choose not to vote." And then I'll go buy more apples or Cokes.

# Core conclusion

"Risk comes from you not knowing what you're doing."

The current valuation of the USS is indeed cheap (PS 1.99x, PB 3.15x, EV/Revenue < 1x), and net cash provides a solid downside protection. But the central bet of the USI is:** when the takeout war will end and how much profit will return to after it.**

- If the profit level in 2024 (35.8 billion) represents a normal profitability, the current stock price implies a huge margin of increase (100 per cent +)
- If competition becomes a new norm, profits are permanently reduced to 10-15 billion, and the current valuation is "reasonable" rather than "favourable"
- If the Kimdong/Shake continues to add up to zero profits in the industry, the Corps will become a value trap

**Buffett: pass, but not in the core circle. This is a "receipt" investment, not a "determinate" investment. It is suggested that small warehouses be involved, waiting for the end of the takeout war to be loaded.**
