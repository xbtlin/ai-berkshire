# Among Douyin, JD.com and rider costs, who is the real threat to Meituan?

> The impact of AI and Douyin on Meituan’s moat · Chapter 02
> Reading time approx. 8 minutes

---

When discussing the threat of Meituan, it is easiest to focus on two names: Douyin and JD.com.

One has traffic and the other has logistics.

The former makes people worry that in-store business will be taken away by content platforms, and the latter makes people worry that the takeout fulfillment network will be rebuilt by new players.

But if you break down the problem, you will find that the real most difficult threat to Meituan is not necessarily from competitors.

Douyin is more like cutting off part of the high gross profit increase.

JD.com is more likely to disrupt the share of food delivery in the short term.

Rider costs and regulations are like the hand that suppresses profit margins for a long time.

---

## Douyin hits the store, the logic is very smooth

Douyin is about local life, not trying to break into it.

Its logic is very natural: users watch a short video, see a store, and feel the urge to buy a coupon.

This is called "finding people with goods".

For low-frequency, impulsive, and experiential consumption, Douyin is very strong.

For example, users have no clear needs for newly opened restaurants, dessert shops, B&Bs, bars, and urban weekend games, but they are willing to give it a try after being attracted by the video content.

Meituan and Dianping are better at another scenario:

Users already have needs and open the app to find reliable options nearby.

This is called "people looking for goods".

| Scene | Douyin is stronger | Meituan is stronger |
|------|----------|----------|
| Acquiring customers for new stores | Content exposure, experts planting grass, short-term explosion | Search traffic may not necessarily go to new stores immediately |
| Impulsive consumption | The video is highly stimulating and suitable for buying coupons to try out | Conversion is more rational |
| Repurchase decision | Content popularity decays quickly | Evaluation, distance, price, and write-off are more stable |
| Merchant operations | Need to shoot videos, find experts, and create content | Putting on packages, placing ads, and making evaluations more standardized |

Therefore, Douyin will indeed cut off Meituan.

Especially the new marketing budget for in-store business and some merchant advertising budgets.

But this does not mean that Douyin can completely replace Meituan’s in-store business.

Just because a user visits a store on Douyin to buy coupons does not mean that he will rely on videos every time he finds a restaurant in the future.

When the need is clear, search, reviews, distance, price, write-off certainty still matter.

The end result is more likely to be coexistence: Douyin takes away part of the grass-growing and customer acquisition, while Meituan maintains repurchase and deterministic transactions.

---

## Why is it difficult to do takeout on Douyin?

Takeaway is not content cultivation.

The demand for takeout is usually very clear:

I'm hungry now.

I want to eat it in about 30 minutes.

I only look at businesses within 3 to 5 kilometers of my location.

I don’t want to decide on lunch over a video.

This is the natural difficulty of Douyin’s takeout service.

It has traffic, but the takeaway real moat is in supply and fulfillment.

| Takeaway barriers | Restrictions on Douyin |
|----------|--------------|
| Delivery network | It requires riders, scheduling, and real-time fulfillment, which cannot be directly solved by recommendation algorithms |
| Merchant Density | There must be enough delivery merchants around each community |
| User Mind | Takeaway is a deterministic demand, not a random demand generated by browsing content |
| After-sales capability | Late arrival, missed delivery, wrong delivery, and refund will all be borne by the platform |

Douyin once tried to build its own food delivery, but later basically turned to the cooperation route.

This shows that it also realizes that takeout is not just a matter of having traffic.

Traffic can guide some orders to Ele.me, but it is difficult to change the fulfillment structure of the food delivery industry.

This is why the phrase “Douyin threatens Meituan” should be looked at by business:

The threat of in-store visits is medium, the threat of take-out is low, and the threat of wine travel is somewhere in between.

---

## Is the threat from JD.com overestimated?

JD.com entered the food delivery business, and the market responded greatly.

The reason is simple: JD.com has a logistics mindset and is willing to invest, and it also plays the rider treatment card very loudly.

But JD Logistics and takeout delivery are not the same thing.

JD.com’s strength is the warehouse distribution model: goods go from the warehouse to the user’s home.

The best thing about takeout is real-time dispatch: the meal is delivered from nearby merchants to the user.

These two capabilities overlap, but cannot be directly transferred.

| Capabilities | JD.com’s traditional strengths | The real needs of food delivery |
|------|--------------|--------------|
| Network form | Warehouses, sorting, trunk lines, terminal distribution | Merchants, riders, and users are matched in real time within the block |
| Order rhythm | Can be planned, sorted, and batched | The afternoon and evening peaks burst out instantly |
| Product status | Standardized products | Hot food, cold drinks, easy to spill, unstable food delivery |
| Fulfillment radius | Warehouse to home | Store to home, usually 3 to 5 kilometers |
| Merchant relations | Brand and supply chain | Local catering merchants, mom-and-pop shops, chain stores |

JD.com can use subsidies to buy orders, and it can also make a name for itself in some cities.

However, food delivery requires an extremely high density of local merchants and rider dispatching experience.

Historically, Baidu Takeout and Didi Takeout have both tried, but neither succeeded in the end.

This does not mean that JD.com will definitely fail.

A more accurate judgment is: JD.com can create short-term share disturbances, but to shake Meituan’s fundamentals in the long term, it needs to prove that it can still retain users, merchants and riders after subsidies are withdrawn.

This proof is not yet complete.

## The most certain threat: rider costs

Meituan’s delivery network is both a moat and a cost network.

This judgment sounds contradictory, but it is the core problem of Meituan.

The larger the delivery network, the stronger Meituan’s ability to fulfill contracts; however, the stricter the rider rights, social security, work-related injury insurance, and algorithm supervision, the harder the platform costs will be.

The original report listed "rigid increase in rider costs" as a threat with high certainty in the medium term. This is a very critical judgment.

There are three reasons.

First, labor security in the gig economy will become increasingly standardized.

Riders are not numbers in the air, but real workers. Social security, work-related injury liability, and algorithm transparency are the long-term directions.

Second, the supply of young labor will tighten.

Demographic changes will make low-cost, flexible labor less abundant.

Third, consumers have limited affordability for price increases.

Takeaway users are very price-sensitive, especially when consumption is weak. Increases in delivery fees and meal prices will directly affect the frequency of orders.

So Meituan was caught in the middle:

The cost side is getting tougher and harder, and it is not easy to raise prices on the revenue side.

This is not a question of a competitor launching a price war, but a question of the profit ceiling of the business model itself.

---

## Is unmanned delivery a benefit, or is it tearing down walls?

Unmanned delivery sounds like good news for Meituan.

If unmanned vehicles and drones can replace some riders on a large scale, the average delivery cost per order will decrease and the efficiency of contract fulfillment will also increase.

But it also has another side.

Today, the barriers to Meituan’s delivery network come from millions of riders, complex scheduling systems and years of local operations.

If future fulfillment infrastructure becomes unmanned fleets and drone networks, the barriers may shift from “people and density” to “capital and technology.”

Of course there are barriers to capital and technology, but they are easier for large companies to catch up with.

| Scenario | Impact on Meituan |
|------|--------------|
| Meituan takes the lead in implementing unmanned delivery | Reduce costs, strengthen contract fulfillment efficiency, and the moat continues to expand |
| Technology has been generalized | JD.com, Alibaba, and new players can purchase or build similar capabilities themselves, and the network barriers for existing riders have dropped |
| Policy opening is slow | Unmanned delivery improvements are limited, and rider costs are still the main pressure |

Therefore, unmanned delivery is not simply a good thing.

It may make Meituan stronger, and it may also transform the distribution network from a "ten-year operating asset" into a "procureable infrastructure."

The time window for this variable could be anywhere from 5 to 10 years.

There is no need to panic in the short term, but it cannot be ignored in the long term.

## Threat ranking

Putting Douyin, JD.com, rider cost, and unmanned delivery together, you can get a clearer ranking.

| Threat | Impact | Time | Judgment |
|------|------|------|------|
| Rider costs and regulations | Compressed margins | Ongoing | The most certain and hardest to get around |
| Douyin to the store | The gross profit increase and merchant budget have increased separately | It has happened | The impact is real, but it is more like coexistence than substitution |
| JD.com | Short-term disturbance share and subsidy intensity | 1 to 2 years | Loud voice, but needs to be verified in the long term |
| Generalization of unmanned delivery | Possible rewriting of performance barriers | 5 to 10 years | High uncertainty, important direction |
| Consumption downgrade | Lower customer price and ability to raise prices | Cyclicality | Will amplify competitive pressure |

The company most likely to be overvalued by the market is JD.com.

What is most easily underestimated by the market is rider cost.

The most easily misunderstood is Douyin.

Douyin is not here to seize the main battlefield of food delivery. It is more like cutting a piece of cake from the profit pool of in-store delivery.

JD.com is not without threats, but it must prove that it can do real-time dispatch, not just warehousing and distribution logistics.

Rider costs may not seem exciting, but they may slightly change Meituan’s long-term profit margins.

---

## Conclusion of this article

Meituan is most afraid of price wars in the short term, cost rigidity in the medium term, and changes in the delivery technology paradigm in the long term.

Douyin will weaken the incremental growth and advertising budget of in-store business, but it is unlikely to directly penetrate takeout.

JD.com will create disruption in the food delivery battlefield, but there is a complete set of local operational capabilities between warehouse logistics and instant delivery.

Rider costs and regulations are the least dramatic and most realistic.

Meituan’s real risk is not necessarily being defeated by someone.

What is more likely is that after it defeated all its competitors, it discovered that the long-term profit margin of this business was not as high as the market imagined.
