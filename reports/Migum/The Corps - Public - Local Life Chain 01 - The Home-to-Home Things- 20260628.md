# The key to all things at home is not traffic, but the distribution network

> Local Life Industry Chain Series · Chapter 01 · Industry Chain
> Reading time approx. 8 minutes

---

In 2025, the size of China's instant retail market will be close to one trillion yuan; in the same year, Meituan's flash sale GMV was approximately 262 billion yuan, and there were more than 50,000 flash warehouses.

The numbers look like a simple consumption story: people are getting lazier, things are getting faster, and platforms are getting bigger.

But if you only understand local life as “takeout” and “group buying”, you will miss the most important part.

The real destiny of this industry is not whose entrance on the App is more conspicuous, nor which company is more ruthless in issuing coupons, but who can organize enough riders, merchants and orders in enough cities and blocks at the same time. **

In other words, the core asset of local life is not traffic, but fulfillment density.

---

## One industrial chain, three types of roles

Local life services seem to be very fragmented: takeout, in-store, wine and travel, buying medicines, vegetables, flowers, convenience stores, and errands.

But if you break it down into industrial chains, the structure is actually very clear.

| Hierarchy | Main Characters | Who Makes the Money Here |
|------|----------|--------------|
| Upstream | Catering, hotels, retail, and life service merchants; food and commodity suppliers; SaaS and payment service providers | Merchants earn operating profits, while SaaS and payment earn tool fees and handling fees |
| Midstream | Platforms such as Meituan, Ele.me, Taobao Flash Sale, JD Daojia, Douyin Local Life, Ctrip and other platforms | Platforms earn commissions, advertising, delivery fees and membership fees |
| Infrastructure | Meituan Delivery, Hummingbird Delivery, Dada, SF Express Intra-city, Flash Delivery | The delivery network charges per order, with low gross profit but high strategic value |
| Downstream | C-side consumers and B-side corporate customers | Users pay delivery fees, service fees, and membership fees, and companies buy group meals and procurement services |

Local life is not just about information matching.

If it’s just to “help you find a nearby store”, Douyin, Xiaohongshu, Amap, and WeChat can all do it. The really hard part is the second half: after placing the order, having it delivered within 30 minutes.

This is why takeout and instant retail are more difficult than ordinary e-commerce.

The fulfillment of e-commerce contracts is "warehouse-to-person", which can be centralized warehousing, sorting, and trunk transportation; the fulfillment of local life is "nearby to you", and each order occurs in a real neighborhood and is affected by weather, road conditions, rider supply, merchant delivery speed, and users' immediate needs.

It is not a national map, but thousands of cities, tens of thousands of business districts, and countless noon and evening peak hours stacked together.

---

## Three neck-stuck links

The original research report breaks down the most critical links in the local life industry chain into three layers.

I would rather summarize it in one sentence:

**The delivery network determines whether it can be delivered, the content in the store determines whether it can be selected, and the takeout platform determines whether it can be continuously monetized. **

### First, logistics distribution network

This is the hardest sticking point.

Meituan has about 7.45 million delivery riders, and its average daily delivery volume reaches tens of millions of orders. Even with the money, it would be difficult for a new platform to replicate the same density immediately.

The reason is not as simple as "cannot recruit riders".

The efficiency of a delivery network comes from three things:

| Variables | Why it matters |
|------|------------|
| Order Density | The denser the orders in the same area, the fewer riders will drive empty and the lower the average order cost |
| Category reuse | The same rider can deliver food, medicine, flowers, and convenience store goods, with a higher utilization rate |
| Scheduling data | Massive order training and dispatching system, the more you run, the better you know which neighborhood, which time period, and which type of merchant are most likely to have problems |

This is not an asset that can be bought with a drop of money.

Money can buy subsidies, ads, and one-time orders from users; but money can hardly buy ten years of block-level operating experience.

### Second, the store platform

In-store business is Meituan’s highest profit margin.

It does not need to send a rider for every order, but more about information matching, advertising and commission. Dianping’s reviews, lists, store information and user insights accumulated over the years are its core assets.

The beauty of this business is that it has low marginal costs and strong advertising monetization.

But it is also the layer most easily challenged by content platforms.

When users look for restaurants, they used to “open Dianping and search”; now more and more people are looking for a restaurant by “finding a restaurant on Douyin”. Search-based decision-making and content-based grass farming are colliding head-on.

This is also the most subtle thing about Meituan: the most profitable business may not be the safest business. **

### Third, takeaway platform

Takeaway is a high-frequency entrance.

The frequency of users ordering takeout is much higher than booking hotels, buying group purchases, and getting medical beauty treatments. Whoever controls the takeout entrance will have the opportunity to direct users to stores, hotels, hotels, medicines, groceries, and flash sales.

But takeout itself is not a particularly lucrative business.

Commissions, advertising and delivery fees can all make money, but once the subsidy war starts, the profits will disappear quickly. Meituan's net profit in 2024 was 35.8 billion yuan, which turned into a net loss of 23.4 billion yuan in 2025, with profit fluctuations of nearly 60 billion yuan before and after.

This shows that food delivery has scale effects, but its pricing power is not stable.

## Why “Trillions of Instant Retail” Is Easily Misjudged

Just-in-time retail is the industry’s most alluring narrative.

The market size will be approximately 650 billion yuan in 2023, approximately 781 billion yuan in 2024, and approximately 971.4 billion yuan in 2025. At this rate, the trillion-dollar scale is just around the corner.

But the most dangerous place in investing is also here.

**Trillion GMV does not equal a trillion profit pool. **

Profit from local living has to go through many layers of filtering:

| Caliber | Meaning | What to be wary of when investing |
|------|------|------------------|
| GMV | The total number of transactions matched by the platform | The number is large, but it does not equal the platform’s revenue |
| Income | Commissions, advertising, delivery fees, self-operated spreads, etc. | You need to see whether subsidies can offset income |
| Gross profit | Profit after deducting direct costs such as fulfillment, goods, services, etc. | Gross profit for distribution and immediate retail is not huge |
| Operating profit | After deducting marketing, R&D, and management expenses | Subsidy war will directly destroy profits |
| Free cash flow | The cash that really stays in the hands of the company | Platform wars are most likely to reveal their truth here |

Therefore, when judging real-time retail, we cannot only look at "whether the scale reaches one trillion yuan."

More crucial are three things:

1. Can the average fulfillment cost per order be reduced?
2. Are merchants and users willing to continue using it without subsidies?
3. Can the platform find a stable profit combination among commissions, advertising, distribution and membership?

If the answer is no, trillions of GMV may just be trillions of turnover.

##Why is this industry so fiercely fought?

Local life is a rare industry: it is high-frequency, localized, and can be extended to multiple consumption scenarios.

For Meituan, food delivery is the entrance, in-store sales are profits, instant retail sales are growth, and overseas Keeta is the second curve.

For Alibaba, Ele.me and Taobao’s flash sales are not just food delivery businesses, but Taobao’s attempt to return to high-frequency consumption scenarios.

For JD.com, takeout and instant retail can improve the utilization of logistics infrastructure and give users more reasons to shop at home.

For Douyin, local life is a natural extension of the commercialization of content traffic.

Every family doesn’t just want to make a small amount of money from takeout. What they are competing for is the next "daily consumption entrance."

This is why the three-party marketing war will burn a total of about 150 billion to 173 billion yuan in 2025.

On the surface, they are competing for a lunch box; in fact, they are competing for which app users will open in the next ten years to solve local needs.

---

## What’s really worth looking at is not who has the most subsidies.

In short-term competition, subsidies are the most conspicuous.

But subsidies are not a moat. Subsidies only temporarily move users to your doorstep.

What is really worth watching is where the orders will remain after the subsidies are reduced.

| Observation Metrics | Why It Matters |
|----------|------------|
| Takeaway GTV share | See if the platform can maintain the transaction scale |
| High customer order share | See if core users are still there after the subsidy is reduced |
| In-store write-off rate | See whether content planting can be converted into real consumption |
| Average delivery order cost | See if the scale effect still exists |
| Operating cash flow | See if the platform war has hurt the balance sheet |
| Merchant’s willingness to advertise | See whether the platform is still the main position for merchants to acquire customers |

Judging by these indicators, Meituan has not yet been penetrated.

Its delivery network is still the densest fulfillment infrastructure in the industry; Dianping still has long-term accumulation of evaluation content; there is still cross-traffic between in-store, takeout, and flash sales.

But it's also not as invincible as it once seemed.

Alibaba and JD.com have proven one thing: the switching costs for users and merchants are very low, and as long as the subsidies are large enough, the share will move.

This is what local life looks like in its truest form: **The fulfillment network is deep and user lock-in is shallow. **

---

## Conclusion of this article

Local life is not a simple "consumer Internet" story.

It is more like a neighborhood-level infrastructure war: there are tens of millions of merchants upstream, and hundreds of millions of consumers downstream. The real-time delivery network in the middle determines whether transactions can actually take place.

For investors, the most important thing is not to ask "Is the trend of everything coming home?" The answer is almost yes.

The real question is:

1. Who can still retain orders without subsidies?
2. Who can turn the fulfillment network into stable profits?
3. Who can continue to cross-monetize between in-store, takeout, and flash sales?
4. Whose cash flow can last until competition cools down?

The certainty of trends is very high, but the certainty of individual stocks is not that high.

This is why the study of Meituan must look at the entire industry chain: Meituan is not a simple food delivery company, it is a fulfillment network covering local consumption in China.

But whether this network is a moat or an increasingly expensive cost network depends on the next article.
