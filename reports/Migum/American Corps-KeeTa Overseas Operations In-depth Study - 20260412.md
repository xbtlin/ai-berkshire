# Three: What is the condition for success?

** Feasibility analysis**:
- Saudi Arabia: HungerStation+Jahez totals 85 per cent, but both are under the Delivery Hero flag - not excluding internal consumption and the financial pressure on Delivery Hero's parent company (declining stock prices after Talabat IPO, and debt to Delivery Hero)

** Probability of success**: Medium (40-50%). Saudi Arabia can win, Talabat market takes more than five years.

Condition 2: Brazil -- must find the cracks in iFood

The iFood's Historical Vulnerabilities:
1. ** Poor coverage of small and medium-sized cities**: iFood dominates large cities with low service density in small and medium-sized cities
2. ** Rideer tension**: iFood rider poorly treated, protests frequent and KeeTa high-paying strategy attractive
3. ** High level of commercial commissions**: 30% iFood commission rate + new entrants can use low commission to peddle traders
4. ** The exclusive possession agreement is being dismantled**: the Brazilian antimonopoly agency, CADE, has investigated the iFood exclusive agreement and has been partially suspended

** But the iFood moat is also very deep **
- 80% of businesses in iFood, users have developed habits
- Logistics infrastructure covering all of Brazil (including rural)
- extended to grocery, financial loans - User sticky is not just takeout

** Conditional**:
- Time-bound run-off in São Paulo core area (e.g. 30 minutes vs iFood 50 minutes)
- Side of the rider: supply advantage through higher pay plus better treatment
- Commercial side: low commission (or prior zero commission) for in-country placement, creation of alternative one
- ** Brazilian regulatory support**: investment agreements signed by the Lula Government and political endorsements have been helpful

** Probability of success**: Low (20-30%, within 5 years). iFood's defence budget of R$3.3 billion indicates that it will not be left to die and has failed (Uber Eats has withdrawn).
