## Question 1: Risks of mandatory social security for riders

**Status: ✅ Researched**

### Will full social security be mandatory?

The current policy path is **progressive** and will not be one size fits all in the short term:

| fact signals | content |
|---------|------|
| Current Path | Occupational Injury Protection Pilot (7 provinces) → Gradually expand coverage, billing per order at a few cents per order |
| The Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security's position | It has repeatedly emphasized "categorized policies" and has never stated its position on "one size fits all" social security |
| Since the release of the 2021 guidance | It has not been upgraded to compulsory full social security in 4 years |
| 200 million people in flexible employment | Policies need to be extremely prudent and priority should be given to maintaining employment |
| JD Takeaway | Only full-time riders (150,000 people) have five insurances and one housing fund, which is incomparable with Meituan’s 7.45 million |
| Meituan’s own measures | Pension insurance subsidies for riders will cover the whole country in October 2025 (the first in the industry) |

### If forced, how much impact will it have on profits?

| Scenario | Annual cost increment | Ratio of adjusted net profit (23.3 billion) in 2023 |
|------|-----------|---------------------------|
| National promotion of occupational injury protection only | 5-11 billion | 21-47% |
| Full-time riders (2.5 million) social security | ~56.7 billion | **243%** |
| Social security + provident fund for full-time riders | ~73.5 billion | **315%** |
| All riders based on actual month | ~84.5 billion | **363%** |

**Key conclusion**: If full social security is mandatory, the cost increase will exceed Meituan’s entire profit. However, the current policy path is "occupational injury protection" (costing about 5 billion to 11 billion), not full social security.

Data sources: Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security policy documents, CICC/Goldman Sachs research reports and calculations, Meituan annual report

## Question 2: Douyin’s impact on Meituan Liquor Travel’s profits

**Status: ✅ Researched**

### Core Data

| Indicators | Meituan to stores | Douyin local life |
|------|---------|------------|
| GTV in 2024 | ~1,000 billion | ~560 billion |
| GTV 2025 | — | ~850 billion (+59%) |
| Write-off rate | 80-90% | 50-60% |
| Actual scale after write-off | 800-900 billion | 425-510 billion |
| Commission rate | ~4.5% | 2.5-3% |

### Quantification of impact on profit dimension

1. **Monetization rate decline**: Meituan’s in-store monetization rate dropped from 9.3% in 2022Q4 to approximately 6% in 2023Q4, a decrease of 3.3 percentage points, resulting in a revenue loss of approximately 23 billion based on 700 billion GTV (partly due to competition from Douyin, and partly due to post-epidemic subsidies)
2. **Profit margin decline**: In-store OPM dropped from 43.3% in 2021 to approximately 34.5% in 2024, a decrease of approximately 9 percentage points, and a profit loss of approximately 5 billion based on 56.6 billion revenue
3. **But the in-store business itself is still profitable**: The core local business loss of 6.9 billion in 2025 is mainly caused by the takeout war, and non-in-store sales are dragged down. Huachuang Securities estimates that in-store operating profits in 2024 will be approximately 19.5 billion

### Douyin slowdown signal

- GTV growth rate in 2025Q4 is 80% → dropped to less than 25% in 2026Q1
- Merchants’ ROI declined, and some merchants reduced their investment
- Douyin itself has shifted from subsidies in exchange for growth to the pursuit of profitability

### Wine and Travel Share

| Ctrip | 56-57% |
| Meituan | 13-15% |
| Tongcheng | 13-15% |
| Flying Pig | ~8% |

Douyin Hotels and Travel’s share is only 3%, its hotel supply is only 10% of Meituan/Ctrip’s, and its contract fulfillment rate is only 30% (Ctrip’s 90%).

## Question 3: Does the state allow Meituan to make a lot of money?

**Status: ✅ Researched**

### Three stages of policy evolution

| Stage | Time | Key Signals |
|------|------|---------|
| Strong supervision | End of 2020-2022 | Antitrust fine of 3.442 billion, requirements for commission reduction, and guidance on rider rights |
| Policy warming | End of 2022-2024 | "Support platform companies to show their talents", "Special rectification completed", anti-monopoly acceptance passed |
| Normalized supervision | 2024-present | Happy to see competition, not intervening in JD.com’s entry into food delivery, and not limiting profit margins |

### KEY FACTS

| Dimensions | Facts |
|------|------|
| Commission rate control | Requires "transparency" and "guided fee reduction", **no hard upper limit** |
| Industry positioning | **Market-oriented services**, not defined as "people's livelihood infrastructure" (different from water and electricity) |
| Profit margin tolerance | Profits will rebound significantly in 2023 (adjusted net profit of 23.3 billion), and the regulator has not issued negative comments |
| Competitive attitude | JD.com will enter the food delivery industry in 2025, and supervision will not intervene, and it is happy to see market competition |
| Comparison with education and training | Education and training are "not for profit", but takeaway** has never been given a similar qualification** |
| Price control | There is currently no price control policy for takeout terminals. There is a commission cap for online ride-hailing but not for takeout |

### Structural constraints (not restricting making money, but increasing costs)

Regulation does not directly limit Meituan’s profit margin, but increases costs through the following channels:
- Rider social security/occupational injury protection → cost increase
- Commission transparency → pressure to reduce fees
- Antitrust compliance → restrict some means of competition

Data sources: State Council policy documents, announcements from the State Administration for Market Regulation, and statements from the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security

## Question 4: Can KeeTa succeed?

**Status: ✅ Researched**

### Competitive landscape of each market

#### Hong Kong (successful)
| Indicators | Data |
|------|------|
| Online time | May 2023 |
| Profitable time | October 2025 (29 months online) |
| Current share | 50%+ (after Deliveroo exits in early 2025) |
| Main rivals | Foodpanda (only one rival left) |
| Profit strategy | High salary for riders → Sufficient supply → Fast delivery → User growth → Merchant follow-up |

#### Middle East (ongoing)
| Country | Entry time | Share | Pattern |
|------|---------|------|------|
| Saudi Arabia | September 2024 | ~10% (3rd place) | HungerStation(Delivery Hero)+Jahez Dominated |
| Qatar | August 2025 | Early Stage | Dominated by Talabat (Delivery Hero) |
| Kuwait | September 2025 | Early Stage | Talabat Dominated |
| UAE | September 2025 | Early | Talabat Dominated (50-70%) |

**Main Rivals in the Middle East**:
- **Talabat** (owned by Delivery Hero): UAE/Qatar/Kuwait share 50-70%, absolutely dominant
- **HungerStation** (owned by Delivery Hero): Saudi Arabia-led
- **Careem** (owned by Uber): Super App, integrating travel + food delivery + payment
- Uber Eats has exited Saudi Arabia (competition failed)

**Middle East Market Data**:
- In November 2025, daily order volume in the Middle East will be approximately 700,000 orders
- Morgan Stanley predicts: the GCC food delivery market will reach US$300 billion in 2028, with an annual growth rate of 15%
- Saudi unit economic benefit: expected to become positive by the end of 2026

#### Brazil (just starting)
| Opponents | Share |
|------|------|
| iFood | 80-86% |
| Rappi | ~9% |
| 99Food (Didi, re-entering in 2025) | New entry |
| KeeTa | New entry (end of 2025) |

- Brazilian market size: 140 billion reais
- Didi invested R$1 billion to restart 99Food during the same period, and the two Chinese companies competed directly
- KeeTa plan: Covering 15 major metropolitan areas in June 2026, covering 1,000 cities in 5 years

### Can the Hong Kong model be replicated?

| Dimensions | Hong Kong | Middle East | Brazil |
|------|------|------|------|
| Competitive Landscape | Two Strongmen→KeeTa+Foodpanda | Delivery Hero has been deeply rooted for many years | iFood has an absolute monopoly |
| Market maturity | High (high APP penetration rate) | Medium (high consumption power but different habits) | Medium (large but dispersed population) |
| Winning rate | Verified ✅ | Medium (Delivery Hero is harder to beat) | Low (iFood86% is solid) |
| Differentiation | Delivery speed + subsidy | Same as above, but Talabat is already strong | Requires huge investment |

**Specialities of Hong Kong’s success**: Hong Kong’s market is small, its density is high, Foodpanda’s service is poor, and its users are sensitive to subsidies—these conditions may not necessarily hold true at the same time in the Middle East/Brazil.

### Overseas business profit timeline

| Market | Expected to become regular |
|------|---------|
| Hong Kong | Profitable (October 2025) |
| Saudi Arabia | Unit economic benefits will turn positive by the end of 2026 |
| Other Middle Eastern Countries | After 2027 |
| Brazil | After 2028 (conservative estimate) |
| Overall Overseas | After 2027 |

Meituan’s management stated that the overall loss of overseas business in 2026 will not exceed that in 2025.

### Applicability of Meituan China’s experience

**Advantages** (migration possible):
1. **Algorithmic Scheduling**: China’s extreme optimization of 90 million orders/day, leading the world in distribution efficiency
2. **Rider Management**: Refined operations, instant feedback system, rider income management
3. **Merchant Operations**: SaaS tools + data analysis, verified in China

**Disadvantages** (difficult to migrate):
1. **Language/Culture**: Arabic and Portuguese operation teams need to be established from scratch
2. **Supervision**: Labor laws, data localization, and the legal status of riders vary greatly between countries.
3. **Payment system**: China’s WeChat/Alipay ecosystem is not applicable and needs to be connected to local payment
4. **Consumption Habits**: Special factors such as Ramadan in the Middle East and Brazilian religious festivals

### Core conclusion

**Short term (3 years)**: The Middle East may copy the Hong Kong model and gain a foothold in 1-2 countries, but Talabat is not Foodpanda, and it is more difficult.

**Long-term (5-10 years)**: Brazil is a real big opportunity (population 210 million), but iFood has a very deep moat, requires a lot of capital + time, and the outcome is uncertain.

**Impact on Meituan’s valuation**: Even if KeeTa successfully accounts for 5% of the Middle East and 10% of Brazil, it is a pure cost center at the current stage. Overseas losses in 2026 will still be a drag, but as a "forward option" the valuation significance is greater than the current profit contribution.

**Analogy**: KeeTa's situation overseas is very similar to Meituan's Hundred Regiments War in China in 2013 - the direction is right, but it will take 3-5 years to see who will win.

Data sources: TechBuzz China, KR Asia, Rest of World, Morgan Stanley forecasts, Meituan earnings conference call
