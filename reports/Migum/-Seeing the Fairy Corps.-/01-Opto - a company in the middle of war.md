## A set of numbers that will make you re-examine Meituan

Open Meituan’s financial reports for 2024 and 2025 and put the key figures together:

| Indicators | 2024 | 2025 | Changes |
|------|---------|---------|------|
| Total revenue | 337.6 billion | 364.9 billion | +8.1% |
| Net profit | **+35.8 billion** | **-23.4 billion** | **Profit fluctuation 59.2 billion** |
| Core Local Business OPM | **+20.9%** | **-2.6%** | -23.5 percentage points |
| Peak daily takeout orders | — | **150 million orders** | All-time high |
| Cash Reserves | — | **166.9 billion** | Stable |
| PS | — | **1.29x** | Lowest since listing |

In one year, profits went from plus 35.8 billion to minus 23.4 billion — nearly $60 billion in profits evaporated. PS fell to 1.29 times, the cheapest moment since Meituan went public in 2018.

But another set of figures tells you the opposite: the daily peak orders for takeaways are 150 million, a record high, and the cash reserves on hand are 166.9 billion (cash 106.8 billion + short-term financial management 60.1 billion), which is enough to fight for another 3-4 years.

**There is only one core contradiction: profits have been wiped out, but the business itself has not deteriorated. **

To understand Meituan, you must first understand the three most widespread misconceptions.

---

## Three common misconceptions about Meituan

### Illusion 1: "Meituan's moat has been breached"

This is what bearish people like to say. Alibaba’s local life share will hit from 27% in 2023 to about 42% in 2025, and Meituan will be squeezed from about 75% to about 50% – it does look like half of the city wall has been knocked down.

But if you only look at share percentage, you'll miss another key fact:

| Indicators | 2023 | 2025 | Changes |
|------|---------|---------|------|
| Meituan food delivery market share | ~75% | ~50% | Decline |
| Meituan’s average daily orders (estimated) | ~78 million | Over 90 million | **Rise** |
| Daily peak orders | — | 150 million orders | All-time high |
| The total size of the food delivery market | Rapidly expanding | Continue to expand | — |

The share dropped from 75% to 50%, but the average daily order volume increased from about 78 million to over 90 million (confirmed by Meituan in June 2025).

This is not "the moat has been breached" - this is the pie growing, with more people coming to share the pie. Meituan’s absolute volume is rising, but it’s just not rising as fast as the subsidies injected by its competitors.

Analogy: A person occupies 75 square meters in a room of 100 square meters. Now the room has been expanded to 200 square meters, and he occupies 100 square meters - the share has dropped from 75% to 50%, but the area he owns has increased.

**Share decline and absolute volume growth occur simultaneously - these two things taken together give a complete picture. **

### Illusion 2: "Takeaway is an unprofitable business"

Meituan lost 23.4 billion in 2025, and core local business OPM plummeted from +20.9% to -2.6%. Many people have concluded that the food delivery business is inherently unprofitable.

This conclusion is based on cause and effect.

Let’s first look at the data in 2024 (when there is no war):

| Indicators | 2024 |
|------|---------|
| Core local business revenue | 250.2 billion |
| Core local business operating profit | **52.4 billion** |
| Core Local Business OPM | **+20.9%** |

52.4 billion operating profit, 20.9% profit margin - this is not like a "business that can't make money."

Let’s look at the reasons why profits will disappear in 2025:

| Expenses | 2024 (estimate) | 2025 (estimate) | Changes |
|--------|-------------|-------------|------|
| Sales and marketing expenses | 64 billion | 102.9 billion | **+60.9%** |
| Revenue growth | — | — | +8.1% |

Sales expenses surged by 61%, while revenue increased by only 8% - the extra 38.9 billion was basically subsidies and marketing. Delivery service revenue even fell 2% year-on-year (order volume was rising) because subsidies were directly deducted from delivery revenue.

**The problem is not that business cannot make money, but that war cannot make money. ** The profit of 52.4 billion in 2024 proves the profitability of this business; the loss of 6.9 billion in 2025 shows the cost of competition, not the flaws of the business model.

Of course, we should also see the flip side: if competition comes, all profits must be spit out, which means that pricing power is limited - this is indeed a reflection of Meituan's "wide but shallow" moat.

### Illusion 3: "Ali has already won"

Alibaba’s local life share has increased from 27% to 42%, which looks like a strong offensive. But let’s take a look at the “battle” between the two sides:

| Comparison Dimensions | Meituan | Ali Local Life |
|---------|------|------------|
| Share 2025 | ~50% | ~42% |
| High-value orders (more than 30 yuan) share | **70%+** (estimated by the seller) | A large number of low-price subsidy orders |
| Profit and loss in 2025 | Loss of 23.4 billion | Losses continue to expand (cumulative loss of 230 billion+) |
| Core logic | Exchange profits for share stability | Exchange huge subsidies for share growth |

What is the price for Alibaba to gain 42% share? Cumulative losses exceeded 230 billion—nearly 10 times Meituan’s annual losses. Among this 42% share, a large number of orders come from users attracted by low-price subsidies. Once the subsidies are reduced, the retention rate will be questionable.

Meituan holds more than 70% of high-value orders above 30 yuan (estimated by the seller, unofficial data). This means that the core user group with stronger willingness to pay and higher repurchase rate is still in the hands of Meituan.

**But this does not mean that Ali will definitely lose - at least Ali himself thinks so. ** The core narrative supporting Alibaba’s continued investment is the “synergy effect”: Ele.me + AutoNavi + Taobao Daojia form a local life matrix, using high-frequency takeaway traffic to feed back the Taobao main website. If this logic holds true, the 230 billion loss is not a pure war of attrition, but an investment in customer acquisition at the group level.

But there is a fatal problem with this narrative: It has been told for many years and has never been realized in the financial data. ** It has been 7 years since Ele.me merged with Alibaba. The organizational structure of Koubei, AutoNavi, and Taobao Daojia has been adjusted repeatedly. The "synergy effect" has always remained at the level of strategic stories and has not been reflected in measurable improvements in customer acquisition efficiency or cross-business penetration.

From the perspective of business laws, this is a decisive issue: if the synergy effect does not hold, Alibaba Local Life will be a sector that continues to lose money and cannot be "supported" by other businesses of the parent company. The new management led by Tsai Chongxin and Wu Yongming cares more about profits and capital discipline than before. As they continue to inject blood into a business with no visible path to profitability, internal resistance will only grow. **For a business that has been losing money and has no substantial synergy with its main business, according to business rules, the winner has been decided - the only thing left is how long it will take for it to end decently. **

Of course, we must also see the flip side: Even if the synergy is doubtful, Alibaba may continue to invest for the strategic defense purpose of "not allowing Meituan to monopolize local life." This is not economic rationality, but an industrial game. In this case, there may be a long time between "the victory is decided" and "the war is over".

The core question is not "who wins", but whether Ali's collaborative narrative can be fulfilled - this determines whether there is "still reason to fight" or "just one step down".

## So why is there no profit? ——A story of four quarters

Profits in 2025 will not disappear at a uniform speed, but will have a clear collapse timeline:

| Quarterly | Core local business operating profit (billion yuan) | What happened |
|------|---------------------------|----------|
| Q1 | **+135** | Jingdong Waimai has not yet launched a full-scale offensive, the last good days |
| Q2 | **+37** | Subsidy war begins, profits plummet 75.6% |
| Q3 | **-141** | The darkest moment, a huge loss in a single quarter |
| Q4 | **-100** | Losses narrowed 29% quarter-on-quarter, showing signs of stabilization |

It only took two quarters to go from +13.5 billion in Q1 to -14.1 billion in Q3. But Q4 losses narrowed to 10 billion -** management said the loss reduction trend will continue, which is the most important marginal signal at present. **

There will still be losses in Q1 of 2026 (management has given clear guidance), but whether it can reduce losses or even return to profitability throughout the year is the core variable that determines whether Meituan's current valuation can be repaired.
