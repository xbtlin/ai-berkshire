# 8.1 The methodological value of "segregation-depressive"

The first and two-time "scraper-reducing" of the Quest offers a classic methodology for major shareholders to withdraw:
- ** Volatilization**: decisions to transform concentrated sales pressure into decentralized decisions of hundreds of thousands of shareholders with minimal impact on single-day markets;
- ** Maximizing shareholder interest**: discounts are often given in comparison to large reductions in ownership, whereby the shareholdings are given directly to the shareholders in the company that is invested at market prices;
- ** Regulatory signal is clear**: exit in the name of red, compliance is good and does not trigger negative interpretation of "depressive".

** Apocalypse to investors**: When large institutional shareholders choose "send" rather than "sale" markets, it is a more moderate exit sign, short-term shocks are manageable and the shares of the distributors may not necessarily be sold.

# 8.2 "Popportunity Shareholders" Risk: Take Prosus, for example

Prosus, as the largest shareholder in the communications, received passively some 261 million shares of the United States dollar, but Prosus' own portfolio strategy did not require the United States dollar, and therefore chose to sell it on a continuous basis — creating a “bond-style” pressure: to keep Prosus on the US, and to reduce the US.

** Apocalypse**: Focus on the equity structure of a company to identify which shareholders are "active holders" (stockholdings for business recognition) and which are "passive holders" (passive acquisitions for other reasons), which are the source of hidden sales pressures.
