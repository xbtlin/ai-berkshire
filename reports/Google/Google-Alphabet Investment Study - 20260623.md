# Step 2: Business nature analysis -- a piece of "right business"

# One word of definition

**Google is the essence of the portal to human attention - to bring the largest user attention in the world through free products (search, YouTube, Android, Gmail) and then to make it happen with accurate advertising; and to export accumulated technical capabilities (AI/Cloud computing) to the service of enterprises**.

# Business model canvass

- ** Core engine: Advertising** (about 75% of revenue) - Users search, look at YouTube, use Android, Google to make their attention come out through precision advertising. This is a typical bilateral platform: the more users <unk> The more the advertisers put in the data <unk> The more accurate the user experience.
- ** Engine of growth: Claude** (about 12% of income, 63% increase) - export AI infrastructure capacity to business clients, extending from consumer Internet to business-level services.
- **Engine of the future: AI** (Gemini 650 million months) - both defensive search for the moat and new scenery.
- ** options assets: Waymo** (valued $12.6 billion) - Autonomous drivers leading the way, but still in large-scale investment periods.
- ** Cash cow: YouTube subscription + Google One** (19% increase) - extending from purely advertising to subscription mode.

# Profitability analysis

The Māori rate has steadily increased from 55.4 per cent in 2022 to 59.7 per cent in 2025, and the operating profit rate has increased significantly from 26.5 per cent to 39.4 per cent, which suggests that the economies of scale of Google are still being released. The 15 per cent increase in income has been accompanied by a 32 per cent increase in profits and significant leverage.

But there is a concern: **$18-190 billion 2026 Capex**. This means that Google spends nearly 1.4 times its net profit annually. If AI invests less than expected, the profit margin will be under severe pressure.

# A piece of eternally tangled questions

** Where's the business? If there's only one word to describe it, what?**

Google has the largest "Focus of Attention" in the world -- billions of searches, billions of hours of YouTube viewing, 3 billion Android devices. Control of the entrance to this funnel is at the heart of the business. The question is: is the funnel wide in the AI era or is it bypassed?

# Barfith-style questioning

** Is this moat still in place in 10 years? What can destroy it?**

The search for the ads is probably still on the moat, but the pattern may change -- from "Adverts on the search results page" to "Recommends in the AI Dialogue." The scene that destroys the moat is that a completely different paradigm of information acquisition makes search unnecessary. For now, AI dialogue searches are more like an evolution than a substitute for search -- users still need to click, buy, access websites after they get answers from AI, which are all of Google's realizations.

The real danger is that the moat will be weakened if Apple or other equipment plants trade their own AI assistants to replace Google search as the default entry, and antimonopoly judgements prohibit Google ' s default search protocol.

The most likely error in judgement is that AI is undervalued for the destructive effects of searching for advertising economic models. # Simulation masters say

> ** Business model perspective: ** Google is a small group of companies that actually own "user-free" products. Searching, YouTube, Android -- these three products cover the most core needs of human information acquisition, recreational consumption, mobile devices. But Capex at $180B is disturbing me -- good business should not require 1.4 times the annual profit to maintain competitiveness. If AI turns search business from "light asset-printing machine" to "hard asset infrastructure," the nature of business changes.

> ** Inverse reflection:** Conversely, ** think — in what case Google loses? Not technological backwardness (Google technology first), not managerial failure (Pichai good enough), **, structural changes in industry have worsened the unit economy of the business model searching for advertising. ** Each AI search cost 10-100 times the traditional search, but advertising income may be lower (because users directly get answers and do not click on the advertisement). Google may win the AI competition but lose the search economics.

> ** Management and Values Perspective:** Pichai was the most impressive decision-making in 2019 to retain Deepmind -- when Hassanis wanted to go out independently, and if released, Google would be in a very different position in the AI field today. But the two-tier equity structure of the founder, 52.7% of voting rights, warned me that it was not the best structure for the buyer, because the interests of the "man" (the founder) were not exactly the same as me (the average shareholder).

> ** Civilization perspective:** From a 20-year perspective, Google controls the three most important digital portals of human civilization: information access (search), visual consumption (Youtube), mobile computing (Android). AI will not eliminate these portals, but will only change their shape. The key question is: Can Google maintain a "toll station" position in the transformation of shape? If AI searches to turn information access into "dialogue" rather than "list" , the way the advertising is embedded and the realization efficiency needs to be re-tested.
