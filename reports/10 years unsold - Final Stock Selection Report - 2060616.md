## Detailed Assessment of the Top 5

### 1. Tencent (700.HK) — Confirmed for Selection

**10-year certainty: extremely high**

| Dimension | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| Moat direction | Widening: WeChat's 1.43 billion-user social graph is irreplaceable, and AI is more likely to strengthen WeChat than replace it |
| Management | Capital allocation has shifted from "poor" to "excellent": HK$80 billion in annual buybacks + exits from JD.com and Meituan investments + focus on AI |
| Biggest risk | Pony Ma succession issue (age 55, age 65 in 10 years); structural discount on Chinese ADRs/H-shares |
| 10-year return | Bull case 22% / base case 14-15% / bear case 6-7%; probability-weighted ~13% |

### 2. Mastercard (MA) — Confirmed for Selection

**10-year certainty: extremely high**

| Dimension | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| Moat direction | Widening: global payment network effects + value-added services (41% of revenue, 23% growth), forming a dual engine |
| Biggest risk has been defused | CBDC threat disproved (China has abandoned the digital cash path); UPI mainly affects domestic small-ticket payments, while the cross-border moat remains intact |
| Antitrust | $38 billion settlement has received preliminary approval; rate caps are manageable and offset by value-added services |
| 10-year return | Bull case 14.7% / base case 10.4% / bear case 4.2%; probability-weighted ~10% |

### 3. Kweichow Moutai (600519) — Confirmed for Selection (Conditional)

**10-year certainty: high**

| Dimension | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| Moat direction | Stable: cultural monopoly + pricing power; Feitian wholesale price (RMB 1,640) still exceeds retail price (RMB 1,539) |
| Consumption-tax risk | Reform would affect profits by 7-17%, but Moutai can pass part of the burden on to consumers |
| Management | Four chairmen in five years is a common state-owned-enterprise problem, but the fact that "anyone can run it well" precisely shows that the moat lies in the product, not management |
| 10-year return | Bull case 9.7% / neutral case 5.4% / bear case 1.1%. **Conditional: wait for PE to fall below 17x before buying** |

### 4. Progressive (PGR) — Confirmed for Selection (Conditional)

**10-year certainty: high**

| Dimension | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| Moat direction | Stable to widening: data pricing flywheel + 40% ROE + 40 years of underwriting discipline |
| Autonomous driving | Penetration at most 20-30% within 10 years; market shrinks 10-15%, giving Progressive time to transform |
| Cyclicality | Current 87% loss ratio is a hard-cycle outcome; after normalization it should return to 92-94%, and EPS may fall from 19 to 14-16 |
| 10-year return | Bull case 15% / neutral case 6.7% / bear case 0.5%; probability-weighted ~7%. **Conditional: monitor whether the loss ratio stays above 96% for consecutive periods** |

### 5. Booking (BKNG) — Selected After Reversal Review (Replacing NetEase)

**10-year certainty: medium-high**

| Dimension | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| Moat direction | Stable: supply-side aggregation of 28 million listings + global payment and compliance infrastructure, which AI cannot replicate quickly |
| AI threat | Over-priced by the market: Booking's moat is on the supply side (hotel integration), not the demand side (search) |
| Management | Extremely disciplined: consistent buybacks, no foolish acquisitions, steadily improving margins |
| 10-year return | 21x PE is near a historical low (10-year median 31.6x); base-case annualized return of 12-15% |

## Shared Traits of the Top 5

1. **Moats widen over time instead of narrowing** — social graph (Tencent), payment network (MA), cultural monopoly (Kweichow Moutai), data flywheel (PGR), supply aggregation (BKNG)
2. **No money lost even in the bear case** — each company still produces a positive 10-year return under the worst assumptions
3. **Not dependent on a single technology, and hard to disrupt** — moats come from user habits, culture, and institutional lock-in
4. **Management can "win without being that brilliant"** — the business itself is good enough and does not require a genius CEO to sustain it
5. **Geographic diversification** — 2 China companies + 3 U.S. companies, naturally hedging geopolitical risk
