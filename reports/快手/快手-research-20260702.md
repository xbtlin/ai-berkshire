## 2. Business Model

### One-line definition

**Kuaishou is China's second-largest short-video platform. It monetizes 400 million daily active users through ads, live-stream tipping, and e-commerce, while incubating Kling, a globally leading AI video generation tool.**

### Revenue breakdown

**Full-year 2025 (total revenue RMB 142.776 billion, +12.5%):**

| Segment | Revenue (RMB bn) | Mix | YoY growth | Trend |
|----------|------------|------|---------|------|
| Online marketing (ads) | 81.462 | 57.1% | +12.5% | Core engine, efficiency gains from AI |
| Live-stream tipping | 39.087 | 27.4% | +5.5% | Already in structural decline |
| Other services (e-commerce + AI) | 22.227 | 15.5% | +27.6% | Dual driver from e-commerce commissions and Kling AI |

**Q1 2026 (total revenue RMB 33.7 billion, +3.4%) - growth cliff:**

| Segment | Revenue (RMB bn) | Mix | YoY growth | Alert |
|----------|------------|------|---------|------|
| Online marketing | 19.6 | 58.3% | +9.3% | Slowing, but still growing |
| Live-stream tipping | 8.5 | 25.2% | **-13.5%** | ⚠️ Accelerating decline |
| Other services | 5.6 | 16.5% | +15.9% | Kling AI contributes >RMB 650 million |

**Core trend**: ads are still the growth engine, but growth slowed from 12.5% to 9.3%. Live-stream tipping flipped from slight growth to double-digit decline and is now the biggest drag. Within other services, Kling AI is becoming the largest marginal contributor.

Source: Kuaishou 2025 annual report, Q1 2026 quarterly report, Sina Finance, Securities Times

### Operating leverage

Gross margin improved by 13 percentage points over five years (42% -> 55%), showing that revenue growth does not require proportional cost growth.

**But on the other hand**: gross margin reversed and fell 3.8 percentage points in Q1 2026 to 51.2%. That is the direct cost of AI compute spending. If Kling AI cannot become profitable quickly, operating leverage will flip from positive to negative.

**Question**: what is actually good about this business?

Kuaishou's core business is "selling ads and goods with the attention of 400 million people spending about two hours a day." Attention is valuable, but Kuaishou faces two structural issues: (1) Douyin monetizes the same attention more efficiently - Douyin's ad revenue is RMB 360.1 billion vs. Kuaishou's RMB 81.5 billion, so the efficiency gap is roughly 4x; and (2) lower-tier-market users have a relatively low ARPU ceiling, which limits the upside per user.

## 3. Moat Assessment

### Five moat tests

| Moat type | Strength | Assessment |
|-----------|------|------|
| **Brand / pricing power** | **Weak** | Ad CPM is lower than Douyin's, and e-commerce basket size is driven mainly by low-price white-label goods. Kuaishou cannot raise prices without losing customers. The brand is tied to the "down-market" and "low-brow" labels, so brand premium is limited |
| **Switching costs** | **Moderately weak** | Consumers have zero switching cost (they can use Kuaishou and Douyin at the same time). Creators face some switching cost from fan relationships and private traffic built on Kuaishou, but top creators run multi-platform operations. Merchants can list on multiple platforms |
| **Network effects** | **Moderate** | There is a two-sided "creator -> content -> user -> creator" network effect, but Douyin offers similar or even stronger network effects thanks to a much larger user base. Compared with WeChat's powerful social network effects, Kuaishou's are weaker |
| **Scale effects** | **Moderate** | Behavioral data from 400 million DAU provides massive training samples for recommendation algorithms, which is hard for newcomers to copy. But Douyin has 700 million+ DAU, so its scale effect is stronger. Kuaishou's scale advantage only matters against new entrants, not against Douyin |
| **Technology barrier** | **Moderately strong** | Kling AI is a globally leading AI video generation product (~30% market share), with ARR of about US$500 million. But video generation technology evolves extremely quickly, and competitors like ByteDance's Seedance are closing in, so the lead may not last |

### Moat trend

**Over the past five years**: the moat has narrowed overall. User growth slowed (DAU stalled after rising from 300 million to 400 million), e-commerce GMV growth fell from 78% to 15%, and the ad revenue gap vs. Douyin widened. Kling AI is the only dimension that has widened.

**Five-year outlook**: it depends on whether Kling AI can build a technology barrier independent of short-video platforms. If Kling becomes the "standard tool" in AI video generation, similar to Adobe's position in creative software, the moat would widen materially. Otherwise, the core business moat will keep shrinking.

**Question**: will this moat still exist in 10 years, and what could destroy it?

Kuaishou's core moat - the 400 million DAU lower-tier-market user base - will probably still exist in 10 years, but monetization efficiency could lag Douyin even further. The real risk is not that Kuaishou "goes bust," but that it becomes a living company that is stagnant and continuously de-rated (see Weibo). Kling AI is the only variable that can break that fate.

## 4. Contrarian Thinking and Risk List

### Failure paths

| Failure path | Probability | Impact | Notes |
|---------|------|---------|------|
| **"Forever number two" slow bleed** | High | Extremely high | Douyin dominates Kuaishou across watch time (50.8%), ad revenue (4-5x), and e-commerce GMV (2x). The Weibo precedent shows that the runner-up in a duopoly can be continuously marginalized. Market cap fell from US$30 billion to US$3 billion |
| **AI bet fails** | Medium | Extremely high | Kuaishou is putting RMB 26 billion of Capex into AI, but Kling's 2025 revenue is only RMB 1.04 billion. If the AI video market ends up smaller than expected (only US$700 million globally), or if competitors catch up, the investment turns directly into lower profits |
| **Core growth goes to zero** | High (already happening) | High | Q1 2026 revenue growth was only +3.4%, and live streaming was -13.5%. If ad growth also falls to low single digits, the company loses its growth narrative and the valuation could compress further |
| **Parent-company hollowing after Kling spin-off** | Medium | High | If Kling is Kuaishou's most valuable asset (rumored at US$20 billion vs. Kuaishou's total value of US$23.2 billion), then the core business becomes far less investable after a spin-off |
| **Regulatory gray rhino** | Medium | Medium-high | RMB 119.1 million fine in December 2025. Live-stream tipping limits, algorithm regulation, and youth protection rules continue to tighten |
| **Technical talent exodus hurts competitiveness** | Medium-high | Medium | More than 10 VP-level technical leaders left in 2024-2025, including leaders in large models, recommendation architecture, and audio/video systems |

### Historical analogs

| Comparable company | Similarity | What happened next | Lesson for Kuaishou |
|---------|--------|---------|------------|
| **Weibo** | China's number two social media company, deeply rooted in lower-tier markets, tried short video, e-commerce, and live-stream selling | Under pressure from WeChat and Douyin, user growth stagnated and market cap fell from US$30 billion to below US$3 billion | The runner-up in a duopoly can be marginalized for a very long time |
| **Momo / Hello Group** | Live streaming is the core revenue source, with 180 million users | 2024 revenue fell 12% and both active users and revenue declined. Live-stream cash cows are hit hard by short-video platforms | Live-stream tipping has a limited lifecycle |
| **YY / JOYY** | A pioneer in show-style live streaming | Its domestic business was marginalized by short-video platforms and was eventually sold to Baidu | When a new paradigm emerges, the old platform has little defense |

**But on the other hand**: Kuaishou differs from Weibo, Momo, and YY in one fundamental way - it has Kling AI as a "second growth curve." Weibo and Momo never successfully incubated a high-value product independent of their core businesses. Kling makes Kuaishou's story more than just "an old platform in decline."

### Cross-disciplinary analysis

- **Technology adoption curve**: Kuaishou's core business (short video / live streaming) has passed the steep growth phase of the S-curve and is now in the plateau stage. Kling AI is still in the early adoption stage
- **Competitive game theory**: Kuaishou vs. Douyin is a classic asymmetric duopoly - Kuaishou is only about 30-50% of Douyin in every dimension (users, ads, e-commerce), and it lacks a path to reverse the gap
- **Network effect theory**: Kuaishou's network effects are weaker than Douyin's (smaller user base -> fewer creators -> lower recommendation accuracy -> lower ad efficiency), creating a self-reinforcing negative gap

### Bias self-check

- **Narrative bias**: it is easy to get seduced by the "US$20 billion Kling valuation" story and ignore the fact that the core business is still slowing
- **Anchoring**: Kuaishou fell from the IPO price of HKD 115 to HKD 41.6, which makes it easy to think "it is already cheap." But if core growth goes to zero, the stock may not actually be cheap
- **Survivorship bias**: using the past success of "cost cutting and efficiency gains" to assume that the AI bet will also succeed. These are fundamentally different problems

**Question**: why wouldn't smart people buy or short Kuaishou?

1. All three core legs are slowing - DAU growth is 2%, e-commerce GMV growth fell from 15% to 12.9%, and live streaming is -13.5%. Nothing is accelerating
2. RMB 26 billion of Capex vs. RMB 1 billion of AI revenue means a 1:26 input-output ratio. The global AI video generation market is only US$700 million, so even a 30% share would be only US$200 million
3. The "sacrifice profit for AI investment" strategy is unpopular in the 2026 capital-markets environment - investors want profit, not stories
4. The US$20 billion Kling valuation is still unproven - pre-IPO rounds often carry a 20-40% premium, and whether the public market will accept it remains uncertain

Cheng Yixiao is the soul of Kuaishou - he stays close to frontline operations and drives product direction personally. Su Hua has already effectively exited. If Cheng leaves, Kuaishou would face a serious leadership vacuum. Combined with the loss of technical managers, organizational resilience is a concern. But on the other hand, Kling AI head Gai Kun is rising - if the spin-off succeeds, he could become the next leader inside the Kuaishou ecosystem.

## 6. Industry and Civilizational Trends

### Paradigm shift judgment

**Short video**: not a civilizational paradigm shift, but rather a mature-stage product of the previous mobile-internet revolution. China has 1.04 billion short-video users (93.8% penetration), which means the category is already at the ceiling. The industry's focus is shifting from fighting for traffic to fighting for "retention."

**AI video generation**: **possibly an early signal of a civilizational paradigm shift.** Analogy:
- Steam engine -> electricity -> internet -> mobile internet -> **AI-generated content**
- If AI video generation makes it possible for "everyone to create movie-grade content," its impact could be analogous to "the internet letting everyone publish text"

**But on the other hand**: the global AI video generation market is only US$700 million in 2025, and the forecast for 2032 is only US$2.56 billion. Is that market large enough to support a "civilizational paradigm shift" story? Or is it merely a niche tool market? The key variable is whether AI video generation can expand from a "creative tool" into "content infrastructure."

### TAM analysis

| Market | Current size | Growth / forecast |
|------|---------|---------|
| China's short-video industry | RMB 1.22 trillion (2024 online audio-visual) | CAGR 15.6% to 2030 |
| Live-stream e-commerce | ~RMB 5 trillion GMV (2025) | Growth slows from 60% to 10-12% (2026E) |
| Local life services | ~RMB 35 trillion (including offline) | Online penetration still early (dine-in restaurants: 8.9%) |
| AI video generation (global) | US$700 million (2025) | CAGR 20% to ~US$2.6 billion by 2032 |
| China internet advertising | Trillion-yuan scale | Top 10 players hold 96.0% share |

Source: Forward Industry Research Institute, BXTData, QuestMobile

### Value chain position

Kuaishou sits in the industry value chain as a **traffic hub / attention platform** - connecting creators, consumers, advertisers, and e-commerce merchants. The value of that position depends on (1) user scale and time spent, and (2) monetization efficiency. Kuaishou currently lags Douyin on both dimensions.

Kling AI has a different role - it is an **AI infrastructure / tool provider** that charges enterprises and professional users directly. The value of that position depends on technology leadership and user lock-in.

### Customer / supplier concentration

- **Advertisers**: diversified, with no single advertiser contributing more than 10% of revenue
- **E-commerce merchants**: de-concentration is underway (top-streamer GMV share fell from the peak to 10.66%), but concentration risk still exists
- **Kling AI**: 75% of revenue comes from overseas, and the US contributes 46.1% of downloads - geopolitical risk cannot be ignored

**Question**: looking back 20 years from now, will Kuaishou be "the Standard Oil of this era" or "a fleeting 3Com"?

Kuaishou's core business is more likely to be the latter - a platform that prospered in a specific stage of the mobile-internet era, but whose moat was not deep enough and whose efficiency was not high enough, so the industry leader (Douyin / ByteDance) eventually pulled away. Kling AI has a chance to rewrite that ending, but it is still too early to judge. Twenty years from now, Kuaishou will probably still exist, but it may no longer be exciting - unless Kling AI proves itself.

Current P/E of 9.7x is in the **0-10% percentile** since listing (historical low zone). P/S of 1.1x is also at a historical low.

**But on the other hand**: when Kuaishou listed in 2021 it was loss-making (no P/E). When it first became profitable in 2023, the P/E was around 24x. The compression from 24x to 9.7x does not reflect "cheap valuation"; it reflects a continuous market downgrade of the growth outlook. If profits really fall 16% in 2026 (Nomura's forecast), forward P/E would rise back to about 11.5x - not especially cheap.

**Question**: if the market shut down for the next five years, would you still want to hold this stock at this price?

Buying Kuaishou at HKD 41.6 rests on two core arguments: (1) HKD 96.5 billion of net cash (54% of market cap) provides a hard floor; and (2) if Kling AI spins out successfully, value creation could materially lift the parent company's valuation. The risk is that core business growth could keep slowing over the next five years (live streaming shrinking and ad growth falling to low single digits) while the annual AI investment of RMB 26 billion keeps eating into cash.

**Conclusion**: at HKD 41.6, Kuaishou looks more like a "cash pile with a free Kling option." You do not need to believe the core business will reaccelerate - you only need to believe that (1) the net cash will not be wasted, and (2) Kling AI has a decent chance of succeeding.

## 9. 5-Year / 10-Year Outlook

### A. Kuaishou core business (excluding Kling)

#### Ads: the only leg still growing

Ad revenue was RMB 81.5 billion in 2025 (+12.5%), then slowed to +9.3% in Q1 2026. Key variables:

| Driver | Direction | Quantified impact |
|---------|------|---------|
| AI improves ad efficiency (AIGC creatives + smart bidding) | Positive | Already contributed about 5 percentage points of revenue growth |
| Internal e-commerce ads (merchant traffic buying) | Positive | Grows with e-commerce GMV |
| Brand advertisers shifting to short video | Positive but limited | Kuaishou CPM is below Douyin's, and brand advertisers prefer Douyin |
| Slowing overall growth in China internet advertising | Negative | Industry growth slowed from 15% to 5-8% |
| Douyin competitive pressure | Negative | Douyin's ad revenue is 4-5x Kuaishou's, and the gap is still widening |

**5-year forecast**: ad revenue rises from RMB 81.5 billion to RMB 90-110 billion (CAGR 2-6%), with growth slowing to low single digits year by year. AI-driven ad efficiency is the biggest positive variable, but the ceiling depends on whether DAU can stay above 400 million.

#### Live-stream tipping: structural decline

Live-stream revenue was RMB 39.1 billion in 2025 (+5.5%), then plunged to -13.5% in Q1 2026. Global reference points:

- China's pure tipping platforms (Douyu, Huya) have annual declines of 20-35%
- Kuaishou's live-stream revenue share has fallen from 56.5% (2020) to 25.2% (Q1 2026)

**5-year forecast**: live-stream revenue shrinks from RMB 39.1 billion to RMB 20-28 billion (annualized -7% to -12%), with revenue share falling to 15-18%. Regulation (tipping caps and youth protection) will accelerate the decline. **In 10 years**, live-stream tipping could shrink to RMB 10-15 billion and become a fringe business.

#### E-commerce: slower growth, but still room to run

GMV reached RMB 1.6 trillion in 2025 (+15%), but growth has kept slowing from 78% in 2021. GMV will no longer be disclosed starting in 2026.

| Dimension | Current | 5-year forecast |
|------|------|---------|
| GMV | RMB 1.6 trillion | RMB 2.0-2.5 trillion (CAGR 5-9%) |
| Monetization rate | 3.5-4% | 4.5-5.5% |
| E-commerce-related revenue | ~RMB 55 billion (ads + commissions) | RMB 90-137.5 billion |
| Open-shelf share | 32% | 45-50% |
| Brand-merchant GMV share | ~30% | 45-55% |

E-commerce is the only core business segment likely to maintain mid-to-high single-digit growth. Raising the monetization rate from 3.5-4% to 5%+ is the key assumption, and brand upgrading plus open-shelf expansion are the path.

#### Local life: a story, but too small to matter

The Meituan partnership focuses on lower-tier markets. GMV grew 200% in 2024, but from a very small base. Five years out it may contribute RMB 5-10 billion of revenue, or 3-5% of total revenue, which is not enough to change the overall picture.

#### User growth: already at the ceiling

| Metric | Current | In 5 years | In 10 years |
|------|------|------|--------|
| DAU | 413 million | 380-430 million (flat to slightly down) | 350-400 million |
| MAU | 772 million | 750-850 million | 700-800 million |

DAU around 400 million is basically the ceiling. Incremental users will come from people over 45 and lower-tier markets, where ARPU upside is limited. Over a 10-year horizon, there is also an aging-user risk - if younger users stop using Kuaishou, the user base will slowly shrink.

### B. Kling AI business

#### Market size: third-party reports are severely underestimating it

Third-party definitions put the "AI video generation" market at just US$700 million in 2025, but the combined ARR of only five companies - Kling, Runway, Midjourney, Pika, and Vidu - already exceeds US$1.6 billion. The market definition is too narrow.

**Rebuilt TAM** - the markets that AI video generation can replace or penetrate:

| Penetrated market | Current size | AI penetration in 5 years | AI penetration in 10 years |
|------------|---------|:-----------:|:------------:|
| Digital video ad creative production | US$70-100 billion | 15-20% | 35-50% |
| Video production services | US$34.6 billion | 10-15% | 25-35% |
| Film post-production / VFX | US$11.5-18.2 billion | 5-10% | 20-30% |
| Digital content creation tools | US$32.3 billion | 8-12% | 20-30% |
| Creator economy for individuals (new) | New market | US$5-10 billion | US$20-40 billion |

**Redefined TAM**: US$20-50 billion in 5 years, US$80-250 billion in 10 years. By analogy with Adobe Creative Cloud (US$19.2 billion ARR), a long-term TAM in the tens of billions of dollars for AI creative tools is entirely reasonable.

#### Competition: the tech lead is being eaten away

**Important warning**: Kling is no longer number one in technical evaluations.

| Product | ARR | Main threat |
|------|-----|---------|
| **ByteDance Seedance / Jimeng** | Not disclosed | CapCut's 200 million MAU distribution, 20-28x compute budget, and April 2026 traffic already ahead of Kling |
| **Google Veo 3.1** | Embedded in Google | Best physical simulation, YouTube data, free to consumers |
| **Runway** | ~$340 million | Deep lock-in with professional creators |
| **Open-source models (LTX / Wan / Hunyuan)** | - | Direct threat to B-end API business |

**ByteDance is the scariest competitor**: its compute budget is 20-28x Kuaishou's (US$70-100 billion vs. RMB 26 billion), CapCut has 200 million MAU as a distribution channel, and the video training data from TikTok + Douyin is far larger than Kuaishou's. After former Kling VP Zhang Di left and joined Alibaba, his HappyHorse project moved to the top of video-generation rankings.

**Can Kling still lead in five years?** It probably will not retain the "technology lead," but it may retain the "commercial lead" - similar to Midjourney, which is not the strongest technically in image generation but is the most successful commercially. A 100 million-user base, 50,000 enterprise customers, and a mature API ecosystem are real business assets.

#### Commercialization path and unit economics

| Metric | 2026 | 2028 | 2031 | 2036 |
|------|:------:|:------:|:------:|:------:|
| API price (per second) | US$0.075 | US$0.04-0.06 | US$0.02-0.03 | US$0.01-0.02 |
| Inference cost (per second) | ~$0.03-0.04 | ~$0.01-0.02 | ~$0.005-0.01 | <US$0.005 |
| Inference gross margin | 40-50% | 50-60% | 60-70% | 70%+ |
| Overall gross margin (including training) | 15-25% | 35-45% | 50-60% | 60-70% |
| Operating margin | -30% to -50% | -10% to +5% | 10-20% | 20-30% |

**Break-even is expected in 2028-2029.** Midjourney, with a 107-person team, reportedly achieved US$500 million in revenue and profitability - once AI creative tools scale, margins can be very high.

**Pricing power risk**: Google Veo 3.1 Lite has already fallen to US$0.05 per second and is free to consumers. If the giants use AI video generation as a traffic-acquisition tool rather than a profit center, the pricing ceiling for independent vendors will be compressed. In 10 years, basic video generation could become free, with value shifting to professional features and enterprise services.

#### Geopolitics: the sword hanging overhead

75% of revenue comes from overseas, and the US contributes 46% of downloads - that structure is extremely fragile.

| Risk | 5-year probability | Impact |
|------|:-------:|------|
| Direct US ban on Kling | 15-25% | Lose 35-40% of revenue |
| TikTok-style forced divestiture | 10-15% | Operational complexity rises |
| Further GPU supply restrictions | 60-70% | Costs rise, but alternatives exist (Kuaishou's in-house SL200 chip) |

Key difference vs. TikTok: Kling is a tool product, so user-data sensitivity is much lower than for social media. But the precedent of DeepSeek being banned on government devices in 17 states shows that political risk for Chinese AI products is real.

**Geopolitical risk deserves a 20-30% discount to Kling's valuation.**
