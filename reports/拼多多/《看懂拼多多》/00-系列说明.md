## Series Positioning

PDD is not a company that is "insufficiently researched" - it is one of the **most-researched yet most-divisive** companies in China ADRs.

- The bulls say: P/E of **6.3x after cash**, ROE of **31%**, net cash of RMB 422.3 billion - absurdly cheap
- The bears say: the management is completely opaque, RMB 422.3 billion of cash is not returned to shareholders, and Temu's political risk could go to zero at any time

**Both sides are looking at the same company, but their conclusions are completely opposite.** That is very rare in A-shares / Hong Kong stocks / China ADRs.

This series aims to **first explain what it is, and then let you decide whether it is worth buying** - not to decide for you.

## Writing Principles

This series follows a few hard rules:

1. **Start with the data, then the logic, and only then the conclusion** - do not pre-judge bullish or bearish
2. **Attach a counterargument to every major conclusion** - let readers weigh both sides
3. **Delete unreliable data rather than inventing it** - many key PDD metrics (such as Temu GMV and domestic MAU breakdowns) are not publicly disclosed, so this series only uses the most reliable sources as anchors and does not force in fake precision
4. **Do not calculate probability-weighted expected returns** - scenario weights are too subjective and create false precision
5. **Not investment advice** - every article ends with a disclaimer

| Dimension | Tencent | PDD |

| Asset quality | High quality + very deep moat + high certainty | Extreme financial efficiency + medium moat + low certainty |

If you finished the Tencent series and felt "that makes sense," keep one thing in mind when reading PDD: PDD's cheapness comes at a cost, and that cost is not being able to see it clearly.
