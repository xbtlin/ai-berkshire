# There is a gambling clause hidden in Keling’s valuation of US$18 billion

At the closing price on Friday, July 3, Kuaishou was HK$42.60. Today, four trading days later, it is HK$42.10. On closer inspection, almost nothing happens.

But spread it out in the middle: +8% on Monday, -12% on Tuesday, +8.7% on Wednesday, and then gave up another 4.3% today. When the weekly amplitude exceeds 15%, the K-line is drawn as an electrocardiogram. On Tuesday, the market value evaporated by approximately HK$24 billion in a single day.

This week, there is no new information on Kuaishou’s fundamentals. There are no financial reports, no business changes, and no regulatory filings. The market is not pricing Kuaishou, it is pricing "how others view Kuaishou."

Among all the noise this week, the only information worth keeping was actually written in an attachment to an announcement a week ago that not many people read carefully. Read the electrocardiogram first and then read it.

## Everyone thinks they are smart money

There have been at least three groups of people who made big bets this week, so you might as well look through their accounts one by one.

The first wave, Tencent. After the market closed on July 6, 273 million shares were sold in bulk over the counter, cashing out approximately HK$12 billion. The shareholding dropped from 15.68% to 9.37%, and the company withdrew from the ranks of major shareholders. In the same week, two Tencent entities appeared on the list of investors in Kelingxin’s financing. Sell ​​the shopping mall with your left hand and buy the backyard with your right hand - as I wrote in the previous article, this is not a retreat, but a move.

The second dial is the receiving mechanism. They took the 273 million shares at a price range of HK$43.15 to HK$44.53 per share, a discount of about 3% to 6% from Monday's closing price of HK$46. Today's closing price is 42.10, the discount has fallen below, and the book is floating at a loss. This is not the first time that the Hong Kong stock market has seen large quantities of goods received at a discount and continued to fall after receiving them.

The third wave is the panic market on July 7. I saw the title "Tencent Reduces Holdings" and handed over the ticket at the floor price of 40.46 - the same position I wrote about at the beginning of the previous article, which was only 70 cents away from the 52-week low. Hong Kong technology stocks generally rose the next day, with Alibaba rising 11% and Kuaishou rebounding 8.7%. None of their reasons for selling changed the next day, only the price changed.

Three groups of people, three accounts: floating losses in receiving goods, shortfalls in cutting meat, only Tencent's one is not ugly for the time being - but when it rebounded to 44 on Wednesday, it also became the protagonist of "selling out" for a long time. The real lesson of this electrocardiogram is: there is no right or wrong in short-term prices, only the order of being slapped in the face.

## 3 billion US dollars, not a vote of faith

The news that is really worth reading carefully happened a week ago - on July 2, the announcement of Keling's spin-off financing. Most reports stopped at the line "valuation of US$18 billion, joint investment by Tencent, Alibaba and Baidu", and few people turned to the page of terms.

Let’s first clarify the structure: Beijing Keling will take over Keling AI’s assets and business, and will raise approximately US$3 billion in external financing, with a post-investment valuation of approximately US$18 billion. Tencent, Alibaba, and Baidu are all on the list, but their shares are only around 1% - Alibaba Cloud, which has invested the most, only has 1.363 billion yuan.

The key is hidden in the last sentence. Kuaishou's Hong Kong Stock Exchange announcement on the evening of July 2 disclosed that according to the shareholders' agreement: If Beijing Keling fails to complete the IPO before October 30, 2031, investors have the right to require Beijing Keling to repurchase the equity - sell the equity back to Keling, take the money and leave. The repurchase amount is the original investment, plus a return calculated at an annual interest rate of 8% simple interest. Another triggering condition is that if the regulatory procedures for spin-off and reorganization are overdue, a buyback can also be required.

Let me tell you more about the source: This is not hearsay, it is a statutory disclosure of listed companies. The Financial Associated Press, Securities Times, Sina Finance, etc. all quoted the same announcement.

Translation: What these US$3 billion bought is not an ordinary stock, but more like a convertible bond - when it rises, it acts as a stock, and when it loses, it acts as a bond. If Keling is listed, investors will enjoy the increase in valuation, and there is no cap; if it cannot be listed, the principal plus 8% annual interest will be taken back, and the bottom is guaranteed.

A rough calculation: If the financing limit of 20.447 billion yuan is fully subscribed and the funds are on account for more than five years, the total principal and interest due will be approximately 1.4 times the principal - this is a deduction based on the terms of the announcement, not the original text of the announcement. Note that it is still simple interest: 40% in five years, not the nearly 47% derived from compound interest. The guarantee is not generous. Its function is to protect the bottom line, not to make money - what the giants want is the listing.

This type of clause is not uncommon in the primary market. What is rare is that it appears in a financing that is generally interpreted as "the three giants are collectively bullish". It is true to be bullish, but the bullish posture is wearing a seat belt - this detail is more worth remembering than the bullishness itself.

## The gold content of 18 billion US dollars must be read together with the terms.

This clause directly rewrites the reading of two things.

First, there is a question mark on the "valuation of US$18 billion". Equity with a guaranteed minimum repurchase is not the same thing as pure equity - the risk is reduced by the terms, and the valuation number can naturally be higher. The quotation in the primary market is never a number, but a number plus a bunch of terms. To only read the numbers and not the terms is to only look at the price and not the contract.

Second, Ke Ling's schedule is locked. The countdown to October 30, 2031 is already hanging on the wall. If it cannot be listed on the market when it expires, Beijing Keling will have to face a principal of more than 20 billion yuan, plus 8% simple interest accumulated year by year. When the full amount is deduced, the bill will be around 29 billion yuan at most.

Who will carry this money? The person responsible in the announcement is Beijing Keling, not Kuaishou. But Kuaishou is the controlling shareholder holding two-thirds of the shares: its net cash on the account is close to half of its market value according to brokerage estimates, and its annual adjusted net profit is just over 20 billion yuan. On that day, this bill can be afforded, but it is equivalent to burning more than a year's profits. "It's hard to stay out" is not rhetoric, it's arithmetic.

For Kuaishou’s shareholders, this is a double-edged sword. The good side: Once Keling's IPO is completed, more than 60% of the shares held by Kuaishou will be quoted in the public market. The subtraction of "after deducting Keling, and the main business will only have a price-to-earnings ratio of just over 4 times" will be forced by the market to recalculate.

The bad side: In order to meet the exam, a company may make changes in revenue pace and delivery. The five-year period has always been both an engine and a shackle.

Keling's current report card is hard: revenue in the first quarter of 2026 will exceed 650 million yuan, a year-on-year increase of more than 300%, and annualized recurring revenue will be approximately US$500 million. But the better your score, the more important it is to remember that this exam has a deadline.

## The seller also started to calculate the subtraction

Looking back at the +8% gain on Monday, the most widely circulated explanation that day was a report from Morgan Stanley: Kuaishou's "implied valuation of its core business is extremely attractive." Translated into vernacular, it is that subtraction - the market value is deducted from the equity value of Keling, and the remaining main business is priced too cheap.

The fact that sellers have begun to calculate this problem publicly shows that it is changing from an arithmetic problem for a few people to a candidate for mainstream narrative. But after the market closed on the same day, Tencent’s announcement of reduction in holdings destroyed this narrative. The time difference itself is very dramatic: in the morning, someone tells you that it is cheap, and in the evening, the major shareholder who has been with you for many years tells you that he does not want this bargain with 12 billion Hong Kong dollars.

Both signals are true. The seller's report prices the assets, while the major shareholder's reduction in holdings prices its own capital allocation. Tencent did spend money to invest in Keling in the same week, but the two sides were not of the same magnitude - it sold 12 billion Hong Kong dollars and bought back more than one billion yuan, and it was a ticket with a buy-back guarantee. The move is real, it's just that it's a house with a money-back guarantee.

Let's recalculate that subtraction based on today's closing price: the market value is about 182 billion Hong Kong dollars, deducting about 96.5 billion of Keling's equity, the main business is still more than 80 billion - just over 4 times. After a week of electrocardiograms, the word subtraction has not changed.

## The only new fact

Review this week: Monday's big rise, Tuesday's plunge, Wednesday's rebound, and today's retreat. The market value fluctuated by tens of billions of Hong Kong dollars in four days, and none of them was driven by new fundamentals - research reports were opinions, reductions were position adjustments, general gains were the market, and retreats were emotional ebbs.

There are no new facts in a week's fluctuations, only new emotions.

There is only one real new fact, lying quietly in the terms attachment of the announcement on July 2: the countdown to the listing of Keling has begun. Before October 30, 2031, this company will either be listed on the exchange or return the money with interest. All stories about Kuaishou in the next five years cannot avoid this date.

The end of the previous article said that in a winner-takes-all industry, the cheap guy is not the discount boss. You can make up the second half of the sentence this week——

Prices are voted on every day, terms are signed only once. To understand a company, first read what it signed, and then look at what it increased.
