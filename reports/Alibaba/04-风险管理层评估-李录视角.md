## I. Management Assessment

Li Lu has said: "The shorter the time horizon, the more important the human factor becomes." Alibaba is in a critical window of strategic transformation, and management quality will directly determine whether this transition succeeds.

### 1.1 CEO Eddie Wu: The Return of a Technically Oriented Founding Veteran

**Background and circle of competence:**
- One of Alibaba's "Eighteen Arhats," employee number six, with an engineering background
- Became group CEO in September 2023, later also serving as CEO of Taotian Group and Alibaba Cloud Intelligence Group - simultaneously holding the top role across three core businesses
- In April 2026, became head of Alibaba's Technology Committee, consolidating core authority across technology, e-commerce, and cloud computing

**Key decisions since taking office:**

| Decision | Time | Assessment |
|------|------|------|
| Established the dual strategy of "user first, AI-driven" | 2023.9 | Directionally right; corrected the strategic dispersion of the Daniel Zhang era |
| Contracted from "1+6+N" into four major business segments | 2025.8 | A painful but necessary cut, refocusing on the core |
| Announced RMB 380.0 billion of investment over 3 years to build cloud and AI infrastructure | 2025.2 | A major AI bet; the direction is right, but execution risk is high |
| Took over as Taotian CEO and directly managed core e-commerce | 2025.12 | Shows emphasis on e-commerce, but the management span is too broad |
| Established the ATH business group (Token Hub) | 2026.3 | Positions Alibaba for new business models, but the direction still needs time to be validated |
| Launched a full push into instant retail through Taobao Flash Buy | 2025-2026 | Aggressive but necessary; it is eroding Meituan's share |

**Core strength:** His technical background allows him to understand the substance of the AI strategy, unlike purely commercial managers who can be more easily distracted by concepts. As a founding veteran, he also has sufficient organizational authority to drive change.

**Core risk:** One person is simultaneously managing three trillion-level businesses (the group, Taotian, and Alibaba Cloud), creating an overly broad management span. His management style leans toward delegation, in contrast to Daniel Zhang's hands-on approach. Is delegation suitable for an e-commerce battlefield that requires refined operations? The data show that Taotian's GMV growth has not meaningfully improved since he took concurrent charge (comparable growth of about 8% in FY2026 Q4).

**Question from a Li Lu perspective:** Is Eddie Wu operating within his circle of competence? He comes from a technical background and now must simultaneously manage e-commerce operations, cloud computing commercialization, and AI strategy - three domains that each require deep specialization. It is rare in business history for one person to succeed while holding three top operating roles at the same time.

### 1.2 Chairman Joe Tsai: Master of Capital Allocation

**Capital allocation scorecard:**
- Repurchased USD 11.9 billion of stock in FY2025, reducing net shares outstanding by 5.1% - one of the largest buybacks in the history of Chinese ADRs
- Total repurchase authorization has increased to USD 65.0 billion
- Personally increased Alibaba holdings by USD 150 million through the Blue Pool fund
- Pushed divestitures of non-core assets, including Sun Art Retail and Intime Department Store

**Strategic self-reflection (a positive):**
In a March 2026 Stanford speech, Joe Tsai candidly admitted that "Alibaba's biggest mistake was forgetting who the real customer was," and acknowledged that pitching six major business groups to investors had been "stupid" because it confused the company's true focus. This willingness to publicly admit mistakes is extremely rare among Chinese entrepreneurs.

**On the other hand:** Joe Tsai also acknowledged that Alibaba paid a "more than RMB 100.0 billion price" for strategic misjudgments, including poorly integrated assets such as Youku Tudou and Ele.me. While admitting mistakes is commendable, the cost of those mistakes was real: large amounts of capital were consumed in inefficient acquisitions while competitors seized the opportunity to rise.

**Li Lu perspective:** Li Lu emphasizes that "investing is the improvement of one's own temperament." Joe Tsai has shown a willingness to acknowledge and correct mistakes, which is a scarce quality. But the ultimate test of capital allocation is long-term ROIC. Alibaba's overall capital allocation over the past five years has been value-destructive (for comparison: Tencent's investment portfolio continued to appreciate over the same period). The current large-scale buybacks and focused strategy are correcting past mistakes, but correction itself does not create value; it only stops the bleeding.

### 1.3 Jack Ma: The Return of the Soul of the Company - Positive Factor or Risk?

**Positive signals:**
- Became active again in 2025: attended a private enterprise symposium, participated in an Alibaba Cloud launch event, and appeared on Alibaba's campus
- Interpreted by the market as a signal of "normalization of political relations"
- Emphasized that "AI should serve everyday life" - maintaining a pragmatic philosophical orientation
- The Jack Ma family increased its shareholdings and may have become Alibaba's largest single shareholder (surpassing SoftBank)

**Potential risks:**
- Jack Ma's spiritual influence could interfere with the decision-making independence of professional managers - Eddie Wu is, in essence, Jack Ma's proxy
- His political position remains delicate: although he has "returned," his influence is no longer comparable to the pre-2019 period
- The tradition of "godfather worship" in Alibaba's culture could make the organization overly dependent on individual will
- If U.S.-China relations become tense again, Jack Ma, as China's best-known entrepreneur, could still become a political target

**Li Lu perspective:** Jack Ma's return is net positive. His presence stabilizes morale, and his share purchases send the market a cash-backed signal of confidence. But an investment should not depend on any individual's aura. A good company should continue to operate well even after its founder has fully departed.

### 1.4 Comparison With Former CEO Daniel Zhang

| Dimension | Daniel Zhang | Eddie Wu |
|------|------|--------|
| Management style | Hands-on, detail-oriented | More delegation-oriented, focused on strategic direction |
| Core capability | Business operations, financial management (created Singles' Day) | Technical understanding, frontier insight |
| Strategic focus | Diversified expansion ("1+6+N") | Focused contraction (four major segments) |
| Organizational change | Expansion phase - extending the front line | Contraction phase - refocusing on the core |
| Tenure results | GMV rose from RMB 3 trillion to RMB 8 trillion, but market cap fell from a peak of USD 850.0 billion to the trough | Market cap rebounded from the trough; AI strategy launched |
| Judgment on AI | Slow response, missed the ChatGPT window | All-in bet, RMB 380.0 billion investment |

**Key difference:** Daniel Zhang was an "expansion-type" CEO, suited to an incremental growth market. Eddie Wu is a "transformation-type" CEO, better suited to an era of zero-sum competition. But Eddie Wu's challenge is that he must both sharpen strategic focus (subtract) and invest heavily in AI and instant retail (add). This "adding and subtracting at the same time" places extreme demands on execution.

## II. Regulatory Risk

### 2.1 Regulatory Environment After the RMB 18.2 Billion Fine

In 2021, Alibaba was fined RMB 18.2 billion for antitrust violations, becoming the landmark event of China's internet regulatory storm.

**Current status:**
- The fine has been fully paid, and compliance rectification is largely complete
- Jack Ma attended a private enterprise symposium in 2025 - seen as a strong signal of "relationship normalization"
- The regulatory tone has shifted from "crackdown" to "encouraging development"
- Alibaba has received approval to conduct AI large-model business, and the Qwen series is operating normally

**On the other hand:** The regulatory "Sword of Damocles" has not disappeared. China's regulatory system is characterized by "policy reversibility" - encouragement today does not guarantee there will be no tightening tomorrow. Risk would rise especially under the following scenarios:
- Alibaba regains a monopolistic position in a specific field (for example, instant retail surpassing Meituan)
- U.S.-China relations deteriorate, making Alibaba, as the largest Chinese ADR, a possible "signaling target"
- AI regulation tightens further (data security, algorithm review)

### 2.2 Ant Group

**Core issue: the financial holding license remains unresolved.**
- Fined RMB 7.123 billion in July 2023, with compliance rectification completed
- But there has been no major progress on the financial holding license application - the PBOC has so far approved only 3 financial holding companies with central-SOE backgrounds
- Ant has split its businesses: Ant International, OceanBase, and Ant Digital Technologies now operate independently
- In 2025, Ant Consumer Finance was fined another RMB 1.40 million (small amount but signal-releasing), and transferred an RMB 1.185 billion non-performing loan package
- Its listing outlook remains unclear

**Impact on Alibaba:** Alibaba holds about 33% of Ant. Based on Hurun's 2025 valuation of RMB 635.0 billion for Ant, Alibaba's corresponding equity interest is about RMB 210.0 billion. But while Ant cannot list and the financial holding license has not landed, the liquidity and monetizable value of this stake should be heavily discounted. For value investors, Ant should receive a large valuation haircut or even be excluded from valuation.

## III. Competitive Risk: Has the Erosion From Pinduoduo/Douyin Bottomed?

### 3.1 The Harsh Reality of Share Loss

From 2021 to 2024, Alibaba's e-commerce market share fell from 51.3% to about 37.3%, losing 14 percentage points in four years. The lost share mainly flowed to Douyin + Kuaishou (rising from 9.2% to 21.4%) and Pinduoduo (rising from 15.9% to 21%).

**Several institutions forecast that the 2026 share will fall further to about 36.2%.**

### 3.2 Has the Erosion Bottomed?

**Evidence for a bottom:**
- Taotian GMV growth has recovered from negative growth to mid-to-high single-digit growth (about 6% in FY2026)
- 88VIP membership exceeded 53.0 million, and high-value user stickiness is improving
- Douyin e-commerce growth remains fast (30%+), but growth will naturally slow as the base increases
- Taotian customer management revenue continues to grow by about 8-11%, indicating that merchants have not yet left at scale

**Evidence against a bottom:**
- Zheshang Securities forecasts that 2026 share will continue to decline to 36.2%
- Douyin e-commerce completed in just 5 years the GMV scale that Taobao took 19 years to reach (from 0 to RMB 4 trillion), and its growth momentum remains strong
- Pinduoduo's dominance in lower-tier markets is unshakable
- Instant retail is a new battlefield. Although Alibaba's share has caught up with Meituan (45.2% vs 45.0%), the cost is massive subsidy investment and severely eroded profit

**Key judgment from a Li Lu perspective:** The pace of share loss is slowing, but it has not truly bottomed. The essence of the problem is not that Alibaba has become worse, but that competitors have found battlefields where Alibaba is not strong: Douyin's "interest e-commerce" and Pinduoduo's "extreme value-for-money" are both difficult for Alibaba's traditional model to replicate. In Li Lu's terms, one must judge whether this is a "temporary difficulty" or a "permanent structural change." The data lean toward the latter: e-commerce has evolved from the single paradigm of "search shopping" into multi-paradigm competition across "search + interest + social + value-for-money." Alibaba cannot win in every paradigm.

## IV. Business Risk

### 4.1 Taotian Group: Is the Core Still Stable?

**Latest data (FY2026 Q4, January-March 2026):**
- China E-commerce Group revenue of RMB 122.22 billion
- Customer management revenue grew about 8% on a comparable basis
- 88VIP membership exceeded 53.0 million

**User growth trend:** Total domestic e-commerce users are near the ceiling (about 900.0 million online shoppers), with incremental growth coming from:
- ARPU improvement among high-value 88VIP users
- New scenarios and new users brought by instant retail (Taobao Flash Buy)
- But these sources of incremental growth are expensive

**Concerns in GMV trend:**
- Taotian GMV growth of about 6% is below the industry average of about 7%, meaning share is still being lost
- More importantly: Alibaba no longer discloses absolute GMV, only growth rate - this is usually a signal that the data are not attractive
- The return-rate issue was raised with management on the earnings call, indicating that it is a real operational pain point

### 4.2 Cloud Computing: Is Profitability Improvement Sustainable?

**Latest data (FY2026 Q4):**
- Alibaba Cloud revenue of RMB 41.63 billion, with external revenue up 40% year over year
- AI-related product revenue of RMB 8.971 billion, exceeding 30% of external revenue for the first time
- AI revenue has grown triple digits for 11 consecutive quarters
- EBITA margin of about 9%

**Sustainability analysis:**
- Positive side: AI demand is real and structural; the Qwen large model is competitive in the domestic market (MAU exceeded 300.0 million); Alibaba ranks first in China's cloud computing market share (Gartner 32.8%)
- Risk side: The EBITA margin of 9% is far below AWS (30%+) and Azure (30%+), a huge gap; RMB 380.0 billion of capital expenditure will continue to suppress profit over the next 3 years; AI competition is intense (Huawei Cloud, Tencent Cloud, ByteDance Volcano Engine), and price-war risk cannot be ignored

**Credibility of the five-year target:** Management has proposed that annual "cloud + AI" revenue reach USD 100.0 billion (about RMB 690.0 billion). The current annualized level is about RMB 160.0 billion, meaning revenue must grow 4.3x in 5 years. That requires a CAGR of about 34%. Based on the current 40% growth rate, this is not completely impossible, but as the base grows, the difficulty of maintaining that growth will rise exponentially.

### 4.3 International Business (AIDC): When Will Losses Narrow?

**Key progress:**
- FY2026 Q1: losses narrowed sharply from RMB 3.7 billion to RMB 59.0 million - nearly breakeven
- FY2026 Q4: AIDC revenue was RMB 35.43 billion, up 5.5% year over year
- AliExpress and Trendyol are the main growth drivers

**On the other hand:**
- FY2025 full-year losses were RMB 15.1 billion, nearly doubling year over year
- Growth slowed abruptly from 56% to 5.5%, suggesting that the high-speed expansion phase has passed and the growth engine may be stalling
- A deteriorating global trade environment (tariffs, rising compliance requirements) creates structural headwinds for cross-border e-commerce
- Competition with Temu (Pinduoduo's overseas version) and TikTok Shop is extremely intense

**Judgment:** Narrowing losses is a positive signal, but the sharp slowdown in growth is worrying. International e-commerce is a "spend money to buy growth" business; once investment is reduced, growth immediately falls back. Alibaba needs to find a balance between "controlling losses" and "protecting growth." At present, it appears more focused on controlling losses, which may mean giving up part of its growth ambition.

### 4.4 Local Services / Digital Media and Entertainment: How Much Strategic Value?

**Organizational changes:**
- Ele.me has been folded into the China E-commerce Group (instant retail segment)
- Amap, Youku, and Damai have been placed in the "All Others" segment
- Digital media and entertainment EBITA has turned positive, and Youku has become profitable

**Strategic logic:**
Joe Tsai explained at Stanford that Ele.me's 30-minute delivery network is "the infrastructure for future e-commerce." Taobao Flash Buy is using the fulfillment network of Ele.me + Freshippo + Tmall Supermarket to compete with Meituan for the instant retail market.

**Risk assessment:** Instant retail is one of Alibaba's biggest "bets" in 2026. Taobao Flash Buy's market share has reached 45.2% (almost level with Meituan's 45%), but the price was a 57% year-over-year decline in adjusted EBITA in FY2026 Q3. Management has clearly stated that "the 2026 goal is absolute market leadership, and we will firmly increase investment" - meaning profit will remain under pressure for the next 2-4 quarters.

**Li Lu perspective:** Instant retail is the right strategic direction (consumer habits are irreversible), but Alibaba chose the most expensive entry method: using massive subsidies to confront Meituan head-on. This conflicts with the value investing principle of "buying a good business at a reasonable price." If the result is a war of attrition, neither side will be a winner.

## V. Geopolitical Risk

### 5.1 Delisting Risk for Chinese ADRs

**Latest developments (2025-2026):**
- In April 2025, the U.S. Treasury Secretary explicitly stated that "delisting Chinese companies from U.S. exchanges is an open option"
- The SEC Chair said the agency was prepared to launch a "large-scale delisting investigation" into Chinese ADRs
- The "America First Investment Policy" requires a review of the "fraud risk" of VIE structures
- The Department of Justice and FBI were asked to jointly investigate VIE structures

**Alibaba's response:**
- Completed a "dual primary listing" on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange, reducing dependence on a single market
- Hong Kong share trading volume is sufficient, so even if forced to delist from U.S. exchanges, investors can trade through Hong Kong shares
- Included in Stock Connect, allowing mainland capital to allocate directly

**Residual risk assessment:** The worst-case delisting scenario has been greatly mitigated (because of the dual primary listing), but it would still cause:
- Sharp short-term stock price volatility
- Forced selling by some U.S. institutional investors (compliance requirements)
- A wider valuation discount (a deeper "China discount")

### 5.2 VIE Structure Risk

Risks exist on both sides of the VIE structure:
- **U.S. side:** Rising risk that it is deemed a "fraudulent structure"
- **China side:** Although it has been tacitly permitted for the past 20 years, the legality of VIEs has never been formally confirmed in law

**Li Lu perspective:** Geopolitical risk is the largest "unquantifiable factor" in Alibaba's valuation. Value investors need to reserve sufficient margin of safety for this risk. If Alibaba's intrinsic value is X, then because of geopolitical risk, a reasonable purchase price should be 0.6X-0.7X.

### 5.3 Long-Term Impact of U.S.-China Relations

The structural confrontation trend in U.S.-China relations is unlikely to reverse in the short term. For Alibaba:
- The cloud computing business may be restricted from entering certain international markets
- The AI chip supply chain is constrained by U.S. export controls (although T-Head chips are advancing domestic substitution)
- International business expansion faces higher compliance costs

## VI. Governance Structure

### 6.1 Partnership System

**Mechanism:** Alibaba Partnership (currently 36 people) has the right to nominate a majority of board members. It uses a one-person, one-vote system, and a new partner must receive more than 75% affirmative votes to be elected. Jack Ma and Joe Tsai are the only two "permanent partners."

**Advantage:** Ensures strategic stability for the founding team and avoids aggressive actions forced by short-term capital.

**Risks:**
- The partnership system is essentially "insider control," hollowing out the governance rights of external shareholders
- A 36-person partnership team is too large, potentially reducing decision-making efficiency
- No sunset clause, meaning this control structure will never terminate automatically
- When company interests conflict with partners' personal interests, effective checks and balances are lacking

### 6.2 Shareholding Structure

- The Jack Ma family stake may have surpassed SoftBank's (SoftBank's holding has fallen below 0.5%), making it the largest single shareholder
- Joe Tsai is the second-largest individual shareholder
- SoftBank essentially exited in 2025 - both a risk signal (an early core shareholder is no longer bullish) and a positive signal (large-block selling pressure has been removed)

### 6.3 Credibility of the Buyback Commitment

**Data:**
- Actual repurchases of USD 11.9 billion in FY2025 - very strong execution
- Total authorization of USD 65.0 billion - the largest in the history of Chinese ADRs
- FY2026 annual + special dividends totaled USD 4.6 billion
- Founders increased holdings out of pocket - a "vote with their feet" signal

**Credibility assessment: ★★★★☆ (4/5)**
The buyback execution history is consistent, and founder purchases are a positive. One star is deducted because if AI investment materially exceeds expectations (RMB 380.0 billion may still not be enough), the buyback scale could be compressed - management faces a capital competition between "investing for growth" and "buybacks."

## VII. Long-Term Certainty: What Will Alibaba Look Like in 10 Years?

### 7.1 Core Assumption

10 years from now (2036), the probability that Alibaba still exists and remains an important company is: **85-90%**.

**Supporting logic:**
- E-commerce infrastructure (payments, logistics, merchant ecosystem) has strong network effects and switching costs
- Cloud computing has a moat in the Chinese market (data sovereignty requirements + first-mover advantage)
- It holds RMB 570.0 billion of liquid funds, so it will not fail in the short term

### 7.2 Possible Scenarios

| Scenario | Probability | Description |
|------|------|------|
| Bull case: AI-driven second growth curve | 25% | AI + cloud becomes the core growth engine, annual revenue exceeds USD 100.0 billion; e-commerce share stabilizes above 30%+; valuation returns to USD 500.0 billion+ |
| Base case: stable cash cow | 45% | E-commerce share declines slowly but profit remains stable; cloud computing becomes the second-largest profit source; continued buybacks, valuation of USD 300.0-400.0 billion |
| Bear case: gradual decline | 25% | E-commerce share continues to erode to below 25%; AI investment fails to generate sufficient returns; becomes a "Yahoo Japan"-style company |
| Extreme case: disruptive event | 5% | Forced breakup / delisting / major regulatory blow / complete failure of the AI technology path |

### 7.3 What Could Disrupt Its Business Model?

1. **The rise of AI-native e-commerce:** If AI Agents can fully replace the "search-browse-compare-order" shopping process, Taobao's value as a platform would shrink materially. Users would no longer need to open Taobao; they would tell AI, "buy me the most suitable XXX" - and the value of the platform as a traffic gateway would fall to zero.
2. **Decentralized commerce:** If social e-commerce and livestream e-commerce develop further and merchants can reach consumers directly at zero cost, the commission model of intermediary platforms will be challenged.
3. **Forced breakup caused by regulation:** Although the current probability is low, if antitrust policy tightens again in the future, Alibaba could be required to split its e-commerce and cloud computing businesses.
4. **Generational technology shift:** A paradigm shift similar to the move from PC to mobile internet. If new paradigms such as AR/VR shopping or brain-computer interfaces emerge, can Alibaba seize them? In the past, it missed the window for short video / livestream e-commerce.

## VIII. Core Short Thesis: If You Are the Short Seller

### Argument One: Structural Profit Deterioration, Not Short-Term Pain

In FY2026 Q3 (October-December 2025), non-GAAP net income was RMB 16.7 billion, down 67% year over year. This is not quarterly volatility - the full-year trend shows that profit is continuously declining:

- Q1 adjusted net income of RMB 33.5 billion
- Q2 adjusted EBITA down 18% year over year
- Q3 non-GAAP net income down 67% year over year
- FY2025 free cash flow down 53% year over year

Management explains this as a "strategic investment period," but shorts would say: **Alibaba is using profit to buy growth, and the growth has not arrived.** Taotian GMV grew only 6%, below the industry average of 7% - Alibaba spent more money and still lost share.

### Argument Two: RMB 380.0 Billion of AI Investment May Be a Value Trap

Management announced RMB 380.0 billion of investment over 3 years to build AI infrastructure, but:
- Alibaba Cloud's EBITA margin is only 9%, far below AWS's 30%+ - indicating weak pricing power
- Joe Tsai himself has warned that "there is a bubble in AI data centers" - when the chairman himself worries about a bubble, why is the company still increasing investment?
- The Qwen large model faces intense competition from ByteDance's Doubao, Baidu's Ernie, and DeepSeek, with no obvious moat
- If AI commercialization falls short of expectations, this RMB 380.0 billion will become a sunk cost

### Argument Three: Instant Retail Is a War of Attrition With No Winner

Taobao Flash Buy used massive subsidies to push share to 45.2% - but the cost was a 57% year-over-year decline in Q3 adjusted EBITA. Management said it wants to become "absolute number one in 2026" and will invest firmly - meaning cash burn will continue.

But the economics of instant retail are naturally unfavorable for platforms:
- Delivery costs are high and difficult to reduce through scale
- Consumers are highly subsidy-sensitive and leave once subsidies are reduced
- Meituan has 10 years of operating accumulation and delivery network advantages
- The end state may be a duopoly stalemate in which neither side can make money

### Argument Four: E-Commerce Share Loss Is an Irreversible Structural Change

Alibaba's fall from 51.3% to 37.3% is not because Alibaba became worse, but because the e-commerce paradigm changed fundamentally:
- "Search e-commerce" -> "interest e-commerce" (Douyin) -> "social e-commerce" (Pinduoduo)
- Alibaba's search e-commerce model cannot effectively compete with interest e-commerce and social e-commerce
- Share will continue to slide, with 2026 expected to fall to 36.2%
- When share falls below 30%, the platform network effect will enter a negative feedback loop - merchants leave because traffic is insufficient, and users leave because product selection is incomplete

### Argument Five: Governance Structure Protects Insiders and Harms External Shareholders

The partnership system gives external shareholders almost no governance voice. When management decides to burn money on instant retail or spend RMB 380.0 billion on AI, external shareholders cannot stop it. History shows that these large investments have generated returns below the cost of capital - acquisitions such as Youku Tudou, Ele.me, and RT-Mart are evidence.

**The short seller's strongest blow:** Alibaba is fighting three wars at the same time - the e-commerce defense war (vs Pinduoduo/Douyin), the instant retail assault (vs Meituan), and the AI/cloud investment war. Each war requires massive investment, while the company's profit and cash flow can no longer bear the burden. In history, companies fighting multiple wars simultaneously have almost never won them all.

## IX. Overall Assessment From a Li Lu Perspective

### 9.1 Testing With Li Lu's Framework

**Moat assessment: ★★★☆☆ (3/5)**
- The e-commerce business has network effects, but they are weakening (the evidence of share loss is conclusive)
- Cloud computing has customer switching costs but weak pricing power
- The brand moat is being eroded by Pinduoduo and Douyin
- The data moat exists but has not yet been fully monetized

**Management assessment: ★★★★☆ (4/5)**
- Joe Tsai's ability to admit mistakes and his capital allocation discipline are positives
- Eddie Wu's direction is right, but his management span is too broad, which is a negative
- Founder purchases are the most convincing signal of confidence
- One star is deducted because overall capital allocation over the past five years has been value-destructive

**Margin of safety assessment:**
- The current P/E ratio of about 18x is below the industry average of 39x - a huge apparent discount
- But the discount exists for good reasons: downward profit trend, geopolitical risk, and governance discount
- The real margin of safety should be based on normalized profit rather than current profit suppressed by AI investment

**Circle-of-competence boundary - conditions under which this investment case fails:**
1. If AI investment fails to generate significant revenue growth after 3 years (cloud revenue growth falls below 15% per year)
2. If Taotian e-commerce share falls below 30%
3. If instant retail still cannot turn profitable after 3 years of fighting
4. If geopolitics forces a U.S. delisting and Hong Kong liquidity is insufficient

### 9.2 Final Risk Rating

**Overall risk rating: Medium-high**

Alibaba is not a low-risk investment. It is in a period of large-scale strategic transformation, profit is being deliberately suppressed, and it is fighting on multiple fronts simultaneously. For value investors, the key question is: **Are you willing to endure 2-3 years of profit decline for a company that is "transforming but moving in the right direction"?**

If the answer is "yes," the current valuation may provide sufficient margin of safety. If the answer is "no," it is not too late to enter after AI investment begins to generate returns and instant retail loss reduction is confirmed - although the price will be higher, certainty will also be higher.

Li Lu has said: "The main reason for using margin of safety is that our ability to predict the future is limited." Alibaba's future is harder to predict than that of most companies - which means it requires a larger margin of safety.
