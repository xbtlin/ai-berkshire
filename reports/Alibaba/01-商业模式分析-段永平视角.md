## I. The Essence of the Business Model: Defining the Core Business

### What Business Is Alibaba Really In?

**One-sentence definition:** Alibaba's core business is "the distribution and monetization of commercial traffic" - connecting merchants and consumers, and making money from traffic advertising and transaction commissions.

This needs to be understood on two levels:

- **Level 1 (a transition that has already happened)**: Alibaba has shifted from an "e-commerce platform" into a dual-core group built around "consumption + cloud computing." In FY2026, the structure was streamlined into four major segments - China E-commerce Group, International Digital Commerce Group, Cloud Intelligence Group, and All Others - with the core strategy clearly defined as "e-commerce + cloud + AI."
- **Level 2 (a narrative still to be proven)**: Management hopes Alibaba can become an "AI-driven technology group," similar to the role AWS plays for Amazon. But Alibaba Cloud + AI revenue currently accounts for roughly 12% of group revenue, so there is still a meaningful gap before the "technology group" narrative is fully justified.

**How would Duan Yongping view this?** He would certainly ask: what is Alibaba's "proper role" or core duty? When a company tries to do e-commerce, cloud computing, instant retail, globalization, digital media and entertainment, logistics, and more at the same time, that itself is a warning signal. Good businesses are usually focused.

4. **Divesting non-core assets is the right direction**: Intime RMB 7.4 bn, Sun Art Retail RMB 13.1 bn, with capital refocused on the core businesses

## II. Platform Flywheel Effect: Is Taobao and Tmall's Flywheel Still Spinning?

### The Classic Flywheel Model

Taobao and Tmall's original flywheel logic was: **more merchants -> more products -> lower prices -> more consumers -> more merchants**.

### Evidence That the Flywheel Has Been Disrupted

**Data facts:**

- Taobao and Tmall GMV share: about 51% in 2020 -> about 31% in 2025. Market share has fallen by 20 percentage points in five years
- Douyin e-commerce GMV: almost zero in 2020 -> about RMB 4.3 tn in 2025, with 24% share and growth above 30%
- Pinduoduo GMV: about RMB 5.2 tn, with 19% share
- At the current pace, the GMV gap between Douyin e-commerce and Taobao and Tmall is narrowing by about RMB 900 bn per year, with parity expected in 2028-2029

**Root causes of the flywheel fracture:**

1. **The traffic entrance has been intercepted**: Douyin has 800 mn DAU vs Taobao's 300-400 mn DAU. Consumer attention has shifted from "search-based shopping" to "content-based discovery." Taobao's flywheel depends on consumers actively searching for demand, while Douyin creates a new demand-discovery path through short video and livestreaming, effectively building a new wheel outside the original flywheel
2. **Mid- and low-end merchants are leaving**: Pinduoduo is friendlier to white-label merchants (RMB 0 entry, deposits as low as RMB 500, commission-free policies), while Douyin saved merchants RMB 32 bn in costs in 2025. Taobao, by contrast, added a 0.6% basic software service fee in September 2024, increasing the burden on merchants
3. **Price competitiveness has been lost**: The average price of Pinduoduo's TOP10 bestsellers is 30%-40% of Taobao's. The low-price mindset is now firmly occupied by Pinduoduo

**But the flywheel has not completely stopped:**

- FY2025 Q4 Taobao and Tmall CMR (customer management revenue) was +12%, indicating a higher monetization rate
- Penetration of full-site promotion tools continues to rise, showing that merchants are still willing to advertise on Taobao and Tmall
- Taobao still has advantages in quality e-commerce and brand e-commerce, and Tmall brand merchants face relatively high switching costs
- Taobao Flash Purchase has entered instant retail, reaching a 45.2% share in 2025 Q4, close to Meituan, opening up a new growth pole

**Key judgment from the Duan Yongping perspective:**

> "If a company's moat needs to be maintained with subsidies, then it is not a real moat."

Taobao and Tmall's flywheel is not dead, but it has shifted from "self-accelerating" to "externally supported." Specifically, the improvement in monetization is coming more from charging merchants higher fees (the 0.6% technology service fee) than from scale effects driven by organic GMV growth. This is "extractive" growth rather than "flywheel" growth, and it is not sustainable over the long term.

## III. Moat-by-Moat Verification

### 3.1 Brand Moat: ★★★☆ (3/5, and weakening)

- **Taobao and Tmall have extremely high brand awareness**, but the meaning of the brand is changing: from "Taobao has everything" to "not cheap enough and not fast enough"
- Tmall still provides brand endorsement value for brand merchants, but Pinduoduo's "10-billion subsidy" has already entered the branded authentic-goods category
- Alibaba Cloud has a strong brand in the government and enterprise market, but consumer AI applications (Tongyi Qianwen with 30 mn MAU) lag far behind ByteDance's Doubao (197 mn MAU)

**Contrary evidence:** The brand did not prevent GMV share from falling from 51% to 31%. If brand were truly a moat, this degree of share loss should not have happened.

### 3.2 Switching Costs: ★★★☆ (3/5, with large differences by segment)

**High-switching-cost groups:**
- Tmall brand flagship-store merchants: They have already built store operations, membership systems, and CRM systems, making migration costly
- Alibaba Cloud enterprise customers: Data migration and system rebuilding costs are huge, making this Alibaba Cloud's strongest moat

**Low-switching-cost groups:**
- Small and medium-sized merchants: They can operate on Taobao, Pinduoduo, and Douyin at the same time, so actual switching costs are almost zero
- Consumers: They have Taobao, Pinduoduo, and Douyin installed on the same phone, and price comparison takes only a few seconds

**Key insight:** E-commerce platforms naturally have low switching costs. When Duan Yongping says he "does not know where the moat is," this is the core reason - consumers have no loyalty to the platform and go wherever prices are lower.

### 3.3 Network Effects: ★★★☆ (3/5, already diluted)

- Two-sided network effects still exist: merchants come because there are consumers, and consumers come because there are products
- But these network effects have been heavily diluted by Pinduoduo and Douyin - merchants and consumers are both "multi-homing"
- The key metric for network effects is "exclusivity." When all merchants open stores on all platforms, network effects no longer constitute a moat
- Taobao and Tmall still have relatively strong network effects in specific categories (such as apparel and beauty brands), but they no longer have an advantage in standardized goods (3C, daily necessities)

### 3.4 Scale Effects: ★★★★ (4/5, the most solid moat)

- Alibaba's technology infrastructure (Taobao + Tmall transaction systems, payment systems, logistics network) has accumulated over many years and has significant scale effects
- Cainiao's network covers more than 200 countries and regions worldwide, which is difficult for new entrants to replicate
- Alibaba Cloud is China's largest public cloud, and scale advantages bring cost advantages
- But scale effects also mean "an elephant is slow to turn" - organizational inertia and low decision-making efficiency have been among the most criticized issues at Alibaba over the past few years

### 3.5 Technology Barriers: ★★★☆ (3/5, AI is a positive factor but not decisive)

- Tongyi Qianwen's open-source models have exceeded 600 mn global downloads and 170,000+ derivative models, making it one of the world's leading open-source model portfolios
- Alibaba Cloud has a 35.8% share of the AI cloud market, exceeding the combined share of the No. 2-4 players
- But Alibaba has a large gap at the consumer AI application layer (Tongyi Qianwen APP MAU 30 mn vs Doubao 197 mn)
- ByteDance's Volcano Engine accounts for 46.4% of public-cloud large-model API calls, posing a direct threat to Alibaba Cloud

### Overall Moat Assessment: Has It Widened or Narrowed Over the Past 3 Years?

**Conclusion: Overall, it is narrowing, but with structural divergence.**

| Dimension | Trend | Explanation |
|------|------|------|
| E-commerce moat | Clearly narrowing | Share fell from 51% -> 31%; this is not merely "narrowing," it is being breached |
| Cloud computing moat | Widening | With the AI wave, share leadership plus scale and technology advantages are expanding |
| Instant retail | Under construction | Taobao Flash Purchase went from zero to 45% share, but it is subsidy-driven and no moat has been established yet |
| International e-commerce | Uncertain | It has first-mover advantages but faces intense competition from Temu/SHEIN |

> Duan Yongping's original 2023 remark: "I cannot see through e-commerce. I do not know where the moat really is, and I do not know who can still live relatively well 10 years from now."

That judgment still holds today. Alibaba's core contradiction is: **the most profitable business (Taobao and Tmall) has a narrowing moat, while the business with a widening moat (Alibaba Cloud) has not yet become the main profit engine.**

## IV. User/Customer Value Analysis

### 4.1 Value to Merchants

| Platform | Core Value Proposition | Merchant Cost | Suitable Merchant Types |
|------|------------|---------|-------------|
| Taobao and Tmall | Full-category coverage, brand endorsement, data tools | Relatively high (0.6% service fee + advertising spend) | Brand merchants, mid-to-large merchants |
| Pinduoduo | Low threshold, high traffic, extreme value for money | Relatively low (many commission-free policies) | White-label merchants, industrial-belt factories |
| Douyin E-commerce | Content-traffic dividend, hit-product logic | Medium (but customer acquisition cost down 10x) | Merchants with content capabilities |
| JD.com | Quality assurance, logistics experience | High (self-operated model) | Brand merchants, 3C digital products |

**Taobao and Tmall's core value to merchants is being squeezed:**
- Full-site promotion tools improve advertising efficiency, but in essence they "make merchants spend more money to buy traffic"
- Multi-platform operation has become the norm; Taobao and Tmall are no longer a "must-have" but merely "one option among many"
- The 0.6% technology service fee added in 2024 improved monetization in the short term, but may accelerate merchant outflow over the long term

### 4.2 Value to Consumers

| Platform | Core Value Proposition | Weaknesses |
|------|------------|------|
| Taobao and Tmall | Broadest category coverage, authentic branded goods, mature shopping experience | Not cheap enough, delivery not fast enough |
| Pinduoduo | Extremely low prices, social sharing growth | Uneven quality, weaker after-sales service |
| Douyin E-commerce | Content-driven discovery, immersive shopping | High return rates, unplanned consumption |
| JD.com | Fastest logistics, quality assurance | Limited categories, higher prices |

**Taobao's consumer value positioning is stuck in a "sandwich layer" dilemma:**
- Want cheap? It is not as good as Pinduoduo
- Want fast? It is not as good as JD.com or Meituan Flash Purchase
- Want fun? It is not as good as Douyin
- Want quality? Tmall has an advantage, but JD.com is also catching up

Taobao Flash Purchase is a meaningful breakthrough point - entering instant retail (45.2% share in 2025 Q4, close to Meituan) and providing consumers with a new value proposition of "30-minute delivery." But this share was purchased with subsidies, and the profit model has not yet been proven.

### 4.3 Where Is the Differentiation?

**Differentiation Taobao and Tmall still have:**
1. The broadest product pool (SKU count remains No. 1 in the industry)
2. Trust endorsement from Tmall brand flagship stores
3. The most mature merchant operation tools and data system
4. Cross-platform benefits from the 88VIP membership system

**But these differentiators are shrinking, not expanding.** Pinduoduo's 10-billion subsidy is entering branded authentic goods, Douyin e-commerce is expanding across categories, and JD.com is opening up to third-party sellers - competitors are all eroding Taobao and Tmall's differentiated territory.

## V. Business Matrix and Synergies: Real Synergy or Pseudo-Synergy?

### Real Synergies Already Achieved

1. **Taobao Flash Purchase + Ele.me = instant retail**: This is the most successful synergy case. After resources were integrated, daily orders exceeded 40 mn, with a 2026 target of becoming the absolute market leader. Consumers can order food delivery and flash purchases inside the Taobao APP, and the traffic reuse is real
2. **Alibaba Cloud + Taobao and Tmall data = AI training advantage**: The massive data generated by e-commerce scenarios provides unique data resources for Tongyi Qianwen training
3. **Cainiao + international e-commerce (AIDC)**: Cainiao's cross-border logistics network directly supports fulfillment capabilities for AliExpress and Lazada

### Pseudo-Synergies or Weak Synergies

1. **Synergy between digital media and entertainment and e-commerce**: The synergy between Youku, Damai, Hujing Digital Media and Entertainment, and e-commerce has long been "PowerPoint synergy" - talked about for many years, with little substantive effect. The value of digital media and entertainment to Alibaba is more like "strategic defense" than "synergistic value creation"
2. **Relationship between Amap/DingTalk/Quark and the core businesses**: These businesses are classified under "All Others," with limited synergy with e-commerce and cloud. DingTalk has some synergy with Alibaba Cloud (enterprise customer cross-selling), but limited standalone value
3. **Alibaba Health/Hema and others**: These businesses look more like "diversified investments" than a "synergy matrix"

### Judgment From the Duan Yongping Perspective

> "When a company says it has synergies, you need to be especially careful. Real synergies do not need to be talked about; fake synergies get talked about the most."

**Alibaba synergy score: ★★★ (3/5) - there are both real and fake synergies, but the proportion of "real synergy" is improving.**

During the 2023 "1+6+N" split-up period, synergies were at their weakest, with independent operations across businesses causing internal resource fragmentation. In 2025-2026, the structure was streamlined into four major segments and synergy was re-emphasized. The integration of Taobao Flash Purchase + Ele.me is a positive signal. But synergy among the "four major segments" (for example, between China e-commerce and Alibaba Cloud) is still at the level of "technical support" and is far from reaching the "profit flywheel" role AWS plays for Amazon.

## VI. Assessment Against Duan Yongping's "Good Business" Standard

### 6.1 Differentiation (★★★, 3/5)

- Taobao and Tmall have differentiation in brand e-commerce, but it is being eroded
- Alibaba Cloud has differentiation in China's public-cloud market (No. 1 share + AI leadership), which is the strongest point of differentiation
- Tongyi Qianwen has differentiation in open-source large models (the world's No. 1 open-source model portfolio), but its consumer application lags
- Overall, Alibaba's differentiation is "broad but shallow" rather than "narrow and deep"

### 6.2 Pricing Power (★★☆, 2.5/5)

**Areas with pricing power:**
- Tmall's advertising pricing for brand merchants: Because brand merchants need Tmall's brand endorsement and traffic, there is some short-term pricing power
- Alibaba Cloud for government and enterprise customers: Data migration costs are high, creating some lock-in effect

**Areas without pricing power:**
- Small and medium-sized merchants: Under multi-platform competition, merchants vote with their feet, and Taobao's additional 0.6% technology service fee has already caused dissatisfaction
- Consumers: Consumers have no loyalty to platforms and go wherever prices are lower
- Instant retail: It relies on subsidies to grab share and has no pricing power at all

**Duan Yongping values pricing power most, and this is precisely where Alibaba is weakest.** An e-commerce platform is essentially an intermediary, and an intermediary's pricing power depends on the alternatives available on both sides - when merchants and consumers both have many alternatives, the intermediary has weak pricing power.

### 6.3 Sustainable Competitive Advantage (★★★, 3/5)

- Alibaba Cloud + AI's competitive advantage is relatively sustainable: scale advantages + technical accumulation + customer lock-in
- E-commerce competitive advantage is not sustainable: the share decline over the past 5 years has already proven this
- Instant retail competitive advantage does not yet exist: share bought with subsidies is not a competitive advantage

### Overall Duan Yongping "Good Business" Rating: ★★★ (3/5)

**Alibaba is not a "good business" as defined by Duan Yongping.** The reasons are as follows:

1. Not focused enough: The business is too dispersed. Even after being streamlined into four major segments, it still spans e-commerce, cloud computing, instant retail, globalization, logistics, digital media and entertainment, and other fields
2. Weak pricing power: The nature of an e-commerce intermediary limits pricing power
3. The moat is narrowing in the core business: The most profitable Taobao and Tmall business continues to lose share
4. It requires continuous large capital investment: AI infrastructure, instant retail subsidies, and international expansion all require ongoing cash burn

This also explains Duan Yongping's actual actions: increased Alibaba holdings in 2024 -> gradual reduction in 2025 (Q3 position cut by more than 25%, followed by further reduction in Q4). He expressed his stance through his actions.

## VII. Deep Dive Into Alibaba Cloud + AI Strategy

### 7.1 Competitiveness of Tongyi Qianwen

**Advantages:**
- Leading open-source ecosystem: 600 mn global downloads, 170,000 derivative models, 12% global share on OpenRouter (fourth)
- Qwen3 series includes 8 open-source models, covering all specifications and modalities
- Deployment advantage in the B-end/enterprise market (leveraging Alibaba Cloud's customer base)

**Disadvantages:**
- Consumer application MAU of 30 mn vs ByteDance's Doubao at 197 mn, a 6.5x gap
- ByteDance's Volcano Engine accounts for a 46.4% share of large-model API calls, and is invading the cloud layer from the application layer
- The rise of DeepSeek has diverted attention within the open-source ecosystem

**Key risk:** ByteDance could replicate the path of "Douyin disrupting Taobao" - entering from the application layer and reverse-eroding Alibaba Cloud's underlying infrastructure market. Volcano Engine has already become a threat in AI cloud.

### 7.2 Alibaba Cloud's Growth Prospects

**Positive data points:**
- FY2026 Q4 external revenue +40%, the fastest growth in 9 quarters
- AI product revenue annualized at RMB 35.8 bn, with triple-digit growth for 11 consecutive quarters
- AI products exceeded 30% of cloud external revenue for the first time
- Management has pledged that "cloud and AI investment over the next three years will exceed the total of the past ten years"

**Risks to watch:**
- The acceleration in Alibaba Cloud's growth comes largely from an explosion in AI inference demand; whether this growth is sustainable depends on the overall development of the AI industry
- ByteDance's Volcano Engine and Huawei Cloud are both accelerating their catch-up
- AI cloud competition may evolve into a "price war" (price cuts for scale), compressing margins
- Daily token consumption data at the end of 2025: Doubao 630 tn tokens/day, with scale advantages shifting toward ByteDance

### 7.3 AI's Impact on Alibaba Overall

**Positive impact:**
- Cloud Intelligence is becoming a new growth pole and may become the group's second-largest profit source within 3-5 years
- AI empowers Taobao and Tmall (smart recommendations, intelligent customer service), improving operating efficiency
- Tongyi Qianwen's open-source strategy can build a developer ecosystem and indirectly drive Alibaba Cloud subscriptions

**Neutral/negative impact:**
- AI infrastructure investment is enormous and suppresses short-term profit (FY2026 full-year net income RMB 105.9 bn vs FY2025 comparable net income of roughly RMB 110 bn+, with no significant profit growth)
- AI's effect on e-commerce is not yet obvious - has AI helped Taobao regain the share lost to Pinduoduo/Douyin? There is no evidence so far

## VIII. Contrary Test: Is the Moat Really Being Repaired?

The market's mainstream narrative is "Alibaba has bottomed and rebounded." Let us test this point by point:

### Narrative 1: "Taobao and Tmall have stabilized"

**Supporting evidence:**
- FY2025 Q4 CMR +12%, FY2026 Q4 comparable CMR +8%
- Monetization rate continues to improve
- Taobao Flash Purchase has opened a new battlefield

**Contrary evidence:**
- CMR growth mainly comes from price increases (0.6% service fee + higher full-site promotion pricing), not organic GMV growth
- GMV share is still declining (31%, with no trend reversal)
- FY2026 full-year revenue grew only +3% YoY (+11% on a comparable basis), and traditional e-commerce growth remains sluggish after excluding instant retail
- Price-increase-driven revenue growth is essentially "passing costs on to merchants," which will accelerate merchant outflow over the long term

**Judgment: Taobao and Tmall's "revenue stabilization" and "moat repair" are two different things.** Revenue can be supported in the short term by price increases, but moat repair requires GMV share to stop falling and rebound, and that has not happened.

### Narrative 2: "Alibaba Cloud + AI is the second growth curve"

**Supporting evidence:**
- External revenue +40%, AI product revenue annualized at RMB 35.8 bn
- No. 1 share in China's AI cloud market, exceeding the combined share of No. 2-4

**Contrary evidence:**
- Cloud Intelligence's full-year revenue is about RMB 130 bn, only 12% of the group. To "support the valuation," it needs to reach at least 20-25%, which would take another 3-4 years at 20% growth
- ByteDance's advantage at the AI application layer may transmit to the cloud layer
- Huge capital expenditures suppress short-term profit

**Judgment: The direction is right, but "the second curve becoming the main engine" takes time and is not yet a fact.**

### Narrative 3: "Management's aggressive buybacks indicate undervaluation"

**Supporting evidence:**
- FY2025 buybacks of USD 11.9 bn, shareholder return yield of 6.5% (the highest among large Chinese ADRs)
- USD 19.1 bn remaining under the buyback program, valid through March 2027
- Sale of non-core assets such as Intime/Sun Art Retail, with capital refocused

**Contrary evidence:**
- Buybacks themselves do not prove the stock is cheap; they only show management believes it is cheap
- Alibaba is simultaneously investing heavily in AI infrastructure ("the next three years will exceed the past ten years"), creating large capital needs
- Buybacks and heavy capital expenditure are running in parallel, so it is necessary to monitor whether free cash flow is sufficient to support both

**Judgment: The buyback intensity is indeed large, but the key is whether FCF can support both buybacks and AI investment.** If buybacks are reduced in the future due to excessive AI investment, the market may reprice the stock.

### Narrative 4: "Organizational restructuring has released efficiency"

**Supporting evidence:**
- The company moved from "1+6+N" to four major segments, reducing internal fragmentation
- The integration of Taobao Flash Purchase + Ele.me is an effective synergy case

**Contrary evidence:**
- Alibaba has experienced frequent organizational restructurings over the past three years (split-up -> reintegration), and organizational instability itself is a cost
- Businesses such as "Digital Media and Entertainment," "Cainiao," and "Amap" have been put into "All Others," turning it into a basket of "things we do not yet know how to handle"

**Judgment: The restructuring direction is correct, but frequent adjustment itself reflects management's wavering on strategic direction.**

## IX. Core Conclusion

### The Essence of Alibaba's Business Model

Alibaba is a **large conglomerate transforming from an "e-commerce empire" into a dual-engine company built around "AI cloud + consumption"**. The transformation direction is right, but the transformation is far from complete.

### Evaluation From Duan Yongping's "Good Business" Perspective

| Assessment Dimension | Rating | Explanation |
|---------|------|------|
| Business model clarity | ★★★ | The business is dispersed, but the focus on "e-commerce + cloud + AI" is now clear |
| Differentiation | ★★★ | Broad but shallow; competitors are catching up in every field |
| Pricing power | ★★☆ | E-commerce intermediaries naturally lack pricing power |
| Moat trend | ★★☆ | Core e-commerce is narrowing, cloud computing is widening, overall narrowing |
| Flywheel effect | ★★★ | The flywheel is still spinning but has slowed, maintained by external force |
| User/customer value | ★★★ | Full category coverage is an advantage, but the "sandwich layer" dilemma is obvious |
| Business synergy | ★★★ | Both real and pseudo-synergies exist, and it is improving |

**Duan Yongping's likely judgment: "Cannot see through it."** In fact, this is precisely why he gradually reduced his position in 2025. Alibaba's problem is not that the businesses are bad, but that the moat is hard to predict - you do not know whether Taobao and Tmall can still defend 30% share in 5 years, whether Alibaba Cloud can remain a leader in the AI era, or whether instant retail can become profitable after the subsidies run out. Too many "unknowns" are a fundamental problem for value investors who seek certainty.

### Three Key Variables to Keep Tracking

1. **Whether Taobao and Tmall GMV share can stop falling**: If it continues to decline below 25%, the "cash cow" status may be at risk
2. **Whether Alibaba Cloud AI revenue can exceed RMB 50 bn in FY2027**: This is the key validation point for whether the "second curve" can support the group's valuation
3. **Whether instant retail can show a profit inflection point in the second half of 2026**: If a cash-burning business shows no hope of profitability, it will become a long-term drag
