## RMB 400 Billion in Revenue, Almost No Profit Contribution

The previous two articles broke down Alibaba's two core businesses: Taobao and Tmall as the profit engine, and Alibaba Cloud as the growth option.

This article examines the hardest part of Alibaba to explain: the "imperial frontier" businesses that are not core, yet account for nearly 40% of revenue. International e-commerce (AIDC), Cainiao Logistics, Digital Media and Entertainment, DingTalk, Freshippo, and others together generate nearly RMB 400 billion in revenue, more than JD Logistics' full-year revenue.

But this RMB 400 billion produces almost no profit. These businesses are either losing money, barely profitable, or have already been sold at a loss.

The collective silence of the frontier businesses is part of Alibaba's ten-year capital allocation bill. This article breaks them down one by one to judge which still have value and which are burdens that should be resolved.

## 1. Alibaba International Digital Commerce (AIDC): From Cash Burn to Stopping the Bleeding

### 1.1 What Is AIDC?

AIDC (Alibaba International Digital Commerce) includes:
- **AliExpress**: Cross-border e-commerce, focused on Europe, Latin America, and the Middle East
- **Lazada**: Southeast Asian e-commerce platform
- **Trendyol**: Turkish e-commerce platform (Alibaba owns 86%), Alibaba's most profitable overseas asset
- **Daraz**: South Asian market
- **Miravia**: Spanish market

### 1.2 Financial Profile

| Metric | FY2025 | FY2026 | Change |
|------|--------|--------|------|
| Annual revenue | RMB 132.3 billion | **RMB 144.2 billion** | +9% |
| Q4 quarterly revenue | - | RMB 35.4 billion | +6% |
| Q4 adjusted EBITA | - | Loss of RMB 138 million | **Substantially narrowed** |
| FY2025 full-year loss | ~RMB 15.1 billion | - | - |

*Data source: Alibaba FY2026 Q4 earnings report*

**Key signal: AIDC is moving from "large losses" toward "break-even."** In FY2026 Q1, the quarterly loss narrowed to RMB 59 million, almost flat. The Q4 loss was only RMB 138 million. If this trend continues, break-even in FY2027 is possible.

### 1.3 Competitive Landscape: A Four-Way Brawl

| Platform | 2025 global GMV (estimated) | Core markets |
|------|-------------------|---------|
| Amazon third-party | ~USD 750 billion | Global |
| Shopee (Sea) | ~USD 130 billion | Southeast Asia + Brazil |
| **Alibaba AIDC** | **~USD 80-90 billion** | Global (fragmented) |
| Temu (PDD) | ~USD 70-80 billion | Europe, the United States + global |
| SHEIN | ~USD 50 billion | Europe, the United States + Middle East |
| TikTok Shop | ~USD 35-40 billion | Southeast Asia + Europe and the United States |

*Data source: Marketplace Pulse 2026Q1 and estimates from company reports*

**Alibaba AIDC's problem is "regional fragmentation and no global network effect":**
- AliExpress has advantages in Europe + Latin America, but faces intense pricing pressure from Temu
- Lazada is second in Southeast Asia (25-28% share), but trails far behind Shopee (45-50%), while TikTok Shop is catching up quickly
- Trendyol is number one in Turkey and is expanding into the Middle East - this is the asset with the highest certainty
- Each region has a different competitive logic, making it hard to build a unified global flywheel

### 1.4 Impact of U.S.-China Relations

- In 2025, the United States ended the "de minimis" rule, hitting the U.S. businesses of Temu, SHEIN, and AliExpress
- AIDC's main battlefield has already shifted from the United States to "non-U.S. markets": Southeast Asia, the Middle East, Latin America, and parts of Europe
- Cross-border compliance costs continue to rise (EU DSA, data localization requirements across countries)

### 1.5 Assessment

| Dimension | Neutral forecast |
|------|---------|
| FY2028 break-even | Highly likely (current trend is already close) |
| Revenue in ten years | RMB 300-530 billion (15% CAGR) |
| Profit margin in ten years | 4-6% (based on Shopee's steady state) |
| Operating profit in ten years | **RMB 12-22 billion** |
| Strategic positioning | Can contribute profit, but **unlikely to succeed Taobao and Tmall** |

**AIDC will not become the next Taobao and Tmall. It is more like a portfolio of regional e-commerce assets.** Trendyol is the most valuable piece. The AliExpress Choice model is improving unit economics. Lazada is likely to be marginalized, but it will not disappear.

## 2. Cainiao Logistics: Fulfillment Backend or Standalone Asset?

### 2.1 How Cainiao's Positioning Has Changed

Cainiao was once Alibaba's spin-off candidate with the greatest imagination, nearly completing an independent IPO in 2023. But the IPO was halted in 2024, and Cainiao was reintegrated into the group and folded into the AIDC system.

**The downgrade from "independent listing candidate" to "AIDC's fulfillment department" is a major signal.**

### 2.2 Financial Profile

| Metric | FY2025 |
|------|--------|
| Annual revenue | RMB 101.3 billion (+2%) |
| EBITA | RMB 300 million (-78%) |

*Data source: Alibaba FY2025 annual report*

### 2.3 Competitive Position

Cainiao's advantage lies in **cross-border logistics**: a logistics network spanning more than 200 countries and regions, with broad overseas warehouse coverage. This is core fulfillment capability for AIDC (AliExpress and Lazada).

But in the domestic logistics market, Cainiao is far from the leader:
- JD Logistics: self-built integrated warehousing and delivery, best user experience
- Tongda operators (ZTO, YTO, Yunda, etc.): lowest prices and largest scale
- J&T Express: the fastest-growing challenger
- SF Express: leader in premium express delivery

### 2.4 Assessment

**Logistics is not a good business by nature**: gross margins of 5-10%, capital intensity, and no pricing power. Duan Yongping almost never invests in logistics companies.

Cainiao's value is that it is the **necessary infrastructure** that keeps AIDC's cross-border e-commerce running. Without Cainiao's logistics network, AliExpress and Lazada's fulfillment capability would deteriorate sharply.

**Cainiao should be viewed as AIDC's "cost department," not as an independent profit center.** In ten years, annual profit may be in the RMB 5-13 billion range, not a large amount.

## 3. Digital Media and Entertainment (Hujing Digital Media): The Largest Historical Burden

### 3.1 The Troubled History of Alibaba Digital Media and Entertainment

Alibaba's investments in media and entertainment are a textbook case of failed cash burn:

| Acquisition/investment | Time | Investment | Result |
|---------|------|------|------|
| Youku Tudou | 2016 | ~RMB 27 billion | Long-term losses; ranked third/fourth in long-form video |
| Xiami Music | 2013 | - | Shut down in 2021 |
| Alibaba Pictures | 2014 | ~RMB 17 billion | Long-term losses followed by modest profit |
| Damai | 2017 | - | Barriers in ticketing market; one of the few bright spots |

In a March 2026 Stanford speech, Joe Tsai candidly admitted that Alibaba paid a "more than RMB 100 billion price" for strategic misjudgments. Digital Media and Entertainment was the largest part of that bill.

### 3.2 Current Status

- Officially renamed "Hujing Digital Media and Entertainment" in 2025
- FY2025 full-year scale of about RMB 22.3 billion, only 2% of the group
- FY2025 still posted a full-year loss of RMB 550 million, but Q4 had turned profitable (Youku returned to profit)
- Damai has a monopoly advantage in live performance ticketing and is the most valuable asset in the segment

### 3.3 Assessment

**Digital Media and Entertainment is one of Alibaba's biggest investment mistakes in history.** The content industry's nature is "hit-driven luck and diseconomies of scale," completely different from Alibaba's strength in platform + traffic models.

Joe Tsai has admitted the mistake, but the RMB 100 billion-plus price has already been paid.

The most likely outcome for Digital Media and Entertainment in ten years is **further downsizing, spin-off, or merger into another business.** It will not be a profit source for Alibaba; at best, it will "stop losing money."

## 4. DingTalk: An Underrated Potential Winner

DingTalk may be the most underrated asset in Alibaba's "Other" segment:

| Metric | Data |
|------|------|
| Users | 700 million+ |
| ARR | >USD 200 million (FY2025 first half) |
| Domestic collaborative office share | Number one |
| Synergy with Alibaba Cloud | Cross-selling to enterprise customers |

DingTalk's network effects are real. Once an enterprise deploys DingTalk as its internal collaboration tool, employee migration costs become very high. This is similar to Alibaba Cloud's enterprise customer lock-in.

**But DingTalk's problem is that it is too small.** ARR of USD 200 million is less than 0.5% of Alibaba's trillion-RMB revenue base. Even if it grows at 30% for five years, revenue would only be RMB 6-7 billion.

In ten years, DingTalk may become a "decent SaaS business," but its impact on Alibaba's overall valuation will be minimal.

## 5. Exited Businesses: The Price of Admitting Mistakes

Alibaba completed two major divestitures in 2024-2025:

| Asset | Sale time | Sale price | Loss |
|------|---------|--------|------|
| Intime Department Store | 2024.12 | RMB 7.4 billion | ~RMB 9.3 billion |
| Sun Art Retail (RT-Mart) | 2025.1 | HKD 13.1 billion | ~RMB 13.0 billion |
| **Total loss** | | | **~RMB 22.5 billion** |

This was the **first time in Alibaba's history that it acknowledged "non-core businesses" at scale and actively exited them**.

Seen positively: admitting mistakes takes courage, and cutting losses is better than continuing to burn cash.
Seen negatively: a RMB 22.5 billion loss is real money, proving that Alibaba's past "buy, buy, buy" capital allocation strategy generated very low returns.

## 6. Overall Assessment of Frontier Businesses: Assets or Burdens?

| Business | Asset or burden | Neutral profit forecast in ten years | Keywords |
|------|-------------|------------------|--------|
| AIDC (international e-commerce) | **Asset** (bleeding is stopping; growth potential remains) | RMB 12-22 billion | Regionalized; hard to dominate |
| Cainiao | **Neutral** (AIDC infrastructure; not valuable independently) | RMB 5-13 billion | Cost department |
| Digital Media and Entertainment | **Burden** (shrinking; historical losses of RMB 100 billion+) | RMB 0-1 billion | Should be resolved quickly |
| DingTalk | **Potential winner** (but too small) | RMB 2-5 billion | Good business, immaterial |
| Instant retail | **Strategic investment** (short-term black hole, long-term defensive asset) | RMB 0-5 billion | See Part 02 |
| Freshippo | **Awaiting divestiture** (retail is not a good business) | Modest profit or loss | Awaiting disposal |

**Combined neutral profit forecast for the frontier businesses in ten years: about RMB 20-45 billion.**

Set against Alibaba Group's total profit pool of RMB 400-500 billion, this would account for no more than 10%.

**Conclusion: the frontier businesses as a whole are shifting from "large loss-making burdens" to "modestly profitable auxiliaries." The trend is right, but they will not become the support pillar for Alibaba's valuation.** Alibaba's valuation story ultimately comes back to the two core assets: Taobao and Tmall + Alibaba Cloud.

## 7. Duan Yongping's "Focus" Test

Duan Yongping values "focus" above all. Good businesses are usually focused.

Alibaba's 2026 business map includes e-commerce, cloud computing, instant retail, internationalization, logistics, media and entertainment, SaaS, local services, AI foundation models, and more.

Even after shrinking into four major segments, it still spans at least 7 different industries. Joe Tsai himself admitted that pitching the six major business groups to investors was "stupid."

The good news: management is subtracting - selling Intime, selling Sun Art, shrinking Digital Media and Entertainment, folding Cainiao into AIDC, and folding Ele.me into Taobao and Tmall.

The bad news: the subtraction is still not fast enough. Freshippo has not been sold, and Digital Media and Entertainment has not been fully resolved. At the same time, while subtracting, Alibaba is also adding heavily in instant retail and AI, with combined annual cash burn of more than RMB 200 billion.

**"Adding and subtracting at the same time" is Alibaba's biggest strategic tension today.**
