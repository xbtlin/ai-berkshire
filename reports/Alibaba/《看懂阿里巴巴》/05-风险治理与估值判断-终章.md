## Returning to the Most Fundamental Question

After four articles, the assets are laid out clearly: Taobao and Tmall are highly profitable but their moat is narrowing; Alibaba Cloud is accelerating but its margin is only one-quarter of AWS's; the frontier businesses are moving from heavy losses to modest profit, but they do not change the valuation.

Putting these together, Alibaba Group's neutral profit forecast in ten years is about **RMB 350-460 billion**: RMB 240-280 billion from Taobao and Tmall, RMB 80-140 billion from Alibaba Cloud, and RMB 20-45 billion from frontier businesses. Alibaba will very likely still exist ten years from now.

*Source: ai-berkshire 10-year profit projection report*

But "still existing" and "worth holding" are two different things. The finale asks only one question: **At the current price, is the money you pay for this company reasonable?** To answer that, we must first assess management credibility, the true scale of the risks, and the logic of the valuation itself.

## 1. Management: Who Is at the Helm?

### 1.1 Eddie Wu: The Return of a Technical Founding Veteran

| Dimension | Assessment |
|------|------|
| Background | One of the "18 founders," employee number six, engineer by training |
| Authority | Concurrently serves as Group CEO + Taobao and Tmall CEO + Alibaba Cloud CEO |
| Direction | Dual strategy of "user first + AI-driven"; direction is correct |
| Risk | One person managing three trillion-RMB-scale businesses; an extremely wide management span |
| Key decisions | Reorganizing into four major segments, RMB 380 billion AI investment, full push into Taobao Flash Purchase |

**Positive assessment:** Eddie Wu's technical background allows him to understand the essence of the AI strategy rather than be fooled by buzzwords. As a founding veteran, he has enough internal authority to drive change. FY2026's strategic direction (focus + AI + instant retail) is the clearest in the past three years.

**Negative assessment:** One person simultaneously holding three number-one roles has rarely succeeded in business history. His management style leans toward delegation, which may be insufficient in the e-commerce and instant retail battlefields that require refined operations. After he took concurrent responsibility for Taobao and Tmall, GMV growth did not improve meaningfully.

### 1.2 Joe Tsai: Capital Allocation Master

Joe Tsai is the person in Alibaba's management team who most reassures investors.

**Positives:**
- FY2025 buybacks of USD 11.9 billion, one of the largest in Chinese ADR history
- Total buyback authorization increased to **USD 65 billion**, valid through March 2027
- Personally increased Alibaba holdings by USD 150 million through the Blue Pool fund
- In a March 2026 Stanford speech, publicly admitted: "Alibaba's biggest mistake was forgetting who the real customer was" and "pitching the six major business groups to investors was stupid"

This kind of courage to publicly admit mistakes is extremely rare among Chinese entrepreneurs.

**Negatives:**
- The premise of admitting mistakes is that these mistakes have already caused a "more than RMB 100 billion price"
- Acquisitions of poorly integrated assets such as Youku, Ele.me, RT-Mart, and Intime were all completed with Joe Tsai's involvement
- Alibaba's capital allocation over the past five years has been **value destructive** overall (compared with Tencent's investment portfolio, which continued to appreciate over the same period)
- Admitting mistakes does not equal creating value; it only stops the bleeding

### 1.3 Jack Ma: The Subtle Return of the Spiritual Leader

- Became active again in 2025: attended a private-enterprise symposium, joined an Alibaba Cloud kickoff meeting, and appeared on Alibaba's campus
- The Jack Ma family increased its shareholdings and may have surpassed SoftBank to become Alibaba's largest single shareholder
- The market interpreted this as a signal of "normalization of political relations"

**But investment should not depend on the halo of any individual.** A good company should continue to operate well after its founder has fully stepped away.

### 1.4 Duan Yongping's Stance

Duan Yongping is one of the most useful barometers for judging a "good business." His actions:

| Time | Action |
|------|------|
| 2024 | Increased Alibaba holdings |
| 2025 Q3 | **Reduced position by more than 25%** |
| 2025 Q4 | Continued reducing holdings |

Duan Yongping's actions show his view: **In his eyes, Alibaba is not an asset that is "obviously so cheap that one must buy it."** His 2023 comment that he "could not fully understand e-commerce" still holds today.

## 2. Governance Structure: The Double-Edged Sword of the Partnership System

### 2.1 System Design

The Alibaba Partnership (currently 36 people) has the right to nominate a majority of the board. Jack Ma and Joe Tsai are the only two "permanent partners."

**This means external shareholders have almost no governance voice.**

### 2.2 Advantages

- Ensures strategic stability for the founding team
- Avoids short-term pressure from activist capital
- Allows management to execute long-term strategies such as the "RMB 380 billion AI investment"; under a shareholder voting system, this plan might be rejected

### 2.3 Risks

- When management decides to burn RMB 86 billion on instant retail and spend RMB 380 billion on AI, external shareholders cannot stop it
- No sunset clause: the control structure will never automatically terminate
- Many historically low-return investments (Youku, Ele.me, RT-Mart, etc.) were completed under this governance structure
- If management makes the wrong judgment, shareholders have no correction mechanism

**For value investors, the partnership system is a risk factor that must be priced in.** If Alibaba's intrinsic value is X, the partnership system should carry at least a 10-20% discount.

## 3. Risk List

### 3.1 Competitive Risk (The Largest Known Risk)

- Taobao and Tmall's share could continue falling from 37% to 25-30%
- Douyin E-commerce GMV may catch up with Taobao and Tmall in 2028-2029
- ByteDance's Volcano Engine is rapidly catching up with Alibaba Cloud
- The three-way instant retail war (Alibaba vs Meituan vs JD) could continue for 3-5 years

### 3.2 Capital Expenditure Risk

- RMB 380 billion AI investment + RMB 86 billion instant retail losses: two simultaneous cash-burn lines
- FY2026 free cash flow has already turned negative at RMB 46.6 billion
- If returns on AI investment fall short of expectations, buyback and dividend capacity will be squeezed
- Joe Tsai himself warned that there may be a bubble in AI data centers

### 3.3 Geopolitical Risk

- U.S. SEC delisting reviews of Chinese ADRs
- VIE structures being questioned for "fraud risk"
- Alibaba has completed a dual primary listing on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange, reducing reliance on a single market
- But the risk remains that U.S. institutional investors may be forced to reduce holdings and valuation discounts may widen

### 3.4 Regulatory Risk

- The RMB 18.2 billion antitrust fine in 2021 has passed, and the current regulatory tone leans toward "promoting development"
- But Chinese regulation is characterized by "policy reversibility": today's encouragement does not guarantee there will be no tightening tomorrow
- The instant retail subsidy war has already drawn regulatory attention (public criticism from Economic Daily in March 2026)
- Ant Group's financial holding company license remains unresolved

### 3.5 Macro Risk

- China's CPI remained low in 2024-2025, and consumption was weak
- If deflation persists for more than 5 years, e-commerce + local services + cloud would all come under pressure at the same time
- This is the **most systemic risk** and is almost impossible to hedge

3. The holding company discount in the Chinese market is usually 20-30% (10-15% in developed markets).

## 5. Margin of Safety Judgment

### 5.1 Evidence Supporting "A Margin of Safety Exists"

1. **Net cash of RMB 389 billion** (FY2025 year-end), about 17% of market cap - provides a hard floor
2. **PE of 17x, 47% below the historical average** - if you believe Alibaba will not disappear, valuation repair potential exists
3. **FY2025 shareholder returns of USD 16.5 billion** - USD 11.9 billion in buybacks + USD 4.6 billion in dividends, annualized shrinkage of 5-6% of the float
4. **Alibaba Cloud accelerating growth** - external revenue +40%, AI revenue share above 30%
5. **Management admitting mistakes + focusing** - selling non-core assets, streamlining the structure, founder increasing holdings

### 5.2 Evidence Supporting "Value Trap"

1. **Non-GAAP net income plunged 62%** - whatever the reason, profits are disappearing
2. **Free cash flow turned negative by RMB 46.6 billion** - RMB 380 billion AI investment + RMB 86 billion Flash Purchase losses are burning cash at the same time
3. **Taobao and Tmall's share is still falling** - the core business moat is narrowing
4. **ROE only 13%** - low among internet companies; large cash balances and inefficient investments drag down returns
5. **Fighting three wars at once** - e-commerce defense, instant retail offense, and AI investment; history has almost no examples of winning all three
6. **Duan Yongping is reducing his position** - the person who best understands "good businesses" is selling

### 5.3 Conclusion

**Alibaba is closer to a "transition company with a margin of safety" than to a classic "value trap."**

Reasons:
- The hallmark of a classic value trap is "profits are disappearing irreversibly"; Alibaba's profit decline is a management choice, not a disappearance of demand
- Net cash + aggressive buybacks provide protection that "even if the judgment is wrong, downside is limited"
- Alibaba Cloud's accelerating growth provides an option where "if the judgment is right, upside is large"

**But the margin of safety is not large enough.**

Using a 30% margin of safety standard (buy price <= 70% of intrinsic value):
- Neutral intrinsic value of about USD 153 x 70% = **USD 107**
- Current USD 134.58 -> **not in the "obviously cheap" range**
- If it pulls back to **USD 100-110** (around HKD 100 for Hong Kong shares), the margin of safety would rise to 30-40%, making it more worth acting on

## 6. Thinking Framework for Different Risk Preferences

The following is not "operating advice." It is a framework for how investors with different risk preferences may view Alibaba:

| Risk preference | Core focus | Signal to wait for |
|---------|-----------|--------------|
| **Can accept 2-3 years of profit decline** | Long-term ROI of AI investment | Whether Alibaba Cloud revenue growth can sustain 30%+ |
| **Need to see an earnings inflection point** | When free cash flow resumes positive growth | Instant retail losses narrow + capex growth slows |
| **Strictly require a margin of safety** | Whether current price is far below conservative value | Alibaba Cloud margin breaks above 15%; Taobao and Tmall share stops falling |

### Add Signals

1. Taobao and Tmall GMV share stops falling sequentially and rebounds
2. Alibaba Cloud AI revenue quarterly growth remains 30%+
3. Instant retail EBITA losses narrow and an earnings inflection point appears
4. Free cash flow returns to positive growth
5. Ant Group IPO makes substantive progress

### Trim Signals

1. Taobao and Tmall CMR growth turns negative (price increase strategy fails)
2. Alibaba Cloud share is further eroded by Volcano Engine / Huawei Cloud
3. RMB 380 billion AI investment fails to produce a clear revenue acceleration
4. Buyback intensity declines significantly
5. Regulatory environment for Chinese ADRs deteriorates

## 7. Final Judgment

Alibaba is a company **in the middle of a strategic transition from "breaking the old" to "building the new."**

**"Old"**: Taobao and Tmall's e-commerce moat is being continuously eroded by PDD and Douyin. It has lost 14 percentage points of share in four years, and this leakage may be structural.

**"New"**: Alibaba Cloud is showing strong momentum. AI cloud share is far ahead at 35.8%, and revenue growth is 34%. But the margin is only 9%, and the ROI on the RMB 380 billion investment is highly uncertain.

**"Cost"**: FY2026 Non-GAAP net income plunged 62%, free cash flow turned negative by RMB 46.6 billion, and Alibaba is simultaneously fighting the e-commerce defense war + instant retail offensive war + AI investment war.

**"Safety cushion"**: PE of 17x is 47% below the historical average, net cash is RMB 389 billion, and FY2025 buybacks + dividends were USD 16.5 billion.

Putting these together:

> **Alibaba is not a "great business that makes money effortlessly." It is a position that "bets on a successful transformation."** You need to accept 2-3 years of profit decline in exchange for potential valuation re-rating after a successful transformation. If you are unwilling to wait, or if you do not believe Alibaba Cloud can replicate AWS's path to success, this is not the right stock for you.

Ten years from now, Alibaba could look like one of several scenarios:

| Scenario | Description |
|------|------|
| Bull | AI cloud becomes the core engine, annual revenue exceeds USD 100 billion; e-commerce share stabilizes above 30%; market cap above USD 500 billion |
| Base | E-commerce share slowly declines but profits remain stable; cloud becomes the second-largest profit source; continued buybacks; market cap of USD 300-400 billion |
| Bear | E-commerce share falls below 25%; AI investment fails to generate sufficient returns; becomes a "Yahoo Japan"-style business |
| Extreme | Forced breakup / delisting / major regulatory blow |

*Source: Li Lu-perspective risk assessment report*

The probability of each scenario depends on your judgment of Taobao and Tmall's moat and Alibaba Cloud's execution capability. This is not a math problem, and there is no standard answer.

In the base scenario, holding Alibaba for ten years would generate annualized returns of about 8-12% (including buybacks and dividends). That is not bad, but it is not outstanding either.

**If you ask "is it worth holding," the answer depends on your opportunity cost**: whether you have a higher-certainty alternative with a return rate of at least 10%. If you do, Alibaba may not be the optimal choice. If you do not, Alibaba's combination of "safety cushion + AI option" is worth considering.
