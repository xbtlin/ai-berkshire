# II. BIG COLOR: Why HBM is the ALI chip 'Munter'

# 2.1 Why is GPU in the HBM

The core bottlenecks of large language models and generation AI are not arithmetic, but a "storage wall" - moving parameters from memory to computing core time and energy consumption far beyond actual mathematical calculations themselves.

A standard DDR5 tape bandwidth of about 50-100GB/s. A HBM3E stack can provide a bandwidth of 1.2 TB/s -- exactly above a level of magnitude. HBM vertically stacks multiple layers of DRAM chips through silicon perforation (TSV) technology, and then tightens the GPU envelope through silicon intermediaries, reducing the physical distance from data removal.

There is not enough HBM, and GPU is an engine that is not fed. The picture itself tells us everything by writing "Please Make More" on Computex in 2026.

#2: #2.2 Why supply should not be needed until 2028-2030

Three structural causes have led to chronic demand for HBM:

** First, the manufacturing is extremely complex.** HBM is not a simple DRAM, it requires high-precision chip stacking, mixing keys, advanced encapsulation, etc.

** Second, production capacity crowding effect.** TrendForce estimates that HBM producing 1GB will consume the equivalent of 4GB standard DRAM crystal area. HBM, while accounting for only 8% of global storage output (in bits), has contributed about 30% of DRAM revenue. Production capacity is tilted towards HBM, further exacerbating the overall DRAM market shortage.

** Third, expansion takes time.** The M15X factory of SF Hercules was inaugurated in October 2025 and will not be fully operational until mid-2027 (6,000 crystals per month). The dragon-in semiconductor cluster is expected to start in 2027. The P5 plant of Samsung will not be operational until 2028. This means that the new production will not be able to keep pace with the outbreak of AI demand until 2028.

SHG Chairman Choi Tae-won stated on Computex 2026 that the supply and demand of the AI-related storage units "may continue to be in 2030." The entire 2026 HBM capacity of SK Hercules has been sold out, and customers have more demand for HBM than the company's planned capacity for the next three years.

# Six, risk factors

# 6.1 DRAM cyclical - the greatest known risk

The memory industry is a regularity of objective facts. The lesson from 2023 is not far: the industry has only one year from a boom to a huge loss. But a distinction needs to be made between the pricing mechanism for HBM products (long-term contracts, customized supplies) and the consumer-level DRAM (staff pricing, bid competition).

** Negative argument:** If AI’s investment slows down systematically (like the 2000 Internet bubble burst), HBM demand could suddenly break off the cliff. But, at present, the global technology giant’s AI capital spending is still accelerating, with the total capital spending of the cloud plants projected to exceed $300 billion in 2026.

# 6.2 Three stars catching - medium-term competitive risk

Three-star HBM4E samples lead the SK Hercules for about six months, showing that the three stars are closing the gap in technology in the next generation. If the three stars break out in the HBM4E production phase, market share patterns may change in 2027-2028.

** Mitigation:** Even if the three stars are technically leveled, the depth of the KK Hercules and British Weida (70% Rubin share) provides a buffer period of 1-2 years. Supply chain trust is not built overnight.

#6.3 Client concentration

In the first half of 2025, British Weida contributed about 27% of the gross income of SK Hercules. In 2026, Q1, this percentage fell to 14.8% (approximately 7.78 trillion won), while a new major client (purchase of 6.54 trillion won, or 12.4%), was created, presumably by Microsoft or Google ' s self-researching AI chip project.

But in the short term, the top 1-2 British Weida clients will still contribute more than 30% of their income. British Weida’s own business situation and capital spending decisions have a direct impact on the SK Hercules.

#6.4 Geopolitics - the uncertainty of Chinese factories

The US removed the status of Korean manufacturer VEU (certification end-user) at the end of 2025 and changed to an annual licensing system. Although the 2026 licence was granted, two important restrictions were imposed: (1) that China’s plant capacity not be allowed to expand; and (2) that it not be allowed to upgrade to more advanced technology nodes.

This means that SK Hercules must gradually transfer advanced production capacity (especially HBM-related) from China, increasing the pressure on capital spending in the medium term. Worse still, if China-US relations deteriorate further, the annual licensing system may tighten or impose additional conditions.

# 6.5 Capital spending swells

Capital spending in 2025 is 30.2 trillion won, and in 2026 is projected to be "a significant increase" over 2025 (the market is expected to exceed 30 trillion won). The M15X plant invests 19 trillion won, and the Long-Indign Cluster will need several hundred and twenty-one billion won. If AI’s demand slows down, and HBM prices fall, huge capital spending will become a heavy financial burden.
