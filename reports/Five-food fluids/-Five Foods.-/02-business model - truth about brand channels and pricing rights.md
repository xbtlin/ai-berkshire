## What Kind of Money Does Wuliangye Make?

A company spends twice as much on selling expenses as its competitor, yet earns only 40% of that competitor's profit. What does that tell us? It suggests the company is using money to prop up a brand whose appeal is fading. That is the core contradiction in Wuliangye's business model.

Start with the revenue mix and let the data answer the question.

### Revenue Breakdown (2024 Annual Report, Pre-Adjustment Basis)

| Product category | Revenue (RMB 100mn) | Share | Gross margin |
|---------|-----------|------|--------|
| **Wuliangye products** | 679 | **76.1%** | **87.02%** |
| Series liquor (Wuliang Spring/Wuliang Mellow, etc.) | 153 | 17.1% | 60.81% |
| Other businesses | 60 | 6.8% | — |
| **Total** | **892** | 100% | **77.05%** |

*Source: Wuliangye 2024 Annual Report*

**76% of revenue comes from one product: the Eighth-Generation Wuliangye.** This is Wuliangye's lifeline.

Looking more closely (based on industry research estimates, not official disclosure):

| Sub-product | Estimated revenue (RMB 100mn) | Notes |
|---------|-------------|------|
| Eighth-Generation Puwu (52% ABV) | **~540-560** | Core single product, about 60% of total revenue |
| Classic Wuliangye series (10/20/30/50) | ~40-50 | Ultra-premium line |
| Wuliangye 1618 | ~30-40 | 2024 growth of 20%+ |
| Total series liquor | 153 | Mid- to low-end, high volume and thin margins |

**A counterintuitive data point**: sales volume of the series liquor is **3 times** that of Wuliangye products (120,000 tons vs 40,000 tons), but it contributes only **17%** of revenue. On a per-ton basis, Wuliangye products are priced at more than **13 times** the series liquor.

Liquor made from the same grains becomes worth more than ten times as much once the name "Wuliangye" is attached. That is the power of brand premium.

**But the issue is this**: is that brand premium supported by real consumer demand, or is it maintained by pushing inventory onto distributors? The RMB 30.3bn revenue inflation incident suggests the answer may not be all that reassuring.

## Pricing Power: The Most Important and Most Controversial Question

If we had to use one metric to judge a baijiu company's brand strength, it would be **the relationship between ex-factory price and wholesale market price**.

### Eighth-Generation Puwu Price System (2025-2026)

```
Ex-factory price (nominal)  →  Invoice price (actual)  →  Wholesale market price  →  Suggested retail price
        RMB 1,019                    RMB 900                     RMB 850                    RMB 1,399
                                (cut from 2026)             (fluctuation range)          (nominal only)
```

Key price points:

| Price point | Amount (RMB/bottle) | Notes |
|---------|-------------|------|
| Ex-factory price (nominal) | **1,019** | Unchanged since 2024 |
| Distributor invoice price | **900** | Cut from 2026, including RMB 119 subsidy |
| Distributor actual cost | ~800-850 | After rebates |
| Wholesale market price | **~850** | 2026Q1 data |
| Suggested retail price | 1,399 | No meaningful channel sells at this price |

*Sources: The Paper, 21jingji.com, channel research data*

### Core Problem: Price Inversion

**Price inversion** means the distributor's procurement price is higher than the market selling price. The distributor loses a little money on every bottle sold.

| Period | Distributor cost | Wholesale price | Profit/loss per bottle |
|------|----------|------|---------|
| 2025 first half | ~RMB 920 | ~RMB 850 | **-RMB 70** |
| 2025 second half | ~RMB 920 | ~RMB 810-830 | **-RMB 90 to -RMB 110** |
| 2026 (after invoice price cut) | ~RMB 800-850 | ~RMB 850 | **RMB 0 to +RMB 50** |

For all of 2025, distributors were basically losing money on every bottle of Puwu they sold. After the invoice price was cut in 2026, they were barely breaking even or earning a small profit.

**What does this mean?**

If a brand's distributors suffer long-term losses, there are only two possibilities:
1. Distributors are "riding along" by making money from Wuliangye's series liquor or other products, while Wuliangye itself serves as a traffic driver
2. Distributors are leaving; in 2025, Wuliangye's distributor count fell by a net **90**

Reality is probably a mix of both.

### Compared With Moutai: Real Pricing Power vs Pseudo Pricing Power

| Product | Ex-factory price | Wholesale price | Premium/inversion |
|------|--------|------|----------|
| Feitian Moutai | RMB 1,169 (before March 2026) → RMB 1,269 | **~RMB 1,560** | **+33% premium** |
| Eighth-Generation Puwu | RMB 1,019→900 | ~RMB 850 | **-6% to -17% inversion** |

*Sources: Sichuan Daily New Media (Moutai wholesale price), channel research (Puwu wholesale price)*

The gap is obvious:

- **Moutai**: ex-factory price of RMB 1,269 after the March 2026 increase, wholesale price of RMB 1,560; the channel earns **~RMB 300** per bottle. Distributors compete for allocation.
- **Wuliangye**: ex-factory price of RMB 1,019, later reduced to RMB 900, wholesale price of RMB 850; the channel **loses RMB 50-100** per bottle. Distributors are dumping inventory.

**The essence of pricing power is not the price you set. It is whether the market is willing to pay that price.** Moutai sets RMB 1,269, and the market gives it RMB 1,560. Wuliangye sets RMB 1,019, and the market gives it only RMB 850.

Wuliangye cut its invoice price to RMB 900 in early 2026. This was its first official price cut in nearly a decade. The previous comparable episode dates back to the deep baijiu industry adjustment in 2014.

**Positive view**: the price cut was a proactive strategic choice: narrowing the gap between ex-factory price and wholesale price, protecting channel profit, and "giving up profit in the short term to stabilize the system over the long term."

**Negative view**: if Wuliangye truly had pricing power, why cut the price? The market is voting with its feet. Wuliangye currently does not have pricing power above RMB 1,000.

## Brand Strength: Twice Moutai's Selling Expenses, Less Than Half the Profit

### Selling Expense Comparison

| Metric | Wuliangye | Moutai | Gap |
|------|--------|------|------|
| 2024 selling expenses | **RMB 10.7bn** | ~RMB 5.5bn | **Wuliangye is 2 times Moutai** |
| Selling expense growth | **+37.15%** | Single digits | Wuliangye is accelerating spending |
| Selling expense ratio | ~12% | ~3.5% | **3.4 times** gap |
| Net profit | RMB 31.9bn | ~RMB 82.3bn | Moutai is **2.6 times** Wuliangye |

*Sources: company 2024/2025 annual reports*

**Wuliangye spent twice as much as Moutai on selling expenses, yet earned only 40% of Moutai's profit.**

What does this show?

- **Moutai is a "pull" business**: the brand's natural demand is extremely strong and needs almost no marketing
- **Wuliangye is a "push" business**: it requires sustained, heavy spending to maintain sell-through

The trend is even more worrying. In 2024, Wuliangye's selling expenses surged **37%** year over year. Spending more and more money to maintain sales means the brand's natural pull is weakening.

### Quantifying Brand Value

| Metric | Wuliangye | Moutai | Gap |
|------|--------|------|------|
| Brand value (2025 Tsinghua ranking) | **RMB 306.0bn** | **RMB 662.6bn** | Moutai is **2.2 times** Wuliangye |
| Market cap (2026.5) | ~RMB 346.0bn | ~RMB 1,760.0bn | Moutai is **5 times** Wuliangye |

*Source: Brand Finance 2025*

The brand value gap has widened from 1.5 times historically to 2.2 times. The market-cap gap has expanded even more, from 3 times to 5 times.

**Is the trend improving or deteriorating? The answer is deteriorating.** Over the past decade, Moutai's brand value compound growth rate has been about **12%**, while Wuliangye's has been only about **2%**. The gap is widening at an exponential rate.

## Moat Checklist: What It Has and What It Lacks

### What Wuliangye Has

| Moat type | Strength | Explanation |
|-----------|------|------|
| Brand recognition | ★★★★ | The leading brand in the thousand-yuan price band; "king of strong-aroma baijiu" |
| Ancient cellar clusters | ★★★★ | Ming-dynasty cellars continuously used for **600 years+**; older cellars produce better liquor |
| Locked-in consumption occasions | ★★★ | A "safe choice" for business banquets and weddings |
| Scale effects | ★★★★ | Annual sales of **160,000 tons**, far above Moutai's ~50,000 tons |
| Financial safety cushion | ★★★★★ | **RMB 127.4bn** in cash and zero interest-bearing debt |

### What Wuliangye Lacks

| Dimension | Problem | Severity |
|------|------|---------|
| Pricing power | Wholesale price has long been below ex-factory price; first forced price cut in nearly a decade | ★★★★★ |
| Consumer mind monopoly | "The first choice below Moutai," but highly substitutable | ★★★★ |
| Management integrity | Two chairmen fell from office + RMB 30.3bn inflation | ★★★★★ |
| Product scarcity | Strong-aroma production has lower process barriers than sauce-aroma production; capacity is not constrained | ★★★ |
| Financial attributes | Moutai has investment and collectible attributes; Wuliangye has almost none | ★★★ |

### Is the Moat Widening or Narrowing?

| Evidence of narrowing | Data |
|-----------|------|
| Brand value gap widening | Moutai/Wuliangye ratio expanded from 1.5 times to 2.2 times |
| Loss of pricing power | First invoice price reduction in nearly a decade |
| Selling expenses soaring | +37% in 2024; more money required to sell product |
| Competition intensifying | Guojiao 1573's wholesale price once converged with Puwu's |
| Governance collapse | Two consecutive chairmen investigated |

| Evidence still supporting the moat | Data |
|-----------|------|
| Leader in the thousand-yuan price band | No one can replace it in the short term |
| Ancient cellars cannot be replicated | 600-year cellar age is the core quality barrier for strong-aroma baijiu |
| Consumption occasions remain sticky | Business banquets still treat it as a "safe choice" |
| Cash reserves | RMB 127.4bn, enough to last several years even with zero revenue |

**Overall judgment: the moat is slowly narrowing, from a "wide moat" to a "medium moat."** It has not collapsed, but it is no longer as secure as it once was.

**On the other hand**, industry downturns are exactly the best time to test the true strength of a moat. If Wuliangye can complete channel destocking and management renewal during this cycle, the moat could widen again. Wuliangye faced a similar predicament during the 2014 deep baijiu adjustment and took 3 years to recover.

## Channel Reality: Are Distributors Still Okay?

### Channel Inventory

| Metric | Data | Assessment |
|------|------|------|
| Inventory balance (2025H1) | **RMB 17.7bn** | +5% from the start of the year |
| Distributor actual inventory | Estimated **2-3 months** | Above the company's disclosed standard of 1.5 months |
| Net decrease in distributors (2025) | **-90** | Active cleanup + natural attrition |

*Sources: Wuliangye annual reports, channel research*

### Cross-Regional Selling

Wuliangye launched a "digital liquor certificate" system to trace product flows, but cross-regional selling remains serious:
- E-commerce platform subsidized prices as low as **RMB 820-860**
- Some offline channel reports cite extreme low prices of **RMB 640-750**
- Large supermarkets and e-commerce platforms have become "price destroyers"

### Vicious Channel Cycle

```
Wholesale price inversion → Distributor losses → Urgent inventory dumping to recover cash → More cross-regional selling → Further wholesale price decline
```

**Positive view**: after the 2026 invoice price cut, the inversion has narrowed, and post-Spring Festival channel feedback indicates inventories fell by more than **30%**. The worst may already be over.

**Negative view**: channel reform is essentially "paying down old debts." In prior years, the company over-pushed inventory to distributors in pursuit of revenue growth, and now it must pay a larger price to digest that inventory. Moutai never needs to do this, because demand exceeds supply.

## A Critical Question: What Are Consumers Really Buying?

Return to the most fundamental question: why do consumers buy Wuliangye?

**Moutai's consumer mindshare**:
- "China's best baijiu": no need for a second choice
- "Social hard currency": gifting Moutai requires no explanation
- "Investment/collectible": it has financial attributes

**Wuliangye's consumer mindshare**:
- "The best choice below Moutai": with one condition attached
- "Value-for-money premium liquor": a substitute when Moutai is unavailable or unaffordable
- "Safe choice": will not embarrass the buyer, but will not surprise anyone either

**The problem lies in the qualifier: "below Moutai."**

This positioning is both Wuliangye's safety zone, because consumers naturally trade down when Moutai is too expensive, and its ceiling, because it is always the "second-best" choice. More importantly, in the "below Moutai" position, Wuliangye's differentiation advantage is being eroded by Guojiao 1573. The wholesale price gap between the two has narrowed from RMB 200 historically to almost zero.

**If consumers start to feel that "Wuliangye and Guojiao are about the same," then the most important part of Wuliangye's moat has cracked.**
