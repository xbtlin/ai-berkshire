## II. Moat Assessment -- Buffett's "Economic Moat"

### Item-by-Item Validation

| Moat Type | Rating | Evidence |
|-----------|------|------|
| **Brand/pricing power** | ★★★☆☆ Medium | Puwu's ex-factory price rose from RMB 609 in 2014 to RMB 1,019 in 2024; however, 2025 wholesale prices fell to RMB 780-810, creating a severe price inversion. **It can raise list prices but cannot hold market prices**; pricing power does not pass the test |
| **Switching costs** | ★★☆☆☆ Relatively weak | Baijiu brand loyalty is limited, and the psychological barrier for consumers to switch from Wuliangye to Guojiao 1573 is low. Unlike Moutai, it has not become the "default choice" in high-end occasions |
| **Network effects** | ★☆☆☆☆ Nonexistent | Consumer goods do not have network effects |
| **Scale effects** | ★★★☆☆ Medium | Its number-two industry scale provides procurement and distribution advantages, but baijiu production is not scale-driven; old-pit capacity is the bottleneck |
| **Production barriers (old fermentation pits)** | ★★★★☆ Relatively strong | 179 Ming-dynasty fermentation pits (650+ years), 679 pits older than 100 years, and 3.30 million pits across vintages. Each gram of old pit mud contains more than 100 billion microorganisms, and new pits require decades to mature, making them non-replicable. However, Luzhou Laojiao also owns Ming-dynasty pits |

### Moat Gap Versus Moutai

| Dimension | Moutai | Wuliangye |
|------|------|--------|
| Category exclusivity | Sauce-aroma leader; category = brand | Strong-aroma segment has many competitors (Luzhou Laojiao/Jiannanchun/Yanghe) |
| Price performance | Feitian wholesale price has long stayed above the guide price (still RMB 1,800+ in 2025) | Puwu wholesale price has long been below the ex-factory price, creating price inversion |
| Social-currency attribute | Extremely strong; "giving Moutai" is the default social option | Exists but weaker than Moutai; a "substitutable second choice" |
| Capacity scarcity | Unique climate of Moutai Town + Chishui River water source, a geographic monopoly | Old pits are scarce, but Yibin does not have geographic exclusivity |

### Moat Trend

**Past 5 years: narrowing.** The price gap between Puwu and Feitian has continued to widen (from RMB 500 to RMB 1,000+), and the gap in brand power is increasing. In the strong-aroma segment, the premiumization pushes by Fenjiu and Luzhou Laojiao are eroding Wuliangye's share.

**Next 5 years: likely to remain under pressure.** The industry's "one superpower, several strong players" structure is becoming further entrenched, and Wuliangye faces an awkward position: "unable to reach Moutai above, chased from below."

### Moat Width Judgment: **Narrow Moat**

Wuliangye has real competitive barriers (old pits + accumulated brand equity), but they do not amount to a wide moat. The core evidence is that **wholesale prices remain below ex-factory prices, indicating supply exceeds demand and brand premium is being eroded.** A consumer company with a truly wide moat should not see persistent price inversion.

> **Buffett-style question**: Will this moat still exist 10 years from now? What could destroy it?
> The old pits will not disappear, and the brand will not go to zero, but the moat may continue to narrow. The greatest threat is not a specific competitor, but **structural contraction in high-end baijiu demand** (young people not drinking baijiu) plus **Moutai's continued siphoning of high-end mindshare**. Wuliangye will probably still be a top-three industry player in 10 years, but "top three" and "worth investing in" are two different things.

## III. Inversion and Risk List -- Munger's "Invert, Always Invert"

### Failure-Path Analysis

| Risk Path | Probability | Impact | Explanation |
|---------|------|------|------|
| Structural decline in high-end baijiu demand | Medium-high | High | Only 31% of millennials drink baijiu, and industry output has declined for 8 consecutive years |
| Continued governance deterioration | Medium | High | Two consecutive chairmen were removed for corruption, the new appointee has no industry experience, and incentives are lacking |
| Channel inventory builds again | Medium | Medium-high | 2025 exposed 6-8 months of inventory. Although it has been reduced to 1 month, the impulse to stuff channels is embedded in SOE performance-assessment mechanisms |
| Moutai attacks from above | Low-medium | High | If Moutai cuts ex-factory prices or sharply increases volume, Wuliangye's pricing room would be completely compressed |
| Anti-corruption/alcohol-ban policy escalation | Low | Medium | Government-related consumption is already below 5%, so marginal impact is limited |
| Consumption-tax reform | Low-medium | Medium-high | If baijiu consumption tax moves from the production end to the retail end, channel margins may be squeezed |

### Historical Analogy

**Comparable case: the 2013-2015 baijiu winter.** After the Eight-Point Regulation, Wuliangye's share price fell from its 2012 high of RMB 36 to its 2014 low of RMB 15 (down 58%), and net profit fell from RMB 99.4 (100m) in 2012 to RMB 69.7 (100m) in 2014 (down 30%). It then took 5 years to recover and surpass the prior high.

**Key difference**: The 2013 shock was policy-driven (cutting government-related consumption), and business and personal consumption filled the gap after the recovery. The 2025 shock is structural (slowing demand growth + young people not drinking baijiu), making the recovery path more uncertain.

> **Munger-style question**: Why would smart people not buy?
> 1. Growth has already slowed to single digits, limiting valuation-repair upside
> 2. SOE governance is a structural problem, not something that can be solved by replacing one person
> 3. The industry's "falling volume, rising price" logic may have run its course: volume is falling, and prices have begun to fall too
> 4. At the same price, Moutai is a much higher-quality business than Wuliangye
