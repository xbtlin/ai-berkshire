# VII. GENERAL CONCLUSIONS

# Core judgment

1. **The `good company'**. Five years of CAGR 19.6%, ROE > 15% long term, brand value Chinese tyres first, and the leading industry in global production and distribution of power - these are objective facts supported by data. Companies collect tyres for research, production, sale, and service as one, and are the largest Chinese tyre industry overseas.

2. ** The fall in profit in 2025 was a cyclical fall, not a deterioration in competitiveness**. The decline in the Māori rate from 28 per cent to 25 per cent was mainly due to the rise in the cost of raw materials and trade frictions, while the revenue and market share continued to grow (+15.7 per cent). The recovery of Māori in 2026Q1 to 26.86 per cent has confirmed this finding — the latter being the key evidence of a low profit rate that is only a phased one.

3. ** The current valuation (PE 12.5x) is within reasonable range**. There is no significant underestimation (e.g. 10 x PE in 2022) or overestimation (e.g. 25 x in 2021). The neutral scenario has a potential return of about 21 per cent (6.5 per cent annually) and about 10-12 per cent annually for five years. The probabilities weighted expectation is $16.7, 23.8 per cent higher than the current price.

4. ** The security margin is not large enough but is reasonable in terms of investment prices**. Graham-style depth discounts do not exist (which need to fall below $9.7). However, as a growth-oriented value unit, 12.5 x PE is associated with 15% + growth, PEG < 1, which has a reasonable investment logic.

5. ** The greatest risk comes from external rather than internal**: the systemic deterioration of globalized trade policies (e.g. the US full tariff addition to South-East Asia, the Euro-American Union anti-dumping) which would destabilize the core strategic logic of corporate overseas deployment. This is the most uncertain variable of the moment.
