The global tyre market is larger than **235 billion United States dollars** (source: Chinese Rubber magazine), and there are several structural features that are of concern:

** First, demand is almost permanent. When a car runs on the road, the tires change. The replacement of the tires accounts for **70% ** of global demand for tyres, which means that even if the new car sales drop, the demand for the tires does not break the cliff.

# Large cycle of natural rubber: an undecorated cost variable

The discussion of the profit prospects for the round cannot avoid the price of natural rubber.

Natural rubber accounts for **30-40% of the cost of tyre raw materials**, the single most important variable affecting the Maori rate of tyre enterprises.

*Source: Research manuscript citing industry data*

The industry has a disturbing judgement:** Natural rubber may be entering a 20-year cycle up-to-date period**.

The reason is that the global supply and demand gap for natural rubber is about **400,000 tons** (source: research background quotes industry data), while the need for new rubber plantations from planting to rubber can be cut ** 6-7 years** and the supply end response is very slow.

If this is true, it means:

1. The Maori hub of the round may be moved down from 27-28 per cent in 2023-24 to **22-24 per cent**
2. Higher costs can raise barriers to entry in industries, favouring leading firms (for wheels)
3. All tyre enterprises will be squeezed, but those with more cost-control capacity (proximity of overseas plants) will be less affected

The wheels have two buffers in dealing with the cost of raw materials: the Cambodian plant, which is close to the natural rubber production zone and has lower procurement costs, and the company ' s hedge system, which has forward lock prices. These are buffers, which cannot be fully hedged.

** This variable is beyond the control of the race wheel management.** It is an external risk that requires investors to determine their probability.

# Ten years later, the race round: the top five of the world is still a big crowd?

Do a bold push: If everything goes well, is the race round 10 years from now likely to enter the top five of the world's tyres?

** Positive path**:

- Five bases overseas are fully productive, double their annual capacity.
- Global branding of liquid gold technology
- OTR Country Substitute, top three globally
- The annual harvest break **80-100 billion yen**
- Top 5-7 places in the world.

** Pessimistic path**:

- Global trade protectionism is extremely deteriorating, and overseas factories cannot avoid customs duties
- Natural rubber is long-standing high, and the Maori rate is down to 20%.
- "Overseas Intra-Coop" has led to a return to domestic profitability in overseas factories.
- Inadequate capacity of replacements and shifting strategic direction
- Growth in the harvest slowed to a single-digit level, and profits continued to decline

The study's working paper gives an estimate of: **The probability of a race round entering the top five globally after 10 years is about 35-40%. ** This probability is not high, but it is not low given that the global tyre industry is undergoing a round of structural changes in "China Substitution".

** The core variable is not in the race cycle itself – it is in the global trading environment.** If the trading environment remains current or moderate, the global layout of the race cycle will continue to deliver on its value. If the trade environment is extremely deteriorating (a global trade war), the core logic of the race round will be shaken.

# This core judgment

1. ** Global tyre patterns are moving from "tripocratic monopoly" to "multipolarization".** The global share of Chinese enterprises is increasing steadily, and this trend is likely to be irreversible in 5-10 years ' perspective.

2. ** "Standing out of the sea" is not an option for Chinese tyre companies, but is essential for survival. The road from China to Europe and America is largely blocked.

** New energy tyres are the next structural opportunity**. The liquid gold technology of the wheels matches the needs of electric cars and is available with an accompanying order, but there is still a significant gap in the international brand OEM.

4. ** The boom in natural rubber is a medium- and long-term profit pressure**. Overseas plants in the race wheels provide a cost buffer but cannot be fully hedged.

5. ** The greatest uncertainty arises from the global trading environment**. This is the core variable that determines the value of the round and the least predictable one.
