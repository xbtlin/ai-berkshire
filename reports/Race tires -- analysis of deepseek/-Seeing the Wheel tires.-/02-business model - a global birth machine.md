# Non-road tyres: undervalued profit engines

Non-road tyres are the most valuable business in the race wheels.

** First, the technical barriers are high**. The mining can be in a 4 m diameter, with tons of heavy tyres, working at extreme temperatures and load conditions. The race round produced the largest global-sized 53/80R63 in 2016, and began to be laid in 2009, with more than 15 years of experience. This is not an area where money can be quickly pursued.

** Second, the customer is sticky**. Mining companies, construction companies, once certified through a branded tyre, are extremely expensive to replace -- because each replacement requires a new safety test and performance test. The wheels are already matched with global head customers like Caterpillar, John Dier, Kashnoe Holland, Triple, and mining giants like Koto are using the wheel tires.

** Third, the national production alternative space**. The global non-road tyre market has long been monopolized by Michelin and Priston. Chinese firms are in the "0 to 1" breakthrough in this area, and the race wheels are the longest in the country.

** Reverse view**: The specific revenue and profit ratio of the OTR business is not broken down in detail in the annual round-up paper. The above-mentioned 30% Māori + is based on industry estimates of the target. If mining cycles (e.g., commodity prices have fallen sharply), OTR demand will fall sharply – a business that may be more cyclical than a semi-styre.

# Liquid gold: The real technology moat or marketing concept?

"Little Golden Tyres" is the most loud label of the race wheels in recent years. What is this?

# Technical nature

Traditional tyre manufacturing is "physical smelting" -- mixing solid rubber with fillings through mechanical mixing. The problem is that fillings are unevenly distributed, leading to tyres being only "three options" on three indicators: rolling resistance, humid-slamping, grinding -- industry called the "demon triangle."

The "liquid gold" technology of the race round is chemically refined -- using liquid phase processes that allow fillings to be evenly integrated with rubber at the molecular level, thus optimizing at the same time on three indicators. This technology was first presented at the International Tyre Technical Conference in Hanover in 2016, when the product was officially produced in 2021.

# Objective assessment

Liquid gold technology is ** substantial innovation**, not pure marketing concepts. TUV certification is a third-party authentication, AUTOBILD is one of the most authoritative tires assessment media in the world, and it's not something that can be bought for money.

But you have to calm down and see two points:

** First, the consumer feels weak**. Most car owners change their tires with a decision path that is "brand <unk> price <unk> size" instead of "what kind of glue process is this fetish." Liquid gold has a reputation in the professional world, but brand premium in the general consumer is far less than "silent" or "persistent" in Michelin.

** Second, there is doubt about the sustainability of technical barriers**. The tyre industry as a whole has a slow technological succession, but the research and development of giants such as Michelin and Pristom are dozens of times more expensive than the wheels. If liquid-combined technologies prove to be of commercial value, the giants cannot afford to ignore them. The time window for the wheels could be **5-10 years**, not permanent.

# A complete profit logic map

The business model of the round, when combined with the above, can be understood as follows:

```
Procurement of raw materials (natural rubber, carbon black etc.)
    ↓
Production and manufacture (Aoshima/East Battalion + Five Bases Overseas)
    ↓
Three product lines (half steel + full steel + non-road)
    ↓
Sales channels
*- Replacement market (70% + demand) * Dealers * Terminal owner/car fleet
- OEM - Main plant (Biadi, Jili, etc.)
```

** Three sources of profit**:

1. ** Volume growth**: Sustained release of overseas production capacity (Mexico, Indonesia, slope to be built in Egypt) to support continued double-digit growth in the harvest
2. ** Structural upgrading**: IQ, large-sized semi-steed, non-road tyres, increasing overall Māori ratio
3. ** Cost arbitrage**: raw materials from overseas plants + labour force + three-tier advantage of customs

** Three threats to profits**:

1. ** Cost of raw materials**: Natural rubber as a percentage of cost 30-40%, price uncontrollable
2. ** Trade barriers**: tariff "crowding" is extending from China to South-East Asia
3. **Overseas in volume**: China-based tyre enterprises have gone out to sea collectively and competition has moved from home to overseas

# Capital expenditure: the cost of expansion

The round is currently in a capital-intensive period** of overseas capacity-building. Estimated annual average capital expenditure **2.5-3.5 billion yuan** (source: research draft calculations), significantly higher than depreciation (estimated at 1-1.5 billion million/year).

That means two things:

1. **The free cash flow period is very tight**. In 2024, operating cash flows amounted to approximately $2.26 billion, and the free cash flow after deducting capital expenditure was close to zero or even negative. In 2025, operating cash flows improved to about $4.14 billion, but reduced capital expenditure by about $3 billion, while free cash flows also amounted to about 1.1 billion** — compared to a net profit of $3.5 billion, with a low rate of free cash flow conversion.

2. ** This is a proactive strategic investment, not a passive deterioration**. The round is investing the money earned in new productive capacities in Mexico, Indonesia, and Egypt, in exchange for tomorrow’s production and market share in cash flows.** The question is: ** Will these investments eventually yield sufficient returns?**

** Inverse view**: The persistently large capital expenditure is typical of the "capital black hole". The tyre industry is not like the Internet -- the factory needs to be maintained and upgraded on a continuous basis. If the industry goes down the cycle and hits the peak in capacity-building, the balance sheet of the racer will be under great pressure. The mobility ratio in 2025 was only **1.13**, and the speed ratio was only **0.66** -- which is tight in manufacturing enterprises.

# This core judgment

1. **The business model for the wheels is clear but structurally weak**. Tyres are made in a business that is asset-intensive, high-cycle, low-conversion costs, with weak pricing authority. The wheels are relatively excellent in this industry, but the industry ' s own ceiling determines that it is not a great business.

2. **The overseas distribution of production capacity is the biggest competitive advantage of the races. ** Five bases on five continents, with five to 10 years of experience, have the strongest tariff acquitting industry. But this advantage is being faced with "circumstance" - trade barriers extend to South-East Asia + the "overseas in volume" brought by the collective movement of middle-income firms.

** Liquid gold technology and non-road tyres are two valuable points of differentiation.** The former increases product premiums while the latter provides high levels of Maori and customer stickyness, but their long-term sustainability requires continuous observation.

4. ** Capital expenditure is strong and free cash flow phase is a normal feature of the expansion period** but also means that the "cash distribution capacity" of companies is limited in the short term.
