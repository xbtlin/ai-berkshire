## Why write about Sailun Tire?

In 2012, a tire boss from Qingdao made a decision that left his peers baffled: to build a factory in Vietnam.

At that time, China's tire industry was booming, and no one felt that "going overseas" was necessary. But Yuan Zhongxue saw something that others did not see - global trade protectionism will rise sooner or later, and the window period for Chinese tires to be exported to Europe and the United States will not be too long. This move was five years ahead of the entire industry and established the core competitive advantage of Sailun Tire (601058.SH) today.

Sailun is not the largest in the industry (Zhongce Rubber has higher revenue), it is not the one with the highest gross profit margin (Senqilin is higher), nor is it the most famous brand (Linglong has wider consumer awareness). But it is the Chinese tire company with the most extensive global production capacity and the strongest ability to avoid tariffs.

**It went to Vietnam to build a factory in 2012 - a full 5 years earlier than the industry. **

The significance of this move was not fully revealed until today, ten years later.

## Three common perceptions about Sailun

### Perception 1: "Tires are a low-end manufacturing industry"

This is the most widespread prejudice.

Yes, the tire industry is an asset-heavy, strong-cycle, low-conversion-cost manufacturing industry. But there’s an underrated characteristic of this industry: **Replacement tires account for more than 70% of global tire demand**. This means that tires are essentially a "repeated consumer product" - a vehicle needs to be replaced every 50,000 to 80,000 kilometers.

The competitive landscape of the global tire market is also undergoing drastic changes. The global share of the three giants, Michelin, Bridgestone and Goodyear, has dropped from **56%** in their heyday to **35-39%** (source: CompaniesMarketCap, industry research report). Robbed by whom? Chinese enterprises.

Sailun is one of the companies that has gone furthest in this wave of "Chinese tires going overseas".

**Contrary view**: The moat of the tire industry is indeed not deep. Low switching costs mean that consumers can easily switch brands and their pricing power is weak. When the cost of raw materials rises in 2025, Sailun's gross profit margin will drop from 27.6% to 24.7%, and it cannot afford to increase the price - this is the embodiment of "no pricing power".

### Perception 2: "Sports Lun is based on cheapness"

Sailun is indeed cheaper than Michelin and Bridgestone. But if you think Sailun just sells tires at low prices, that's as inaccurate as thinking that Pinduoduo just "sells bargains."

Sailun has two competitive weapons that are underestimated by the market:

**First, Liquid Gold Tires**. This is Sailun's world's first "chemical rubber mixing" technology, which breaks through the "devil's triangle" in the tire industry - rolling resistance, wet slip resistance, and wear resistance can usually only be chosen two. Liquid gold technology achieves simultaneous optimization of the three. This technology has passed the world's most stringent TUV MARK certification, and four Sailun tires have been certified at the same time, creating the best result in the history of Chinese tire companies** (Source: Xinhuanet). In the first quarter of 2025, the sales volume of the liquid gold tire replacement market increased by **50%** year-on-year, and it has been equipped with new energy models such as BYD and Geely.

**Second, Off-Highway Tires (OTR)**. This is the most profitable business segment of Sailun, with an estimated gross profit margin of **30%+** (Source: Research Paper Estimates). It has high technical barriers. It has been equipped with the world's top construction machinery brands such as Caterpillar and John Deere, and has entered the supply chain of mining giants such as Rio Tinto. Sailun produced the first all-steel radial giant tire in 2009, and in 2016 the world's largest 53/80R63 giant tire rolled off the production line.

**Contrary view**: Consumer perception of liquid gold technology is still very weak - the vast majority of ordinary car owners will not actively choose "liquid gold" when changing tires, and brand recognition is far inferior to Michelin's "silence" or Pirelli's "performance". Technology leadership also needs to be continuously verified, and other companies may catch up.

### Perception 3: "Building factories overseas is just for cheap labor."

Not entirely true. The core logic of Sailun's overseas factory building is not labor cost - but **tariff avoidance**.

Currently, the United States imposes a comprehensive tax rate of about **270%** on tires directly exported from China (Source: China Trade Remedy Information Network). This is basically equivalent to blocking the way for China's domestically produced tires to be exported to the United States. However, exports from Vietnam to the United States have a comprehensive tax rate of approximately **45%**; exports from Cambodia to the EU can enjoy EBA tariff reductions.

Sailun’s global production capacity layout is as follows:

| Base | Location | Main Products | Status |
|------|------|---------|------|
| Vietnam factory | Ho Chi Minh City | 16 million semi-steel tires + 2.6 million all-steel tires + 100,000 tons of off-highway tires | **Operation at full production** |
| Cambodia factory | Sihanoukville Port | Semi-steel tires + all-steel tires + off-road tires, large comprehensive production capacity | **Operation at full production** |
| Mexico factory | — | 6 million semi-steel tires | The first tire will roll off the assembly line in 2025, **Climbing** |
| Indonesia factory | — | 6 million semi-steel tires + 750,000 all-steel tires + 10,000 tons of off-road tires | The first tire will roll off the production line in 2025, **Climbing** |
| Egypt factory | — | Under planning | Planned to start production in 2026 |

*Data source: Sailun Group official website, public announcements, industry information*

**Five bases on five continents** - This is not as simple as "going to Vietnam to find cheap workers". This is a global chess game that was laid out ten years in advance.

**Contrary view**: The United States has begun to impose anti-dumping duties on tires from Southeast Asian countries. Data in 2025 shows that the potential total tax rate for tire exports to the United States from Thailand and Cambodia has reached **61%**, Vietnam **45%**, and Indonesia **57%** (source: industry public data). If the United States continues to "pursue" Southeast Asian production capacity, Sailun's tariff arbitrage logic will be severely weakened. More importantly, there are currently **11** Chinese-funded tire factories in Cambodia (source: research paper), and "overseas involution" has already begun.
