# Risk two: cost cycle of raw materials

Natural rubber accounts for the cost of raw materials for tyres ** 30-40%**. 2025 average natural rubber prices were about **17,000 yuan/tonne**, an increase of about 24% over the year. Industry presaged natural rubber into **20 years of high-cycle up-to-date period**, supply and demand gap of about 400,000 tons, and new rubber plantations took 6-7 years to produce rubber (source: research background quotes industry data).

The 2023-24 round Maori rate was 27-28% in the year, with about **60%** of the increase being due to lower costs of raw materials (source: research script quotes Southwest securities statistics). If raw materials continue to increase, the long-term Māori ratio centre of the round may stabilize at **22-24%**, rather than 25%+.

** buffers**: the race wheel overseas plant is close to natural rubber production areas (Viet Nam, Cambodia, Indonesia) and is less expensive to purchase than in the country; companies have forward lock-in hedging systems; these are only partially buffered and cannot fully hedge.

# Risk IV: Accounts receivable and refunds

The round balance in 2024 amounted to **$5086 million**, an increase of **50 per cent** over the previous year, which was significantly faster than the rate of increase in the collection (22.4 per cent). The receivable/receivable ratio was approximately **16 per cent**.

This is a signal that needs attention but not panic:

- Receivables are increasing significantly above the rate of revenue, which may mean that the company eases credit terms to rush.
- Or a normal match for overseas expansion - the accounts of large offshore clients (e.g. Caterpillar) usually have longer periods than domestic distributors
- 2025 Annual report data need to continue to verify whether this trend is continuing

** Alert**: If the receivable/receivable ratio continues to soar to **20% + **, the return risk needs to be carefully examined.

# Risk five: "Overseas Intra-Book"

In 2025, in Cambodia alone, there were **11 medium-sized tyre factories. There were also many home-based businesses in Viet Nam, Thailand, and Indonesia. Chinese tyre companies had more than **223 million a year** of production abroad.

This means that the "overprofit" that Chinese tyre companies used to earn from overseas (high Maori rates) is being eroded by competition. The forward dividends of overseas construction are waning and turning into a new "industry label".

The round approach is ** to the harder markets ** — Mexico (near the United States market) and Egypt (for Africa and the Middle East), but these new bases are in the climb phase, and the return will take three to five years to be fully reflected.

# Is the wheel worth investing? It depends on how you see three things.

After four, the "fullness" of the wheel tires is basically present. Ultimately, whether or not to invest depends on your judgement on three core variables:

Variable 1: How will the global trading environment go?

If you think that protectionism is nearing its peak, the future will be mildly relaxed — the global layout of the race round will continue to deliver on value and current prices are attractive.

If you think that protectionism will further escalate (the United States has imposed a full tax on South-East Asia, the EU has established anti-dumping) — the core competitive advantage of the race round will be weakened and the current price margin is not sufficiently secure.

Variable two: How long does the cost of natural rubber stay at a high level?

If you think that the 20-year cycle of natural rubber is too pessimistic, the price will fall in 2-3 years — the racer's Māori rate will return to 26-28%, with a high profit elasticity.

If you think that natural rubber is actually moving into a long cycle - the long-term Māori center of the race wheel moves down to 22-24%, the profit rate will grow slower than the recovery rate.

Variable 3: Can you still be competitive without a race wheel of Yuen Yuen?

If you think that the competitive nature of the race wheels has been "stabilized" into the organization through five major offshore bases and liquid gold technology — the risk of the next generation is manageable.

If you think that the strategic advantage of the wheel is highly dependent on the individual Yuan Junyuki — a risk that needs to be compensated for by a higher discount.

** Three variables, no standard answer. Different combinations of judgements lead to completely different investment conclusions**
