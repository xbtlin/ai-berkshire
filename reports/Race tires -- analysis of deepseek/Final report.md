# 4. Summary of the analysis of dimensions

##4.1 Business model & moat river (Long-Ping perspective)

1. ** Not a permanent "good business"**: tires make heavy assets, strong cycles, low conversion costs, insufficient differentiation. The "I don't know where the moat of this industry is" is said in the Pyramid.
2. **The liquid gold technology is substantially innovative**: the world ' s first liquid-phase-mixed chemical smelting glue, certified by TÜV SÜD, has made a breakthrough in rolling resistance/irrigation/flex resistance to the Devil Triangle. However, consumers are weak, technological sustainability is questionable, and other enterprises can catch up.
3. ** Globalized productive capacity distribution is the largest moat**: five major overseas bases (Viet Nam/Cambodia/Indonesia/Mexico/Egypt) have a five-to-ten-year pre-emergent advantage. But Chinese tyre enterprises have taken a collective trip to the sea, and the risk of "intra-Ocean" has increased.
4. ** Non-Road Tyres (OTR) are the best business spots**: high Māori (estimated at 30 per cent+), high technical barriers, and the companion Caterpillar and Ltao are the most valuable parts of the portfolio.
5. ** Insufficient pricing rights**: The price increases in 2025 were far below the cost increases and the Māori rate trend declined.

#4.2 Financial health & valuation (Buffett perspective)

1. ** Growth in revenue collection is determined**: 19.6 per cent (18.6 billion — 13.6 billion) in five years, with 15.7 per cent still strong in 2025.
2. **The decline in profit from 2025 is a cyclical decline**: the Māori rate declined from 28% to 25%, mainly due to price increases for raw materials and trade frictions, and non-competitiveness worsened. The Māori rate of 2026Q1 has returned to 26.86%, confirming a gradual rather than structural judgement.
** Good cash flow**: 5 years of cumulative operating CF/net profit = 1.10. FCF is strained by capital expenditure for overseas construction (2.5-3.5 billion a year) - a proactive strategic investment, not a deterioration.
4. ** The balance sheet improvement trend is clear**: the liability rate is 57.5 per cent = 50.1 per cent, BVPS is growing from 3.50 § 6.57 (CAGR 17 per cent), while the leverage rate is declining.
** PE 12.5x is within reasonable range**: international vs. Michililin (10-13 x)/Plišton (8-11 x), higher speed of race and attraction of PEG (12.5/15 x 0.83). The rate of return of 8 per cent is reasonable in the current interest rate environment.

##4.3 Industry Pattern & Competition (Manger Perspective)

1. ** Industry demand is sustainable**: $235 billion globally, with 70 per cent replacement + with the "reconsumable consumption" attribute.
2. **The domestic collection of the race round was 2** (31,360 million, followed by 38,355 million in medium policy), compared with Linglong/Senorium/triangular: ROE highest (20.82 per cent), the oldest overseas layout and most marked by technological differences.
3. ** Natural rubber entered the early 20-year cycle**: currently ~17,000 yuan/tonne (+24% YoY) with a supply and demand gap of about 400,000 tonnes. Structural pressure on profits in the long term for tyre enterprises has also raised barriers to entry in the industry.
4. ** New energy car tyres are the greatest structural opportunity**: the electric train is heavier (+20-30%), the more silent and low-rolling-retarded tyres are 50% higher +, CAGR 8-10% higher.
5. ** Industry is fragmented but has a concentrated trend**: CR5 < 50% globally, but trade barriers and scale effects are accelerating small business clean-up.

##4.4 Risk & Management (Li Sue)

1. **The Yuan Yuen strategy is forward-looking**: In 2012, the decision was made to build a factory (the first industry) in Viet Nam, which was three years ahead of the U.S. 2015 double-transaction.
2. ** Global trade protectionism is the greatest risk**: US combined tax rate on tyres ~ 270%, Vietnam/Cambodia combined US tax rate ~ 71-74%. Multi-country synchronized sieges, including the EU, Brazil, UK, exposed 75.9% of overseas earnings.
** succession concerns**: Yuan is still a soul figure at 71, the Uniting Action System holds ~18-20% of the shares, and the family colour is obvious.
4. ** Operational risk control**: natural rubber can be hedged through forward lock prices and the advantage of Cambodian production; exchange rate has a hedging system; overseas plant political risk is low in Viet Nam and medium in Cambodia.
** Ten years later, the TOP5 probability is 35-40%**: the tyre industry is not to be destabilized, electric vehicles are to be beneficial and the real risk is to be extreme changes in external policy.

#8. Summary

The wheel tyres are a company worth studying but not worth repossession.

It does a lot: the world’s first sea-building plant, ten years of research and development of liquid gold, and four years of doubling of revenues by non-road tyres into the Caterpillar and Lita supply chains. In the Chinese tyre industry, the management and enforcement of the race wheels is indeed the first.

But it also has a structural problem that cannot be bypassed: tyre manufacturing requires a sustained and large capital input with limited pricing rights, more than 75 per cent of revenues are exposed to the firepower of global trade protectionism, the cost cycle of natural rubber is uncontrollable, and the 71-year-old founder remains the only soulmaker.

For investors willing to accept cycle fluctuations and watch growth values for 3-5 years, they can build up their own tanks in a gradual manner between $12-14. But for those Graham-type investors who pursue deep security margins, less than $10 is the real hit zone.

> ** Manger would say **: "You'll do well if you buy a good company at a reasonable price and hold it for a long time. But you have to make sure it's a good company, and the price is really reasonable. Is the race wheel good? Not bad. Does the price fit?

> ** Yongping said **: "I don't normally do this kind of company. It's not because it's bad, it's because there's a easier business to choose. If you can't find the feeling of 'great' in the tyre industry, don't force yourself. But if you insist on doing it, buy it when others are afraid to buy it -- like when the US announces a new round of tariffs, market panics and crashes."
