# Jen-Hsun Huang said that AI is a five-layer cake. Who is making money on each layer?

Huang Renxun repeatedly talked about a framework at Davos and GTC this year: AI is not a product, but a whole new industrial system, with five layers from bottom to top.

He calls these five layers "AI cake." I spent a week taking apart each layer and looking at it, and found that the investment logic inside was much clearer than I thought.

## Let’s explain the five layers clearly first.

```
Layer 5 Application Layer AI Agent, autonomous driving, robots
Layer 4: Model layer Large language model, multi-modal model
Layer 3 Infrastructure layer data center, network, cooling
Layer 2 chip layer GPU, memory, advanced packaging
Tier 1 Energy Tier Electricity, Transformers, Nuclear Energy
```

Huang Renxun's original words: "In the future, every company will have two factories-one for product production and one for AI. The AI factory only does one thing every day: produce tokens."

He gave a core indicator to measure the efficiency of AI factories: how many tokens are produced per watt. What this indicator means is that the AI ​​business will eventually be like the manufacturing industry, where the competition is how much intelligence can be produced per kilowatt hour of electricity.

## Level 1: Electricity - the most boring but most certain opportunity

Global data center electricity consumption will reach 485 terawatt hours in 2025 and will double within five years. But nearly half of planned data center projects in the United States have been delayed or canceled. It's not because of the lack of GPU, it's because of the lack of power and transformer.

The delivery date of large power transformers has been extended to 4 years. This means that the production capacity in 2026 was decided as early as 2022, and orders placed now will not be delivered until 2030 at the earliest.

This layer is the least sexy, but the most certain. The transformer shortage is unlikely to be alleviated before 2028. Power equipment companies such as Eaton and GE Vernova are the beneficiaries of the most reasonable valuations in the AI ​​industry chain.

## Layer 2: Chips—the $5 trillion monopoly king

Nvidia remains the "standard metric" for AI computing power. FY2027 Q1 revenue was US$81.6 billion, a year-on-year increase of 85%. The data center business was US$75.2 billion, an increase of 92%, and the gross profit margin was 75%. With a market value of US$5.2 trillion, it ranks first in the world.

But the most noteworthy change is that the growth rate of custom chips (+45%) exceeded that of general-purpose GPUs (+16%) for the first time. Google, Meta, Amazon, and Microsoft are all developing their own AI chips or finding Broadcom/Marvell to customize AI chips, with the goal of reducing their dependence on Nvidia.

Broadcom’s AI revenue this quarter was US$10.8 billion, a year-on-year increase of 143%, which was faster than Nvidia’s. CEO Chen Fuyang said that he is "confident that AI chip revenue will exceed 100 billion in 2027" and has a backlog of orders of 73 billion.

Five key players at the chip layer: Nvidia (standard setter), Broadcom (king of custom chips), AMD (the only universal alternative), TSMC (manufacturing monopoly), SK Hynix (62% share of HBM memory).

## Tier 3: Infrastructure – The $725 Billion Arms Race

The total capital expenditures of the four major cloud vendors in 2026 will be US$725 billion, doubling year-on-year. No one is cutting back.

| Company | 2026 Capital Expenditures | Year-over-Year |
|------|--------------|------|
| Amazon | $200 billion | +60% |
| Microsoft | $190 billion | +138% |
| Google | $180 billion | +97% |
| Meta | $135 billion | +80% |

Where is the money spent? About 40-60% buy GPU/chips, 20-25% build data centers, 15-20% buy server accessories, and 10-15% buy network equipment.

The $725 billion is real money, not PPT. This money flows along the supply chain, supporting companies such as optical modules (Zhongji InnoLight’s net profit in 2025 is 10.8 billion), network equipment (Arista), liquid cooling systems (Vertiv), and servers (Dell).

It is worth mentioning that InnoLight is the point where Chinese companies have the strongest pricing power in the entire AI industry chain. It owns about half of the world's 800G optical modules and 35% of Nvidia's orders.

## Tier 4: Model – Fastest Revenue Growth in History

It only took 14 months for Anthropic’s annualized revenue to rise from 1 billion to 47 billion. This may be the fastest corporate revenue growth in human business history.

| Company | Valuation | Annualized Revenue |
|------|------|----------|
| Anthropic | $965 billion | $47 billion |
| OpenAI | $852 billion | $24 billion |
| xAI (SpaceX merger) | 1.75 trillion target | $3.2 billion (xAI part) |
| DeepSeek | $51.5 billion | Undisclosed |

The second half of 2026 is the IPO year for AI companies - Anthropic has secretly submitted it, and OpenAI aims to be listed in September. This will be the moment to test whether these sky-high valuations can withstand public market scrutiny.

DeepSeek is the most interesting variable in this layer. V4-Pro has surpassed Claude in mathematical reasoning, and its API price is only one-tenth to one-seventy-fifth of that of Western competitors. It proves one thing: the gap in model capabilities is narrowing, but the gap in commercialization is still orders of magnitude.

## Layer 5: Application—few companies really make money from AI

The cruel reality at this level is: the proportion of AI revenue in most "AI concept stocks" is still very low. But the valuation divergence is extreme.

| Company | AI Revenue Growth | P/E |
|------|-----------|-----|
| Palantir | +85% | 105x |
| Tesla | FSD Subscription +51% | 317x |
| ServiceNow | AI cross-sells 10x | 25x |
| Salesforce | Agentforce +200% | 14x |
| Adobe | Firefly points consumed 3 times | 10x |

Palantir is the purest AI application company, with a growth rate of 85%, crushing its peers, but a PE of 105 times is already a lot of overdraft. Salesforce and Adobe are the most cost-effective - 14 times and 10 times PE. AI products are growing rapidly but the valuation given by the market is very low because the total revenue growth rate is only 10-12%.

If you believe that the monetization of the AI ​​application layer is still in its early stages, then Salesforce’s 14 times PE may be the most undervalued target in the entire AI industry chain.

## Who is the strongest in each level in China?

| Hierarchy | China is the strongest | Gap with global leaders |
|------|---------|-------------|
| Chip | Huawei Ascend | The hardware is one generation behind, and the CUDA ecosystem is one era behind |
| Infrastructure | Zhongji InnoLight | Optical modules have global pricing power and are an exception |
| Model | DeepSeek | Technology is 3-6 months behind, commercialization is 100 times behind |
| Cloud | Alibaba Cloud | Revenue difference is 6 times, globalization gap is huge |
| Applications | iFlytek | Industry implementation is not weak, general applications are far behind |

The biggest progress in China's AI is at the model layer (DeepSeek), the biggest bottleneck is at the chip layer (CUDA ecosystem), and the most underestimated gap is in commercialization - technology has equaled, but the gap in the ability to "turn technology into money" is still orders of magnitude.

## A few final words

Jen-Hsun Huang's five-layer cake framework gives a very clear way of thinking: the lower the layer, the more physical, and the more unavoidable the link, the higher the investment certainty.

The sexiest layer is models and applications - Anthropic's 47 billion revenue, Palantir's 85% growth rate. But these are also the least certain: valuations have skyrocketed, and once growth slows down, the retracement will be severe.

The most boring layer is energy and infrastructure - transformers, liquid cooling, optical modules. But these are the real "stuck necks" of the AI ​​industry. The transformer delivery period is four years, and no one can get around this bottleneck.

In the AI industry chain, the certainty of selling shovels is always higher than that of gold diggers. However, the best shovels are no longer cheap these days.
