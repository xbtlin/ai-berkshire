# 1.5 Array of the moat

**(1) Barrier of scale - "Pop effect" in the heavy asset industry**

The crystal circle is a typical asset-intensive industry. China-Cryston spent $8.1 billion in 2025 on capital, with cumulative total assets exceeding RMB 300 billion.

** (2) Alternative "Policy Protector River" to national production**

Lower-lying customers (especially listed businesses) are forced to switch their surrogate orders to China-Chole International, creating natural market barriers. The share of income in China’s region rose from about 65% in 2020 to 89% in the first quarter of 2026, a trend that is directly confirmed.

**(3) Client sticky - conversion costs of process binding**

Chip Design aims to optimize the flow of fluids at the process nodes of specific sub-plants, and once produced, switching plants means redesigning and certification, with extremely high time and cost. The CKI has accumulated a large number of loyal customers, especially in mature processes of 28 nm and above.

** (4) Limitations of the moat**

There is still a fundamental gap between its technological strength and cost structure and power generation in the global market. If the geopolitical environment eases (though short-term probability is not significant), domestic customers may re-enter overseas-generation plants like power generation, which will be tested.

# IV. Risk assessment

##4.1 US sanctions risk - the greatest external uncertainty

- CKI is on the list of U.S. entities, and the current provisions limit the export of "unique" needs technologies. The National Security Council is discussing tightening the provisions to "usable" technologies, which will significantly extend the scope of the embargo.
- If the U.S. supply chain of the most advanced plant is abruptly cut off, depending on the stock situation, it could lead to a three- to nine-month disruption of production.
- The share of equipment produced by companies is only about 37 per cent, while core equipment (light carving, partially corrosion) remains highly import-dependent.
- ** But on the other hand**: Companies have been actively building alternative supply chains, and the collection under sanctions pressure has increased from $3.36 billion to $9.3 billion over the past few years, demonstrating some resilience. The probability of completely cutting off supply extremes is low, because American suppliers are not willing to lose their big customers.

# 4.2 Technology blockade and advanced process bottlenecks

- EUV light-clashing is a hard-press, and cannot be broken in the short term. The center-crystal international can only do 7nm with a DMV multi-exposure program, with high cost and low rate.
- Commercialized volume production is almost impossible without EUV for processes under 5 nm.
- The national EUV light burner is still in its early stages and is expected to be difficult to achieve in a 5-10 year period.
- This means that in the foreseeable future, CKI will be locked in the position of "maturity + limited advanced process" and cannot compete with the power generation at the forefront.

# 4.3 Risk of excess mature process capacity

- Global mature process capacity released between 2025 and 2026, and the combination of electricity and power generation has sent out early warnings of increased price competition.
- The center core international is facing direct competition from its peers in the area of maturity processes, such as the rainbow and the grid core.
- ** But mitigating factors**: A.I.D.-driven power management chips are in high demand, with 8 inch capacity utilization having risen to about 90%; power on the table, three stars have reduced mature production capacity, partially reducing supply pressure. Companies are also actively adjusting product structures to favour high-value products such as BCD processes, storage, high-end MCUs.

#4.4 Depreciation pressure and profitability

- The depreciation in 2025 is approximately $27.3 billion and will continue to increase over the next three to five years (as a result of the continuous depreciation of high capital expenditure in 2023-2025).
- Depreciation is the main reason why the current Māori rate is not able to recover effectively. Even if income increases by 15-20%, the Māori rate may still be stressed if depreciation increases faster.
- The company ROE is only 3-5%, and the capital returns are inefficient. If profits continue to be low during the depreciation cycle, investors may re-examine the reasonableness of "breathing money."

#4.5 Management stability

** Positive factors:**

- The "two engines" of the combined CEO Navy (operational) and Liang Mengsong (technical) were functioning well, and they were stable in 2025.
- The Navy has extensive experience in expanding its capacity and in customer management, which has contributed to a high capacity utilization rate of 93.5 per cent.
- Liang Meng Song, as a technical soul figure, has continued to promote the FinnFET process in its iterative and advanced envelope layout.

**Risk signal:**

In the second half of 2025, Vice-President Chiang Sang-yi (age 75) offered to leave the Board, and Liang Meng-song withdrew. Although Liang is still in the position of United CEO, the signal that he withdrew from the Board is interesting.
- Liang Mengsong is 68 years old, and the problem of the successors is of medium- to long-term concern. If Liang Mengsong leaves, the company’s advanced development capability may be substantially affected.
- Corporate governance has been a source of concern for investors in the history of the Central History International (the succession of Qiu Tzu-yun in 2011, the accession of Liang Meng-song in 2017 and the return of Chiang Sang-yi in 2020).

# 4.6 The biggest downside risk

** The significant escalation of United States sanctions + the depreciation pressure that led to the continued low profitability of the "value killing".**

The situation is: if the US tightens the sanctions clause to "available" technology, which causes disruption in supply of some of the company's production line equipment and a sharp decline in capacity utilization; and if the continued high depreciation prevents the return to over 25% of the Māori rate, net profits stagnate at $5-6 billion. After the market finds that the "state-substitute premium" cannot be realized as real profits, the valuation returns from 189 times PE to 80-100 times the market value of $40-60 billion, or 37-58% of the current downside space.

# V. Consolidated conclusions

# Core judgment

China Chole International is one of the most strategic companies in China’s semiconductor industrial chain, which is irreplaceable in the larger trend of national production substitution. With millions of monthly production, more than 93% of capacity utilization, and breakthroughs in the 7nm process, the company has established the position of the Chinese continental crystal-crystal-round worker as the absolute leader.

** But "strategic importance" is not "investment attraction".** The following core contradictions need to be addressed:

1. ** Growth in revenue but weak profits**: The 67.3 billion yuan won only net profits of $5 billion, with net interest rates of only 7.5%. ROE 3-5% means that equity holders have an extremely inefficient capital return. The root cause is the $8 billion in capital spending and the continuous upward pressure on depreciation.
2. ** The valuation contains excessive expectations**: the market value of $953.7 billion corresponds to 189 times PE, 5.3 times PB, and has fully priced the optimistic expectations of national production substitution and advanced process breakthroughs. If this is not met, the rewinding pressure is high.
3. **The advanced process is called "No Sit"**: 7 nm can do but is costly, low-rate and limited to equipment supply. The commercialization prospects of less than 5 nm in the absence of EUV are unclear.
4. ** When does "corrupt money" end?** Capital spending continues to exceed revenue and free cash flows are negative. This pattern can be tolerated in the industry landscape, but financial pressure will quickly emerge if the down-swing or the escalation of sanctions leads to a decline in capacity utilization.

# Whether it's worth the deep-stalking

** Worth long-term follow-up, but not currently the ideal place to buy.**

The investment logic of China-Chull International is essentially a "state-class option" – the bet is on the autonomous process of China’s semiconductor industrial chain. If this process goes forward, companies, as core beneficiaries, are likely to harvest two to three times more in the next decade.

The ideal timing for buying may be: (1) the escalation of United States sanctions has led to market panic throwing; (2) industry down-scaling valuations are cyclical; and (3) quarterly underperformance has triggered short-term adjustments. At that time, the risk returns would be significantly improved if the market value were to fall to $500-60 billion (100-120 times PE).
