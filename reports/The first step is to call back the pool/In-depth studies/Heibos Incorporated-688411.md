# 1.4 Array of the moat

**(1) scale effects and project experience barriers**

Heibos is the system integrator with the largest load of energy plants delivered in the country, and a large number of project delivery experiences constitute barriers to competition. The storage plants involve complex engineering issues such as safety, fire protection, and grid access, and project experience and the accumulation of data on transport are difficult to replicate rapidly by new entrants.

**(2) BMS/EMS technical barriers**

BMS and EMS platforms are core technology assets. BMS is responsible for battery safety management and state estimates, and EMS is responsible for the intelligent movement of power storage plants – both of which directly determine the safety, efficiency, and economic returns of storage power plants.

**(3) Customer Resource Barrier**

Large storage projects are usually tendered by electricity companies (national energy companies, energy companies, big Tang, etc.), and Hiborg has accumulated deep customer relationships and branding in the context of the inter-country campaign.

** But the limits of the moats**: the moats of system integrators are generally narrow. Core parts (ice cores, PCS) are taken out, and 96.84% of direct material costs imply limited value-added space for companies. The Maori rate is only 18%, with a slight competitive pressure to erode profits.

# IV. Risk assessment

##4.1 Increased competition in the industry has led to further downswings in the Māori rate

The entry threshold for the integrated energy storage system industry is relatively low (core parts can be extrapolated) and the industry is well represented. The price of the domestic electrical and chemical storage system has been down overall since 2024, and the Māori rate has fallen from 23% in 2022 to 18%. If competition increases further:

- The Ninder era, Biadey and other power-core giants have increased the direct sale of storage systems, which may squeeze the survival space of independent system integrators.
- Cross-border players such as Sunshine Power, China Digital Energy, etc. are also increasing their energy reserves.
- At worst, the integration of storage systems may evolve into a low-morigin industry (10-15 per cent) similar to "photovoltaic components", and the valuation logic created by Haebbot will face fundamental challenges.

##4.2 Supply chain concentration risk

- Direct material costs account for 96.84 per cent of the total cost, and companies are highly dependent on the source suppliers (especially Ningde, billion latitudes of lithium).
- Although 200 GWh LLs have been signed, price fluctuations in the core will have a direct impact on the Māori rate. If the price of the core does not increase and the price of the storage system cannot be raised simultaneously, profits will be squeezed.
- 200GWh procurement commitments are also a constraint - companies may face pressure to "coercive procurement" if market demand falls short of expectations.

# 4.3 Risk of productive expansion radical

- Company target 70GWh in 2026 (a growth of 190 per cent over 24GWh in 2025), 100 GWh in 2027, 130 GWh in 2028 - the goal of 300 GWh in three years combined is extremely radical.
- Capital expenditure is more under pressure when it comes to investing $2 billion in smart green storage plants.
- If demand increases in the industry do not accelerate as expected, a decline in capacity utilization will seriously delay profitability.

##4.4 Accounts receivable and cash flow risk

The customers of the storage energy projects are mainly electric power companies and large energy enterprises, usually with a long payment cycle.

- Accounts receivable may grow rapidly, putting pressure on cash flows.
- The first quarterly report for 2026 showed a sharp decline of 76 per cent in the receivable accounts receivable compared to the previous year, and concerns were expressed about the change in the receivable structure.
- The receipt and inspection cycle and the return cycle of large storage capacity projects may be extended.

##4.5 Risk of change of technology routes

- Current storage capacity is based on lithium batteries, but new technology routes such as sodium ion cells, liquid current cells, solid-state batteries are being developed. If there is a major change in the technology route, the system integration experience of the Hiborg project and the BMS technology build-up may need to be rebuilt.
- But the company has demonstrated a technological forward-looking approach through a joint venture with the New Blue Energy (Willand Haibo) layout of semi-solid-state batteries.

# 4.6 The biggest downside risk

** Industry price wars + Māori fall into a survival crisis of 15%.**

The scenario: If the Ninder era, Biadey, and other giants of the core enter the market for integration of the storage system and take their share at cost, and the price of the domestic storage system continues to fall, the Māori rate in Haebbels could fall from 18% to 13-15%, and the net interest rate from 8% to 3%. In this scenario, even if the harvest continues to grow, profits may stagnate or even decline, with the PE expanding significantly from the current 23-fold (expected in 2026) to 40-50 times, and the valuation logic collapses.
