# 1.4 Array of the moat

**(l) Technical Barrier — Fine-cut manufacture of a sword for 10 years**

The harmonics seem to be simple in structure (only three core parts: jug wheels, steel wheels, wave generators), but the requirements for process accuracy, materials, and thermal processes are extremely high. Green harmonics have been developed since 2003, and have only been produced for more than 10 years to achieve international performance and bulk delivery.

**(2) Conversion costs of customer validation cycle**

The harmonics directly determine the accuracy and lifetime of robots, with downstream customers (especially industrial robot manufacturers) usually having a certification cycle of 1-2 years. After certification, customers tend to bind suppliers for long periods because of high risk of switching and high costs of recertification. Green harmonics have been in the domestic mainstream robot manufacturers supply chain for many years, and cooperative relationships are strong.

**(3) Structural opportunities provided by trends in national production substitution**

The US duty on the Chinese industrial robot cores in 2025 was 30%. The supply chain was transformed into a national product. The proportion of domestic robot manufacturers buying domestic production harmonics increased significantly, with green harmonics benefiting first and foremost as the brand of national production.

** But note **: The moat is not as wide as semiconductor equipment, high-end chips, etc. The core technical principles of the harmonic deceleration device are already known, and the barriers to competition are more evident in process accumulation and precision, meaning that the second-line brands of Lei Fubo and the Grand Concordum are continuously closing the gap.

# IV. Risk assessment

##4.1 Human robots do not require the expected risks (maximum risk)

The current market value of 56 billion dollars is almost 100% based on the expected mass production of human robots. But the reality is:

- The actual volume of global human robots delivered in 2025 was only thousands of orders of magnitude, with a huge gap between the hundreds of thousands of units of scale needed to support the current valuation.
- The commercialization of human robots still faces the core issues of "undefined scene", "costs far above traditional solutions", "deficient reliability".
- The greener wave will be the first to be hit by downstream robotics that are being prevented from purchasing as a result of commercialization.

# 4.2 Māori rate continues to fall risk

- The global collator has an effective capacity of about 5 million units, with less than 50 per cent capacity utilization and a clear pattern of oversupply.
- Mainstream model prices have been reduced to $1,000-2000, and price warfare has been developed.
- The second-line brands of Lei Fu, Eusentom and Suikawa Suibo are continuously being pursued, with the industry ' s Māori ratio down from over 50 per cent to 25-35 per cent during peak periods.
- If the Māori rate continues to decline to below 30 per cent, the profitability of companies will be significantly reduced.

# 4.3 Competition increases risk

- **International Competition**: Hamernaco is building 500,000 new capacity in Viet Nam per year (in 2026) to supply global markets at a lower cost, which could squeeze greener collage price advantages.
- ** Internal competition**: Lei Fu Quixiang, Grand Console and other second-line branding technology is fast approaching green glimmers and some new entrants are focusing on the human robotic field.
- Green harmonics are facing a "Hamernako under pressure, a national second-line chase" cynicism.

##4.4 Valuation regression risk

- Nearly 450 times the PE valuation level, extremely sensitive to the news of altruism.
- After the publication of the annual report in April 2026, despite a doubling of net profits, stock prices fell significantly, reflecting market concerns about "double performance but still uninclusive of valuation".
- The valuation may face a break-off counter-reflection if the human robotics industry has a phased negative news (e.g., the main plant has negotiated production delayed and commercial routes blocked).

#4.5 Energy expansion and financial return risk

- The company continues to expand its capacity, but in the context of industries with less than 50 per cent capacity utilization, there is uncertainty about the return on investment in the new capacity.
- Slow growth in R & D inputs (as opposed to the rate of increase in revenue), which may affect the competitiveness of technology in the medium to long term.

# 4.6 The biggest downside risk

** The human robots are not producing as expected + the Davies double-killing for the high-value rewind.**

The situation: If the volume of human robots arriving in 2027-2028 is only 30-50% of the market expectations (e.g., tens of thousands of them globally instead of hundreds of thousands), the green wave growth will be significantly slower, with the price competition in the supersector leading to a continued downswing of Māori to below 30%, a return of PE from 450 to 80-100, and a possible 60-70% downswing in stock prices. This is the typical risk model for high-value growth.
