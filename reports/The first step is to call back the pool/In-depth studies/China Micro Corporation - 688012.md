# IV. Risk assessment

##4.1 Technology to catch up with risk

- ** Advanced process gap**: Although companies claim to have reached 5 nm and more advanced processes, the gap with Lam Research, Tokyo Electronics, remains at the most advanced 3 nm/2 nm node. In particular, the international giant has matured options for the high-level options required for the Gate-All-Around (GAA) architecture, and the Mt. Mt. is in the certification phase.
- **ICP silicon etching is difficult to catch**: Lam Research has a very strong monopoly in silicon etching, and the moderate ICP has grown rapidly, but its global share is still less than 2%. In extreme processes such as 3D NAND ultra-high-wider than carving (over 100 stories), catching up takes time.
- ** On the other hand**: R & D investment in the medium-sized is far above industry average (30%) and the founders' team itself is from the international giants, and the path to technological catch-up is clear. The problem is speed, not direction.

# 4.2 Client concentration risk

- ** The top five clients account for about 69-70%** and the clients are highly concentrated in the domestic head crystal circle (Center Cyclops International, Yangtze Storage, Longsong Storage, China Rainbow, etc.).
- If capital spending shrinks or expands for a large client, it will directly affect the modest income. For example, the Yangtze storage is affected by United States sanctions, and the pace of expansion is uncertain.
- ** But on the other hand**: These large customers are themselves firm implementers of national production substitution and, as the only domestic supplier of high-end etching equipment, have very limited alternatives. High concentration also means, to some extent, deep binding.

##4.3 Geopolitical implications

- Sino-Medium was listed in the US as an entity in 2020, and was removed. But the risk of being re-listed remains in the current context of the deepening decoupling of Sino-American technology.
- In 2025-2026, the US further tightened export controls in the semiconductor sector of China, particularly in the area of advanced range equipment and spare parts. While MSMEs are equipment manufacturers in themselves, rather than in the end restricted crystal circle, their upstream key components (such as radio frequency power sources, precision valves, etc.) are still partially dependent on imports.
- The domestication rate of the company ' s core parts has reached 60-70 per cent, the target has been raised to over 80 per cent by 2026 and is actively reducing supply chain risks.
- **The other side of the coin**: Geopolitics is precisely the biggest catalyst for national production substitution. Every sanctions escalation pushes downstream customers to speed up the procurement of national production equipment.

# 4.4 Māori downside risk

- The Māori rate has been declining steadily from about 66 per cent in 2020 to 39.2 per cent in 2025, a decline of about 27 percentage points in five years.
- Main reasons: (1) significant decline in the share of the MovD industry in high Maori; (2) certification of new products at a lower price than mature products; and (3) increased competition in the industry, with the country ' s northern Chinese creation also active in the field of etching.
- If the Māori rate continues to decline to less than 35 per cent, even if the revenue growth rate is maintained at more than 35 per cent, the profit growth rate will be significantly suppressed.
- ** Need to track: Net profit growth of 197% in the first quarter of 2026, and concern is expressed about whether the Māori rate shows signs of stability.

# 4.5 Balance between R & D inputs and profits

- R & D investment of R & D of R . 37.40 billion yuan in 2025, or 30.2 per cent of revenues, is a very rare proportion in semiconductor equipment companies worldwide (about 14 per cent in Lam Research and 15 per cent in applications).
- High R & D inputs are necessary costs of a platformed strategy, but they also mean that short-term profits are significantly suppressed. The withholding of non-net profits of $1.55 billion represents only 12.5% of the revenue.
- Investors need to judge whether this is a benign input from "pre-sown seed, future harvest" or an over-exploitation of uncertain returns. If multiple new product lines are not able to achieve profitability on scale, high R & D inputs will become "sink costs".

# 4.6 The biggest downside risk

** High valuations are exposed to the risk of a "Davis double-killing" with slower performance.**

The scenario: If the semiconductor industry is at a peak in 2026-2027 (global spending on equipment has declined from over 30% to a single-digit level), while corporate growth has slowed from 35% to less than 20%, the Māori rate continues to decline to 35%, and net profit growth has fallen below 15%. In this case, the market may re-pricing 129 times PE to 50-60 times PE, with a market value of about $13 billion-160 billion, with 40-50% lower than the current level.
