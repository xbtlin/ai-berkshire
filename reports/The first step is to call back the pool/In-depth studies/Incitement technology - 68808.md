# 1.3 Array analysis

** First tier: very high technical barriers**

The memory interface chip is a JEDEC standard defined product that requires joint validation with the DRAM manufacturer, the server CPU manufacturer (Intel, AMD). It usually takes two to three years from the chip design to the platform certification. Only three companies worldwide have access to this technology, and new entrants are almost impossible to break through in the short term.

** Second tier: pre-emptive advantage of standard participatory right-building**

The core player in JEDEC memory standards, which played an important role in the DDR5 standard setting process, was involved in standard setting. Participation meant that product development could be pre-positioned and a mass chip would be launched at the time of the standards release. The company has taken the lead in launching the DDR5 fourth generation RCD chip (support 7200MT/s) and has only completed the upgrade from first to fourth generation in just two years.

** Third layer: locking effect of platform certification**

To enter the server supply chain, the memory module must be certified through a platform in Intel or AMD. The certification cycle is long and costly, and the client will not easily switch once a memory interface chip is authenticated. This creates a natural customer lock-in.

** Fourth floor: oligopoly pricing rights**

Three companies control more than 93% of the market, and the pattern of competition is extremely stable. While there is competition, it is not a bad price war, each has a customer base, and the Māori rate is at a high level. The Māori rate, which has been magnified in the first quarter of 2026, has risen to 69.8%, with the Māori rate of interconnected chips exceeding 70%.

# IV. Risk assessment

##4.1 Industry cyclical risk

** This is the most central risk.** The lessons learned in 2023 were engraved - a 37.8 per cent drop in the harvest and a 65.3 per cent drop in net profits. The demand for memory interface chips is directly linked to the DRAM industry, which is one of the most cyclical subdivisions in the semiconductor industry.

The current high-profile situation is driven by the double-diplomatic drivers of the DDR5 upgrade and the AI server demand. If one of the following conditions occurs, a new down-cycle may be triggered:
- The slower pace of AI investment resulted in lower than expected server requirements
- The rate of penetration of DDR5 is slowing down when it reaches a high level.
- DRAM manufacturers cut capital costs because of oversupply

** Risk level: high** — a strong cyclical industry, and investors must be fully aware of the potential for significant fluctuations in performance.

# 4.2 Client concentration risk

The top five clients contribute more than 80% of the income, while the top three clients (Sisan, SK Hercules, Light) account for more than 70%. This means:
- Any big client's orders are volatile.
- Big clients have a stronger bargaining power.
- If a major client turns to Risa or Rambus, it's going to be a big impact.

On the other hand, the global DRAM market itself is the only three major producers whose concentration is determined by industry structures and not unique. Three DRAM manufacturers are unlikely to rely entirely on single chip suppliers, and it is common practice to diversify procurement.

** Risk level: Medium** - Structural problems, unlikely to change in the short term, but not posing a fatal risk.

##4.3 Risks of changing competition patterns

** Memory interface chip area:**
- The pattern is stable in the short term, and the three oligarchs are inflexible.
- But in the medium and long term, attention needs to be given to whether new competitors are entering the DDR6 standard setting process
- Risa and Rambus may compete for shares through a diffusion strategy.

**PCIE Retimer area:**
- More competition, Astera Labs is about three months ahead of PCIE 6.0.
- The big boys Broadcom and Marvell are also actively laid out.
- We need to catch up on product performance, certification speed.

** Risk level: Medium and low** - Core business landscape is stable and new business faces more competition.

##4.4 Inventory risk

The stock accumulation in the semiconductor sector is often a precursor to a cyclical shift. The year 2023 was marked by a sharp reduction in stock, which led to a 65% drop in net profits.

If industry climate reverses, current stock levels may translate into impairment pressures.

** Risk level: Medium** — stock turnover rate needs to be closely monitored.

##4.5 Geopolitics and technology control risks

The technology chip design, using advanced process processes, relies on plant-based production, such as power and power. If international technology regulation is upgraded, it may affect the company’s supply chain. Moreover, the share of offshore earnings is high, and exchange rate fluctuations can affect financial performance.

On the other hand, the unique high end memory interface chip provider for national production is inescapable in "inventive" and national production substitution strategies. Such strategic scarcity is both a risk (possibility of sanctions) and an advantage (a certainty of policy support).

** Risk level: medium** - high uncertainty, but both sides of the equation.

# 4.6 The biggest downside risk

** Core risk: DRAM industry entered a new round of de-stocking, adding up the AI investment returns less than expected to slow server demand**

If there are industry lows similar to 2023 in 2027-2028, corporate revenue could fall from a high of $7-8 billion to $4 billion, and net profits could fall from over $3 billion to $1 billion-15 billion. Under this scenario, the current market value of $156 billion in shares in the PE would rise sharply, with a 40-50% return in stock prices.

The most extreme scenario is that AI bubble burst + DRAM is completely off the inventory + new product is stagnating, performance is likely to return to 2023 (with net profit of about 450 million yuan), with more than 300 times the current market value of PE and a high share price risk.
