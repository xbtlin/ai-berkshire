# IV. Risk assessment

##4.1 Collect policy risks

Large-scale medical equipment collections are in the normal state stage, and provincial and coalition collections may compress the price of equipment. But the combination is a double-edged sword -- in the evaluation system, technical parameters, brand names, post-sale systems have higher weights than purely value. The full product line and technological strength of the joints have given them a dominant position in the collection.

** Risk level: medium and low.** More crowding by SMEs may benefit from joint production.

# 4.2 Technology catching and being caught up

** Catched:** The production counterparts of East soft medicine, Vandong medicine, etc. are competitive in the lower-middle-end markets, but are far from visible in the upper-end areas. American groups are also able to distribute medical images through acquisitions, but it is difficult to pose a threat in the short term.

** Catching up with the troika:** There is still a gap between the joint film and Siemens and general electrical equipment in the area of MRs 3.0T and above.

** Risk level: medium.** Short-term catch-up risks are low, and catching up with the troika requires sustained high R & D inputs and time.

#4.3 Receivables and refund risk

This is the financial risk that the joint film needs to pay attention to:

- The turnover of accounts receivable increased sharply from 82 to 143 days (2023 to 3rd quarter 2024)
- Receivables in the first quarter of 2026 as a percentage of net profit on the return of the mother amounted to 306 per cent
- downstream clients are dominated by public hospitals, with a natural long payment cycle

The significant improvement in operating cash flows in 2025 indicates that management is aware of the problem and has taken measures, but needs to be followed up on an ongoing basis. Hospital clients have low credit risk (with low probability of bad debt), but capital occupancy can affect capital efficiency.

** Risk level: medium height.** No thunder, but persistently delaying the return on capital.

If fiscal constraints lead to continued low hospital procurement, the growth rate will be significantly slower.
