# 1.3 Array analysis

** E-gas barriers:** E-gas are in the heavy asset industry, and new capacity requires large capital inputs and longer construction cycles (usually 2-3 years). The NF3 and WF6 production-scale advantages of middle-ship-specific gas give them a leading position in terms of cost-side and supply stability.

** Certification barriers:** Semiconductor electron speciality requires rigorous certification for one to two years at downstream crystal circle plants, with extremely high cost of switching once imported. Companies have certified major global customers such as power generation, China-Chole International, Yangtze Storage, Light, Hercules, Kyoto East, and LGD. WF6 products are in the advanced 3nm process, which is a very high certification barrier.

** Technical barriers to purity:** The core of electronic speciality is superpurity (grades 5N to 6N) and impurities are controlled at the ppb or even the ppt level. This technical capability will take years to accumulate and will be difficult to replicate in the short term.

** Background:** As a Chinese ship group enterprise, there is a natural advantage in the promotion of government procurement, defence support and national production substitution policies.

** But note:** The product categories of the China-China speciale are relatively homogeneous (mainly NF3 and WF6) compared to the global giants like Lindh, Airchemists, and do not yet have full product coverage. The mega-coastal river is based on the "one-stop" supply capacity of hundreds of gases and a global network of on-site gas services, which still leaves a large gap between domestic firms.

# IV. Risk assessment

##4.1 Risks of safe production (specific to chemical industries)

Companies are required to possess a number of qualifications, such as a safe production licence, a hazardous chemical operating licence, and other qualifications.

It is worth noting that the electricalization of Japan's Kanto was caused by the 3,700 tons of NF3 capacity that was suspended as a result of the factory explosion, and the expected recovery period of at least one year. This is a true indication of the risk of safe production in chemical industries — accidents that could occur at any time and have a significant impact.

For China’s particulars, while the competitor’s accident creates business opportunities for it, the company itself faces similar risks. Security management pressures on new production lines can increase significantly, especially during the rapid expansion of capacity.

# 4.2 Risk of a continued decline in the Māori rate

This is the most substantial business risk of the day. In 2022-2025, the combined Māori rate for companies fell from 42.86 to 27.77 per cent, and the rate for electro-specialized panels was only 24.93 per cent.

- Increased competition in the NF3 market: the domestic expansion of many enterprises (Technology, South Great Photoelectric, etc.) has been sustained, and the NF3 has been changed from "scarce species" to "relative overstocks"
- Increased bargaining power of clients: downstream crystal mills have increased bargaining power following the increase in the number of national gas suppliers
- Cost fluctuations of raw materials: higher prices of upstream raw materials such as tungsten powder squeeze profits

If the Māori rate continues to fall below 20 per cent, even if the revenue growth increases, it will be difficult to secure profit growth.

# 4.3 WF6 incremental uncertainty

The market’s optimistic expectations of China’s performance in 2026 (net profit of $1.24 billion) are based mainly on the assumption that the WF6 price rises. However, the following factors may lead to a failure of expectations:

- ** Japanese supply may not be completely disrupted:** notifications of Kanton electrochemical and central nitros are "suppressive" rather than "total shutdown", and there may be stock buffers.
- ** Rapid follow-up by domestic competitors:** WF6 new production lines are being built or tested in Xinhua technology, and in distant gas, and in Lake North Green Mitsubishi.
- ** Progress in expansion of company capacity:** 1,000 additional tonnes of WF6 capacity expected to be available in 2027 and WF6 capacity remaining at 2,000 tonnes/year in 2026
- ** The company itself has clarified:** "No new long-term or large-value substantive order agreements have been signed."

##4.4 Client concentration risk

Five major customers sold $1.003 billion, or 44.38% of the total, before 2025. Clients include large enterprises like Yangtze Storage, Central Harbour International, and Kyoto East.

##4.5 Unusual increase in administrative costs

The management costs in 2025, which were 41.14 per cent higher than the growth rate (15.88 per cent), are a signal of continuing concern — whether the one-time cost of the expansion period is to be judged or whether the structural reduction in management efficiency is to be seen.

# 4.6 The biggest downside risk

** Valuation of the rewind risk.** Current stock prices have increased by more than 230% in six weeks, PE (TTM) is at a historical 100% split, and the core catalyst (WF6 orders) has not yet been physically settled. If subsequent quarterly reports fail to confirm market expectations (i.e. WF6 orders are less than expected or their capacity expanded) there may be a significant reversal of the stock price.

** The second is that the Māori rate continues to deteriorate.** If the NF3 price competition increases further and the WF6 increment fails to supplement it in a timely manner, companies may face a decline in profits due to growth in revenue.

# V. Consolidated conclusions

# Core judgment

China’s speciality is a national contemplation of electronics, globally competitive in two major varieties, NF3 and WF6. The Japan WF6 breakout offers potentially significant growth opportunities for companies, with the trifluorooctane acid series showing bright performance as a second-growth curve.

** But there are three obvious problems:**

1. ** The gross rate of gross domestic product (Māori) has declined significantly for the fourth consecutive year** (from 43 per cent to 28 per cent), and the competitive advantage of the core product NF3 has been weakened, which is not a good characteristic of the company.
2. ** WF6 increment expected to be landed** and companies themselves have clarified that the non-senting of substantive orders may be too advanced for market pricing
3. ** Valuation is at historical extreme levels** (PE 180-250 times), and even at the most optimistic 2026 projection (net profit of $1.2-14 billion), PE is 44-65 times.

** Conclusion: Companies have a real competitive advantage (WF6 global first, advanced process certification), but current prices have adequately or even overestimated optimism. There is no security margin** before the Māori rate has recovered steadily and WF6 substantive orders have landed.

If you say "Long slope" in the words of the WF6 breakout, it's a business that has a "long slope" (substitution of semiconductor state production) but not thick enough. The WF6 breakout may change the "sweetness" but it needs to be verified.
