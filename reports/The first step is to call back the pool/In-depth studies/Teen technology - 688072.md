# 1.3 Array analysis

**(1) Technical Barrier - High threshold for Zero tolerance fault rate **

The core feature of the semiconductor equipment industry is "zero tolerance error" - chip manufacturing requires very high accuracy, stability and consistency of equipment, and any minor deviation can lead to the end of the entire crystal circle. This means:

- Long-term and high-profile research and development. In 2025, Takutech invested 859 million yuan, or 13.18 per cent of the revenues, and 726 R & D personnel, or 42.83 per cent of the total number of employees, 65 PhDs and 425 Masters.
- After more than a decade of deep farming, the company has built a well-developed intellectual property system, mastering the core techniques of various types of film deposition, such as PECVD, SACVD, ALD and HDPCVD.
- Equipment already supports more than 100 process applications of all media film material required for logical/storage chips.

**(2) Client sticky and conversion costs - equipment, once validated, is extremely difficult to replace**

The customer certification cycle for semiconductor equipment usually takes one to two years and, after certification, the customer rarely changes the supplier.

- Replacement of equipment requires the re-engineering of all processes at extremely high time and financial cost.
- Equipment is highly co-ordinated with other links in the production line, and replacement of one equipment may affect the entire production line.
- Top five clients of Taku Tsi, who account for 79.15 per cent of the total annual sales (2025), include mainstream domestic crystal-crystal factories such as China-Crys International, China-Corp Group, Yangtze Storage, Long-Silent Storage, etc., with deep-seated partnerships.

**(3) Alternative Policy Barriers to National Production - Geopolitical Imperative**

After the company was added to the United States list of entities in December 2024, the alternative to national production was changed from "optional" to "near option." Lower-stream crystals factories were forced to increase the share of national production equipment in procurement decisions, and Tanaka technology benefited directly from it as the only PECVD/SACVD producer with industrialization capacity in the country.

# IV. Risk assessment

##4.1 Technological iterative risk

- Global membrane sediment technology has evolved towards more advanced process nodes (under 5 nm), with significant increases in equipment accuracy, homogeneity, productivity, etc.
- Takutech currently covers 14 nanometres, and there is still a large gap between advanced processes under 7 nanometres. If the country’s crystal mills are to advance advanced processes on a large scale in the future, and company technology is not followed up in a timely manner, they may be at risk of being replaced by new generation equipment.
- ALD equipment, although growing rapidly, is also being deployed at an accelerated pace by domestic competition against micro-firms in hand (an increase of 224 per cent in ALD revenues in 2025), and competition will increase.

# 4.2 Client concentration risk

- The top five clients account for 79.15 per cent of income and have a very high concentration of clients.
- The main customers are Cyclops International, Yangtze Storage, Longsong Storage, China Rainbow, etc., are domestic head crystals mills.
- On the other hand, these clients themselves benefit from a large trend of national production substitution, with low probability of significant procurement reductions in the short term.

# 4.3 Policies and geopolitical risks

- Listed in December 2024 in the United States as an "entity list", although companies have indicated that they have established stable supply chain systems and multiple procurement channels, there are still uncertainties regarding the security of the long-term supply of core spare parts.
- If the United States further tightens the scope of sanctions (e.g. by restricting the export of more parts and components), it may affect the production delivery of some of the company ' s products.
- On the contrary, sanctions also accelerate customer demand for domestically produced equipment, creating a "in danger organic" pattern.

# 4.5 Māori downside risk

- The Māori rate declined from 51 per cent in 2023 to 34 per cent in 2025, with a rapid decline.
- The main reason is the high cost of the certification phase of new products and the potential existence of low-price strategies for some products to gain market share.
- If the Māori rate does not rebound steadily as the volume of new products expands, the growth of profits will continue to be stressed.
- Companies have indicated that the Māori rate will improve as new product certification is completed into the volume production phase, but that there is a need to keep track of the certification.

# 4.6 The biggest downside risk

** Māori ratio cannot recover steadily + double-kill risk of high valuation returns **

Scenario: If the post-natal Māori rate of entry of new products is not returned to over 40% (due to increased competition or increased client bargaining power), and macro-mobility tightening or the sub-situation of semiconductor industries lead to a return of valuations from 133 to 60-80 times PE, the share price may be 40-50% lower. This risk is particularly pronounced at the current high valuation level.

# V. Consolidated conclusions

# Core judgment

Takutech is one of the most definitive indicators in the semiconductor-state alternative track. The company’s domestic identity in the field of film-dwelling equipment, deep-bound customer relations, and 11 billion dollar hand-held orders form a strong basis for growth.

** But not currently the ideal point of purchase.** The 133-fold PE valuations already reflect optimism, and PEG 2.6 is not cheap. The large fall from 51% to 34% is the core variable that needs to be tracked – if this is a temporary phenomenon in the certification period for new products, and if the volume increases can recover, the current profits are temporarily suppressed; but if the increase in competition results in a structural shift in the Māori rate, the valuation logic needs to be revisited.

# Whether it's worth the deep-stalking

** Worth tracking, but better buy-in timing.**

It is a good company with a scarcely competitive position on the right track, with clear long-term logic. But short-term valuations are high, and trends in Maori rates are unclear, suggesting that there be sustained attention rather than rushing to build a warehouse.

# Key observation indicators

1. ** Māori rate trend** (core): track the progress of the year by season, and see if there are signs of a steady recovery from the second quarter of 2026.
2. **The growth in hand orders and contractual liabilities**: These two indicators are the leading indicators of income over the next 1-2 years.
3. **ALD revenue growth**: Reflecting the progress of the company in the advanced process, it is a key variable that has moved from the "maturity process" to the "advanced process breakthrough".
4. **Down net profit rate**: Discount real profitability unless it is a recurrent gain or gain. Only $102 million (non-recurring gain or gain or gain) of net non-recurring interest in the first quarter of 2026 requires sustained attention to the quality of main profits.
5. ** Major shareholder and senior manager downscaling**: The first phase of withdrawal of the large fund is a definite event, with concern for whether the reduction represents a continuing pressure on stock prices and whether management has repurchased or increased its position after the reduction.
** Valuation range**: A return of equity prices to the $300-350 range (equivalent to 18-21 times PS or 45-55 times PE for the expected net gain of 2026) would provide more marginal buy-in opportunities.
