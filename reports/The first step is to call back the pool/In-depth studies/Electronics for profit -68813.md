# 1.3 Array analysis

** "Technology-PCB" vertical collusive barrier:** This is the unique competitive advantage of a bioelectronics. The parent technology is the second and first leading company in the world (CCL). The co-development of the M9-level high-end copper sheet by the bioelectronics and parent companies has resulted in vertical integration from upstream materials to downstream PCBs, with a cost reduction of about 20%. This "material self-research + PCB manufacturing" synergistic model is unique among domestic counterparts.

** Advanced product certification barriers:** The AI server PCB (especially the high multi-layered, HDI panels with GPU servers) requires a far greater number of layers, line width/wire range, resistance control, signal integrity than traditional servers. It usually takes 12-18 months to pass through the certification cycle for head clients, and the switching costs are very high once they enter the supply chain. Companies have successfully entered the Amazon supply chain and are actively developing ASIC clients such as Meta, Google, etc.

** Technical barriers:** The company has already achieved bulk delivery in the 800G high-speed switch PCB area, while developing the next generation of 1.6T-Ethernet mainboard PPB. Process is extremely difficult in 18-storeys and high multi-layered systems, with a very limited number of manufacturers with stable production capacity in the country.

** But note that:** The PCB sector is not as well as semiconductor material (e.g. polishing fluids). The PCB is a manufacturing enterprise, with relatively limited product differentiation, and competition barriers rely more on client certification and capacity size than on formulas or patents. Deep South circuits, power shares, and winning macrostudies are all actively expanding the capacity of the AI server PCB, and competition may increase in the future.

# IV. Risk assessment

##4.1 AI Risk of volatility of the arithmetic investment cycle (core risk)

The company AI captures more than 60% of its computing-related products and highly binds its performance to the AI ' s computing investment cycle.

- Global cloud producers ' capital spending is slowing down or under-repairing
- The demand for AI model training has not grown as expected
- GPU/ASIC chip rotational rhythm slows down

** Historical lessons:** In 2022-2023, corporate revenue fell for the second consecutive year and lost for 2023, owing to the cyclical downside of downstream demand. AI ' s ability to calculate, though a long-term trend, is inevitable in the short term.

** On the other hand:** The current 2026 ACI Capital Expenditure Directions of the world’s leading cloud producers (MSC, Amazon, Google, Meta, etc.) are still up and have a high short-term demand profile. But there is a need to closely track quarterly capital expenditure data.

# 4.2 Client concentration risk

The ASI server, PCB, is naturally concentrated in a few cloud and server producers, which is determined by industry characteristics. If a single large customer (e.g. Amazon) adjusts the supplier’s strategy or reduces the procurement volume, the impact on the company’s performance may be very direct.

** Mitigating factors:** The company is expanding its ACIS clients such as Meta, Google and Tesla in the electronics of cars, and client diversification is advancing.

# 4.3 Energy expansion and competition increase risks

The company’s own expansion (860,000 square metres of additional capacity) and collective expansion of the industry (the power supply of 4.3 billion tons, the deep South-South expansion of four million years, and the multi-billion target) could result in oversupply in 2027-2028. If the supply is released at a time when the growth in the AIS’s capacity for computing is slowing down, it will stifle the industry’s Māori rate and profitability.

##4.4 Accounts receivable and fund occupancy risk

Receivables rose significantly in 2025 and were normal during the high growth period, but attention was paid to the pace of repayment.

# 4.5 The biggest downside risk

**The "Davis double-kill" released by the build-up of production capacity at the bottom of the AI ' s calculated investment cycle.** If global AI capital spending slowed significantly from 2027-2028 (from the current 50% to less than 10%) and the industry ' s new production capacity was concentrated, companies could face a substantial price drop. This risk is not impossible, considering the experience of 2023 (earnings - 7.4%, losses). In this scenario, corporate profits could fall from 2.5-2.8 billion in 2026 to 1.5 billion or less, and stock prices could be hit by a double-deep "performance plus valuation contraction".

** Probability assessment:** The probability of this scenario occurring in the short term (2026-2027) is low (global AI investment is still accelerating) and needs to be closely tracked in the medium term (after 2028).
