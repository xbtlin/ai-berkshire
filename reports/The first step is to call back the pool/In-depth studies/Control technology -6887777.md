# IV. Risk assessment

##4.1 Process industry capital expenditure cycle risk

- The high reliance on capital expenditure in downstream chemical and petrochemical industries for company performance
- The revenue in 2025 of 11.9 per cent, net profit of 59.7 per cent, which is a direct reflection of the downward cycle
- If macroeconomic weaknesses are weak, fixed-asset investments in industries such as chemicals may continue to be depressed
- The high sensitivity of profits to falling revenue (business leverage) has magnified cyclical risk

** Risk level: high** - this is the most immediate business risk at present and already occurring.

# 4.2 Industrial AI commercialization is not as good as expected

- The management income target for AI is extremely radical (US$ 1 billion in 2026 and US$ 5 billion in 2028)
- The transformation of industrial AI from project to product is a huge challenge
- The client ' s willingness to pay for and accept AI products remains uncertain
- If AI ' s income falls far short of the target, it will severely impact the market ' s growth expectations for companies

** Risk level: Medium and High ** - AI is the core underpinning of the valuation and is under high pressure.

# 4.3 Competition with transnational giants

- Emerson, ABB, Siemens, Hornwell still have an absolute advantage in the global market.
- The internationalization of the CM technology is concentrated in emerging markets and the positive competition with the transnational giants in high-end markets is still weak
- If the multinational giants launch price wars or increase technological inputs in the Chinese market, they may squeeze the market share of the controlled technology
- In turn, the trend of national production alternatives is favourable to controlled technologies

** Risk level: Medium** - Policy protection in domestic markets and gaps in global markets.

#4.4 Risk of a continuous decline in the Māori rate

- The Māori rate fell from ~35 per cent in 2024 to ~32 per cent in 2025 and further fell from 2026Q1 to 30.99 per cent
- If the decline in the Māori rate is trendy (as a result of increased competition or changes in product structure), it will have a serious impact on profitability
- The initial Māori rate for new operations such as Industrial AI may be low, lowering the overall level
- The trend in Maori rates over the next quarter needs to be closely monitored

** Risk level: Medium** - Māori rate decline is alarming but may be partly influenced by cyclical factors.

#4.5 Receivables and refund risk

- Corporate receivables are large and are the focus of analysts such as the securities star
- Long repayment cycles in the industrial automation industry and increased risk of bad debts in the event of difficulties in running downstream customers
- The quality of accounts receivable requires particular attention in the context of the decline in performance in 2025

** Risk level: Medium** - Long-standing industry characteristics, short-term concern rising.

# 4.6 The biggest downside risk

** Core risk scenario: Continued low capital spending for process industries + Commercialization of industrial AI is far below expectations + Maori rate continues to decline **

If these three negative factors occur simultaneously, the company’s revenue may remain at $7.5 to 85 billion, with net profits falling to $300 to $50 billion. At the market value of $62.6 billion, the value would be 125 to 209 times the PE, with serious re-engineering.

If the benchmark of $450 million in real net profits in 2025 were to be given 40 times the PE, the corresponding market value would be only $18 billion, with greater downside space.
