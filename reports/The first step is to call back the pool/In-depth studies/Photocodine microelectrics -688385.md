# V. Consolidated conclusions

# # Overall evaluation

Microelectricity is the absolute leader of the national FPGA track, with 20 years of technological accumulation and a dominant position in high-security areas (military/space). Multi-product line layout (FPGA+Scree+Mechanical Chip) provides some income diversification.

But the problem that needs to be sobered:

1. **The sharp fall in profit in 2025, 59%** is a major warning sign, with 439 million assets impaired to indicate that some product lines are under-required.
2. ** The technological gap with Cyrus/AMD (2-3 generations)** is a hard constraint, and there is significant uncertainty as to when the advanced FPGA will break through
3. **FPGA income is 91% dependent on high-reliability areas**, civilian FPGA is slowly expanding and growth space is limited
4. ** very high valuation** (2.45 times PE) which is more than 100 times more than normal profit
5. 30% of R & D intensity, while reflecting technological inputs, also indicates that companies are still in "input" rather than "harvesting"

Microelectrics are more like a "strategic holding" standard -- buying the long-term strategic value of the national production of the FPGA than short-term performance. If trusting the national production of the FPGA is finally going to break through the advanced process, it is now the long-term layout window; if certainty and security margins are pursued, the current point is not right.
