# IV. Risk assessment

##4.1 Core risk

**(1) AIDC investment cycle risk**

If the global technology giants slow down in capital spending, data centre transformers demand may be rewinded in a phased manner. Corporate data centre orders have reached 42%, and dependence on a single downstream rises.

**(2) Overseas trade policy and tariff risks**

Despite corporate risk of hedging tariffs through global production capacity distribution, US tariffs on equipment manufactured in China may still affect competitiveness. US domestic plants are still in the preparatory stage, with uncertainty before they are fully settled.

**(3) Price wars in the domestic market for new energy sources**

The domestic wind and photovoltaic markets are highly competitive, with transformer prices compressing profit margins. Companies have adapted their strategies, but domestic business growth is under pressure.

**(4) Extension of capacity and financial risks**

Companies simultaneously promote multi-field plant construction and debt-transferable finance, with an asset-liability rate of 57.86 per cent. If the release of new production is slower than expected, this may affect the return on investment.

**(5) Price volatility of raw materials**

The main raw materials of transformers include silicon steel chips, copper wires, etc., and price fluctuations have a direct impact on the Māori rate.
