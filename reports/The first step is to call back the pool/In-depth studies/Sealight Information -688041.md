# IV. Risk assessment

# 4.1 AMD Mandate Extension and Technology ceiling Risk

** Risk level: medium-high**

- Although the company stated that it had completed technology absorption, the bottom authorization of the x86 structure was essentially "limited" - limited in the areas of servers and workstations and limited to mainland China.
- The extension of the AMD product iterative cycle from two to four to five years after the break-up indicates that the upgrading of autonomous iterative capacity is costly.
- AMD will pose a significant risk if it is to further limit the scope of its mandate in the future, for example by challenging whether sealight derivatives are beyond the scope of the mandate through legal means, owing to geopolitical or commercial considerations.
- On the other hand, the Technical Licensing Agreement was signed with clear legal agreement and there are no restrictions on the use of authorized technology by companies.

# 4.2 Risks of technology failure

** Risk level: medium**

- Sealight CPU is based on the Zen 1 architecture, while the AMD has reached Zen 5, with an intergenerational gap of about 4-5 generations. It is difficult to catch up with absolute performance.
- But in the credit creation market, "sufficient" is enough — customers value supply chain security and ecological compatibility more than extreme performance.
- In the DCU, performance gaps are more pronounced between the deep-seated series and the British Weidelberg H100/A100, especially in the large-scale AI training scene.
- Process processes are limited to surrogate plant capabilities (after listing entities, advanced substitutes such as power generation are restricted), which may affect product competitiveness.

#4.3 Creature policy change risk

** Risk level: medium**

- Creature policy is a central driver of sea light income growth. If policy pushes the pace down, budget cuts or alternative standards change, it will have a direct impact on corporate performance.
- The strength and direction of the subsequent incremental policy is uncertain after the 2027 end of the business creation of the country.
- If the credit-creation market moves from "x86 priority" to "multistructured parallel" (e.g. more support for Arm, RISC-V), the monopoly of sea light will be weakened.

## 4.4 Competition increases risk

** Risk level: medium-high**

- **Full + Tung**: China is making rapid progress in the ecological construction of the credit and AI numeracy markets, creating two-line pressure on the sea light. The advantage of China is in the integration of hardware and software and government relations.
- **Flying**: The Arm Architecture CPU, which has a share in the party-initiative market, is improving, although not as ecologically compatible as the sealight.
- **Vida/AMD national production alternatives**: If the international chip giant re-enters the Chinese market through special channels or custom versions, the competitive pressure on the sealight will increase dramatically.

##4.5 Supply chain risk

** Risk level: high**

- Access to advanced production options (up to 7nm in power) and HBM high bandwidth memory is restricted by inclusion in the list of United States entities.
- The United States continues to strengthen export controls on advanced semiconductor technologies, which may further reduce the supply chain space for sealight.
- Companies are currently presumed to be partially producing through domestic sub-plants such as China-Chole International, but the advanced process capability of domestic sub-plants (below 14 nm) is far from being at the international lead level.

# 4.6 The biggest downside risk

** Valuation high + high demand for information and communication technology at the top + risk of increased competition.**

The situation is marked by a steep decline in demand for centralized procurement after the end of the IT replacement policy in 2027; a steady expansion in China’s share in the IT market, with the sea light CPU slowing down significantly to less than 20%; and a failure by DCU to break through the AI market as expected, which could lead to an effective second-growth curve. In this scenario, the PE returns from the current 277 to 80-100 times the market’s enthusiasm for the IT-AI trend, with a slower increase in the profits, and shares may face 50-60% downside space.

This risk is particularly acute at the current market value level of $700 billion — the space for error is extremely small and the performance of any quarter may trigger a larger valuation adjustment than expected.
