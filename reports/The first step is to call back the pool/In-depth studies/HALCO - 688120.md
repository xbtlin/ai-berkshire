# III. Growing up and valuation

#3.1 Nationally produced replacement space for CMP equipment

CMP is one of the core processes for crystal circle manufacturing, each of which requires multiple CMP polishing (over 30 paths for advanced process). The core drivers of alternative space:

- **Main China CMP market space**: estimated at approximately $1.47 billion (approximately RMB 10.6 billion) in 2026, CMP material-consuming market space is not less than $1.18 billion.
- ** There is still room for a significant improvement in the national production rate**: the proportion of CMP equipment imported fell from 89 per cent in 2022 to 63 per cent in 2025, meaning that domestic producers have taken some 37 per cent of the share, but 63 per cent of import replacement space remains.
- **Hae-Chingko has a share of more than 90 per cent of the domestic production of CMP equipment** and almost exclusive access to the replacement dividend of national production.

Simple calculation: Assuming that China ' s CMP annual market space for equipment is RMB 10 billion, the rate of national production has increased from 37 to 60 per cent, and that China ' s share of national production is 90 per cent, the corresponding space is RMB 5.4 billion, or about 15 per cent more than the $4.648 billion collected in 2025. If the rate of national production is further raised to 80 per cent, the space is RMB 7.2 billion.

** On the other hand,** this measure considers only stock substitution. Real growth elasticity comes from: (1) incremental demand for equipment resulting from the significant increase in the number of advanced process CMP steps; (2) incremental demand for new production lines; and (3) long-tail income such as crystallized regeneration, and waste services.

# V. Consolidated conclusions

# Qualitative judgment

Hsingko is a rare indicator with a clear "national substitution" logic: the almost only small-scale supplier of domestic CMP equipment, downstream binding of mainstream domestic crystal mills, benefits from a definite trend towards higher national production rates.

** But three structural problems currently require continuous follow-up**

1. **No gains**: 36% increase in business in 2025 but only 6% increase in profits, a gradual phenomenon of the climb of new products or a trend sign of a worsening of the pattern of competition.
**Maori rate is stable**: for three years, it has fallen by 6 percentage points to less than 42%, which is below the level of internationally comparable companies.
3. **The margin for valuation is inadequate**: 87 times the PE corresponds to a 6% profit increase, with a significant gap between market implicit expectations of high growth and current performance. Unless the profit growth rate returns to over 30% in 2026, the current valuation is not supported.

# Investment rating

Current stage: **Observation, pending confirmation of profit growth point**

- If the mid-year/tri-quarterly report for 2026 shows a return to profit growth of over 25 per cent and a stable Mäori rate of over 42 per cent, price intervention of less than 60 times PE may be considered.
- If profits continue to grow at a rate below 15 per cent, then the company needs to be reassessed whether it is worth 87 times the PE premium.
