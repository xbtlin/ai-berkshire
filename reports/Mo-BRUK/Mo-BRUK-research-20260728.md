## 2. The Nature of the Business — Duan Yongping's "Right Business"

### One sentence

**Mo-BRUK is paid to permanently absorb legal liability that its customers are forbidden by law to keep — and the permits that let it do so cannot be bought, only granted.**

That is the whole business. Customers do not buy a service; they buy discharge of a statutory obligation. The waste has negative value to its producer, which is why the producer pays to be rid of it.

### Business model characteristics

| Dimension | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Revenue type | Per-tonne gate fees. Volume × price |
| Recurring vs one-off | **Mixed and this is the central tension.** Industrial/medical waste streams are recurring and contracted. "Ecological bomb" state remediation tenders are lumpy, politically-funded, and finite |
| Asset intensity | High. Incinerators, solidification plants, RDF lines, landfill cells |
| Operating leverage | **High.** Fixed-cost plant; incremental tonne carries very high incremental margin. Q1'26 revenue +32.6% drove operating profit +33.4% |
| Pricing | Scarcity-driven. Limited licensed capacity nationally |
| Customer concentration | Diversified in industrial waste; concentrated in state tenders |

### Profitability trend — the uncomfortable part

Core EBITDA margin remains outstanding: ~45% in 2024 and H1'25, 41.3% in Q1'26. For an asset-heavy industrial business these are exceptional numbers and are the single strongest evidence of a moat.

But **net margin has fallen for four consecutive years: 44.1% → 43.0% → 37.9% → 33.3% → 24.8% → 4.8%.** Even excluding the 2025 one-off, the trend from 2021 to 2024 (43% → 25%) is real and pre-dates the write-off. Causes include rising depreciation from the capex cycle, higher financial expenses on growing debt, and mix.

The 2021 peak was inflated by an unusually rich cycle of ecological-bomb contracts. **Treating 2021 as the baseline is an anchoring error.** The correct baseline is closer to 2023–2024.

### Duan Yongping's question: *What is good about this business, in one sentence?*

Customers cannot legally do nothing, cannot legally do it themselves, and have almost nowhere else to go. Demand is created by statute rather than by preference, which makes it insensitive to both price and economic cycle — reflected in a beta of 0.22.

## 3. Economic Moat — Buffett

| Moat type | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Regulatory / permit barrier** | ★★★★★ | Integrated permits (*pozwolenie zintegrowane*) for hazardous-waste incineration take years, require environmental impact consent, and face intense local opposition. Capital alone cannot buy entry |
| **Scale** | ★★★★☆ | Largest player nationally, only listed pure-play in the sector. Multi-site network across Poland enables logistics optimisation |
| **Technology / know-how** | ★★★☆☆ | Proprietary laboratories, certified processes, ability to handle waste streams competitors cannot accept safely |
| **Switching costs** | ★★☆☆☆ | Moderate. Contracts are re-tenderable, but the alternative-supplier set is tiny |
| **Brand / pricing power** | ★★☆☆☆ | B2B and B2G. Pricing power comes from scarcity, not brand |
| **Network effects** | ☆☆☆☆☆ | None |

### Competitive structure

In solidification and stabilisation, Mo-BRUK has **essentially one competitor** — the Konin Waste Utilisation Facility (ZUO Konin), which generated under PLN 24m of revenue in 2019, less than half of Mo-BRUK's revenue in that segment at the time. This is close to a duopoly with a dominant leader.

### Moat trend

**Past 5 years: widening.** Tightening EU and Polish environmental law, stricter enforcement against illegal disposal, and rising landfill restrictions all expand the pool of waste that must be handled by licensed operators.

**Next 5 years: widening, with one large caveat.** EU circular-economy and landfill directives continue to push volumes toward licensed processors. The caveat is in §4.

### Buffett's question: *Will this moat exist in 10 years? What destroys it?*

The moat is almost certainly intact in 10 years — permits do not become easier to obtain. What destroys it is not competition but **the state itself**: the same regulator that grants the moat can impose retroactive fees, revoke a permit after an incident, or change the fee basis. Mo-BRUK's moat and Mo-BRUK's largest risk are the same institution. That is an unusual and under-appreciated structure.

## 4. Inversion and Risk Register — Munger

### 4.1 The 2025 write-off, in detail

This is the dominant fact of the current investment case.

In Q3 2025, Mo-BRUK recognised impairments totalling **PLN 65.2m**, comprising a write-down of receivables for elevated environmental fees (*opłaty podwyższone*) paid plus interest: **PLN 48.6m for 2018 and PLN 16.6m for 2019.** A further **PLN 2.2m** was charged for liquidation of fixed assets at the Wałbrzych branch. Total one-off charges: **PLN 67.4m.**

The fees relate to waste storage at the Wałbrzych landfill. The company had paid the disputed fees (PLN 52.4m was remitted to the marshal's office) and carried them as recoverable receivables. Following adverse Supreme Administrative Court (NSA) rulings on 1 July 2025 concerning the 2015 and 2017 fee years, the company wrote the receivables down.

**Three things follow, and they point in different directions:**

1. **The charge is largely non-cash in 2025** — the cash left the company earlier. It is an accounting recognition that a previously-assumed recovery is no longer probable.
2. **A recovery option survives.** The company continues to contest the assessments in the administrative courts. It has stated that if it prevails, the amounts will be returned with interest. This is an unvalued call option on roughly PLN 65m — about 4.8% of market cap.
3. **The exposure may not be bounded at 2018–2019.** NSA rulings have now gone against the company on 2015, 2017, and by implication 2018–2019. Whether further years remain exposed is **not established in this research** and is the single most important open question for a prospective investor.

### 4.2 Failure path register

| # | Path to failure | Probability | Impact | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Elevated-fee disputes extend to further years | **Medium** | **High** | Pattern of adverse rulings established. Bounds unknown |
| 2 | Ecological-bomb tenders dry up as state funding ends | Medium | Medium-High | Order book PLN 127m + pipeline ~108m gives ~2 years visibility, no more |
| 3 | Environmental incident / permit revocation at a facility | Low | **Very High** | Single-facility loss would be severe; this is the tail risk that matters |
| 4 | New licensed incineration capacity enters | Low-Medium | Medium | Permitting difficulty cuts both ways — protects Mo-BRUK, but the state can fast-track municipal capacity |
| 5 | Cement-kiln demand for RDF falls | Medium | Low-Medium | RDF is 19% of revenue; cement output is cyclical |
| 6 | Controlling family acts against minorities | Low-Medium | Medium | 51.1% of votes on 41.4% of capital. See §5 |
| 7 | Capex cycle fails to earn its cost of capital | Medium | Medium | Depreciation already compressing net margin since 2021 |
| 8 | Eco Point acquisition integration disappoints | Medium | Low | Small relative to group |
| 9 | Key-person risk / founder succession | Medium | Medium | Family-controlled, professional CEO |

### 4.3 Historical analogues

- **Clean Harbors / US Ecology (US hazardous waste)** — permit-scarcity businesses that compounded for decades. The bull analogue.
- **Companies destroyed by retroactive environmental liability** — the same asset base that generates fees also generates legacy obligations that surface years later. Mo-BRUK's Wałbrzych fee dispute is exactly this pattern in miniature. The bear analogue.
- The distinction between these two outcomes is almost entirely **regulatory relationship quality**, not operational skill.

### 4.4 The bear case, stated fairly

- Reported PE is **90×**. Reported ROE is 7.4%.
- The dividend is **not covered by earnings** — see §5.
- Net debt has grown to PLN 134m; net debt/equity 0.59×.
- Net margin has declined for four straight years, and the pre-write-off portion of that decline is real.
- The stock is up 33% in 12 months and trading at or above its 52-week high, into a valuation that requires the adjusted-earnings interpretation to be correct.
- The 2021 earnings peak has never been recovered, five years on.
- The elevated-fee litigation history shows management previously carried as recoverable an asset that courts repeatedly said was not.

### 4.5 Bias self-check

- **Narrative bias**: "waste is a wonderful regulated business" is seductive and mostly true — but it is precisely the story that makes an investor forgive four years of margin decline.
- **Anchoring**: adjusting 2025 EPS back to 23.51 and calling it "normal" is a choice, not a fact. It assumes zero recurrence of a charge type that has now recurred across multiple fee years.
- **Survivorship**: Clean Harbors is remembered; the hazardous-waste operators that hit a liability wall are not.

### Munger's question: *Where am I most likely wrong, and why would a smart person not buy this?*

Most likely wrong on **normalising the write-off**. A smart person declines because they cannot bound the regulatory liability — you are buying an asset whose principal counterparty is a state that has repeatedly won against it in court, at a valuation that only works if you assume that stops happening.

### The dividend question

Management has recommended a dividend of **PLN 15.23 per share for FY2025** — a total outlay of **PLN 53.5m**. Record date **30 July 2026**, payment **5 October 2026**.

Critically, the funding split is: **PLN 3.5m from 2025 profit and PLN 49.99m from retained earnings held in the supplementary capital reserve.**

| Coverage test | Ratio |
|---|---|
| DPS 15.23 vs reported 2025 EPS 4.33 | **3.52× uncovered** |
| DPS 15.23 vs adjusted 2025 EPS 23.51 | 0.65× — covered |

Two readings, and an investor must choose:

- **Charitable**: management is looking through a non-cash accounting charge, signalling confidence that normalised earning power is ~PLN 82m, and maintaining a policy investors rely on. FCF of PLN 81.6m comfortably covers PLN 53.5m.
- **Skeptical**: a raised dividend paid 93% out of reserves, while net debt sits at PLN 134m and a buyback runs concurrently, transfers cash to a controlling family that holds 41% of the economics — at a moment when reported earnings collapsed.

The FCF coverage is the strongest argument for the charitable reading. FCF of PLN 81.6m against PLN 53.5m of dividends is a 65% payout of actual cash — defensible.

Dividend history (PLN/share): **2021: 20.15 · 2022: 31.50 · 2023: 13.17 · 2024: 13.17 · 2025: 13.17 · 2026: 15.23 (recommended)**. Note the sharp step down after 2022 — the dividend already reset once when the ecological-bomb boom faded. ⚠️ Attribution of each payment to profit-year vs payment-year is not fully resolved; treat the sequence as indicative.

Stated policy: distribute **at least 50% of net profit**, conditional on leverage not exceeding 2.5× at year-end. Current leverage 1.3× — within policy.

### Duan Yongping's question: *If the CEO retired, would the company still be competitive?*

Yes. The moat is in the permits and the plant, not in the person. This is a business where mediocre management still earns 40%+ core EBITDA margins. That is a compliment to the asset, not the team.

## 6. Industry and Civilisational Trend — Li Lu

### Market context

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Polish hazardous waste generated annually | ~2m tonnes |
| Polish municipal waste generated annually | ~12m tonnes |
| Mo-BRUK 2025 throughput | 361.3k tonnes (+12% YoY) |
| Implied share of hazardous stream | **~18%** (rough — not all of the 2m t is addressable by Mo-BRUK's permit set) |

### Structural drivers

1. **EU circular-economy and landfill directives** — progressively restrict landfilling, forcing volumes toward incineration and material recovery. Regulatory tailwind with a multi-decade horizon.
2. **"Ecological bombs"** (*bomby ekologiczne*) — remediation of historic illegal waste dumps, state-funded. Order book **PLN 127m**, additional identified pipeline **~PLN 108m**. This is real but finite and politically dependent.
3. **RDF demand from cement kilns** — Poland is a major cement producer; co-incineration of alternative fuels is established practice.
4. **Enforcement intensity** — the economics only work if illegal disposal is actually punished. Mo-BRUK's addressable market is a direct function of state enforcement capacity.

### Position in the value chain

Mo-BRUK sits at the **terminal node** — the point where waste stops being anyone's problem. Terminal nodes in regulated chains typically capture good economics because they are the bottleneck and the liability sink. It does not, however, control the upstream volume, which is set by industrial activity and regulation.

### Technology route risk: **Low**

Incineration and solidification are mature. There is no plausible technology that eliminates the need to destroy hazardous waste within a 10-year horizon. Chemical recycling could shift *some* streams but not the hazardous core.

### Management guidance for 2026

Revenue growth of **~20%** (implying ~PLN 384m), with **capex reduced to PLN 20–30m** after completion of the main investment programme. Falling capex against rising revenue is the single most favourable combination available to an asset-heavy business — if delivered, FCF expands sharply.

### Li Lu's question: *Looking back from 20 years out — Standard Oil, or 3Com?*

**Neither.** Mo-BRUK is not a paradigm-shifting franchise and does not need to be. The correct analogue is a **regional toll road with political risk** — a licensed, capacity-constrained, statutorily-demanded piece of infrastructure that compounds modestly and pays out cash, whose value is set by the durability of its concession rather than by innovation. This is not a civilisational paradigm shift; it is a compliance annuity. Priced correctly, that is a perfectly good thing to own. Priced as a growth company, it is not.

## 7. Valuation and Margin of Safety

The entire valuation debate reduces to which earnings number is real.

| Metric | On reported 2025 | On adjusted 2025 (ex PLN 67.4m one-off) |
|---|---|---|
| EPS | 4.33 | 23.51 |
| **PE** | **89.95×** | **16.57×** |
| Earnings yield | 1.11% | 6.04% |
| ROE | 6.63% | 36.03% |
| P/B | 5.97× | 5.97× |
| P/FCF | 16.77× | 16.77× |
| FCF yield | 5.96% | 5.96% |
| Dividend yield | 3.91% | 3.91% |
| EV/EBITDA | 17.04× (reported EBITDA 87.5) | **10.35×** (est. core EBITDA ~144m) |

*Adjusted EPS = (15.2 + 65.2 + 2.2) ÷ 3.512885 = 23.51. **This is a pre-tax add-back and therefore flatters the result** — the true tax-effected figure is somewhat lower. Estimate, not a reported number.*

*Core EBITDA of ~PLN 144m is derived by applying the company's stated ~45% core-business EBITDA margin to 2025 revenue. **Estimate.** Reported EBITDA was 87.5m.*

**The most reliable anchor is FCF, because it sidesteps the accounting argument entirely**: P/FCF of 16.8× and an FCF yield of 5.96% on PLN 81.6m of actual cash generation. Forward PE is 14.13×, implying consensus 2026 EPS of ~PLN 27.57.

### 7.2 Reverse DCF — what does PLN 389.50 imply?

Using FCF per share of PLN 23.23 in a perpetuity-growth frame:

| Discount rate | Implied perpetual FCF growth |
|---|---|
| 10% | **4.04%** |
| 12% | **6.04%** |

At a 10% cost of equity, the market is pricing roughly **4% perpetual FCF growth**. Against a 12.4% five-year revenue CAGR, ~20% guided 2026 revenue growth, and capex falling from a completed investment cycle, **that is not a demanding hurdle.** The market is not pricing this as a growth company.

This is the strongest single argument for the stock.

### 7.3 Three-scenario valuation (3-year horizon)

Base EPS 23.51 (adjusted 2025). Computed via `financial_rigor.py three-scenario`.

| Scenario | Annual growth | Exit PE | Year-3 EPS | Target price | Return |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bull** | 18% | 20× | 38.63 | **PLN 772.6** | **+98.3%** |
| **Base** | 10% | 15× | 31.29 | **PLN 469.4** | **+20.5%** |

Scenario assumptions:
- **Bull**: ecological-bomb pipeline converts, Eco Point scales, RDF capacity fills, capex stays low, no further fee liabilities. Re-rating to 20×.
- **Base**: mid-single to low-double-digit organic growth, mix shift to incineration continues, multiple holds at 15×.
- **Bear**: further adverse fee rulings, tender pipeline dries up, no growth, de-rating to 10× (roughly where the stock has traded historically).

Excluding dividends, the risk/reward is roughly symmetric at the base case with a fat left tail. Including the ~3.9% dividend yield, the base case return improves to roughly +24% annualised over three years.

### 7.4 Historical and peer context

- **Historical**: on 2021 EPS of 32.73 the stock traded around 10–12×. Today's 16.6× adjusted is **above its own history**, and the stock is up 33% in 12 months at or above its 52-week high.
- **Peers**: Western hazardous-waste operators typically trade around 10–14× EV/EBITDA. Mo-BRUK at an estimated 10.35× on core EBITDA is **at or slightly below** that band — appropriate for a small-cap, single-country, less-liquid name with a live regulatory dispute.

### Duan Yongping's question: *If the market shut for 5 years, would you hold at this price?*

At PLN 389.50 — **marginally, yes, but with a smaller position than the business quality alone would justify.** The FCF yield of ~6% and the ~4% implied perpetual growth provide real support. What prevents conviction is that a five-year closure removes the ability to react to the one risk that can permanently impair value: an unbounded regulatory liability. You would be holding blind through exactly the risk you cannot quantify.
