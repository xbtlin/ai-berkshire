## A counter-intuitive starting point

Among the world's most valuable AI companies, DeepSeek is the only one that actively does not make money.

OpenAI’s 2025 revenue is estimated to exceed **$10 billion**. Anthropic raised over **$15 billion** and started generating significant revenue. Google DeepMind is backed by Alphabet's trillion-dollar market capitalization.

What about DeepSeek? API pricing is 1/10 to 1/50 of competing products, all core models are free and open source, and the founder publicly stated that he "does not pursue profits."

Then why does it survive to this day? Why is the valuation **350 billion yuan**?

The answer lies within its parent company.

## Magic Square Quantification: DeepSeek's "Money Printing Machine"

### What is the magic square?

| Project | Content |
|------|------|
| Full name | Hangzhou Magic Square Quantitative Asset Management |
| Founder | Liang Wenfeng (also the founder of DeepSeek) |
| Established | 2015 |
| Nature | Quantitative Hedge Funds |
| Management scale | **70 billion yuan+** (Source: Private Equity Ranking Network) |
| 2025 Yield | **56.55%** (Source: 36Kr) |
| Estimated profit in 2025 | About **5 billion** |
| GPU cluster | About **10,000** NVIDIA A100 (purchased in 2023, before chip ban) |

Magic Square Quantitative is one of the largest quantitative hedge funds in China. Its core capability is to use AI to do quantitative trading - this is also the starting point for Liang Wenfeng to found DeepSeek: ** He accumulated a lot of AI computing power and engineering experience in the process of doing quantitative trading, and then decided to use these resources to build a general-purpose large model. **

### How long can Magic Fang support DeepSeek?

It is estimated that the profit in 2025 will be about 5 billion yuan:

| Project | Estimate |
|------|------|
| DeepSeek annual operating cost | About **1.5-3 billion** (estimated, including computing power, manpower, API subsidies) |
| Magic Square Annual Profit | About **5 billion** |
| Coverage multiple | **1.7-3.3 times** |

Looks like it can be covered. **But there is a fatal assumption here: the magic square's rate of return can be sustained. **

The returns on quantitative funds fluctuate greatly. Magic Square's return of 56.55% in 2025 is **exceptionally high** -- it has experienced significant drawdowns before. If the magic square income reaches zero or even loses money in a certain year, DeepSeek’s source of funding will be cut off.

**Contrary view**: Magic Square’s management scale exceeds 70 billion. Even if the return rate drops to 10-15%, the annual profit will still be 7-10 billion - enough to cover DeepSeek. Moreover, Liang Wenfeng's investment in DeepSeek is "long-term will" and will not cut off supply due to short-term fluctuations.

**Contrary again**: The tail risk of the quantitative fund industry is real. In 2018, China's quantitative fund industry experienced systematic losses, and Magic Square was no exception. Using quantified fund profits to do AI research is essentially using the income from a highly volatile asset to support a pure expenditure project - if both encounter headwinds at the same time, the situation will be ugly.

## First round of financing of 50 billion yuan: turning point

In May 2026, DeepSeek finally launched its first round of external financing since its establishment. Key information:

| Project | Content |
|------|------|
| Financing target | **50 billion yuan** (Source: Wall Street News, Sina Finance) |
| Liang Wenfeng’s personal investment | Maximum **20 billion yuan** |
| Leading investor | Negotiating the National Integrated Circuit Industry Investment Fund (Large Fund) |
| Post-money valuation | Approximately **350 billion** (~$45 billion) |
| Other potential investors | Multiple Internet giants and state-owned funds |

(Source: Wall Street News, Sina Finance, 36Kr, investment community)

### What does this round of financing mean?

**See more interpretations**:

1. Led by a large national fund = endorsed by the national team, chip supply chain guarantee upgrade
2. 50 billion yuan is sufficient ammunition to cover the computing power investment in the next 2-3 years.
3. Liang Wenfeng spent 20 billion out of his own pocket, which shows that the founder has strong confidence
4. A valuation of 350 billion yuan corresponds to the positioning of the first echelon of global AI

**Short interpretation**:

1. The "idealism of not financing" is over - financing means having shareholders, and with shareholders there are expectations of returns
2. Participation of large national funds = political factors are involved, and research directions may be limited
3. A valuation of 350 billion yuan corresponds to a company with **zero revenue** (or nearly zero revenue)
4. Liang Wenfeng invested 20 billion, possibly because external investors were unwilling to invest so much - or it may be due to control rights considerations

**A logic to be wary of**: The market is using "OpenAI valuation of US$300 billion" to anchor DeepSeek's valuation. But OpenAI has more than $10 billion in annual revenue and 150 million+ weekly active users. What does DeepSeek have? Technical influence, open source ecology, and an option of "possible commercialization in the future". The valuation logic of the two is completely different.

## Monetization path: Where are you now? Where to go in the future?

### Current source of income

| Source | Size | Nature |
|------|------|------|
| API service | Extremely low (price is 1/10-1/50 of competing products) | Deliberately low price |
| Corporate customers | **3,200+** (Source: DemandSage) | Considerable scale but low unit price |
| Monthly API calls | **5.7 billion** (Source: DemandSage) | Large usage, low unit price |

Rough estimate: Assuming average API revenue of $0.50/million tokens, 5.7 billion monthly calls, and an average of 1,000 tokens each time, the annual revenue is about **hundreds of millions of yuan** - almost negligible compared to operating costs and valuation.

### Potential monetization path

| Path | Feasibility | Description |
|------|--------|------|
| Increase API pricing | Medium | There is an ecological lock-in basis, but "low price" is the core of the brand |
| Enterprise-level customization | Medium to high | 3,200+ enterprise customers are the starting point |
| Government/National Defense | High (domestic) | The probability of government-enterprise cooperation increases after large funds invest in shares |
| C-end products | Low | Product capabilities are DeepSeek’s biggest shortcoming |
| License fee | Low | Conflict with MIT open source agreement |

**Opposite view**: DeepSeek's "not making money" may not be a problem - if its goal is to become "AI infrastructure". Linux doesn't make money, but Red Hat (acquired by IBM for $34 billion) does. Android does not make money, but Google has made profits through the Android ecosystem that far exceed development costs. There are many successful examples of commercialization of open source.

**Another objection**: Behind Linux and Android, giants like IBM/Google continue to invest. Behind DeepSeek is a quantitative fund. The depth of resources between the two is not the same.

## Competitive Landscape: Four-pole World

The global AI competition landscape in May 2026 can be clearly explained in a table:

| Dimensions | OpenAI | Anthropic | Google DeepMind | DeepSeek |
|------|--------|-----------|-----------------|----------|
| Latest flagship | GPT-5.4 | Claude Opus 4.6 | Gemini 3.1 Pro | V4-Pro |
| Strategy | Closed source + subscription + enterprise | Closed source + security first | Closed source + ecological integration | **Open source + low price** |
| Annual revenue | **$10 billion+** | Billions of dollars | Including Google revenue | **Near zero** |
| Total financing | **$30 billion+** | **$15 billion+** | Inside Google | **350 billion yuan valuation in financing** |
| Core Advantages | Brand + Number of Users | Security + Long Text | Data + Computing Power | **Cost + Open Source** |
| Core disadvantages | High cost | Small scale | Slow productization | **Commercialization is almost blank** |
| C-side users | **150 million+ weekly active users** | Tens of millions | Hundreds of millions (including search) | **96.9 million monthly active users** |

(Source: Public data of each company, DemandSage, Bloomberg)

### DeepSeek’s unique position among the competition

DeepSeek does not compete with the other three companies on the same dimension.

What OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google are competing for is the commercial market for closed-source AI—whose model is better, who has more customers, and whose subscription revenue is higher.

DeepSeek competes for the ecological influence of open source AI - whose model is used by the most people, who defines the standard of open source AI, and who becomes the "Linux of AI".

**The two wars overlapped but did not completely overlap. ** DeepSeek may have lost to OpenAI and Anthropic in commercial competition, but it has established an unshakable position in the open source ecosystem.

**Opposite view**: Meta’s Llama series is also open source and is backed by Meta, which has a market value of trillions of dollars. DeepSeek’s lead in the open source track is not unchallengeable. In addition, if the closed-source model continues to create a gap in capabilities, the appeal of the open-source ecosystem may decrease.

## Anthropic’s Allegations: Distillation Controversy

In February 2026, Anthropic publicly accused DeepSeek, Moonshot and MiniMax of conducting a "coordinated distillation attack" on Claude - that is, by calling Claude's API in large numbers to extract its capabilities for training its own model (source: CNBC).

OpenAI has made similar accusations before.

**The essence of this controversy** is: When the closed source model is strong enough, are open source companies "parasitic" on the results of the closed source model?

DeepSeek has not publicly responded to the allegation. But from a technical perspective:

- Distillation is a common technology in the AI field and is not academically illegal.
- Large-scale commercial distillation may violate API terms of use
- If the accusations are true, some of DeepSeek's "technological leadership" needs to be discounted

**This is an open dispute and no conclusion should be drawn without more evidence. But it reminds one thing: DeepSeek's technical achievements cannot simply be explained by "the pure originality of a 270-person team." **
