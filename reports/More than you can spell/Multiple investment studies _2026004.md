# 4. Summary of the analysis of dimensions

# 4.1 Business model and moat (<unk> )

- **In essence, the "Treatment of Flows and Transactions" business**: no stock holdings, sales of exposures and trading rights to businesses, over 56% Māori and less than 0.3% of capital expenditure.
- ** Low-price mind is not a moat, but cost structure. ** Test evidence: The Goo Kingdong was completely low in 2023-2024, and then abandoned the GMV orientation after July 2024 – either by not wanting to learn or by learning to lose. About 431.8 billion dollars in income per person, with a per capita output of about $20 million per year, is the industry’s extreme value. But this moat protects the right to survival, not the right to pricing.
- ** The growth of profit engines is nearing a halt**: 2026Q1 domestic online marketing revenue is only +2.5% (lowest since listing), and the income structure has drifted from a profit ad to a profit-trading service, explaining the four-way decline in the Māori rate.
- ** Some historical profits are being "systematicly confiscated"**: only refunds were eliminated (April 2025), $1.522 billion in fines (April 2026), hundreds of millions of support concessions, small parcels were terminated from tax exemption - profits derived from transfer of costs to traders and policy arbitrage, and the part of the proceeds that were taken was not the moat. The fall in profits in 2025 was a one-time downward shift in the centre of profit rates, not simply a cyclical input.
- **Temu model "Build but thin"**: US tax exemption was cancelled with semi-custody/local performance surviving from "high-cost self-employment" to a common cross-border platform; Europe accounted for about 40% of the GMV's first market, while EU exemption of 150 euros was already in the legislative process – and the US script could be repeated. Official zero disclosure of Temu is a black box for information in A-class companies.

# 4.2 Finance and valuation (<unk> )

- **The profit decline in 2025 was about "70% active input, 30% structural damage"**: the time line was precisely synchronized with "100 billion support", with management warning ahead of time and historical patterns of behaviour that had been strained by 2021; cash flows were key witnesses – the worst year view still produced 105.8 billion free cash flows and the ability to collect was zero. But the rate of Maori moved downwards to a change in income structure, irreversible.
- ** Cash fortress**: Net cash is about $431 billion, or $43.9 per share, or 53 per cent of the share price; the market price for all operations (nearly 100 billion annual net profit + Temu global) is only about $55 billion.

Net cash of $43.9 per unit held up most of the downside space under pessimistic scenarios — provided that cash is not squandered.
