### Amazon Haul benchmarks against Temu

Amazon launched Haul to directly target Temu's low-price market, which has expanded to 25 markets, with a 400% increase in product selection. But the monthly usage rate is only 16%, far behind Temu’s 28%. Progress was less than expected.

### Counter-intuitive discovery

**Prime members prefer to shop at Temu more than non-members** - the two platforms are more complementary than substitutes. Consumers regard Amazon as "quality goods that need to be received quickly" and Temu as "cheap goods that are not needed urgently".

## 4. Four major structural gaps

### 1. Profit engine: AWS makes Amazon invincible

AWS annual revenue is $128.7 billion, a growth rate of 20%+, and an operating profit margin of 30%+. This means that even if Amazon's e-commerce business makes no money at all, AWS's profits will be enough to support the entire company. PDD does not have a similar second curve - Temu's profit prospects depend entirely on the e-commerce itself.

### 2. User stickiness: Prime’s ecological lock-in vs Temu’s price drive

200 million U.S. Prime members pay annual membership fees of $139-$180 and receive free shipping + Prime Video + Music and other services. This creates extremely strong switching costs. Temu's user retention relies entirely on low prices - once the price advantage disappears (tariffs), users immediately lose (DAU plummets by 58%).

### 3. Logistics infrastructure: 100 times difference

Amazon's 1,200+ fulfillment centers achieve delivery within 1-2 days, while Temu's 13 self-operated warehouses can only deliver within 5-10 days. This gap will take tens of billions of dollars and 5-10 years to close.

### 4. Ad monetization: $68 billion vs just starting

Amazon is the third largest digital advertising platform in the world. The marginal cost of its advertising business is close to zero, which is pure profit. Temu's advertising ecosystem has not yet been established - and this is precisely the biggest variable in Temu's future profits.

## 5. Forecast after 10 years (2036)

### Neutral scenario comparison

| Metrics | Amazon 2036 | PDD 2036 | Gap Multiple |
|------|------------|---------|---------|
| Total revenue | **$1.8 trillion** | $110 billion | **16.4x** (expanded from 12.8x) |
| Net profit | **$180 billion** | $19 billion | **9.5x** (expanded from 5.7x) |
| Of which AWS | 160 billion profit | — | Amazon exclusive |
| Of which advertising | 130 billion profits | — | Amazon exclusive |
| Market value (estimated) | ~5-6 trillion | ~300-400 billion | ~15 times |

### Why is the gap widening?

Amazon has three super growth engines running simultaneously:
1. **AWS**: The biggest beneficiary of the AI era, the cloud market is expected to reach $2 trillion in 2030
2. **Advertising**: E-commerce advertising is still in the early stages of penetration
3. **International e-commerce**: Continued expansion in multiple markets around the world

PDD has only Temu as a high-growth engine, and it is severely restricted by tariffs and geopolitics.

### But the return on investment PDD may be slightly higher

| Metrics | Amazon | PDD |
|------|--------|-----|
| Current PE | 32x | **10x** |
| Probability-weighted annualized return | 3.6% | **4.3%** |
| Optimistic Scenario Annualized | 8.2% | **12.6%** |
| Pessimistic scenario annualized | 0.5% | **-7.4%** |

**PDD has a slightly higher expected return, but the variance is much larger than Amazon. ** The reason is simple: PE 10x vs 32x - you buy cheaper and the return is likely to be higher even if the company grows slower. However, the downside risk of PDD's pessimistic scenario (annualized -7.4%) is much greater than Amazon (annualized +0.5%).

## 6. Investment Enlightenment

### Amazon is a better business, PDD may be a better bet

| Dimensions | Amazon | PDD |
|------|--------|-----|
| Business quality | **Extremely high** (multi-engine + AWS moat) | Medium (main site strong, Temu uncertain) |
| Certainty | **High** (AWS+ advertising growth visible) | Low (tariffs/geopolitics uncontrollable) |
| Valuation | Expensive (PE 32x) | **Cheap (PE 10x)** |
| Downside protection | Strong (AWS cover) | Medium (main site + cash cover, but no dividends) |
| Upside elasticity | Medium (large volume growth slows down) | **High (Temu success + New Pinmu = room for doubling) ** |

### What if I can only choose one?

Depends on your risk appetite:
- **Pursuing Certainty**: Amazon - There is a high probability that Amazon will still be one of the most valuable companies in the world in 10 years
- **Pursuing high returns**: PDD — PE 10x + Temu option + New Temu option, with better odds but large variance
- **The optimal solution between the two**: The research team's conclusion is - **Tencent may be a better choice** (certainty is higher than PDD, growth rate is higher than Amazon, and valuation is between the two)
