** Other gains from $3.3 billion to $2 billion -- what is the bottom of the $5.3 billion pit?**

This is the most critical and irritating aspect of the entire financial statement: the PDD does not split in detail in the quarterly financial statement (6-K), only in the notes to the annual report (20-F) will disclose each item, while the annual report of FY 2026 will not be released until April 2027.

According to the disclosure pattern 20-F for the previous PDF calendar year, "other gains (net)" usually include a few things: first, a change in the fair value of financial assets — $33.1 billion in short-term investments on the PDD account (structural deposits, bank finance, bonds), the market value of which fluctuates with the interest rate environment and can be substantial; second, government subsidies and tax returns; and third, the tax proceeds of merchants — more than a fine for offenders, but the $100 million support scheme substantially reduces the cost to traders, which is likely to shrink significantly.

The Q120225 +33 billion margin includes a larger fair value gain for financial assets (the market environment was good during the same period last year). Q12026 became two billion, with the reasonable assumption that there was a market-market reversal of the fiscal/bond investment and a reduction in the accumulated vendor fine revenue due to preferential policies.

** "Investment gains" from 200 million to 600 million -- what did you lose?**

The interest and investment income of the PDD throughout the year — 25.6 billion dollars in the business and an average of more than 6 billion dollars per quarter in 2025 — are quite significant. Q1 earned only 200 million (last year) and 600 million (this year) – indicating that interest income itself is still generated, but is offset by a loss in a particular investment. The "Loss from fair value change of certain investments" in the Non-GAAP adjustment table is very small (only 25 million) – suggesting that it is not equity investments that are strided, and more likely that interest rate changes cause valuation fluctuations in bonds/fiscal products.

The net profit has thus declined by 15%, most directly because of non-operational projects below the operating profit line. These projects have high quarterly volatility and can seriously distort the single-quarter profit figures.

This does not mean that business is not a problem. The 22% increase in operating profits, mainly from a 43% surge to a 1% increase in marketing costs – saving money, not from a faster income. The trend is one that needs to be addressed.

# And say the last thing

After a line-by-line dismantling of the profit sheet, the thing is much clearer than the apparent "net profit reduction of 15%." The biggest push for a decline in net profit is the deterioration of non-operational projects below the operating profit line – $5.3 billion of "other gains" – and the loss of investment returns of $850 million, which is not directly related to the over-sale of non-saleable goods.

The business-level growth of 22% is not a good one. The main factor that emerged from the sharp contraction in the marketing costs of Temu (from 43% to only 1%) is that the income end is accelerating. The rate of domestic advertising revenue has fallen from 56% to 2.5%, the most alarming sign of which is that domestic vendors are less willing to advertise with more effort and less attractive to the platform.

Management repeatedly stressed at the telephone conference that "a decline in profit is inevitable." This is a very rare term in China's Internet companies – most management, even if it fails, has to say, "The next quarter will be better." In many ways, it is either a great deal of confidence in long-term strategies or an advance precautionary injection of statements for the next few quarters.

Eight times the PE, more than half the value is cash. If you believe in the story of "three years of making more than one piece of paper," this price is safe. If you don't believe it, profits go down, red or no buy, and $63.2 billion is on the books, it's a classic value trap.

Ultimately, it is a question of trust.

The logic is that the biggest push for a decline in net profits is the collapse of non-operational fluctuations and non-core operations, with eight times the PE plus more than half the market value cash providing sufficient security margins. The slowdown in domestic advertising is a real concern, but the price already reflects a considerable pessimistic expectation.
