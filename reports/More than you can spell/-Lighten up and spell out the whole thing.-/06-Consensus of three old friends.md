# A few things that need to be thought out

And here you might think, "Three smart guys bought it, that's right." But good investment decisions can't be based on "Who's Who Who Buys It." A few questions you think about:

**1. Their information advantage, you did not.**

For 25 years, Ding Yongping has been an angel investor and Lee Siu has a personal network. Their trust in a multi-mandate management is based on long-term private contacts — and a multi-manufacturing management transparency is almost zero for external investors (no telephone calls, no road shows, no investors). You can rely on public accounts and SEC documents.

**2. Smart people can be wrong.**

The Lee Siu Rektak Meta lost 24%. The Q2 cleanup in Eongping in 2020 was much more complex -- if he gets the $212 high point in February 2021, the return is much higher than the current storage cost.** The past operation does not prove the future.**

**3. Their holding costs are not the same as yours**

The price of Suh-pyung is only a few cents for an angel investor. The same is true for a second-tier market, where the overall return may be positive, even if there is a large loss. If you buy it today, you will face a risk gain that is completely different from that of them.

**4. The multiplicity of core uncertainties does not disappear because of "who bought them".**

- The political risks of Temu (United States customs policy, elimination of tax exemptions)
- Management's intentions regarding the use of more than 400 billion dollars in cash are unclear.
- Wong is not on line, and Chen's long-term performance has not been fully tested
- The domestic electricity suppliers are slowing down, and the takeout war is costing the industry.

These problems will not be solved automatically by the purchase of Shunhei, Li and Ding.
