# Three major hidden dangers - cash, management and risk

> "Understanding Pinduoduo" Series · Part 04 · Hidden Danger
> Reading time approx. 8 minutes

---

## The hardest part of Pinduoduo

The previous article talked about the essence of Pinduoduo’s business – algorithmic advertising machine + Temu political option.

PE 10.5x, ROE 31%, FCF Yield 7%. Looking at these numbers alone, Pinduoduo should be a textbook-level value stock. But the market just doesn’t give a price.

The reason is not the business, but three things outside the business: cash is not returned, the founder does not show up, and the risks are not clear.

This article is dismantled piece by piece.

---

## 1. 422.3 billion cash

### Profits are indeed declining in 2025

Let’s lay out the numbers first:

| Projects (100 million yuan) | 2024 | 2025 | Changes |
|-----------|------|------|------|
| Total Revenue | 3,938 | 4,318 | +10% |
| Operating profit | 1,519 | 1,179 | -22% |
| Net Profit | 1,135 | 994 | -12% |

Revenue increased by 10%, costs increased by 30%, and profits were cut by a scissors. Where is the money spent? Temu promotion, tariff war transformation, and domestic fundamentals are defended. These are all proactive investments, not business deterioration.

ROE dropped from 49% to 31%, which sounds scary. But the 49% in 2024 is inherently abnormal - just like a player averaging 35 points per game in one season and returning to 28 points the next season, you can't say he is useless. The ROE of 31% is still the top among global e-commerce companies, with Amazon only having 25% and Alibaba 12%.

### What do you think of this cash?

422.3 billion in net cash, accounting for 42% of market capitalization. The free cash flow is 106.9 billion in one year, and another one can be saved in four years.

This is a point that many people struggle with - peers are spending large sums of money on repurchases and dividends (Alibaba gives back $16.5 billion a year, Tencent gives back over HK$110 billion), while Pinduoduo has not paid out a dime in six years.

But I tend not to see this as an issue.

Huang Zheng's judgment in spending money has been repeatedly verified. When everyone was hesitant in 2022, he made the decision to start Temu; when the tariff war broke out in 2024, while others were still in meetings, he had already cut half into custody and built a local warehouse. Each of these decisions costs money, and each one is right in hindsight.

So a more reasonable understanding of this 422.3 billion is: ammunition left by an extremely rational person for an uncertain future. Temu is still expanding, the tariff war is escalating at any time, and there may be an M&A opportunity someday - all of which require money.

Of course, there is a premise for this judgment: you have to trust Huang Zheng. Those who believe in him see a safety cushion, while those who do not believe see a black hole. The market gives PE 10x but not 15x because not everyone believes it.

As for how it will evolve in the future - there is a high probability that it will continue to remain unchanged. If a buyback is suddenly announced one day, regardless of the amount, it will be a major signal, and the stock price can rise by 20% that day.

## 2. Huang Zheng and Chen Lei

He stepped down as CEO in 2020 and as chairman in 2021. He has not been seen much in five years. However, the super voting rights of Class B shares are still in hand, and they actually control more than 50% of the voting rights. He has resigned in name, but in fact the steering wheel of the company is still in his hands.

The most likely operating model is: Huang Zheng sets the general direction, and Chen Lei is responsible for fighting.

There is no doubt about Chen Lei's ability to fight. It was under his hands that Temu built GMV from zero to $70 billion, and he was also responsible for the rapid transformation during the tariff war. If I had to give a score, execution would be 4.5 out of 5.

The shortcoming is communication. Without investor meetings or interviews, the company is basically a black box to the outside world. This is not a fatal problem, but it does prevent the market from fully valuing it - if you don't say anything, others can only make conservative guesses.

Speaking of Duan Yongping. His position in Pinduoduo is very heavy - 150,000 shares in 2023, increased to 11.56 million shares in two years, an increase of 77 times, and a total investment of $1.1-1.3 billion. But he never publicly recommended others to buy it, and even described the target as "can't see clearly."

What does this mean? This shows that even an investor of Duan Yongping's level is "I am willing to bet on Pinduoduo, but I cannot tell others that the certainty is high." This attitude speaks for itself.

## 3. Risks

Pinduoduo’s risk is not as known and controllable as Tencent’s. It is the type that has a low probability but will be very violent once it happens.

There are only two things that really matter:

First, Temu is effectively banned by the United States. If it follows TikTok's path, Temu's US market (accounting for about 25% of GMV) will be cleared directly, and its valuation will drop by 25-35%. The probability is not high, but it is not zero.

Second, the decoupling between China and the United States has caused problems in the VIE structure. Pinduoduo has not yet had a secondary listing on Hong Kong stocks - if it is forced to delist from the US stock market, the redeemable value of this 420 billion cash will be a big question mark.

The structural threat is Douyin e-commerce. If Douyin continues to advance in e-commerce and its market share exceeds 30%, Pinduoduo’s low-price mentality will gradually be eroded. It won’t happen overnight, but the five-year dimension is worth paying attention to.

Others - regulatory fines, merchant rights protection, employee issues - are all staged, affecting the stock price but not damaging the fundamentals.

Taken together, these risks are why the market gives PE 10x. It's not cheap, it's a risk premium.

But think about it the other way around: If you judge that the probability of these tail risks is lower than what the market is pricing in, then PE 10x is really cheap. This is not a question of right or wrong, but whether your judgment of risk probability is different from the market.

---

## 4. How to stare

Six signals, if any one is triggered, you have to think again:

1. Temu’s U.S. GMV share fell below 20%
2. Major new charges emerge from UFLPA
3. Douyin’s e-commerce market share exceeded 30%
4. Pinduoduo’s ROE fell below 22% for two consecutive quarters
5. Large-scale rights protection for merchants (more than 1,000 at a time)
6. Management initiates buybacks or dividends - this is a positive signal, and it is worth adding to the position if it appears.

---

## Summary

The profit decline is the result of active investment, and the ROE of 31% is a return to normal rather than a deterioration. 422.3 billion in cash, regardless of whether it is repurchased or not, depends on whether you believe Huang Zheng or not - believe him, this is a strategic reserve; otherwise, this is a black hole of value. The market's pricing of PE 10x is neutral to pessimistic. If you trust this business and this person more than the market, Pinduoduo is undervalued.
