#1.4 Logistics model comparison

**Amazon FAA Global Logistics Network**:
- 1,200+ Compliance Centers worldwide
- 40000 plus semi-trailer
- 25,000 plus electric transporter
- 110+ cargo aircraft
- American parcels are delivered in excess of the sum of UPS and FedEx.
- $20.6 billion in performance expenditure in 2025
- Core advantages: 1 - 2 days of positive experience

**Temu logistics system** (fast-evolving):
- 13 self-employed warehouses worldwide (10 in Europe, 2 in the United States and 1 in Mexico)
- United States self-employed warehouse handling amounts to 15-20 per cent of the United States total, and is expected to increase to 20-25 per cent in 2026
- European target: 80 per cent of European orders processed through local warehouses by 2025
- Savings of approximately $0.60 per return
- Core bottlenecks: High reliance on direct cross-border mail, with clear time frames and cost disadvantages

** Gap summary**: Amazon built the largest commercial logistics network in the world in 20 years, and Temu has just begun building a local warehouse. This gap is not being pursued in years, but Temu is rapidly closing the statute of limitations gap using the semi-host + local warehouse model.

# IV. Summary of core differences

##4.1 Profit engine: AWS vs None

This is the most fundamental difference between the two companies.

**Arazon's AWS**:
- US$ 12.8 billion in 2025, 20 per cent increase
- It's Amazon's profit engine and strategy.
- The presence of AWS allows Amazon to tolerate low profits or even losses in the power company.
- A huge ALI and technical capability to feed Amazon.

**Temu/PDD Holdings**：
- No cloud computing, no second-growth curve.
- The profit comes mainly from the multi-country main station.
- Temu is not profitable, and is expected to approach the balance of gains and losses in 2025-2026
- PD Holdings net profit of 29.3 billion yuan (approximately $4.1 billion) in 2025, +17 per cent in comparison

** Key implication**: Amazon can subsidize the electric power provider (including Haul) for a long time with AWS profits, while Temu must make a profit on the power vendor's own. This is a structural imbalance.

##4.2 User sticky: Prime member vs price-driven

**Amazon Prime**：
- 200 million American members, 240 million worldwide.
- Annual subscription income of $49.6 billion
- Prime Day 2025 consumer expenditure $24.1 billion, +70 per cent
- Membership coverage: Next day, Prime Video, Prime Music, Prime Reading, etc.
- High conversion costs: once paid, users tend to concentrate on Amazon for "valued return."

**Temu user retained**:
- No membership system, low-cost user attraction
- Play-up design (rotation, coupons, social sharing) to increase opening rates
- Large subsidy recipients, CAC (cost of access)
- Tariff shocks prove that when price advantages are weakened, users lose extremely fast (58 per cent daily drop)
- Lack of "locking effect" like Prime.

** Conclusion**: The user sticker of Amazon comes from "ecosystem locking," and the user sticker of Temu comes from "continuing price advantage". The former is more durable and the latter is more vulnerable.

# 4.4 Advertising: 68 billion vs. just starting

**Amazon ads**:
- $68 billion in 2025, compared to 23 per cent
- It's the third digital advertising platform after Google and Meta.
- Advertising is a high-profit business that directly enhances Amazon's overall profitability
- Spoonsored Products, Spoonsored Brands, DSP, etc.

** Temu ads**:
- Currently, it is mainly the platform that advertises the public (the huge advertising expenditure on Meta, Google)
- Platform advertising (business pays) is just starting.
- Not mandatory advertising by the entire trustee in 2025, but gradually expanded since 2026

- Advertising used to be more than 80% of income (2022), but this is mainly a domestic effort.

** Key gap**: Amazon's advertising business is a "money printing machine" and the merchants are paying for the flow. Temu is still in the "burn money buying traffic" phase, from spending on advertising to earning advertising fees, a shift that requires sufficient user size and ecological support.

# V. Summary of the pattern of competition

# 5.1 The real threat to Amazon by Temu

** Low-price class stream**: in the $10-30 area of daily supplies, clothing, small household items, Temu does divert part of Amazon's orders
2. **The share of cross-border electricity suppliers is being levelled**: each has accounted for 24 per cent of the cross-border electricity suppliers, indicating that Temu is truly competitive in this segmented area
3. ** User mind occupation**: 26% of United States consumers have already shopped in Temu, a penetration rate not low
4. ** High overlap of items**: 77% overlap of commodities means that consumers can be valued between platforms

# 5.2 Temu can't shake Amazon's field

1. **Prime Ecolock**: 200 million US members are not sticky enough to break at low prices
2. ** Logistics infrastructure**: 1-2 days vs 5-10 days, with a wide gap in experience
3. ** Brand trust**: big brands preferred Amazon, Temu remains predominantly white/workshop
4. ** Profits depth**: AWS+ commercials have two major profit engines that give Amazon unlimited "mammunition"
5. ** High-value goods**: high unit price areas such as electronics, branded goods are the Amazon ' s moat
** Policy resilience**: 58% daily drop in the Temu under tariff shocks, with Amazon almost unaffected

#5.3 Conclusion

Temu and Amazon are not so much direct competitors as they operate at different levels in the electric power market**:

- **Amazon** is the full-grade, full-priced, full-linked electrical infrastructure, with cloud computing and digital media as the technological giant
- **Temu** is a cross-border power provider platform focused on low-price white-card commodities, moving from "price butcher" to "brand incubator"

Competition between the two is concentrated mainly on the ** low-priced daily goods segment of the battlefield**, where Temu has a price advantage but lacks time-frame and trust; and Amazon has an ecological advantage but Haul is currently slow. In the short term, the two are more likely to coexist than to play zero-sum games – after all, Prime members prefer to visit Temu, suggesting that consumers view the two platforms as shopping options for different scenarios.

The real variable is **Policy risk**: De Minimis’ elimination has proven that Temu’s business model is highly sensitive to trade policy. If China’s trade friction continues to escalate, Temu will face structural challenges in the American market, and Amazon will be almost unaffected.
