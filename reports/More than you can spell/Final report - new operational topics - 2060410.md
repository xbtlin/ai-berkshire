- Current PE-10x, back-purchase implied return of about 10%

- ** But rebuttal**: Did Temu ' s DAU drop by 58% and reinvested more than repurchase?**

## Reason 3: Cash transfer restrictions under VIE (reasonableness: medium)
- Most of the cash is deposited in the VIE entity in China.
- 10% Chinese withholding tax on the share of the vie up.
- Cross-border transfers are subject to exchange controls
- ** But rebuttal**: Ali, Kyouta, Tung Tong, same VIE structure, red-back purchase, same as usual

#10 Summary

More than a combination of "good business plus free options plus questionable governance".

** Good business**: 30% of the main station ROE, 15.7 billion FCF, zero liability is China’s most efficient power-market platform. The share of the extra food is profitable.

** Free options**: The possibility of buying a global power-market empire with a loss of $1.03 billion per year (and a narrow harvest). Lost profits rebounded, and profits doubled.

**Suspicious governance**: $60.4 billion in zero cash returns, 80% of yellow Xinjiang voting rights but retired for scientific research, and the worst in information disclosure. You buy not one that will repay your business, but one that you bet it will appreciate.

** Final judgement**: In the case of PE 10x, cash is at a market value of 42%, and there is a security margin. This is not an "buy-in" investment, which requires continuous tracking of catalysts. It is not suitable for a "low value + options" warehouse in the portfolio.
