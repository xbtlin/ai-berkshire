# The mystery of Pinduoduo’s 420 billion cash: an experiment in delayed gratification

Pinduoduo’s stock price fell to near its 52-week low of $74. The price-to-earnings ratio is 7.9 times.

But do you know how much cash it has on its books? 420 billion yuan, about 57.5 billion US dollars. Accounting for 55% of market capitalization.

In other words, if you spend $74 to buy a share of Pinduoduo, $40.5 of it is cash. You actually spent only $33.50 and bought a business that makes hundreds of billions a year.

Is this price reasonable?

---

## If Temu disappears tomorrow

Many people's hesitations in buying Pinduoduo come from Temu - money burning, tariffs, and geopolitics. Then let’s simply assume the worst case scenario: Temu will completely return to zero, and all overseas investments will be in vain.

After stripping off Temu, the figures for Pinduoduo’s domestic main website look like this:

| Indicators | Values |
|------|------|
| Main website revenue | About 300 billion yuan |
| Main site net profit | **About 105 billion yuan** |
| Operating profit margin | **About 40%** |

Yes, you read that right. According to the consolidated statement, Pinduoduo's net profit in 2025 is 99.4 billion, but the main website alone is even higher - because Temu's loss of about 23 billion is holding it back.

What is the main site? It is a pure platform model that does not touch goods or logistics. 900 million users and 10 million merchants trade on it. Pinduoduo charges advertising fees and commissions. Marginal cost approaches zero, and every extra dollar earned is almost entirely profit.

Use the profits of the main website to calculate the current stock price: the market value is 760 billion, minus the net cash of 420 billion, and the enterprise value is only 340 billion. Divided by the net profit of the main website of 105 billion - 3.3 times. **

For a business that makes hundreds of billions a year and has a profit margin of 40%, the market only values it at 3.3 times.

Even if Temu fails, Pinduoduo will still be very profitable. Then the problem comes——

---

## Why not repurchase?

420 billion in cash on hand. The stock price fell to an all-time low. The price-to-earnings ratio of its own stocks is 7.9 times, which means that the implied annualized return on buybacks is more than 12%.

It's hard to find a better investment on the market than "buying your own company at 7.9 times".

And there are no obstacles in the process. Alibaba has repurchased more than US$30 billion in total under the exact same VIE structure. JD.com has also done more than 5 billion US dollars. If Pinduoduo doesn’t repurchase, it’s not because it can’t be done, it’s because it’s a choice not to do it.

Management's attitude is clear. In the second quarter 2024 conference call, Chen Lei said, "Now is not the right time to buy back, and there will be no such need in the foreseeable years." The stock price plummeted 28% that day, and the market value evaporated by US$55 billion.

By the fourth quarter of 2025, Zhao Jiazhen's words changed to "Between short-term financial performance and the long-term value of the platform ecosystem, the company will resolutely choose the latter. This will be the norm."

**From "inappropriate" to "normal" - the position is hardening, not softening. **

Where did the money go? Three directions: New Pinmu (first batch of capital injection of 15 billion), 100 billion support plan (investment of more than 100 billion in three years), Temu localization transformation (from direct mail in China to overseas warehouse building). Management believes these investments have a higher long-term return than buybacks.

---

## Huang Zheng doesn’t care about money

So how do you trust management to eventually return money to shareholders?

Many analysts will say: Huang Zheng holds more than 25% of the shares. His economic interests are consistent with those of shareholders. If the stock price falls to a certain level, he will definitely promote buybacks.

There is a problem with this reasoning - Huang Zheng may really not care about the amount of money in his hand. **

His 25% is worth $26 billion at current prices. For a man who retired at the age of 35 to do basic scientific research and donated 100 million US dollars to his alma mater, there is no difference between 26 billion and 30 billion. They are both endless numbers.

He personally witnessed the "side effects of getting rich overnight" among his colleagues at Google, and wrote on the public account that "money is a tool, not a purpose." For a person who does not regard personal wealth as a goal, you cannot use "your money is shrinking" to motivate him to do anything.

Therefore, the logical chain of "stock price drops → major shareholders feel distressed → promotes buyback" is likely to be broken in Huang Zheng.

---

## Then why do you believe it?

Huang Zheng has repeatedly talked about a philosophy, the core of which is two words: compound interest. **

He wrote in the public account article:

>Choice is more important than effort. Moving forward on the right path, even a little slowly, is like compound interest in investment. **A 20% annual return for 20 consecutive years is very impressive, far higher than a 100% increase this year and a 50% decrease next year. **

When he resigned as chairman in 2021, he said:

> If you want to ensure its high-speed and high-quality development **10 years from now**, then it is time to make some explorations now. As a founder, I jumped out to touch the stones on the road 10 years later.

In his 2020 letter to shareholders, he wrote:

> Any idea of trying to take advantage of others' danger or exploit loopholes to benefit oneself will appear extremely stupid in the face of time. **

This is not a scene. Look at what he has done:

- Retiring at the age of 35 is not a lack of ambition, but a plan for things to happen in 10 years.
- It only took Pinduoduo 3 years from its founding to listing, but he never pursued short-term profit maximization.
- The tens of billions of subsidies were a waste of money in 2019, but looking back, they opened the door to first- and second-tier cities
-Temu looks to be adventurous in 2022 and has covered more than 100 countries by 2025

**It’s not that he is unwilling to return returns to shareholders, he believes that the money invested now will come back at a greater multiple in the future. ** This is the logic of delayed gratification—giving up immediate certainty in exchange for greater rewards in the future.

---

## The price and bottom line of trust

Choosing to trust Huang Zheng is essentially betting on two things:

**First, his judgment of the direction was correct. ** The premise of compound interest is that the direction is correct. If Temu and Xinpinmu are successful, the rewards of delayed gratification will be huge; if the direction is wrong, delayed gratification will become delayed stop loss.

Judging from past records—social group-building has subverted the e-commerce landscape, tens of billions of subsidies have penetrated first- and second-tier cities, and Temu has covered the world in two years—he has yet to make a fatal mistake in judging a major direction. This is the strongest evidence of trusting him.

**Second, the money can eventually return to shareholders. ** There is a problem with the VIE structure - there is no legal mechanism to force a Cayman shell company to pay dividends to ADR holders. This can only rely on trust, there is no data to verify.

But think about it from another perspective: there are two roads without dividends, both of which ultimately lead to shareholder returns——

If the money invested is earned, profit growth will naturally push up the stock price, and it does not matter whether dividends are paid or not.

If the money invested is not earned and there is no better use of the funds after the investment, repurchase becomes the most rational choice.

**The difference between the two roads is only the timetable and route, and the end point is the same. **

---

## Write at the end

At US$74, Pinduoduo has a price-to-earnings ratio of 7.9 times and a core business valuation of 3.3 times after deducting net cash. The pricing given by the market implies extremely pessimistic assumptions - either profits will fall sharply, or the cash will never belong to shareholders, or there will be structural risks that will bring the company to zero.

Everyone can judge for themselves whether these assumptions are correct.

But one thing is worth remembering - Huang Zheng once said, "A 20% annual return for 20 consecutive years is amazing." He himself is putting this statement into practice. The question is just: **Are you willing to accompany him to delay gratification? **
