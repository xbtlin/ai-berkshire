# Happiness: 8 years in, why doesn't it change the pattern

In 2018, China officially introduced its "good-looking" brand into the security market, carrying the technological power of China, brand influence and channel resources. The market thought that "wolfs come," and the security pattern could be re-established.

But eight years later, the hope of Hua has not changed the basic pattern of "Hai Daewoo". Why?

** Reason I: Barriers to security are not technology**

Many people think security is a technology-driven industry -- who has a good algorithm, who has a strong chip, who wins. But the real barriers to security are ** channels, services and customer relations.

A city's video surveillance system, from bidding to deployment to a carrier, can last two to three years. Heikang has service teams in every province and city, and has access to the door within 24 hours of the problem. The Chinese system of ICT channels, while strong, is fundamentally different from the secure project system and territorialization of services.

** Reason II: too low probabilities**

For China, security is a Maori industry with a **30%-45%** - and the core ICT business with a much higher rate than that. Security is not the strategic focus of China, and there is limited commitment.

**Cause III: Ecological lock-in effects**

The customers of the Haikang system have been deployed, and the cost of switching to other brands is high - rewiring, reconfiguring, retraining. This conversion cost is one of the most central moats in the security industry.

** What does this mean for the view?**

The eight years of China's failure to shake patterns suggest that the barriers to the security sector are ** systematic** -- one that can be broken without a technological advantage. This is a two-sided approach:

- **Good news**: The security industry has real barriers that are not easily subverted by new entrants, and the stock share is relatively secure
- ** Bad news**: The same barriers prevent the view from breaking up and taking the share of Heicon Dao.

# The final judgement of the security industry

The defense industry is undergoing a structural change: ** from "sell hardware" to "sell AioT solutions" **. AI's penetration is currently only **8-10%**, which means there's plenty of incremental space.

But who would benefit from this incremental space?

Scenario one: head concentration continues to rise

This is the most likely scenario for the moment. AI upgrades require a lot of research and development investment, only Haikang and Da-hwa have enough to keep up. The industry has evolved from "one strong, one good, three" to "one strong, one more, one more, and more than one rich player."

** Seeing fate in this scenario** The market share is slowly losing, forcing the choice of several sub-areas to deep-deep, and the harvest may be maintained at the size of $5 billion-6 billion, but it is difficult to break through.

# Scenario two: AI brings a new round of shuffling

If AI technology brings about subversive product patterns (e.g., security systems defined by pure software), the advantages of channel and hardware size may be weakened.

** Vision of opportunities in this scenario**: Access to a place on the new track with the genes and big models of the IT architecture.

** But the probability of this scenario is low**. The reason is that the AI upgrade in the defense industry is more "progressive" -- adding AI functionality to existing hardware, not "subversive" re-inverting. Progressive upgrading is beneficial to the person in place.
