### 🔺 Signal B: Helium supply cut off - Taiwan and South Korea wafer fabs enter rationing mode

**Triggering event**: Qatar's Ras Laffan Industrial Park was attacked by Iranian missiles/drone, the situation in Hormuz superimposed, and about 1/3 of the global helium production capacity was damaged; Taiwan/South Korea wafer fabs have entered **rationing mode** (Source: Manufacturing Dive, Digitimes, Semiconductor Supply Chain 2026-03/04)

#### Bottleneck core data

| Metrics | Data | Sources |
|------|------|------|
| Qatar accounts for global helium production | ~1/3 | Values Reports |
| Proportion of Korean helium from Qatar | **65%** (6N ultra-pure grade is higher) | J2 Sourcing |
| Taiwan helium from GCC ratio | **69%** | Carraglobe |
| Fab Inventory Cycle | **Approximately 1 week** (no strategic reserve) | Carraglobe |
| Spot price changes | **Double** (vs the same period in 2025) | Smith 2026-04-09 |
| Main affected parties | TSMC, Samsung, SK Hynix | Multiple sources |
| Mitigating factors | Air Liquide opens new helium production facility in Taiwan (2026-04) | Digitimes |

#### Bottleneck rating (6 criteria)

| Standards | Assessment | Grades |
|------|------|------|
| Supply concentration | 4-5 largest manufacturers in the world, but Qatar accounts for 1/3 | 🔴 ≤2 companies (effective production capacity) |
| Expansion cycle | Helium comes from by-products of natural gas fields, and construction of new production capacity takes 2-5 years | 🔴 >2 years |
| Difficulty of substitution | Helium is used for wafer cooling and leak detection, there is no real substitute | 🔴 Irreplaceable |
| Capacity utilization rate | Already in rationing state | 🔴 >90% |
| Demand growth | AI computing power expansion drives wafer production capacity to continue to climb | 🔴 >50%/year (AI related) |
| Customer Validation | New Supplier Gas Purity Certification 12-24 months | 🔴 >1 year |

**Bottleneck Rating: S-Class (6/6 Red Light)**

#### Mitigating factors (lowering sustainability expectations)

1. **Air Liquide Taiwan’s new production capacity (2026-04)**: Partially reduce Taiwan’s dependence on seaborne imports
2. **US-Iran Agreement (2026-06-14, signed on 6/19)**: If Hormuz is fully opened, Qatar’s export capacity will be restored; however, **damaged production facilities will not be automatically repaired due to the resumption of shipping**
3. **US Helium Production**: The United States is the second largest producer, BLM reserve auction may increase emergency supply

#### Main beneficiaries/losers

- **Beneficiaries** (helium suppliers): Air Products (APD), Linde (LIN), Air Liquide (AIR.PA) — all mega-cap, priced
- **Benefits (small market cap)**: Emerging helium producers in the United States, such as Pulsar Helium (TSX-V) - early stage, extremely risky
- **Damage**: TSMC, Samsung, SK Hynix - there is a risk of production reduction, but these companies are not investment targets

**Conclusion: The helium bottleneck is extremely serious, but the investment targets (large helium companies) have been fully priced; small-market helium companies are high-risk early-stage speculations. It is temporarily not included in the watch list and is listed as a monitoring signal. **
