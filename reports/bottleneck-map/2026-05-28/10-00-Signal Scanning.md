## In-depth analysis of gallium/germanium supply cutoff in Japan

### New data quantification
| Indicator | Value | Meaning |
|------|------|------|
| China's gallium exports to Japan January-February 2026 | **0 kg** (vs. 8,007 kg in the same period in 2025) | -100% year-on-year, not "falling" but "returning to zero" |
| China’s germanium exports to Japan January-February 2026 | **0 kg** (vs. 400 kg in the same period in 2025) | Year-on-year -100% |
| Gallium price | **$2,100/kg** (+123% since early 2025) | No economically viable expansion without subsidy sources |
| China’s share of global gallium production | **99%** | Virtual monopoly |

### Why is it important (difference from the known situation with the United States)

- **To the United States**: Ban in December 2024 → Suspended from November 2025 to 2026-11-27, controlled by license management, there are still exports (GaN/GaAs chip design companies can obtain it, and there is an exemption period in the United States)
- **For Japan**: Control will be implemented from January 2026, **No similar exemption arrangement**. According to actual measurements, exports from January to February have returned to zero. Japan is the world’s major GaN/GaAs semiconductor manufacturing base (Sumitomo Electric, Mitsubishi Electric RF chips, Murata power devices)

### Why is there no investment target at the moment?

- Sumitomo Electric (5802.T, Tokyo Stock Exchange): Revenue ¥4.6 trillion, GaN/InP business accounted for <10%, non-pure target, market value >$25B
- Mitsubishi Electric (6503.T): A conglomerate with a similarly large market capitalization
- Renesas (6723.T): large market capitalization, GaN is a non-core business
- Currently, Sumitomo/Mitsubishi claims to have "sufficient inventory" - the inventory consumption timeline has not been disclosed, and the acute crisis time point cannot be confirmed.
- **Conclusion**: The bottleneck is real, but there is no pure target of small market capitalization for investment. In the short term, the Japanese semiconductor GaN supply chain is scarce in the upstream, and midstream manufacturers are under pressure rather than beneficiaries.

### Upgrade path
- If Japanese GaN/GaAs manufacturers begin to disclose inventory shortages or production reduction warnings → this signal is upgraded to level A and drives up the demand for alternative materials (SiC, etc.)
- If Sino-US relations deteriorate again or Japan-US coordination fails on the Ga/Ge issue → Re-evaluate
- **This rating**: B+ level (a notch higher than the existing B level, reason: The actual measurement of Japan has returned to zero, not just "risk")
