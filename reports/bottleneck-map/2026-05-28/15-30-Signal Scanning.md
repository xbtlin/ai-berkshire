## Signal interpretation

### ① Nvidia $150B Taiwan + Constellation Campus starts construction

Qualitative signal: **Macro trend confirmation, does not change any specific bottleneck rating, but significantly strengthens the long-term structure of the AI supply chain that is highly concentrated in Taiwan**.

Nvidia has identified Taiwan as an "AI center of excellence" with an annual procurement scale of ~$100 billion and continuing to grow. The Vera Rubin system requires 150 Taiwanese partners and nearly 2 million components - this complexity means that the demand for Taiwan's chain L2-L3 materials (T-glass, ABF substrate, InP substrate, CoWoS carrier board) will not be dispersed due to short-term price pressure. Meaning for existing watchlist:

- **Nittobo (3110.T)**: T-glass demand is confirmed to continue, current ¥25,310-28,100, target entry ¥22,000-24,000 unchanged
- **Kanto Denka (4047.T)**: NF3/WF6 is used in all advanced process wafer fabs in Taiwan, and there is no looseness on the demand side
- **InP/EML (S-level)**: 800G → 1.6T optical module upgrade trend remains unchanged, Vera Rubin HBM4 demand is verified again

### ② Fastmarkets "ample supply"——UAMY reverse argument

This is the most substantive new information of this round, a reverse verification for UAMY (★★★).

**Facts** (Fastmarkets, 2026 Outlook): New production capacity in Southeast Asia (Tajikistan, Vietnam and other countries) has made global commercial antimony supply sufficient, and antimony prices have been -18% from the peak. Southeast Asian sellers are seizing European warehouse market share at low prices.

**Positive arguments** (reasons to maintain UAMY thesis):
- DoD $248M five-year contract is a fixed-price government procurement and is not directly affected by commercial antimony prices
- DoD strategic stockpile logic ("Establishing U.S. Domestic Supply") independent of commercial antimony prices
- Thompson Falls is the only operating domestic antimony smelter in the U.S. – strategic value not lost due to capacity expansion in Southeast Asia

**Contrary arguments** (new, cannot be ignored):
- UAMY $125M revenue guidance may imply higher average commercial antimony price assumptions; competition in Southeast Asia compresses non-DoD contract profit margins
- The impact of -18% antimony price on UAMY's commercial contracts will be reflected in the Q2 2026 financial report
- The previous "global antimony gap of 40,000 MT/year" was based on historical production capacity data. If capacity expansion in Southeast Asia continues, the gap size may be revised

**Conclusion**: UAMY's rating ★★★ is maintained, but it needs to be verified in the Q2 financial report: if non-DoD commercial revenue fails to climb quickly, or the average selling price is significantly lower than the guidance assumption, the rating needs to be re-evaluated.

### ③ India-US Critical Minerals Framework

The diplomatic framework has no substantial impact on recent bottleneck investments. India's mining/processing capabilities are extremely weak and will not be able to provide meaningful supplies of antimony, tungsten, and REE within five years. Logging only, does not affect any bottleneck ratings.
