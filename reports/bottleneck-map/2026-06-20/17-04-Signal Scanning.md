### Trigger reason

A search for HALEU supply chain signals revealed that ASPI holds multiple 2026 8-K filings (SEC):
- **February 2026**: Subsidiary QLE signed a preliminary service contract with South African nuclear energy company NECSA to build a HALEU enrichment facility in Pelindaba
- **March 2026**: Signed HALEU supply MOU with a US energy company
- **May 11, 2026**: Signed HALEU cooperation MOU with European Nuclear Technologies

ASPI holds the exclusive global license for Pneumatic Separation Process (ASP) and Laser Quantum Enrichment (QE) technology, which can theoretically replace the traditional centrifuge route. ASPI **does not appear in the current watch list** and is being monitored for the first time.

### Long vs Short

**Look long**:
1. If the ASP pneumatic separation process is successful, the construction cost and regulatory threshold will be lower than that of traditional centrifuges, and it can be quickly scaled up
2. Multi-country cooperation (the United States, Europe, South Africa) diversifies risks, and has received DOE attention and endorsement from multiple MOUs
3. The market value is $904M, which is lower than Centrus’ $3.77B. If the technical route is realized, the discount will be obvious.

**Short**:
1. Current $0 HALEU revenue + PS 53x + loss expansion, purely narrative pricing
2. ASP technology has never been proven on a large scale in the field of uranium enrichment. Pelindaba plant construction + NRC certification will take at least 4-6 years
3. Unlike Centrus, ASPI does not have a long-term government contract and all MOUs are non-binding
4. In FY2025, losses expanded from $32M to $175M (a 440% increase), cash burn accelerated and risks were diluted.

**Recommendation**: Add to the watch list ★★ (highest rating, red light due to valuation); waiting for: ① first batch of commercial sales of enriched isotope (Silicon-28 / HALEU); ② loss narrowing (quarterly loss <$20M); ③ stock price correction (market value <$400M).
