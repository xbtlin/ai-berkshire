**Look at the long logic**:
1. **Unlocking of the first pricing power in five years**: No price increase for 5 years means that the pricing power has been suppressed for a long time; price increases in a shortage environment are a direct trigger for profit margin expansion, which is equivalent to pressing a spring. Anpec has taken the lead and has risen by 15%. GMT's follow-up is industry confirmation rather than isolated behavior.
2. **Pure PMIC target + extremely low PE**: PE 12x is significantly discounted in Taiwan’s semiconductor industry (average 31.7x); the stock price has not yet fully reflected the PMIC shortage logic, with only +5.49% on the day the price increase was announced, and the market has not fully priced in the subsequent profit elasticity.
3. **AI driver demand is predictable**: The number of high-performance PMICs per GPU of an AI server is 10-20 times that of a PC; the downstream demand growth path is clear (driven by the full production capacity of NVIDIA GB200/GB300/Rubin).

**Short selling logic**:
1. **Historical growth rate is low (5%)**: Growth has been slow in the past, and there is no evidence that this price increase can continue to be transformed into high-speed revenue growth; there may be customer negotiation games (customers require mandatory secondary supply certification, etc.).
2. **Dense competitors**: Major manufacturers such as MPS (Monolithic Power), TI, and ADI have stronger technical barriers and greater production capacity in AI PMIC; whether GMT's mid-to-high-end products can truly enter the GB300 ecosystem has not yet been verified.
3. **Taiwan geo-risk**: The situation in the Taiwan Strait affects all Taiwanese semiconductor companies, and GMT's small market capitalization liquidity may amplify the pressure in the risk-off market.
