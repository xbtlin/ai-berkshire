## Details of the second explosion in Ras Laffan, Qatar (6/21)

**Event**: On June 21, 2026, the Ras Laffan LNG plant in Qatar Industrial City exploded again during the restart process after the Iranian strike (since March) suspended production.

**Casualties**: 13 killed, 54 injured, 18 missing.

**Official response**:
- Qatar government and QatarEnergy claim explosion will not affect LNG exports (Bloomberg 6/22)
- However, the restart timeline has been further delayed, and uncertainty about the recovery of global LNG supply has increased.

**Market Background**:
- Iran crackdown in March removed about 20% of global LNG supply
- Taiwan's LNG reserves are at risk of "LNG cliff" of about 11 days (Morgan Stanley analysis), 1/3 of the supply has evaporated due to the war in the Middle East
- If LNG prices return to 2022 levels, Taiwan's electricity prices will increase the cost of chip fabs by about 40% (TSMC alone consumes 10% of Taiwan's electricity)

**Impact on supply chain bottlenecks**:
- Direct impact: None (Qatar said it will not affect exports and there is no new data showing a significant increase in chip manufacturing costs in the short term)
- Indirect impact: Strengthening energy security → Nuclear power argument (LEU ★★★★ logic is further consolidated)
- Source cross-verification: Al Jazeera + Bloomberg + OilPrice reported by three parties, the incident itself was confirmed; however, there are differences on the impact of LNG exports

**Conclusion**: The signal exists but has been cooled down by Qatari officials. It does not change any existing target ratings, but strengthens LEU’s energy security background narrative.
