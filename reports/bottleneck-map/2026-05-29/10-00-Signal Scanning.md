## Comprehensive analysis

### Focus: The substantial impact of China MRL June 15 new regulations

China's "Mineral Resources Law" Implementation Regulations (released on 2026-05-20, effective on June 15) is the most important uncaught new signal in this round. This event was not recorded in the first 43 scans.

**Differences from existing export controls**:
- Existing control: License approval by category/destination (gallium/germanium/tungsten/rare earth are controlled separately)
- New regulatory framework: **Systematic Total Quantity Control Law** — Allows Beijing to set mining caps on all strategic minerals without the need for separate legislation at the product level
- Increasing new regulations: Overseas investment security review → posing a new legal risk layer for foreign mining companies in China

**Conduction path for traced bottlenecks**:
- Tungsten (0c S grade): If China's domestic APT production is compressed by total volume control, international prices may further rise from the current high level
- Heavy rare earths (0c2 S grade): There are already export license controls, and the new MRL regulations form a double constraint; mining options of Western companies such as USAR/REalloys are more valuable due to reduced competition from China (but the valuations are extremely overvalued)
- Gallium/Germanium (14 B+): Exports to the United States are suspended until 11/27. If the total quantity control of the new June 15 regulations also applies to Gallium and Germanium, it will constitute a new upgrade signal.

### EU Reserve Plan + China MRL = Two-way Pincer Attack

```
Supply side: China MRL total volume control → China’s strategic mineral export available volume is compressed
Demand side: EU Joint Reserve Procurement → Increased demand for minerals available in the West
Caught in the middle: Western suppliers (Almonty/Centrus/UAMY) are on the beneficiary side of both forces
```

**The key constraint is still valuation**: The above logic strengthens the existing bottleneck narrative, but does not change the valuation judgments of Almonty (no safety margin★★) and UAMY (loss yellow light★★★).
