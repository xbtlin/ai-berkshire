## Key background: Detailed analysis of TNT bottleneck

### Bottleneck logic
- TNT (trinitrotoluene) is the main explosive filler in traditional ammunition such as 155mm artillery shells and bombs
- Completely independent from the tracked NC (nitrocellulose, used in propellant) bottleneck: they are different chemicals used in different aspects of ammunition manufacturing
- All TNT in Europe** comes from one Nitro-Chem** factory (Bydgoszcz, Poland) - construction of a second production unit will begin in the second half of 2025 and is expected to be put into production in 2026-2027, but capacity expansion is limited
- Swebal €30M financing (2026-05-07, confirmed by Tech.eu) → Build Sweden’s first TNT factory, with an annual target of 4,500 tons, and will not be put into production until 2028
- Current European TNT actual production capacity vs. 2030 target demand gap: 20,000 tons/year vs >50,000 tons/year (estimated)
- Russia comparison: about 50,000 tons/year (estimated), about 2-3 times the European production capacity

### Relationship with NC bottleneck
| Variety | Usage | European Suppliers | Gaps |
|------|------|----------|------|
| NC (nitrocellulose) | Solid propellant (propellant) | Chemring Nobel / Eurenco / Nitro-Chem (NC is also produced) | >50% (supply cut off in China) |
| TNT (trinitrotoluene) | Explosive filling | **Nitro-Chem (sole major source)** | Severe, several times capacity |

→ **Chemring’s MCX/HMX/RDX/NC business does not involve TNT, but the TNT bottleneck reflects the overall precarious supply of NATO energy materials and is background verification of Chemring’s moat**. Chemring Nobel occupies the NC and high explosives segments. The shortage of TNT further illustrates the systemic vulnerability of the entire NATO ammunition supply chain - higher demand will flow to suppliers who can produce it.
