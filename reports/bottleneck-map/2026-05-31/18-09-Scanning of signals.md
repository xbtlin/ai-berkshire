## China’s rare earth extraterritorial enforcement is extended to 2026-11 — Detailed analysis

**Event**: China October 2025 introduced the rare earth/critical minerals **extraterritorial jurisdiction** clause (license review and approval for re-export of non-Chinese entities processing Chinese source materials). After diplomatic consultations, enforcement was postponed to November 2026.  
Source: [White & Case Legal Analysis](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/china-imposes-extraterritorial-jurisdiction-and-50-rule-export-controls-rare-earth) / [discoveryalert](https://discoveryalert.com.au/china-rare-earth-export-controls-critical-minerals-trade-2026/) (dual source)

**Relationship to tracked content**:
- watchlist has recorded "2026-11-27 China antimony/gallium/germanium moratorium expiration" (effectiveness of October 2025 measures) - same time frame
- **New information in this round**: It is confirmed that the postponement of extraterritorial enforcement (compliance requirements for non-Chinese entities) and the "expiration of the moratorium" are two different concepts: the former is the scope of extraterritorial enforcement, and the latter is the export control license requirement for specific materials.

**Impact Assessment**:
- ✅ **June 15 MRL domestic law enforcement will not be affected** - On June 15, the implementation regulations of the "Mineral Resources Law" came into effect (total volume control + overseas investment review), which is an independent domestic legal framework
- 🟡 **Extraterritorial Clause Delay**: European and American companies will have a slightly reduced compliance burden when purchasing mineral materials that have been exported from China (such as rare earths re-exported from Europe/South Korea); giving Western supply chains 6 more months to adjust
- ❌ **Core bottleneck logic remains unchanged**: Direct export license of tungsten/rare earth (export from China) is still implemented, and WF6/InP/NF3 supply constraints are not lifted

**Rating Impact**: None. All tracked S/A/B bottleneck ratings remain unchanged.
