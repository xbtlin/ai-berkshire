## Detailed analysis of two new signals

### Signal 1: DoD $191M SRM Industrial Infrastructure Investment (2026-05-15)

**Signal content**: The Pentagon has launched a total of 9 DPA Title III fund investments, totaling $191 million, covering key subsystems of the entire SRM supply chain:
- Fuze/safety ignition device (PacSci EMC, $27.3M)
- Nozzle capacity expansion (Systima, $5M; General Dynamics Ordnance, $20.9M)
- Propellant case insulation material (R.E. Darling, $27.7M)
- Carbonized viscose phenolic ablation material (Americarb, $12.6M) - key high-temperature structural parts
- Solid rocket assembly (SPARC, $5.1M)

**Meaning for tracked frames**:
- ✅ Once again independently confirmed that the "throat" of the SRM supply chain is a multi-node, systemic shortage (not just of the propellant itself)
- ✅ The government identifies the nozzle, shell insulation, and ablation materials as bottlenecks - complementary confirmation with the tracked NC/HMX/propellant chain
- ✅ Together with REPKON USA ($435M), Anduril ($43.7M DPA + $14.3M), Mach/Exquadrum ($50M), and SWEBAL TNT, it is the sixth node for NATO/Five Eyes supply-side repair
- ❌ All beneficiaries are private or large prime contractors, and there are no pure investable targets with small market capitalization.

**Impact on CHG logic**: SRM nozzle/insulation and Chemring’s NC/HMX/pyrotechnics technology are different product families. DoD’s investment in nozzle production capacity ≠ Chemring NC’s moat is threatened. Looking in reverse, the government is willing to invest $190 million in the entire SRM chain, verifying the scale of structural demand for precision guided munitions, **CHG★★★★ remains unchanged**.

## TOK (4186.T) preparation tracking update

**New data point**: Tokyo Ohka Kogyo confirmed that it will invest ¥20 billion (approximately $135 million) to build a new photoresist factory in South Korea. It is expected to be put into production in 2030, when South Korea's production capacity will be expanded to 3-4 times the current level. Source: TrendForce/Semiconductor Today confirmed.

**Significance**: This is a signal of supply-side expansion rather than an increase in bottlenecks. But it also implies that TOK believes that photoresist/solvent demand will continue to be high in the 2030s (otherwise there would not be such a large-scale expansion of production). The critical period of PGME/PGMEA shortage (forecast to be around September-October 2026) is before the new factory is put into operation, and the tight supply pattern remains unchanged in the medium term.

**Rating Status**: Still in "Preparatory Tracking", P/E and photoresist segment account for >30% have not been confirmed in the annual report and will not enter the official watchlist.
