**Look at the long logic (3 items)**:

1. **Sole-source + long contract = extremely high visibility**: 12-year Diehl/Nammo contract + 85% order coverage + order book of £1.345 billion, FY2026 revenue has ~85% visibility, and there is no revenue fluctuation risk common to traditional industrial stocks.
2. **NC supply cuts off and deepens moat**: China has cut off the source of NATO nitrocellulose (NC); Europe produces less than 50% of its demand; Chemring Nobel is already developing alternative propellant technology that does not rely on NC (pilot stage). If competitors want to enter the 155mm MCX market, they need certification qualifications (>2 years) and face a shortage of raw materials - the entry barrier has essentially been locked by NC supply cuts.
3. **European defense spending supercycle**: The defense budget of NATO member states has generally increased to more than 2% of GDP; Germany’s Sondervermögen €100B; ammunition demand far exceeds current production capacity. Chemring Norway's 275% expansion is already supported by customer contracts and is not a bet investment.

**Short-selling logic (2 items)**:
1. **UK Fiscal Pressure**: The UK’s domestic defense expenditure is subject to financial constraints. If Chemring’s proportion of UK domestic orders is too high, profit margins may be under pressure. The FY2025 annual report shows that there are production facilities in 12 countries (geographically dispersed), but the UK's revenue share needs to be verified.
2. **Technological Substitution (Medium and Long-Term)**: BAE Systems is developing alternative propellants that do not require NC/NG (expected to be industrially mature by the end of 2026). If the alternative technology matures, Chemring Nobel's raw material moat will be partially weakened, but the MCX product itself does not rely on NC/NG (MCX is an insensitive explosive with different ingredients) and will not be affected in the short term.
