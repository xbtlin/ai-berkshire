### Signal 2: GOES bottleneck - CLF is the only American manufacturer, but it is not a pure standard

**Signal Description** (Source: PV Magazine 2026-05-11; PowerMag 2026; Fastmarkets):

- Delivery of large power transformers (high capacity) has been extended to **4 years** (PwC data)
- Core bottleneck: Grain-oriented electrical steel (GOES), a key raw material for transformer cores
- Cleveland-Cliffs (CLF) is the only GOES producer in the United States, with prices almost doubling in the past two years
- More than half of data center projects may be delayed due to shortage of power equipment

**Why CLF is not a good pure target**:

| Dimensions | Data | Assessment |
|------|------|------|
| Market Cap | ~$5.8 billion (2026-05) | Medium |
| GOES revenue ratio | ~3% production volume | 🔴 Purity less than 30% |
| Company as a whole | Maintain losses in Q1 2026 | 🔴 Poor financial quality |
| Main exposure | Automobile steel, flat rolled steel | Too strong macro cyclicality |

**Conclusion**: The GOES bottleneck is real (Grade B), but CLF does not meet the underlying conditions due to insufficient purity (~3% revenue) and loss status.

**Is there a better pure GOES standard? **: International GOES manufacturers (Nippon Steel, JFE, Thyssenkrupp, Posco) are all large-scale diversified steel companies, and they also lack purity. There is currently no suitable target, and it is recorded as a background signal.
