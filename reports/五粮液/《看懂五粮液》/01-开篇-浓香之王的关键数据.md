## A Set of Facts That Will Make You Reconsider Wuliangye

What does the classic A-share value trap look like? PE of 11x, dividend yield of 5.6%, cash on the books of 127.4bn -- by the numbers, it looks "extremely cheap" from every angle. But two consecutive chairmen have fallen from office, 30.3bn of inflated revenue has been exposed, and financial-statement credibility has gone to zero. Cheap, but nobody dares to buy. That is Wuliangye today.

On April 30, 2026, the final day of the annual-report disclosure deadline, Wuliangye released its Announcement on Correction of Prior-Period Accounting Errors at 22:00 after the market close. The adjustment results:

- 2025 revenue plunged from the expected **~89.1bn** to **40.5bn**, down **-54.6%** year on year
- Net profit attributable to the parent plunged from the expected **~31.8bn** to **8.95bn**, down **-71.9%** year on year
- For the first three quarters, cumulative reductions were **30.3bn** in revenue and **16.9bn** in net profit

The vanished 30.3bn was not an operating loss, but an accounting-basis switch -- in the past, "shipment to dealers counted as revenue"; now it has changed to "revenue is recognized only after end-market sell-through."

**In one sentence: Wuliangye has admitted that, in its revenue over the past few years, a large amount of goods was sitting in dealer warehouses and had not really been sold to consumers.**

The SZSE issued an inquiry letter that same evening. Two months earlier (February 2026), Wuliangye chairman Zeng Congqin had been placed under investigation and detention. Further back (January 2025), former chairman Li Shuguang had also come under investigation.

**Two consecutive chairmen fallen from office + 30.3bn of inflated revenue -- this is one of the most severe governance crises in the history of the A-share baijiu sector.**

But there is another side:

- 2026Q1 revenue was **22.8bn** (+33.7%) and net profit was **8.06bn** (+82.6%), so underlying profitability has not collapsed
- The major shareholder announced a **3-5bn** shareholding increase, a **8-10bn** repurchase, and a **20bn** full-year dividend
- As of mid-May 2026, the share price was about **89 yuan**, total market cap was about **346.0bn**, and PE (based on 2024 net profit of 31.9bn) was about **11x**

**A company with annual profit at the 30bn level, an 11x valuation, a 5.6% dividend yield, and 127.4bn of cash on the books -- but two consecutive chairmen have run into trouble and trust in the financial statements has collapsed.**

Cheap? Or cheap for a reason? That is the question this series aims to answer.

## What Kind of Business Is Baijiu?

Before dissecting Wuliangye, first understand the essence of the baijiu business.

### Why Baijiu Is Called "One of the Best Business Models"

| Feature | Baijiu | General manufacturing |
|------|------|----------|
| Gross margin | **77-92%** | 15-30% |
| Capex/revenue | **~3%** | 20-30% |
| Inventory impairment risk | **Becomes more valuable with age** | Depreciates with age |
| Accounts receivable | **Almost zero** (cash before goods) | Significant credit sales |
| Prepayment model | **Dealers pay first, then take delivery** | Ship first, collect later |
| Product life cycle | **Unchanged for hundreds of years** | 2-5 year iteration cycle |

Baijiu is a "low-input, high-output" money-printing machine -- it does not require continual heavy spending on R&D for new products, it does not need more factories, old cellars become more valuable with age, and older brands carry larger premiums.

**Wuliangye's financial metrics** (2024):

| Metric | Value |
|------|------|
| Gross margin | **77.05%** |
| Net margin | **37.22%** |
| ROE | **23.35%** |
| Free cash flow | **~31.3bn** (est.) |
| Capex/net profit | **~6%** |
| Interest-bearing debt | **Close to zero** |
| Cash and cash equivalents | **127.4bn** |

*Data source: Wuliangye 2024 annual report*

**A business that can maintain competitiveness with almost no reinvestment, has extremely high earnings quality, and operates with zero leverage.** These characteristics would be top-tier in any industry.

**But the baijiu industry also faces long-term structural challenges -- China's population decline, changing drinking habits among young people, and restrictions on government consumption.** Among consumers under 30, baijiu accounts for only **8%** of alcohol consumption (industry survey data), while baijiu drinking frequency among people aged 25-35 fell **22%** year on year (United Ratings research report). Baijiu's core consumer group (ages 30-59) is shrinking at roughly **1%** per year.

The business is good. The question is: is Wuliangye the best company in this good business?
