# Qualcomm (QCOM) Financial and Valuation Analysis

> Analysis dimensions: financial quality and intrinsic value | Data cutoff: May 2026

## 1. Revenue and Profit Trends

### Total Revenue
| Fiscal year | Revenue (USD billions) | YoY growth |
|------|---------------|---------|
| FY2023 | 35.8 | -19.2% |
| FY2024 | 38.9 | +8.7% |
| FY2025 | 44.3 | +13.7% |
| FY2026 Q1 | 12.3 | +5% YoY |

The sharp decline in FY2023 came from the global smartphone inventory correction; FY2024-FY2025 showed a strong recovery. Three-year CAGR (FY2022-FY2025) is about 0%, reflecting how cyclical semiconductors are. But over the last two years, growth momentum has returned.

### Segment Performance (FY2025 Q2)
| Segment | Quarterly revenue (USD billions) | YoY growth |
|------|------------------|---------|
| QCT mobile | 6.93 | +12% |
| QCT automotive | 0.96 | +59% |
| QCT IoT | 1.58 | +27% |
| QTL licensing | ~1.60 | +4% |

Automotive is the fastest-growing engine, rising from about USD 600 million per quarter in FY2024 to USD 1.1 billion per quarter in FY2026 Q1. Management targets USD 4 billion for FY2026 and USD 8 billion for FY2029.

### Profitability
- **Net margin**: 12.5% in FY2025 (GAAP), heavily affected by stock compensation and intangible amortization
- **Operating margin**: 27.9% in FY2025, up 2.1 percentage points YoY
- **QTL pretax margin**: above 63%, the real profit engine
- **Non-GAAP EPS**: USD 3.50 in FY2026 Q1, above the market expectation of USD 3.40

## 2. Capital Efficiency and Cash Flow

### ROIC
The five-year average ROIC from FY2021-FY2025 is 29.0%, with a median of 26.8%. That is top-tier for semiconductors and reflects the huge lift from the licensing business. As of March 2026, TTM ROIC is about 23.6%, somewhat lower but still excellent.

### Free Cash Flow
FY2025 free cash flow was USD 12.8 billion, with very high cash conversion. The company has been returning a lot of that cash to shareholders - in March 2026 it announced a new USD 20 billion buyback plan and raised the quarterly dividend from USD 0.89 to USD 0.92. By April 2026, it had completed USD 5.4 billion of repurchases.

### Balance Sheet
| Metric | FY2025 data |
|------|------------|
| Cash and equivalents | USD 5.5 billion |
| Long-term debt | USD 14.8 billion |
| Net debt | USD 9.3 billion |
| Debt / equity | 0.70 |
| Current ratio | 2.82 |
| Altman Z-score | 6.35 (safe zone) |

The balance sheet is healthy, liquidity is ample, and long-term debt is manageable. Net debt of about USD 9.3 billion is less than one year of free cash flow.

## 3. Valuation Analysis

### Current Valuation (May 2026)
| Metric | Value |
|------|------|
| Share price | USD 201.49 |
| Market cap | USD 212.4 billion |
| TTM P/E | ~19x |
| Dividend yield | 1.77% |
| FCF yield | ~6.0% |

### Valuation Assessment

**What does 19x P/E mean?** For a company with close to 30% ROIC, abundant free cash flow, and a licensing business that prints money, 19x earnings is not expensive. But a few discounts matter:
1. **Cyclicality discount**: semiconductors are highly cyclical, and the industry is currently in an upcycle, so the P/E may look cheaper than it really is
2. **Apple customer-loss discount**: Apple will fully stop using Qualcomm modems before 2027, putting about USD 2 billion of annual revenue at risk
3. **Geopolitical discount**: about 60% of Qualcomm's revenue comes from China, and U.S.-China tech tensions add uncertainty

### Simplified DCF Framework
Assumptions:
- FY2026 FCF of about USD 13 billion, with 6%-8% CAGR over the next 5 years (automotive / IoT offsets Apple losses)
- Terminal growth of 3%, discount rate of 10%
- Intrinsic value range of about USD 180-240 per share

At USD 201, the stock sits in the lower-middle of that range. That offers some margin of safety, but it is not extremely cheap.

## 4. Peer Comparison

| Company | P/E (TTM) | ROIC | Net margin | Revenue growth |
|------|---------|------|--------|---------|
| Qualcomm | ~19x | 23.6% | 12.5% | +5% |
| Broadcom | ~35x | 15.2% | 24.1% | +22% |
| MediaTek | ~18x | 18.0% | 14.2% | +15% |
| Texas Instruments | ~32x | 20.5% | 30.1% | +3% |

Qualcomm's valuation is lower than peers, mainly reflecting concerns about Apple modem loss and slower smartphone-market growth.

## 5. Core Judgment

At 19x earnings, Qualcomm offers about a 6% free-cash-flow yield and nearly a 2% dividend yield. Its capital efficiency (ROIC close to 30%) is top-tier for semis, and the balance sheet is healthy.

The key variable is whether automotive and IoT growth can offset the loss of Apple's modem business. If automotive reaches USD 8 billion in FY2029 as planned (about USD 4 billion annualized today), the Apple loss can be absorbed.

The current valuation is **reasonably cheap** - not a screaming bargain, but for investors willing to hold 3-5 years and who like AI edge devices and automotive intelligence, the risk/reward is acceptable.
