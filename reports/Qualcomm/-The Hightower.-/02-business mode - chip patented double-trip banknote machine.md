# First banknote: QTL - a toll station for wireless communications

# The logic of charging

Since its inception in 1985, Hightower has been developing wireless communication technologies (CDMA) that have accumulated over 40 years and hold the largest combination of essential wireless communication standards (SEP) that cover the core communications protocols of 3G, 4G, 5G.

What does "standard necessity patent" mean? ** You can't bypass it**. Any device that supports 5G communications – whether it's a Samsung phone, an apple iPhone, a millet or a 5G-net sensor — must be used for high-tech patent-holding technology. No high-speed chip can do without paying high-speed patent fees.

The fee is also unique: HTK charges a certain percentage of the patent fee (usually **3-5% of the whole machine price of a mobile phone) on the sale price of the terminal equipment**, rather than the chip price. This means that HFL can charge a patent fee of **30-50 ** – even if it uses a UNDG chip.

This is the strength of the "toll station" model -- you can choose not to go a certain road (without high access chips), but as long as you have to communicate, you cannot bypass the toll station (standard essential patent).

# QTL ceiling

But there are also clear limitations to QTL:

1. ** Growth is at the top**: global smartphone output has stabilized (approximately 1.2 billion units/year), QTL revenue growth is driven only by the increase in mobile ASP (average sales price) and space is limited
2. **Negotiation pressure**: Chinese manufacturers such as Huai Hong Kong are also accumulating 5G patents, and there may be future cross-licensing negotiations to reduce the high-cost rate
**6G Uncertainty**: High-level proprietary voice may be diluted if the technical route of the 6G standard (e.g. China-led standard setting) changes significantly

** The counter-view**: Even if the QTL is at its peak, it has been able to contribute steadily $4 billion a year in pre-tax profits, with little need for additional capital inputs – a source of extremely scarce cash flows in itself. The number of companies with similar "sustains to the toll station" in global technology companies is very small.

(Source: Qualcomm SY2025 10-K, AppleInsider)

# Oryon Self-researching: A key weapon with high access

In 2021, Hightower acquired Nuvia, a CPU design company founded by the core members of the former Apple Chip team, with **1.4 billion dollars**. This is the most important strategic decision of hightower in recent years.

Nuvia brought **Oryon** Self-research CPU core architecture, which freed Hightower from the reliance on the ARM core. Previously, Hightower used the ARM core of Cortex, just like UNDG, with limited performance differences; and, with Oryon, Hightotower has re-growed the gap in CPU performance.

In September 2025, Chase won a full-fledged **-win in patent litigation with ARM, confirming that the Nuvia structure permits were legal. This removed the biggest legal obstacle to the long-term use of the Oryon structure.

** But ARM has announced that it will continue to appeal.** If the Court of Appeal overturns the sentence, QOC may be forced to abandon the Oryon structure or pay a higher licence fee — a risk of low probability but high impact.

(Source: Qualcomm ARM Litigation Judgement, Qualcomm FY2025 10-K)

# Two-wheeler: QCT and QTL how to reinforce each other

The reason why the high-tech business model is unique is that QCT and QTL form a flying wheel:

```
QCT research and development of new chips <unk> Production of new patents <unk> TL patent pool
                                    ↓
QTL High Profit (> 63%) <unk> Financing QCT R & D (FY2025 R & D investment ~ $9 billion)
                                    ↓
QCT keeps technology ahead of schedule <unk> continues to generate new patents <unk>
```

The significance of this flying wheel is:** Hightower can withstand the intense competition and cyclical fluctuations in the QCT chip business, because QTL provides "free ammunition".** The UNRISD Section also spends billions of dollars a year on research and development, but it does not have a patent toll.

** Inverse view** The flying wheel logic is established, but there is a weak link - if the chip business continues to shrink (e.g., the mobile market is falling sharply + apple decoupling + the SWDR) and the new patents are reduced, the speed of the flying wheels may slow down. The flying wheel is not a permanent motive, and it needs QCT to keep technology competitive.

# Core judgment

OTCM operates the most unique two-wheel business model in the semiconductor industry**:

- **QTL** is the real "note-printer" -- a profit margin of 63%, near-zero marginal cost, global 5G equipment that has to be paid. This is rare in the technology industry.
- **QCT** is a chip design machine called "Year in War" - leading the flagship mobile market but facing a UNDG search, expanding multi-linely into cars, PCs, and the Internet.
- They form flying wheels, and they support each other.

** Good aspect**: Patent authorization is free security pads, car chip is a high-visibility growth engine, Oryon self-researched architecture improves chip competitiveness.

** Incomplete **: The pricing of cellular chips is being eroded by UNRISD, the apple-coated strip decoupling is in place, and the QTL growth is at its peak.

In one sentence, it is summarized:** the high-quality business model "good but imperfect" -- patent toll stations are its unique advantage, but chip competition is its eternal destiny**.
