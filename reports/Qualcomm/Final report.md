## Investment thesis

### Look at bullish logic (why it’s worth paying attention to)

1. **Patent licensing is a "perpetual toll booth"**: QTL contributes $4 billion in pre-tax profits every year (profit margin 63%), and this money will continue to flow in as long as there are 5G devices in the world. This provides a free safety mat for the entire company.

2. **Automotive chips are the second growth curve**: Quarterly revenue in FY2025 Q4 exceeded US$1 billion, FY2026 Q1 reached US$1.1 billion, and design win pipeline reached US$45 billion. In-depth cooperation with BMW, GM and other car companies, coupled with a 3-7 year platform locking cycle, provides high visibility of long-term growth.

3. **Core beneficiary of AI terminals**: Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5’s on-device AI capabilities (37% NPU speed increase) make mobile phones truly capable of running large language models. If the era of AI agents arrives, the value of terminal chips will increase significantly.

4. **Valuation provides margin of safety**: 19x PE, 6% FCF yield, $20 billion buyback plan, undervalued in the semiconductor industry.

### Bearish logic (why you need to be cautious)

1. **Apple customer churn is a foregone conclusion**: About $2 billion/year of baseband chip revenue will return to zero by 2027, requiring other business growth to fill it.

2. **China’s market concentration is too high**: About 60% of revenue comes from China, and geopolitical risks are the biggest uncontrollable variable.

3. **Mobile phone chip competition intensifies**: MediaTek Dimensity 9400 is approaching Snapdragon in the flagship field, and the risk of price war increases.

4. **Semiconductor Cyclicity**: FY2026 Q1 has seen signs of Chinese OEM destocking, and short-term performance may be under pressure.

## Four-dimensional review

### Business model dimensions

Qualcomm operates the most unique business model in the semiconductor industry - the "chip + patent" two-wheel drive. The patent licensing part is a real "money printing machine", which is extremely rare among global technology companies. However, the competitive nature of the chip business determines that this is a business that "has to fight every year." The core contradiction of two-wheel drive is that the most profitable part (QTL) is growing the slowest, and the fastest growing part (automotive chips) is still in the proof period.

### Financial valuation dimensions

Qualcomm's financial data presents a "good but not expensive" picture: ROIC of nearly 30%, free cash flow of $12.8 billion, and a healthy balance sheet (net debt of only $9.3 billion). PE of 19x is low among peers, reflecting market concerns about Apple's customer churn and slowing growth. But if the automotive business grows to $8 billion (FY2029) as planned, the current valuation clearly undervalues ​​the company's long-term value.

### Competitive moat dimension

The moat is layered: the patent layer is nearly impenetrable, the chip layer is being eroded, and the automotive platform layer is being built. The biggest structural risk is not the rise of a certain competitor, but the intensification of multiple competitions at the same time - MediaTek attacks the flagship market from below, Apple completes its own research and replacement from above, and ARM tries to change the rules of the game from the basic layer. Qualcomm's Oryon self-developed architecture is a key weapon in dealing with this multi-line pressure, and winning the first instance provides legal protection for this.

### Management Risk Dimensions

Cristiano Amon is Qualcomm's best CEO candidate in the past decade - with a technical background, clear strategy and strong execution ability. The best endorsement is that the automobile business exceeds its promises. But he also faces the most complex challenge of any Qualcomm CEO: juggle Apple decoupling, the ARM dispute, China risks and the post-smartphone transition. The performance so far has been excellent, but the next 2-3 years will be a critical verification period.

## Investment advice

### Qualitative judgment: **Observe positions/small positions and wait for better buying opportunities**

Qualcomm is a good company and reasonably valued, but it's not an "obvious bargain" right now. Although 19 times PE is not expensive, considering the cyclical nature of semiconductors and the superposition of multiple risks, the lack of sufficient margin of safety makes people decisively take positions.

### Ideal buying conditions

1. **Valuation level**: PE falls back below 15 times (corresponding to a stock price of about $160), or there may be a panic decline due to short-term negative factors (such as quarterly results that are worse than expected, geopolitical events)
2. **Fundamental level**: The automotive business has exceeded expectations for 2-3 consecutive quarters, proving that the growth curve is sustainable
3. **Risk Elimination**: ARM litigation is completely settled, or Sino-US relations substantively ease

### Key monitoring indicators

- Automotive chip quarterly revenue (goal: continued growth to over $1.5 billion/quarter)
- QTL licensing revenue stability (validates whether there are cracks in the patent moat)
- Apple baseband procurement ratio (observe whether the decoupling speed is accelerated)
- Changes in revenue share from Chinese customers
- ARM appeal progress
