## Let’s look at a set of facts first

In April 2026, Sora officially stopped serving. With a daily burn of US$15 million and a total revenue of only US$2.1 million, OpenAI spent two years proving one thing: having the best technology does not mean being able to survive.

In the same month that Sora closed, a Chinese startup company completed a Series B financing of nearly 2 billion yuan, with a valuation of more than 2 billion US dollars. It's called Shengshu Technology. It was established just three years ago and has less than a hundred employees. Its core product, Vidu, ranks second in the global technology rankings.

The company's situation is as follows:

Putting these together - **a startup company that was established just 3 years ago with less than 100 employees has a valuation of more than 2 billion US dollars and ranks second in the world in terms of technology**.

If you think this is amazing – you’re not wrong, but you’re only seeing half of it.

The other half is: **The ARR of this company’s competitor Keling (Kuaishou) is already 25 times that of it. Behind ByteDance’s Dream is an annual capital expenditure of 200 billion yuan. Sora, the most famous pioneer on this track, officially stopped serving in April 2026 after burning US$15 million a day. **

This is the reality faced by Shengshu Technology: the technology is strong enough, but strong enough may not necessarily be enough to survive. **

## What exactly is Shengshu Technology?

In a sentence: **Shengshu Technology is an AI video generation company with the original Diffusion+Transformer architecture as the core. The core product is called Vidu. **

What Vidu does is: you enter a text description (such as "a cat playing guitar on the moon"), and it uses AI to help you generate a high-quality video. Actual application scenarios are expanding rapidly:

| Application scenarios | Specific usage | Market status |
|---------|---------|---------|
| Advertising marketing | Generate advertising films at a cost of 100 yuan (traditional production costs 10,000 yuan) | The fastest growing, with a closed business loop |
| Short plays/comics | AI-generated animated short plays, **122,000 units will be online in Q1 of 2026** | Explosive growth, but the hit rate is only **0.12%** |
| E-commerce | Digital live broadcasts, product display videos | Market size is estimated to double year-on-year |
| Film and television production | Storyboard preview, special effects assistance | Early exploration stage |
| Personal creation | Social media content generation | Large number of users but low willingness to pay |

**Industry consensus is that the market size of AI video generation will grow from approximately US$1.1 billion in 2025 to approximately US$12.8 billion in 2033** (Source: Estimates from market research institutions, each caliber is different). The direction of the track is determined, and the question is who can survive to the end.

To understand Shengshu Technology, you must first understand its team.

| Character | Role | One sentence summary |
|------|------|-----------|

| **Bao Fan** | CTO/Co-founder | Post-95s Ph.D. from Tsinghua University, first author of U-ViT paper (ICLR 2023 Outstanding Paper Award). The core engine of technical execution |
| **Luo Yihang** | CEO (joined in March 2025) | Ph.D. in the Department of Automation, Tsinghua University, former head of the Byte Volcano Engine AI application product line. Airborne takes charge of commercialization |

This team configuration has a clear signal: **The founding team is purely academic and has insufficient commercialization capabilities, so a person with experience in a large company was hired from Byte to be the CEO. **

Those who are bullish say: This shows that the team is self-aware and can introduce complementary talents.
Bearish people say: The founding CEOs have resigned, indicating that commercialization has not been promoted well. There is a big question mark whether the parachute CEO can integrate with the academic founding team.

**Both sides make sense. ** The key depends on whether Luo Yihang can increase his income in the next 12 months.

## Financing rhythm: five rounds in three years, the faster the financing, the faster

| Round | Time | Amount | Lead investor | Post-money valuation |
|------|------|------|--------|---------|
| Angel round | June 2023 | Nearly 100 million yuan | Ant Group | About 100 million US dollars |
| Pre-A round | June 2024 | Hundreds of millions of yuan | Baidu, Beijing AI Fund | Undisclosed |
| Series A | September 2025 | Hundreds of millions of yuan | Imperial Capital | Undisclosed |
| Round A+ | February 2026 | Over 600 million yuan | Zhongguancun Science City, Starlink Capital | Undisclosed |
| Series B | April 2026 | Nearly **2 billion yuan** | **Alibaba Cloud** | Over **2 billion U.S. dollars** |

*Source: Bloomberg, CNBC, Financial Associated Press, 36Kr (multi-source cross-validation)*

Several signals worth noting:

1. **2.6 billion raised in two months** (A+ round 600 million + round B 2 billion) - either it is too popular or too short of money. Most likely both
2. **Alibaba Cloud leads the Series B investment** - This is not just a financial investment, the two parties have signed a strategic cooperation agreement, and Vidu has been launched on Alibaba Cloud Bailian Model Plaza. Alibaba Cloud is both an investor and a distribution channel
3. **Valuation increased by 20 times in 3 years** (100 million → 2 billion US dollars) - This is not an exaggeration in the primary market, but the premise is that the growth can be sustained
4. **The shareholding reform will be completed in March 2026** - changing from "limited company" to "joint stock limited company". This is a standard pre-action for Hong Kong stock IPO.

**Opposite view**: Intensive financing may also indicate that the company is burning money very quickly - based on the current financing balance and reasoning costs, it may not be able to last long without continued financing. The valuation of US$2 billion in the primary market is set by VC, which does not mean that the secondary market is willing to recognize this price.

## A key analogy: Sora’s lesson

Before studying digital technology, you must first read a negative textbook.

On March 24, 2026, OpenAI announced the closure of Sora, the most famous product in the AI video generation track.

Sora’s financial numbers are shocking:

| Metrics | Data | Sources |
|------|------|------|
| Average daily inference cost | **15 million US dollars** | Forbes, Cantor Fitzgerald estimate |
| Total lifetime revenue | **$2.1 million** | Media coverage |
| Cost of a single 10-second video inference | Approximately **$1.30** | Analyst estimate |
| Ending | **Officially discontinued on April 26, 2026** | OpenAI Announcement |

** Daily burn: $15 million, total revenue: $2.1 million. ** This means Sora burns more money in one day than it makes in a lifetime.

Sora's failure has taught the entire industry a lesson: technological leadership does not mean commercial viability, and reasoning costs are a matter of life and death. **

Vidu of Shengshu Technology has a longer life span than Sora and has better cost control (API pricing is **55%** lower than the industry average price), but it faces the same underlying industry problem: **Can the inference cost of AI video generation be reduced to a level that allows the business model to run smoothly? **

We discuss this issue in detail in Part 4.

## Why it’s worth studying

Shengshu Technology is not yet on the market, and most people may have never heard of the name. Why spend time studying it?

Three reasons:

**First, AI video generation is one of the most certain technology trends in 2026. **Global VC will invest **4.7 billion** in this track in 2025 (+189% year-on-year). Regardless of whether Shengshu Technology itself is successful or not, understanding this track is crucial to understanding the future content industry.

**Second, Shengshu Technology is the best sample to understand the core issue of "Can independent AI startups survive?" ** There are more than 800 AI video companies, and less than 20% of them are commercial closed loops. Big companies have traffic, computing power, and distribution channels, so how can independent companies survive?

**Third, it is likely to have a Hong Kong IPO in 2026-2027. ** If it goes public, investors need to understand the nature of the company in advance - whether it is a real technology platform or another "storytelling financing" AI bubble.
