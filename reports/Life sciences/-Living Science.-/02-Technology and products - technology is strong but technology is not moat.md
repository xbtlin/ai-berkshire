# From a paper

In September 2022, Qinghua University, Qinghua Chu Army team doctoral student Bován published a paper presenting**U-Vit architecture** - the first architecture design to integrate the diffusion model (Diffusion Model) into Transformer in depth around the globe.

The paper received the ICLR 2023 Award for Outstanding Thesis (less than 1 per cent of the top global AI conference).

Why is this paper important? It is a year earlier than OpenAI’s Dit (Difident Transformer, Sora’s Technical Base)**. In other words, the core structure of Sora’s ideas, which led to the Qinghua team, has gone through much earlier.

This is the technological starting point for life-long technology:** not for technological improvements, but for original architecture.**

# A moat: Dismantling each of the five dimensions

Technology is not the same as a moat. We assess it using a classic five-dimensional framework:

## 1. Brand and pricing rights: <unk> (weak)

- API pricing is lower than average industry prices **55% ** ** which means it's buying the market** and not selling brand names**
- I know a lot about the professional creative world, but C is far from the sound of a dream.
- No "Vidu = Professional" brand mind.

##2. Conversion cost: <unk> (weak)

- C-end users can switch to the fine, dream, Runway, etc. at zero cost
- B-end API clients have some integration costs (symmetry of documents, workflow matching), but do not constitute a strong lock-in
- The crediting prepayment creates a little sticky, but it's a weak lock.

##3. Network effects: <unk> (very weak)

- Video generation is a tool-type product**, with virtually no network effect among users
- Not like a micro-mail or a treasure-hunting.
- It's a disease of A.I. Tool type products, not just a matter of life-tech.

##4. Scale effect: <unk> (medium)

- Model training is high fixed costs, and the greater the number of users, the lower the marginal cost of reasoning.
- ** But the scale is clearly **: bytes beat capital expenditure in 2025** 200 billion**, fast hands can be measured against fast hands — the scale of the calculus of life-tech is not comparable
- The scale effect exists, but in this track, the size of an independent start-up company will never catch up with the big plant.

##5. Technology/Picticity Barrier: <unk> (stronger)

- U-ViT original structure, **65 patents**
- ICEE Fellow, IEEE FELLO, MILOR, BAUAN
Vidu Q3 is the top two in the world in Artificial Analysis.

The U-ViT architecture is an example of itself: less than a year after it was introduced, the idea is absorbed by the industry. All mainstream video production models now use similar Diffusion+Transformer structures.** The originals’ pre-emptive advantage has been absorbed largely by the industry’s quick follow-up.**

The current technological advantages of biotech are more evident in the engineering capabilities** (the speed at which structural advantages are translated into product experience) and** the continuous iterative capabilities** (the degree of upgrading of each generation of models), rather than the monopoly of the architecture itself.

** The multi-rater said: ** Although the technical barriers are declining, the team of life-techs can keep running ahead — the intellectual ability of the Zhu army ensures directional judgment, and the enforcement of the Bovan forces guarantees the project's landing.

** Airseers say **: Technical barriers are rapidly declining + big plants have more than 10 times the arithmetic advantage = technology leads no longer. Open source models (Ali Wan 2.2, XinyuanVideo) are fast approaching the quality of closed source models, and API pricing will be zero once the open source is rounded up.

** Both sides make sense.** Technical barriers are the most powerful moats of life-long technology today, but they are also the most vulnerable. It's worth it not "now deep," but "can you dig deep enough"? It depends on whether the team can maintain the second highest level of the world on every model of the next generation.

# 65 patents: How much protection?

The protection of patents in the AI area deserves a question mark.

** A positive side**:
- 65 patents covering U-Vit architecture and related technologies
- In the Big B Client Negotiations, the patent combination is a signal.
- If patent licences are involved in the future, this is a potential source of revenue.

** The adverse side**:
- The patent in the AI field is relatively easy to bypass - a few modules can be used to avoid
- There's very little patent fight between China's A.I.
- Open source models are not patented - Ali can achieve similar effects with completely different structures

** Conclusion: patents are more defensive (prevent legal recourse in the event of a copying) than offensive weapons (cannot prevent competition from emerging).**
