The charm of the traditional SaaS is that the marginal cost is closer to zero – with one user at little cost. But AI video generation** consumes a lot of GPU ** per video generation, with marginal costs high.

This means:** the more users, the more the losses** — unless unit costs can be reduced to the level that users can cover at their fees.

# Summing up: three layers of the truth about life and technology

Look at the whole series. Life-tech paintings can be summarized on three levels:

** First tier (most sure): very technological.** U-ViT original structure, Vidu Q3 first global evaluation, top team Qinghua -- these are third-party facts.

** Second tier (dissenting): Commercialization is difficult.** ARR has only $20 million, dreams of a 25-fold income gap and unlimited ammunition, 80% of industry's losses - there is no answer as to whether technology leads to commercial success.

** Third tier (most uncertain): The valuation is expensive.** PS 100 times (based on known ARR) pricing is highly optimistic and the security margin is inadequate. Unless ARR is $200 million (down to 10 times) by 2025, the current valuation is not friendly to investors.

** One sentence concludes**:

> ** Life sciences is an AI video-generated start-up company with the first two technologies globally, and the central issue is not "Technology strong enough" but "Is it possible for an independent start-up company to survive the day of profit in a big factory run-down and burning-money competition?"**

There is no standard answer to this question. The answer depends on the growth of the AI video market, the decline in the cost of reasoning, the synergy depth of the Aliun ecology and whether the cruise can be commercialized within 12 months.
