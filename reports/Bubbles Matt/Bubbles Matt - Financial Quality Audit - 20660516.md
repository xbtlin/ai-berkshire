# One, conclusions first: 13 billion is real money, but there are two flaws

** General remarks: Quality of profit <unk> (4/5)**

The 13 billion profit in 2025 for Bubble Matt is basically "real silver." The cash flow is 12,865 million, almost 1:1 match to the net profit of GAAP of 12,776 million, and the cash flow conversion is extremely efficient. The bank borrowing is zero, and the cash reserve is 13,775 million. This is a textbook-level financial statement.

** But two flaws are alarming**:
1. Inventory growth (246 per cent) significantly exceeded the income increase (18.5 per cent) and turnover days deteriorated by 21 days
Free cash flow rate (FCF/net profit = 77 per cent) is depressed by capital expenditure and stock expansion

> "A good company's profit statement can be a lie, but a cash flow statement is hard to lie." > > > > >

# Core judgment

** "Is 13 billion real money?" ** Yes, it's very good real money.**

Evidence:
1. Operating cash flows of $12,865 million versus net profits of $12,776 million, almost 1:1 and "feat fat" without accrued profits
2. Zero bank borrowing, zero interest expenditure, profit not dependent on financial leverage
3. GAAP/Non-GAAP differences are only 300 million (238 per cent), and companies do not abuse adjustment items
4. 7 days of working days in accounts receivable, indicating the extremely rapid realization of income

** "What will the quality of profits be in 2026?" ** -- about the rate of high quality, but stock will need to be monitored**

Key observation points:
- If the turnover of inventory continues to deteriorate from 123 days to more than 140 days, this may mean a risk of product de-marketing
- Broadening capital spending (Eurland, Overseas Door) will reduce free cash flow
- Maori certainty decrease of 0.5 pct (price increases for raw materials) and it is necessary to see whether the transfer is made through price increases

> "Assessing the quality of a company ' s profits, it is essential to see three things: whether cash flows keep up with profits, whether inventories keep up with sales, whether receivables keep up with income. "
