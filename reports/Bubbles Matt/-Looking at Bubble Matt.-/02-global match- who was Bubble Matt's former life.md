# How did it fall?

The story is divided into three paragraphs:

** First paragraph: High growth (2017-2021)** Through the constant authorization of new visual roles, the collection went from $650 million to $1.03 billion. The logic is simple — every new movie is a wave of hand-made work.

** Second paragraph: Stock crisis (2022-2023)** The problem was in 2022. The company was overly optimistic about the large stock, and the demand was so sharp that it spent $39 million on surplus stock management alone in 2022.

** Third paragraph: struggling to survive (2024-2026)** In 2024, the deficit barely narrowed to $14.7 million, but the revenue continued to fall to $1.05 billion. In 2025, the year was worse, the same year-on-year income fell by more than 20 per cent, with $41 million in the second quarter alone. By early 2026, the company had only $250 million in market value and debt was $225 million. The 2026 guideline was "salve to 3 per cent growth" — which was basically flat for a tidal company.

# What's the difference between Bubble Matt and it?

** Difference one: A role-based comparison of roles.** This is the most central difference. Over 80% of the company's income comes from other people's roles, with a fee for each hand sold, and a profit margin naturally limited. More deadly, it has no pricing power — the role is not its own, consumer loyalty is in the role and not in the brand. The bubble Matt's Labouble, Jasmine, is in the role, the license fee is zero, and the profit is much higher.

** Difference two: Emotional connection depths differ.** The company's hands are essentially "vision perimeter." Consumers buy Spider-Man because they like it, not because they like it. Bubble-mart users buy Rabbu, not because it's "represent" stories, but just because it's a picture itself. This is more sticky without middleman emotional connections.

** Difference three: Weak management.** The company's strategy is "What fire does what," "What fire does," "What" and finally the stock piles up. Bubble Matt did better scarce management -- at least in the early stages -- blind box mechanisms, hidden money, restricted money, all creating a feeling that "we can't get it."

** There is also a similar note of alarm:** both companies are highly dependent on "continuing innovation" to sustain growth. The company needs to keep on using new visual roles, and Bubble Matt needs to keep on rolling new series and roles. Once the pace slows down, or consumer enthusiasm for new products, growth breaks down. The company has fallen into this trap.
