# II. Pricing power for spiritual goods: What makes it more expensive

The most valuable feature of the consumer 's psycho-consumptions is not a "high Maori rate", but rather a pricing power that can grow as per capita income increases**.

That needs to be said.

Mau-Dai's retail price in 2005 was about $300 and in 2025 was about 1500. The price increased fivefold in 20 years, far exceeding the inflation rate during the same period.

Why? Because the higher the income, the higher the absolute amount people would pay for "face" and "social." At the time of the 3000 monthly salary, 300 puppies were luxury; at the time of the 30,000 monthly salary, 1,500 puppies were just "decent".

The price of psycho-consumable goods goes not with cost, but with income. Raw materials rise by 10%, and end prices remain twirl-free — because consumers buy nothing but raw materials.

That's the right to price.

And this pricing power has a self-enhanced cycle: the stronger the brand, the less price-sensitive consumers are, the more price-free, the more profit-making, the more input the brand, the stronger the brand.

The poohouse has been turning for 70 years. Bubble Matt is just starting.

# Six, conclusion: 70% of the hut

Bubbles Matt does have the pricing power of psycho-consumable goods.

Seventy-two percent of the Māori rate is not based on price fighting, but on brand premiums. For three years, Māori rate has risen, prices are far higher overseas than domestic prices and consumers are not competitive - all of this is solid evidence of the right to price.

But it's not price-fixing enough "hard."

There are no non-reproducible supply bottlenecks like the huts. There are no down-scale consumption scenarios rooted in the millennium culture. There are no down-stamp brands that have been tested for 70 years. The core population has lower consumption ceilings than down-stamps.

If you have to quantify -- Bubble Matt's pricing is probably "78 percent of the hut."

Yes, and quite strong. But weaker than Mao Dai.

What does this judgment mean by investment?

** If you believe Bubbles Matt can upgrade from a "tide premium" to "cultural symbol"** -- Laboub film success, brand intergenerational transmission, and a lovely economy proved to be permanent demand -- its pricing power will continue to be reinforced, with 72% or even higher Māori rates sustained for many years. The current 15-fold city surplus is a serious underestimation.

** If you think that Bubbles Matt's pricing power is in essence a "tide dividend" ** - heat will decline, young people will move on and Labouble will eventually be replaced by a lovely image - the current Māori rate is the peak and the extreme, and the 15-fold market rate is just reasonable.

My view: The probability is between.

Bubbles Matt has a better pricing than most people think – $596 is not a bad idea, and it is not a marketing function, but a brand-style expression. Nor is it a price-fixing power that can make people sleep perfectly – the certainty of the hut, the bubble Matt, is far from it.

Ultimately, pricing authority cannot be reinforced, not by the analyst ' s model, but by time.

It took the Cat 52 years to prove himself. It took the hut 70 years. Bubble Matt only left 10 years.
