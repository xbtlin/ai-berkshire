> ** "First, Labubu accounts for 40% of income, which is still rising. This is not a healthy structure. Historically, consumer goods companies that rely heavily on a single product have been able to waver their valuation base once the product ' s heat has subsided."**
>
> ** 'Second, there is no precedent for the tidal industry to show that a brand can last more than 30 years and maintain high growth. It took the trufflegus 50 years to harvest $93 billion, and Bubble Matt 5.1 billion in three years. Either it's a genius or the market has compressed growth over the next 30 years to three years."
>
> ** 'Third, 72% of the Māori rate means that consumers are willing to pay for the emotional premium. The question is, is the premium solid when the economy goes down or the tide turns? The premium for luxury goods is based on centuries of brand deposition, and the premium for bubble Matt is based on the spread of the virus in social media. The former is the moat, the latter is more like a smooth wind.

#6.3 Can the industry grow? Final judgment

** The tidal industry itself can grow ** - The human need for collections, for beauty, for emotional trust is eternal. The generations sell models for 45 years, the truffles for Hello Kitty live 52 years, and Lego live 90 years.

** But specifically, Bubble Matt**, the long-term greens need to meet the following conditions:
1. Proves that new blast IPs (not just Labbu) can be hatched continuously
2. Moving brands from "tide" to "culture symbols"
3. Maintenance of supply discipline and non-extract of production due to growth pressures
4. The overall harvest can still increase when core IP heat is naturally declining

** These conditions are still in "to be verified" status.**
