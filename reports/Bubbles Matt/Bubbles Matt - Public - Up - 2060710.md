# A little monster supports 37.1 billion: What kind of business is Bubble Mart｜Part 1

In April 2024, Lisa from the Korean girl group Blackpink posted on social platforms a small doll hanging on her bag - fanged, grinning, and a little ugly.

That week, this little monster named Labubu caused Bubble Mart’s sales in Southeast Asia to increase by more than 30%, and the secondary market premium was once as high as 400%. Since then, Rihanna and Lady Gaga have successively brought the product, and long queues have begun to form at the entrances of shopping malls around the world.

The company behind it, Bubble Mart, had revenue of only 6.3 billion in 2023 and 37.1 billion in 2025. Two years, nearly 6 times.

A person selling blind boxes, why?

## What kind of business is it?

Let me make one thing clear first: Bubble Mart is not a toy company, it is an "IP monetization platform."

Its carrier is trendy fun, but what it really sells is emotion. An artist draws an image, and Pop Mart makes it into a small doll that can be purchased repeatedly, and then uses the blind box mechanism to make you pay for it again and again.

The design of the blind box is very exquisite. There are about 12 regular models plus 1 hidden model in each series. The regular models cost 59 to 99 yuan each. You don’t know which one you will get before you open it. The probability of a hidden classic appearing is 1/144. If you want to get a complete set or bet on a hidden model, you have to keep buying.

Psychology has long explained why this mechanism is addictive: uncertain rewards are more addictive than certain rewards. Slot machines follow this principle, as do blind boxes. But I prefer to use a calm number to illustrate its stickiness - the repurchase rate of Bubble Mart members in mainland China is 55.7%.

What is the concept of this number? This means that more than half of those who have bought will come back to buy again. And it is going up year by year, and it will still be 49.1% in 2024. There are 72.58 million registered members, contributing 93.7% of sales. This is no longer impulse consumption, but a stable and continuous emotional payment by a group of people.

The word "platform" is reflected in how densely it reaches you.

Offline, there are 630 stores around the world, plus 2,637 self-service box-drawing robots placed in shopping malls. Online, a box-drawing machine mini program sold 3.42 billion in the whole year. There are also self-developed applications, official websites and TikTok live broadcasts overseas. Among them, the revenue of self-developed applications in the Americas has increased nearly 14 times in a year.

Stores, machines, mini programs, live broadcasts - five or six channels are like five or six hands, handing the same doll to you repeatedly.

What's even more interesting is the way Bubble Mart is made - it's "made backwards".

Disney first has stories and movies to make you fall in love with the characters, and then sells peripherals. Bubble Mart does the opposite: first rely on blind boxes to spread out its products and channels and sell its image, and then go back to add stories and world views. This is where it is most efficient - there is no need to gamble on a movie costing hundreds of millions; but as we will see in the next article, this may also be where it is most vulnerable.

## Achieved nearly 6 times in two years, relying on three engines

Let’s go back to the question of “nearly 6 times in two years”. 6.3 billion in 2023, 13 billion in 2024, and 37.1 billion in 2025. Behind this steep curve, there are three engines - the first two manage how fast the growth is, and the third manages how valuable the growth is.

The first engine is the little monster Labubu who was made popular by the star at the beginning.

It is derived from the 2015 picture book by Hong Kong artist Kasing Lung. It has sharp teeth and is a bit ugly. When it was made into a blind box in 2019, it was tepid. It was not until 2024 that it was picked up by global stars and became an international status symbol.

Here we must first clarify something that is easy to get confused: Labubu is the leading character in the series "THE MONSTERS", and the company's financial report calculates revenue based on the series. So that staggering number you see is talking about the entire series.

How exaggerated is the revenue it brings? In 2024, a single image of Labubu will be worth about 3 billion. By 2025, the entire THE MONSTERS series will have reached 14.16 billion, a year-on-year increase of 365.7%. One series accounts for about 38% of the company's total revenue. This is the first "10 billion IP" in the history of trendy games.

I would like to add a word of fairness here, lest you think it is just a "one trick". In addition to Labubu, it also has self-raised images such as SKULLPANDA and CRYBABY - in 2025, it will have 6 IPs with revenue exceeding 2 billion and 17 with revenue exceeding 100 million. In other words, from the earliest Molly to today's Labubu, it has proven one thing: it does not just incubate one hit, but can create one after another. How stable this IP production line is will be the core of the controversy in the next article, but the matrix itself is the confidence that it dares to call itself a "platform".

The second engine is the flipping of categories. In the past, everyone thought that Bubble Mart sold hard plastic figures, but in 2025, its plush toys sold 18.71 billion, an increase of 560.6%, surpassing figures for the first time and becoming the largest category. Labubu, the plush pendant that can be hung on your bag, falls into this category. Once the category changes, the ceiling will be raised a bit.

The first two engines manage how fast the growth is, and the third engine manages how valuable the growth is—that is, gross profit margin. In 2025, Bubble Mart's gross profit margin will reach 72.1%, and its net profit margin will be 35.1%. For a company that sells plastic dolls, its gross profit margin is on the same level as luxury goods and software companies. The cost of plastic is very low. What you buy is the image, the scarcity, and the heartbeat of unboxing.

With the three engines superimposed, net profit jumped from an adjusted 3.4 billion in 2024 to an adjusted 13.08 billion in 2025, an increase of nearly three times.

## Going to sea: from zero to half the world

If Labubu is the fire, the place where the fire is burning the most is actually overseas.

The overseas story of Bubble Mart only took two or three years. Overseas revenue will be approximately 1.07 billion in 2023, 5.07 billion in 2024, and 16.27 billion in 2025, accounting for 43.8% of total revenue—overseas already accounts for half of the country. The Americas market will grow by 748% in 2025, and Europe by 506%.

Even more ruthless is the single store. In 2024, the average annual revenue of a Pop Mart overseas directly-operated store will be more than three times that of a single store in mainland China. And it’s even more expensive – the same Labubu is priced 5% to 10% higher in Thailand than in China, and there are still long queues in the store.

The bridgehead to the sea is Southeast Asia, and Thailand is phenomenal. The Labubu theme store in MEGA BANGNA in Bangkok had a turnover of more than 10 million yuan on the first day of its opening, setting a single-day record for an overseas single store; the Tourism Authority of Thailand even held a welcome ceremony for Labubu at the airport and named it a "Magical Thailand Experience Officer". A doll was used as a cultural business card by a country's tourism bureau.

The style of play has also changed. Before 2022, it was engaged in wholesale of "global goods", but later it was converted into direct sales, and it specially selected the most expensive locations to open the largest stores - Oxford Street in London, the Louvre in Paris, and Ximending in Taipei. Wang Ning's original words were: "Overseas, we need to be braver and have the courage to open bigger stores in better locations."

And these landmark stores, which are getting bigger and bigger, sell more than just dolls. Life-size sculptures, scenes where you can check in and take pictures, and immersive displays divided by IP series - the store is made into a space where you can browse, take photos, and stay for half a day.

Do you see that upgrade line? From "selling blind boxes" to "selling scenes".

In the early days, Bubble Mart sold boxes for 59 yuan each; now it sells a place where you can stay, take photos, and post to friends, turning the store into a small theme park entrance. Going to the extreme end of this line is the real paradise - Beijing's Bubble Mart City Park. The passenger flow increased by 70% in 2025, and the location selection in Shanghai is also advancing.

Looking in this direction, what this company wants to do may be bigger than the term "China's Disney".

## Wang Ning: From Henan grocery store to Henan’s richest man

The person who made this happen is called Wang Ning. He was born in 1987 in a county town in Xinxiang, Henan. His parents ran a grocery store.

His starting point was not high. I studied advertising in college. In 2010, I went to Hong Kong to visit my girlfriend. I was inspired by a local trendy grocery store and came back to open a store selling trendy haberdashery. The real turning point came in 2016, when he signed a designer, launched the first exclusive IP image Molly, and transformed into a blind box. At the end of 2020, the 33-year-old brought Bubble Mart to the Hong Kong stock market.

In June 2025, he became the richest man in Henan with a net worth of approximately 146.7 billion yuan, holding nearly half of the company's shares.

One of his words is worth remembering: "Don't be China's Disney, but be the world's Bubble Mart." Another sentence is, "The CEO only focuses on the biggest things and the smallest things, and leaves the middle to the team." A man who grew up in a county grocery store sold an ugly little monster opposite the Louvre Museum.

## So, here comes the question

At this point, Bubble Mart looks almost perfect: 72% gross profit, 55.7% repurchase, half of the market overseas, an IP that sweeps the world, and a very young founder.

In June 2025, its market value reached approximately HK$380 billion.

Then, on March 25, 2026, it handed over an almost impeccable annual report - revenue of 37.1 billion and net profit tripled. On that day, its stock price plummeted in a single day, and its market value was wiped out by more than $30 billion.

Why did a perfect financial report create a big hole?

Because the market read a sentence in the financial report: the company's growth guidance for 2026 is "not less than 20%." In the past year, the growth rate was 184.7%. From 184% to 20%, what the market sees is not a company growing, but a train braking hard.

So everyone began to ask the most important question again: Is Bubble Mart the next Disney or the next "Beanie Babies"? Is the nearly 40% of its revenue supported by the series led by Labubu a sustainable profit or the peak of a trend? Even Duan Yongping, who has always said "If you don't understand, don't touch it" started to build positions after the collapse - but was he betting on this business or Wang Ning?

Are those people queuing up at the entrance of shopping malls around the world, and those flagship stores opening bigger and bigger, the starting point of an everlasting business, or the busiest moment of a feast?

This is the question to be answered in the next article.
