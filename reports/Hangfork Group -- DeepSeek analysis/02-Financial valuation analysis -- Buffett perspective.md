##2.2 Analysis of the reasons for the increase in the Maori rate

The Māori rate rose from 15.51% in 2021 to 24.78% in 2025, with a cumulative increase of 9.27 percentage points.

** (1) Reduced prices of raw materials steel (cyclical factor)**
- Forklifts are mainly raw materials of steel (hot rolls, styles, etc.), with a price of steel that has been falling since its height in 2021
- The average price of the screw steel in 2021 was about $5,000 per ton, down to about 3,500-3800 per ton by 2025, or about 25-30%
- Steel accounts for about 15-20 per cent of the manufacturing cost of forklifts, and steel prices have fallen by 3-5 percentage points to the rate of kola-mouri.
- **Assight**: This is a cyclical external advantage that is not sustainable. If steel prices bounce, the Maori rate will be held back.

**(2) Product structural upgrading (structural factors)**
- The Humboldt Group has been shifting towards electric forklifts, high-end warehousing trucks, AGV unmanned forklifts in recent years
- The ratio of gross margins for electric forklifts is usually higher than the 3-5 percentage point for in-fire forklifts
- According to the Chinese Association of Engineering and Mechanical Industries, the Humboldt Group ' s share of electric forklift sales has increased year by year
- ** Evidence**: Continuous additions in R & D inputs and intelligence between 2021 and 2025
- **Assight**: This is a structural improvement and is more sustainable.

**(3) scale effect**
- Increase in the number of camps from $14,490 million to $17,739 million in 2021-2025, an increase of 22.4 per cent
- The scale effect contribution is relatively limited, however, considering that the increase in the Māori rate is much greater than the increase in the harvest rate
- ** Perspective**: The scale effect has contributed, but not the main cause of the increase in the Maori rate.

**(4) Exchange rate factor (export operations)**
- Hangfort group overseas revenues ranged from about 30 to 35 per cent, and the renminbi depreciation cycle was a good export business Māori rate
- The renminbi has been weak against the dollar since 2022, and good export settlements have been made.

** Combined judgements**: about 60 per cent of the increase in the Maori rate is due to structural factors (product upgrading + exports) and about 40 per cent to cyclical factors (steel prices fall).** Structural factors are sustainable, but a rebound in steel prices is a potential risk**.
