# Business model nature

FICO is a standard supplier of bilateral fees**:
**B2B End:** Bank pays the sum of 0.6 to $10 per wholesale licence per credit rating (in the case of loan application, credit card, mortgage)

** Income quality:** Scores is a typical "pass fee" model, with marginal costs close to zero without additional capital expenditure; software side is being transformed towards ARR/SaaaS, with platforms increasing by 35 per cent per year + but still small ($264 million ARR).

# The heart of the moat: not the algorithm, but the standard status

The FICO moat is not a technical moat, but a system moat** — it has become part of the United States credit system:
- More than 90% of borrowers use FICO ratings for decision making
- Fannie Mae/Freddie Mac has long been required by FIO as a statutory requirement
- Historical data validation for FIO 10T takes years to accumulate
- Any change in the rating market by Congress/Regulation is very resistant.

# The trend of the moat: change in the past five years

- ** Widening part:** Pricing authority proved extremely strong in the moat (16 times price rise, no apparent contraction of demand)
- ** Potential narrowness:** FHFA approved VantageScore 4.0 into the GSE market, the first true moat in 30 years; the DOJ survey of Senator Hawley brought about regulatory uncertainty

> **Buffett asks: 10 years later, is this moat still there? What can destroy it?**
> Most likely path to destruction: ** Regulating mandatory substitution (non-market substitution)** - FIO’s B2B billing rights would collapse if the US Congress passed legislation imposing competitive ratings. This requires a strong political will, a coordinated credit sector shift, and a combination of consumer education. There is little precedent in history.

# Opportunities and threats of the AI era

**Hastance:**
- FICO Platform is evolving as "AI decision-making hub", and Gartner 2026 Magic Quadrant is rated as the leader of the smart decision-making platform.

** Threat:**
- AI-origin dynamic risk assessment (without a fixed rating) could theoretically replace FICO, but regulatory recognition would require 10-20 years of validation

But if AI re-engineer the loan approval logic (e.g. AI directly assesses bank currents rather than credit points), 20 years later, FICO may be a company "important but marginalized" in history.
