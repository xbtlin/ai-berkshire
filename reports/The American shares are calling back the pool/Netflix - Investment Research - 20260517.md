# Key features of business models

**Operative leverage analysis**:
- Content costs are set at quasi-fixed costs - a popular set of production costs are fixed, but marginal costs are paid to the first user and 325 million users at almost zero
- 6.5 per cent growth in income in 2021, but three points of decline in operating profitability (leveraging back when size is insufficient)
- Income growth of 15.6 per cent and 15.9 per cent in 2024-2025, respectively, increased by 6 and 3 percentage points in operating margins (leveraging is emerging when the size is adequate)
- Approximately 40-50 cents per new $1 income converted into free cash flows

**The Eternity of the **: Where is the business?

It's a ** content-sized flying wheel. The subscription fee for 325 million users worldwide makes Netflix capable of spending $18B/year on content purchases that no single country competition can afford; a large quantity of quality content increases in user buy-backs (renewed) to provide space for price increases; price increases bring more cash and more content inputs — the flying wheel starts to move faster after 2023. Bad things happen when it takes ** sustained, large-scale input to sustain** and when the moat stops, the river will quickly fade.

# A moat of judgment #

Netflix's moat is essentially a combination of ** economies of scale + content barriers**, not a single moat. This is slightly different from the "someone willing to pay higher prices" that Buffett loves – the moat of Netflix is a moat ** that requires sustained capital maintenance ** and that is self-enhanced without additional maintenance costs, as is the case with Coca Cola.

**Blockbuster had a physical channel to protect the river by Netflix. Who could destabilize the river? Most likely path: When AI content generation is technologically mature and the cost of content production is drastically reduced, the cost barriers for Netflix to rely on large-scale content input will be significantly reduced.

**Buffettian **: 10 years later, is this moat still there?

The biggest variable is the AI’s cost curve for generating content. If AI can produce a good quality content at a cost of one-ten years, the size moat of Netflix, currently built on $18B input, will be partially broken.

# Three of the biggest risks

**Risk 1: AI Content Revolution (in probability, significant impact)**

If AI produces a series of dramas of close to 70-80% artificially produced in five years, with production costs reduced by 90%, the barriers to scale built by Netflix on $18B input will be significantly weakened. By May 2026, Netflix-WBD mergers and acquisitions were still in progress (supposed to multiple challenges such as Paramount competition, antimonopoly reviews, etc.). The Q1,2026 financial statement shows that, despite both higher income and higher profitability expectations, stock prices have fallen by 10 per cent – because the Q2 guidelines are below market expectations. Netflix has stopped disclosing quarterly user numbers, which is a management narrative that is leading the market from "user growth" to "income/profit growth" and that there are expected management risks during the transition period.
