# Intuit (INTU) Investment Research Report

**Study Date: May 17, 2026 | Share Price: Approximately $393**

---

## 1. Business model analysis

**Summary in one sentence:** Intuit relies on its monopoly on the digital entrance to finance and taxation of small and medium-sized enterprises in the United States, charging users subscription fees and transaction service fees.

### Four product lines

| Business Segment | FY2025 Revenue | Growth Rate | Core Logic |
|---|---|---|---|
| Global Business Solutions (QuickBooks + Mailchimp) | $11.1 billion | +16% | Small Business Accounting/Marketing SaaS Subscriptions |
| Consumer Tax (TurboTax) | $4.9 billion | +10% | Tax preparation tool, highly seasonal but extremely sticky |
| Credit Karma | $2.3 billion | +32% | Monetizing personal financial flows |
| ProTax | $620 million | +4% | B2B tax software |

### Moat Assessment

- **Switching Cost (★★★★★)**: QuickBooks has 80% market share (cloud accounting for small businesses in the United States), and data migration costs are extremely high
- **Data Moat (★★★★☆)**: Covers about 1/3 of U.S. tax returns and cannot be copied in the AI era
- **Brand (★★★★☆)**: TurboTax and QuickBooks are as close to category as brand

### Moat trend: strengthening

- Launch of 6 QuickBooks AI intelligent agents in 2025
- Signed a US$100 million cooperation agreement with OpenAI
- AI is being upgraded from an add-on feature to a tiered service that can be priced separately

### Management Capital Allocation

- CEO Sasan Goodarzi (appointed in 2019), revenue increased from $9.3 billion to $18.8 billion
- Credit Karma acquisition results exceeded expectations ($7.1 billion → $2.3 billion annual revenue growth rate 32%)
- FY2026 adds $3.2 billion in repurchase authorization, and dividends increase by 15%

## 3. Industry competition landscape

### Industry structure: oligopoly

- TurboTax accounts for 60% of the US consumer tax filing market share
- QuickBooks accounts for 80% of the U.S. small business cloud accounting market share
- IRS Direct File is offline (2026), regulatory threat eliminated

### Competitors

- H&R Block: Digital transformation behind the curve
- Xero: limited US market share
- FreshBooks/Wave: nowhere near the scale

### Industry Trends

- AI agent wave: Intuit’s AI agent strategy is at the forefront
- Embedded financial services: evolution from tools to financial platforms
- Internationalization: QuickBooks accelerates overseas expansion

---

## 4. Risk and Management

### Three major risks

1. **AI Disruption Paradox (Medium-High)**: AI may reduce the complexity of tax filing—but counterargument: The complexity of tax law will not be reduced by AI, but Intuit’s data advantage will be strengthened
2. **Mailchimp integration falls short of expectations (medium)**: $12 billion acquisition integration is progressing slowly
3. **Uncertain pace of valuation repair (low to medium)**: Market sentiment is unpredictable in the short term.

### Management evaluation: Above average (★★★★☆)

### Worst case scenario

Extreme pessimism (AI disrupts the tax filing market): ~$200-220 (another 45% drop from the current level)
Moderately pessimistic (growth slows to 8-10%): ~$300-330

### Judgment after 10 years: Stronger

Data barriers are strengthening in the AI era, evolving from tools to platforms, and ARPU has been on the rise for a long time.

---

## Overall rating: Excellent

**Summary in one sentence:** Intuit is a high-quality compound interest machine with a deep moat and abundant cash flow. Currently, it has been given the lowest valuation in ten years due to concerns about AI disruption. This is a rare price and value misalignment opportunity for long-term investors.
