# 2.3 Will Zhang Yang be privatized?

** Factors supporting privatization**:
- Long-term market value is far below cash and listed with no financing value
- Maintenance of annual cost of listing $5-10 million (audit, compliance, investor relations)
- Successful experience of free-riding privatization
- The uncertainty of US-China relations increases the attractiveness of de-marketing
- We can focus on IP without having to deal with quarterly pressure after privatization.

** Factors against privatization**:
- Zhang Xiaoyang may have ** enjoyed the social halo of the CEO identity of listed companies**
- The label "Red CEO of Physics" relies partly on the identity of the founder of the listed company.
- Privatization requires litigation and regulatory approval of small shareholders
- Zhang may feel "no hurry" - cash in hand, buy back slowly.

** Sensitization**: Privatization is likely to be moderate, but not always known. It may occur within 3-5 years or may be prolonged.

** The biggest risk is not "not privatization" but "privatization with a very low premium."** The reference to the new wave (only 18% premium) is that if Zhang Xiaoyang privatized with a 20-30% premium (equivalent to $17-18/ADS), the silos have profits but far below their intrinsic value. The 68% privatization premium is a good precedent, but it does not guarantee that the search for the foxes will be treated equally.

# Seven, the ultimate problem of the fox search

Four articles in this series are essentially answering the same question:

> **Fox search is a "liquidation asset with gold" or "value trap that looks cheap and never gets"?**

The honest answer is:** Both are possible and depend on what Zhang Yang does next.**

Before 2025, the answer was more "value trap" — management does not buy back, does not make red, does not privatize, and the discount is always there.

After 2025, $150 million of buybacks changed narratives - management started to return, although both size and continuity needed to be validated.

** Ultimately, the value of the investment in search of the foxes does not depend on its business (the business has no future), but on a person's decision-making — Zhang Xiaoyang chose to use $1.2 billion in cash for what**

- If he keeps buying back and + cents, the discounts are gradually shrinking, investors get a reasonable return
- If he starts privatization, investors get a one-time cash.
- If he does nothing, the discount will always exist, and the investors will have to wait.

It's not a story about "good business." It's a story about "what an honest but mediocre founder would do with $1.2 billion."
