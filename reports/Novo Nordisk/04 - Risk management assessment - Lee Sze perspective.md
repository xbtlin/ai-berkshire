# Novo Nordisk (NVO) — Risk and Management Assessment

> Core question: Can management lead Novo through this difficult period? What are the main risks?

## 1. Management changes and evaluation

### 1.1 CEO replacement: from Lars to Maziar

**Former CEO Lars Fruergaard Jorgensen:**
During his tenure (2017-2025), Novo experienced a GLP-1 explosion period, with revenue growing from approximately 111 billion crowns to 309.1 billion crowns. However, he has also been criticized for failing to effectively deal with competition from Eli Lilly and encountering setbacks in the development of CagriSema. In August 2025, Lars "agreed with the board" to leave office - a term that usually means being forced to step down.

**New CEO Maziar Mike Doustdar:**
- **Background**: More than 20 years with Novo, most recently as Executive Vice President of International Operations, leading 80 country affiliates. During his tenure, international operating income more than doubled to approximately SEK 112 billion (2024)
- **Core Strengths**: Deep understanding of commercialization and market execution, this is precisely the ability Novo needs most at the moment - not R&D and innovation (that was the strength of its predecessor), but maintaining and expanding market share in an environment of intensified competition.
- **Strategic Direction**: Three major priorities will be clarified at the JPM conference in January 2026:
  1. Accelerate business execution
  2. Advance the R&D pipeline
  3. Strike a balance between investing and financial discipline

### 1.2 Drastic reforms

**9,000 layoffs:** Approximately 11.5% of 78,400 global employees, expected to save 8 billion kroner (approximately $1.3 billion) per year by the end of 2026. The savings are reinvested into R&D, commercialization and capacity expansion.

**Organizational Structure Reorganization:**
- Merge the research and early development department and the development department, and establish a chief scientific officer for unified management
- Emil Kongshoj Larsen takes over as executive vice president of international operations
- Strengthening direct-to-patient (DTP) and self-pay payment channels, the CEO made it clear that this is more like a consumer products business

**Board of Directors Changes:** Novo’s former CEO Lars Sorensen (not the former Lars Fruergaard Jorgensen) was nominated as chairman, signaling greater governance oversight.

### 1.3 Management evaluation

**Front:**
- The new CEO has a strong execution background and is suitable for the current "war" stage
- A combination of layoffs + restructuring + focusing on core business, with a clear and decisive direction
- Oral Wegovy Q1 performed beyond expectations, proving that commercial execution is online
- DKK 1.5 billion share buyback program for 2026, demonstrating management's confidence in long-term value

**Concerns:**
- CEO replacement occurs during the most difficult period of the company, and the execution risks during the transition period cannot be ignored.
- 11.5% layoffs may affect organizational morale and R&D efficiency
- The new CEO lacks a research and development background, and there is a question mark whether he can manage the key clinical and regulatory milestones of CagriSema and Amycretin.

## 2. In-depth evaluation of R&D pipeline

### 2.1 Near-term catalysts (12-18 months)

| Pipeline | Progress | Expected time points | Importance |
|------|------|-------------|--------|
| CagriSema (injection) | NDA has been submitted to FDA | It is expected to be approved in Q4 of 2026 and launched in early 2027 | **Extremely high** - Novo's core counterattack weapon |
| Oral Wegovy | **Already on the market** | Continued volume increase | **High** - Q1 verified demand |
| Oral version of Ozempic | Planned | Expected to be launched in the United States under the brand "Ozempic Pill" in Q2 2026 | Medium to high - consolidating the diabetes business |
| Oral Wegovy indications for adolescents | Clinical | 2026-2027 | Medium - expanding population coverage |

### 2.2 Medium and long-term pipeline (2-5 years)

| Pipeline | Mechanism | Progress | Potential |
|------|------|------|------|
| Amycretin/Zenagamtide | GLP-1+ Amylolysin dual agonist | Phase III has been initiated (Q1, 2026) | **Extremely high** - early data >20% weight loss, may equal Eli Lilly |
| IcoSema | Once-weekly basal insulin | Approved/Ongoing | Medium – Insulin Upgrade |
| NASH/MASH indications | Semaglutide for fatty liver disease | Phase III | High - new big indication |
| Cardiovascular/renal indications | Semaglutide | Expanding research | Medium to high - platform value |

**Pipeline Judgment:** Amycretin is Novo’s “ trump card ”. If Phase III data confirms >20% weight loss and good safety, Novo has the opportunity to narrow the efficacy gap with Eli Lilly again in 2028-2029. CagriSema is a "bridge product" during the transition period.

## 3. Core Risk Matrix

### High probability risk

| Risk | Probability | Impact | Description |
|------|------|------|------|
| Revenue does decline in 2026 | Very high | Medium | Company's own guidance confirmed, -4%~-12% CER |
| Eli Lilly's share continues to expand | High | High | Clinical advantages of tilpotide + Oral drug launched |
| U.S. pricing continues to be under pressure | High | High | Dual pressures from policy and competition |

### Medium probability risk

| Risk | Probability | Impact | Description |
|------|------|------|------|
| CagriSema FDA approval delays/conditions | Medium | Very high | Single-chamber injection device abandoned in favor of dual-chamber device, increasing uncertainty |
| The gray market after tighter regulation of compound drugs | Medium | Medium | The demand has been proven and it is difficult to completely eliminate |
| Danish krone exchange rate fluctuations | Medium | Medium | ADR investors are exposed to additional currency risk |

### Low probability but high impact risk

| Risk | Probability | Impact | Description |
|------|------|------|------|
| Long-term safety issues of GLP-1 | Low | Very high | If serious adverse reaction signals occur, the entire category will be affected |
| Amycretin Phase III Failure | Low-Medium | Extremely High | Novo's long-term counterattack plan completely failed |
| U.S. health insurance price control legislation | Low-Medium | High | Systematically reducing the profitability of the entire industry |

## 4. Core judgment from Li Lu’s perspective

> "The most important thing about investing is understanding the nature of the business and whether management is doing the right things."

**Key management judgment:**

The new CEO Doustdar has done several things worthy of recognition:
1. **Face the problem**——Proactively reduce prices, lay off employees and reorganize, without whitewashing the peace.
2. **Focus on core** – cut off non-core businesses and concentrate resources on diabetes and obesity
3. **Accelerated Pipeline** - CagriSema NDA has been submitted and Amycretin Phase III has been launched

But there are also signs worth being wary of:
1. CEO said in CNBC interview that "things will get worse in the short term before they get better" - this kind of language may suggest that 2026 results may be close to the lower end of guidance
2. It will take time to verify whether the mentality change from "monopolist" to "chaser" can penetrate the entire organization.

**Judgment of risk-benefit ratio:**

If you buy at the current 10-12 times PE, the downside is limited (unless there are safety issues at the GLP-1 category level), and the upside depends on:
1. Can the oral product line continue to exceed expectations (the current trend is positive)
2. Can CagriSema be approved on time?
3. Can positive growth resume in 2027?

**Risk Management Rating: ★★★☆☆ (3/5)**

The direction is right after the management change, but execution is still in the early stages. Competition risks are substantial, and CagriSema's approval results are the biggest short-term uncertainty. Fortunately, valuations have fully priced in most of the negatives.
