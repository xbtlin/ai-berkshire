## Summary of investment thesis

### Why it’s worth paying attention to (bull market argument)

1. **Extremely Compressed Valuation**: 10 times PE vs 24 times the 10-year median, the market is pricing in a "permanent recession", but Q1 2026 data has shown signs of an inflection point
2. **Oral Wegovy exceeds expectations**: Revenue in the first quarter of the market was 2.26 billion kroner (twice what analysts expected), 1.3 million prescriptions were filled, and it opened up a huge new patient group
3. **Financial quality remains first-rate**: 33% net profit margin, 21% ROIC, free cash flow restored to 28.3 billion kronor
4. **Sufficient pipeline ammunition**: CagriSema (expected to be approved in Q4 2026) + Amycretin/Zenagamtide (Phase III started, >20% weight reduction potential)
5. **The market is big enough**: GLP-1 is expected to exceed US$150 billion in 2030. Even if the share declines, the absolute scale can still grow.
6. **New CEO takes decisive action**: lay off 9,000 people, save US$1.3 billion annually, focus on core and accelerate pipeline

### Why caution is needed (bear market argument)

1. **It is a fact that Eli Lilly's product power is leading**: In head-to-head trials, tilpotide is "better" than semaglutide. This is not a marketing issue, but a scientific issue.
2. **Market share continues to lose**: The United States dropped from ~60% to ~40%, and Eli Lilly has surpassed Novo in the international market
3. **Revenue will decline materially in 2026**: Guidance -4%~-12% (CER), which is the first negative growth in many years
4. **Pricing power is eroded**: Compound drug competition + insurance coverage negotiation + proactive price reduction, triple pressure
5. **Uncertainty about CagriSema**: The single-chamber device was abandoned and replaced with a dual-chamber device, and the pace of approval and commercialization may be slower than expected.
6. **Execution risks during CEO transition**: Large-scale layoffs + organizational restructuring may drag down operational efficiency in the short term

## Comments from the Four Masters

### Duan Yongping’s perspective: looking at the nature of business
> GLP-1 is one of the best businesses in the past ten years - high repurchase, strong demand, and huge market. The question is not the track, but whether Novo can maintain its position on the track. The success of oral Wegovy proves that Novo still has the ability to innovate, but Eli Lilly’s dual-target molecules are indeed stronger in terms of product power. This is a good business, but the competitive landscape has changed from "one company alone" to "two strong companies working side by side." To buy the second best business in a good business at 10 times PE, you need to be patient and wait for the verification of the next generation product.

### Buffett’s Perspective: Looking at the Margin of Safety
> 33% net profit margin, 21% ROIC, and recovering free cash flow - these figures prove that Novo is still a high-quality company. If you buy such a company with a PE of 10 times, even if you make a mistake, the downside will be limited. The key question is: Is the predicament in 2026 temporary (similar to Coca-Cola's trough in 1999) or structural (similar to Nokia's facing the iPhone in 2010)? Judging from the unexpected performance and pipeline reserves of oral Wegovy, it is more likely to be the former. But it must be admitted that the uncertainty this time is greater than the usual "buy at the bottom of the cycle" - because the competitor (Eli Lilly) is indeed very strong.

### Munger’s Perspective: Looking at the Competitive Landscape
> Duopoly markets are not inherently bad - look at Coca-Cola and PepsiCo, Boeing and Airbus. The question is whether the two parties in the duopoly are equally balanced. The current situation is that Eli Lilly is leading in terms of product strength, while Novo has accumulated experience in brands and channels but is being overtaken. If Amycretin's phase III data proves that Novo has caught up with the efficacy gap, then this is an excellent buying opportunity. If not, Novo may become the "Pepsi" of the GLP-1 market - profitable but not outstanding.

### Li Lu’s perspective: looking at management and risks
>Investment should not only look at odds, but also at probability. The new CEO did the right thing - focusing on layoffs, accelerating the pipeline, and pragmatic pricing. But he is facing an organization that has already made strategic mistakes (underestimating Eli Lilly and CagriSema's R&D setbacks). Positioning should be restrained until management proves itself. CagriSema Q4 approved + oral product line continues to increase volume = signal to add positions. Amycretin Phase III readout = long-term holding basis.
