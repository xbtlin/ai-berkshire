# Novo Nordisk (NVO) Business Model Analysis

> Core question: Is GLP-1 a good business? What are the user stickiness, pricing power, and business essence?

## 1. The Essence of the Business

The business essence of GLP-1 drugs is a **subscription model for chronic disease treatment**. Obesity and diabetes are lifelong conditions, and once patients start using GLP-1 drugs, weight regain after stopping is common. That means:

- **Very high repeat usage**: clinical data show that after stopping semaglutide, patients regain about two-thirds of the weight they lost within a year. This is not a one-time drug; it must be used continuously.
- **Huge market ceiling**: about 650 million adults worldwide have obesity, while GLP-1 penetration is still below 5%. This is a hundred-billion-dollar end market.
- **From "treating disease" to "improving quality of life"**: GLP-1 is no longer only about diabetes. It is expanding into weight loss and cardiovascular protection, so the user base stretches beyond patients into people pursuing healthier lifestyles.

## 2. User Stickiness Analysis

**Sources of stickiness:**

1. **Physiological dependence**: weight regain after stopping creates a natural incentive to continue
2. **Strong effect, high switching cost**: Wegovy can reduce weight by about 15-17%; once patients see the effect, they do not want to switch easily
3. **First-mover branding**: Ozempic and Wegovy are much more recognizable than competitors and have become synonymous with GLP-1 in consumers' minds
4. **Strategic importance of the oral form**: in January 2026 the FDA approved oral Wegovy (25mg once daily), the world's first oral GLP-1 weight-loss drug. The oral format lowers the usage barrier and could massively expand the patient pool. In Q1 2026, oral Wegovy generated DKK 2.26 billion (about USD 355 million) in revenue, far above analysts' expectation of DKK 1.16 billion

**Weaknesses in stickiness:**

- If a competitor such as Eli Lilly's tirzepatide has better efficacy and fewer side effects, patients can switch
- In head-to-head trials, Lilly's tirzepatide has been shown to be superior to semaglutide in both weight-loss effect and tolerability

## 3. Pricing Power Assessment

**Positive factors:**

- Demand for chronic disease drugs is rigid, and once insurance covers them patients become less price-sensitive
- Manufacturing GLP-1 drugs requires complex biopharmaceutical processes, so the barrier to entry is higher than for small-molecule drugs
- Semaglutide's core patent protection lasts until December 2031, and Novo has built a patent thicket with 320 U.S. patent applications

**Negative factors:**

- U.S. payer pricing pressure is rising, and Novo has proactively lowered prices to expand access (the oral Wegovy starting dose costs only USD 149 per month)
- Competition from compounding pharmacies: cheap compounded semaglutide once diverted demand at scale. Although the FDA tightened regulation in February 2026 and Novo pursued legal action, the threat has not fully disappeared
- Full-year 2026 guidance: sales and operating profit are expected to fall 4-12%, with pricing pressure as the main cause

## 4. Duan Yongping-Style Judgment

> "A good business model is doing the right thing and then doing the thing right."

**Doing the right thing:** The GLP-1 market is the biggest structural opportunity in biopharma over the last decade. As an early mover, Novo defined the category and built brand, channel, and manufacturing advantages. That is the "right thing."

**Doing the thing right:** This is where the debate is. Novo is facing strong competition from Lilly, and market share is being lost. In Q1 2026, Lilly's share of the U.S. GLP-1 market reached 60%. Novo's response - early oral-form launch, proactive price cuts, and layoffs / cost savings - is directionally correct, but execution still needs to be proven.

**Key consumer-side question:** GLP-1 at the moment looks more like a "category business" than a "brand business." Patients choose Wegovy or Zepbound more based on efficacy and price than on brand loyalty. That means Novo's moat is somewhat narrower than it looks on the surface.

**Business model score: 4/5**

The category is excellent, but within the category battle Novo is shifting from monopoly player to one half of a duopoly. The successful launch of oral Wegovy is an important positive signal, but Lilly's oral drug (orforglipron / Foundayo) is also coming soon.
