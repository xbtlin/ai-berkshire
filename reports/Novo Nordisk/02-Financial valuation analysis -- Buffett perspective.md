# Novo Nordisk (NVO) - Financial and Valuation Analysis

> Core question: why is a GLP-1 leader trading at only about 10x earnings, and what is the true valuation?

## 1. Key Financial Data (FY2025)

| Metric | FY2025 | YoY change |
|------|--------|----------|
| Total revenue | DKK 309.1 billion (about USD 45.9 billion) | +6% (+10% CER) |
| Operating profit | DKK 127.7 billion (about USD 18.9 billion) | +6% CER |
| Net income | DKK 102.4 billion (about USD 15.2 billion) | +1% |
| Net margin | 33.1% | Slightly down |
| Diluted EPS | DKK 23.03 | +2% |
| Free cash flow | DKK 28.3 billion | Improved sharply (FY2024 was -14.7 billion) |
| ROIC | ~21% | Remained at a high level |

**Obesity business:** revenue of DKK 82.3 billion, up 26% (+31% CER), and the main growth engine  
**Diabetes business:** revenue of DKK 207.1 billion, flat (+4% CER), now in a plateau phase

## 2. Why Is the P/E Only Around 10x?

This is the key question. Novo Nordisk's current TTM P/E is about 10-12x, versus a 10-year median of 24x. The reasons:

### 2.1 Immediate cause: a 2026 guidance shock

On 2026-02-04, Novo issued a 2026 full-year outlook that shocked the market: **sales and operating profit were expected to decline 5%-13% at constant exchange rates**. This was the first time in years the company guided to negative growth. The stock fell 17%-18% that day.

Main drivers:
- **U.S. price cuts**: to respond to compounded-drug competition and improve access, Novo cut prices aggressively
- **Compounded-drug pressure**: cheap compounded semaglutide diverted a large amount of demand
- **Lilly's competitive pressure**: Mounjaro and Zepbound had already reached 60% U.S. share
- **Patent expirations outside the U.S.**: patent protection ended in several markets

### 2.2 ADR context

NVO is an ADR (American depositary receipt) linked 1:1 to the Copenhagen-listed B shares after the September 2023 split. Since ADRs are quoted in U.S. dollars:
- Novo reports in Danish kroner, so when the krone weakens against the dollar, the ADR price faces extra pressure
- FY2025 revenue rose 6% in kroner, but the increase was smaller in dollar terms
- **FX is not the main reason for the low P/E**, but it did amplify the decline

### 2.3 Was there a one-time impairment?

There was no major one-time impairment in FY2025. Slower net-income growth (+1%) was mainly due to:
- Operating margin falling from prior highs to 41.3%
- Higher R&D spending in preparation for pipeline advancement
- FY2024 negative free cash flow was caused by large capital expenditures (capacity expansion), not an operating issue

### 2.4 Core conclusion: the market is pricing a "growth cliff"

The P/E compression from 24x to 10x means the market has effectively re-rated Novo from a "high-growth biotech" to a "mature pharma company with peaking growth." That pricing assumes:
- Revenue really does decline in 2026
- The competitive landscape permanently worsens
- Pricing power keeps eroding

## 3. Latest Q1 2026 Data - Is There a Turning Point?

| Metric | Q1 2026 |
|------|---------|
| Adjusted operating profit | DKK 32.9 billion |
| Ozempic revenue | ~DKK 28 billion (down 8% YoY, but above expectations) |
| Wegovy injection revenue | DKK 18.2 billion (+12% YoY) |
| Wegovy oral revenue | DKK 2.26 billion (far above the expected DKK 1.16 billion) |
| Full-year guidance | Improved from -5% to -13% to -4% to -12% |

**The outperformance of oral Wegovy is the biggest bright spot.** It launched only one quarter ago and already generated about 1.3 million prescriptions, with revenue nearly 2x analysts' expectations. This suggests:
1. Oral formulations can indeed open up new patient groups
2. The USD 149/month pricing strategy works
3. Novo's competitiveness has not collapsed the way the market priced it

## 4. True Valuation Assessment

**Current valuation parameters (mid-May 2026):**
- ADR share price: about USD 44-46
- Market cap: about USD 155-206 billion (slight differences across sources)
- TTM P/E: about 10-12x
- Forward P/E (based on 2026E earnings): likely in the 12-15x range

**Comparison set:**
- Lilly (LLY) current P/E: about 50-60x
- Global large-pharma average P/E: 15-20x
- Novo's own 10-year median P/E: 24x

**Buffett-style judgment:**

> "It's far better to buy an excellent company at a fair price than a mediocre company at a cheap price."

Novo Nordisk's financial quality is still excellent - 33% net margin, 21% ROIC, and a strong recovery in free cash flow. A 10x P/E means either the market is right (Novo's growth engine is permanently dead) or the market is too pessimistic (today's problems are temporary price wars and product-cycle transitions).

Based on Q1 2026's oral Wegovy performance, the second explanation looks more likely. If Novo can stabilize revenue and return to growth in 2027 (with CagriSema approval and the oral pipeline scaling), a P/E re-rating to 15-18x would be reasonable, implying 50%-80% upside.

**Financial valuation score: ★★★★☆ (4/5)**

Financial quality is top-tier, and the valuation is at an extreme historical low. The risks are whether 2026 revenue declines worse than guided and whether Lilly's competitive advantage keeps widening.
