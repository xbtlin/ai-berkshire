# Novo Nordisk (NVO) - Industry Competition Analysis

> Core question: in the GLP-1 duopoly, is Novo's moat deepening or being eroded?

## 1. Competitive Landscape Overview

### 1.1 The Duopoly: Novo Nordisk vs. Eli Lilly

The GLP-1 market has clearly become a duopoly, but the balance of power is shifting:

| Dimension | Novo Nordisk | Eli Lilly |
|------|-------------|-----------|
| Core molecule | Semaglutide (single-target GLP-1) | Tirzepatide (dual-target GLP-1/GIP) |
| Diabetes brands | Ozempic (injectable), Rybelsus (oral) | Mounjaro |
| Weight-loss brands | Wegovy (injectable + oral) | Zepbound |
| Q1 2026 U.S. GLP-1 share | ~40% | ~60% |
| Head-to-head clinical result | — | Tirzepatide was shown to be "superior" to semaglutide in efficacy and tolerability |
| Oral GLP-1 progress | **Already approved and launched** (oral Wegovy, January 2026) | orforglipron / Foundayo **just approved**, 20,000 prescriptions within 20 days of launch |
| Q1 2026 weight-loss drug revenue | Wegovy injection DKK 18.2 billion + oral DKK 2.26 billion | Zepbound USD 4.16 billion (+80%) |
| Q1 2026 diabetes drug revenue | Ozempic ~DKK 28 billion | Mounjaro USD 8.66 billion (+125%) |

**Key fact:** Lilly's Q1 2026 Mounjaro revenue jumped 125% YoY to USD 8.66 billion, and Zepbound grew 80% to USD 4.16 billion. By contrast, Novo's Ozempic revenue fell 8% YoY. The competitive balance has clearly tilted toward Lilly.

### 1.2 Second-Tier Competitors

- **Amgen**: MariTide (dual-target GLP-1/GIPR antagonist), once-monthly injection, in Phase III
- **Viking Therapeutics**: VK2735, both oral and injectable forms under development
- **Pfizer**: danuglipron (oral GLP-1), previously hit setbacks but still in development
- Smaller biotech companies such as **Structure Therapeutics** are also positioning themselves

## 2. Moat Analysis

### 2.1 Moats That Are Still Solid

**1. A century of diabetes expertise and brand strength**  
Novo Nordisk was founded in 1923 and has more than 100 years of diabetes-treatment history. Ozempic and Wegovy are unmatched in consumer brand recognition - "Ozempic" has effectively become synonymous with GLP-1.

**2. The world's largest GLP-1 production capacity**  
Biologics manufacturing barriers are very high. Novo has spent hundreds of billions of kroner over many years expanding capacity; FY2024 free cash flow was negative mainly because of this buildout. That is a hard asset moat that latecomers cannot quickly replicate.

**3. First-mover advantage in oral formulations**  
Oral Wegovy is the first approved oral GLP-1 weight-loss drug in the world. Its stronger-than-expected Q1 2026 performance (DKK 2.26 billion, about 1.3 million prescriptions) proves the value of first-mover advantage. Lilly's Foundayo has also been approved, but Novo still has a lead of several months in oral GLP-1.

**4. Patent thicket**  
The patent network includes 320 U.S. patent applications and 154 granted patents, with core compound protection running until December 2031.

### 2.2 Moats That Are Being Eroded

**1. Clinical efficacy is no longer leading**  
This is the biggest concern. Lilly's tirzepatide (dual-target mechanism) has been shown to be "superior" to semaglutide in head-to-head trials. In an efficacy-driven market, being behind on product performance is dangerous.

**2. U.S. share continues to slip**  
Lilly's U.S. GLP-1 share rose from about 45% in 2024 to 60% in Q1 2026, and the trend is still ongoing. More concerning, Lilly has already surpassed Novo outside the U.S. as well.

**3. The compounded-drug threat**  
Cheap compounded semaglutide has been met with tighter FDA enforcement and Novo lawsuits, but the demand for "affordable GLP-1s" has already been validated. Novo was forced to launch oral Wegovy at a USD 149/month starting dose, materially weakening pricing power.

**4. A worse pricing environment**  
The main reason for the 2026 guidance cut is U.S. price pressure. When a pharma company lowers prices on its own initiative, it is a sign that competitive pressure has reached the income statement.

## 3. Competition in the Next-Generation Pipeline

| Product | Company | Mechanism | Stage | Expected weight-loss effect |
|------|------|------|------|-------------|
| CagriSema | Novo | GLP-1 + amylin | NDA submitted to the FDA, expected approval in Q4 2026 | ~15.7% (in diabetes patients) |
| Amycretin / Zenagamtide | Novo | GLP-1 + amylin dual agonist | Phase III started (Q1 2026) | Early data show >20% weight loss |
| Retatrutide | Lilly | Triple agonist GLP-1 / GIP / glucagon | Phase III ongoing | Early data show ~24% weight loss |
| Foundayo (orforglipron) | Lilly | Oral GLP-1 | **Already approved and launched** | ~7%-10% (oral) |

**Key judgment:** whether Novo's next-generation pipeline (CagriSema + Amycretin) can close the efficacy gap with Lilly is the key variable in the long-term competitive landscape. CagriSema's 15.7% weight-loss result in diabetes patients is not especially impressive, but Amycretin's early data are more encouraging.

## 4. Munger-Style Core Judgment

> "The essence of investing is calculating probabilities and payoffs. You do not need to understand everything - only the key variables inside your circle of competence."

**Structural view of the GLP-1 market:**

This is a market that is large enough (expected to exceed USD 150 billion by 2030) and growing fast enough. Even if Novo's share falls from 60% to 35%-40%, absolute revenue can still grow. **The key question is not who wins the share war, but whether the market is big enough to support two winners.**

**Moat assessment:**

Novo's moat is moving from "first-mover monopolist" to "one of two duopolists." That does not mean the moat is gone; it means the nature of the moat is changing - from exclusive dominance to a combined competition based on scale, brand, and pipeline. In a duopoly, both companies can still earn excellent returns (think Coca-Cola and Pepsi).

**Biggest competitive risk:** it is not Lilly pushing Novo out of the market (that will not happen), but a systemic decline in pricing power. If GLP-1 becomes a must-cover insurance category, final pricing may be set by payers rather than by the drug companies.

**Industry competition score: ★★★☆☆ (3/5)**

This is a top-tier industry, but Novo is losing the share war. As the market moves from monopoly to duopoly, the valuation must be reset. The good news is the market is big enough; the bad news is that Lilly really does have the stronger product.
