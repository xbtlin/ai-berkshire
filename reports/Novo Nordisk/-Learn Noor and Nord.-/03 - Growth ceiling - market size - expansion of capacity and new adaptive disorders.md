# Why did Goldman Sachs turn down?

The logic chain of Goldman Sachs is clear:

** The patient's detoxification rate is higher than expected** — the annual rate of detoxification of GLP-1 diet drugs is about **40-50%** (source: multiple tracking studies), well above the rate of diabetes mellitus
** Self-funded patients are price sensitive** - Even if the price is reduced to $149 per month (oral Wegzy), a large number of people are unable or unwilling to pay for the long term
** Insurance coverage is moving slower than expected** — although Medicare has begun to cover, implementation takes time to land; approximately 40% of commercial insurance still does not cover dietary drugs
** The peak of the United States market was revised from $950 billion to $70 billion** and the international market was moved from $35 billion to $50 billion — overall net downwards

# Why is Morgan Stanley up?

Morgan Stanley saw different drivers:

** The emergence of oral preparations is a game changer** - the "fear of needles" group finally has access to the market and potential patient pools have expanded considerably.
** Medicare coverage is structural benefits** - 67 million Medicare beneficiaries in the United States, large numbers of obese people will be eligible for GLP-1 prescriptions, with patients paying at a maximum of only 50 months
** Rapid start in international markets** — The prevalence of obesity in large population countries such as Brazil and China continues to rise, and i.e., generic drugs can push down prices but also increase the population using drugs
** New adaptive growth** - New adaptive conditions such as cardiovascular, fat liver, kidney protection opened up the market space for GLP-1 "outside of weight loss"

** Combined logic:** Goldman Sachs’ prudential and Morgan Stanley’s optimism. Under neutral assumptions, **2030, the global GLP-1 market size is a reasonable range of $80-12 billion. Even if the floor was set at $80 billion, it would still be 1-1.5 times the global GLP-1 income (about $80 billion, with a courtesy) -- growth space does exist, but not "infinity expansion".

** Counterpart:** ** All these projections are based on the assumption that the long-term safety of the GLP-1 drug will not pose a major problem. A study in 2026 suggested that the link between GLP-1 and certain digestive diseases is of concern (source: ScienceDaily, May 2026). All projections will be overtaken by the risk of safety at the grade level of the product.
