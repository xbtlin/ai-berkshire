# One, GLP-1, what kind of business is it?

You stopped Wegboy, bounced two thirds of your body weight in a year.

This clinical fact determines what a business GLP-1 is.

The business model of the GLP-1 drug is essentially ** subscription to chronic diseases**. It is not "repair or go," but "stopping is giving up." This explains why the ceiling of the business can be so high, and why capital markets used to value it far beyond traditional pharmaceuticals.

# Why "subscription" #

The commercial logic of traditional drugs is "repair and go" -- antibiotics take a course and the surgery is done and they're out. But the GLP-1 diets are completely different:

** The rebound of weight after dedose is common.** Clinical data show that after decommissioning Smégroux patients recover approximately **2/3** weight reduction in one year (source: STEP 1 test extension data).

- ** Repurchase rate is very high**: not "one time, just one time," but it needs to be used on a continuous basis.
- ** Life cycle is of great value**: a patient may continue to use drugs for 5 years, 10 years or more
- **The cost of loss of customers is extremely high**: Drug cessation = body weight rebound = physical and psychological double impact

In the language of the Internet, this is a subscription model that ** has a very high retention rate, very high LTV (life-long value of clients), and a natural negative loss**.

** Counterpart:** "Subscription" presupposes that patients will be able to pay on a continuous basis. The GLP-1 drug price (the injection version Weghy price at $1,349/month), the United States, approximately 40% **40% of commercial insurance and most federal health insurance (until recently) does not cover the reduced use of drugs. If the payout end is not closed, "subscription" will be interrupted by economic pressure. However, the 2026 change is positive - the US Federal Health Insurance (MEDICAre) has begun to cover the GLP-1 diet, with patients paying the maximum on their own only **50/month** (source: NBC News, January 2026).

# Two, Nord and Nord's moat

A good business requires good barriers to competition. The moats of Noor and Nord in the GLP-1 market can be divided into four layers, but each level faces varying degrees of erosion.

# First level: branding awareness (intensity: <unk> )

The name "OZEPic" has become the adage of GLP-1 -- like "100 degrees" for searching, "dropping" for Internet car.

- Wegboy's share of the new prescription** for United States heavy medication** reached **65%** (source: CNBC, May 2026)

** But brand recognition is not like brand loyalty.** In the prescription drug market, patients’ choices depend more on doctor referrals, insurance coverage and treatment effectiveness than on brand preferences. If the gifted product is more effective, less expensive, easier to use -- patients change.

# Second layer: energy barriers (intensity: <unk> )

Biopharmaceuticals are produced and small molecular chemicals are completely different. Smégrum is a multipolymer molecule that requires complex biological engineering and rigorous quality control.

Non and Nord have invested continuously over the years in the expansion of capacity by tens of billions of kroner - **FY2024 Free cash flows are negative because of the expansion** and FY2025 has recovered to 28.3 billion kroner.

This means that even if someone gets a copy of the drug, it is difficult to build enough capacity to meet market demand in the short term. It is a "hard-guard river" – money can fix it, but it takes time.

** The counter-view:** The production barriers are real, but not permanent. # Three, one core question: is this "goods business" or "brand business"?

This distinction is crucial, as it determines the true depth of the moat.

** Characteristics of brand business:** Consumers pay premiums for brands themselves - Mae, Aise, Coca-Cola. Even with cheaper alternatives, consumers are reluctant to change.

** Characteristics of the trade in goods:** Consumers buy this type of product (fat reduction pills) rather than a specific brand. Whoever is therapeuticly effective, cheap, easy to use, buys.

** GLP-1 is now more of a product business.** The evidence is as follows:

1. When the court-in-charge delbot proved in the head-to-head test that "better than" Smegrum, a large number of patients moved from Ozempic to Mounjaro - suggesting that the therapeutic power-driven choice was limited in brand loyalty.
The cheap copying of the Migropus' demand for a large-scale diversion - which indicates high price sensitivity - of the polypharmacists
Insurance companies tend to cover cheaper options - that is, the payer does not recognize brand names either.

** What does this mean for Nord and Nord?** If GLP-1 is a product business, the moat comes mainly from therapeutic efficiency, price and convenience -- not brand itself. In these three dimensions, Nord and Nord are now leading only "brand perception," and the healing and convenience are being trodden and even overtaken by courtesy.

** Inverse:** Even in the case of the good business, the two oligarchs of Coca-Cola vs Pepsi can make a lot of money for both. The GLP-1 market is big enough for Noor and Nord to grow without a monopoly – just 35-40%.

# Four, $149/month pricing — smart strategy or self-destructive profit?

The price of the oral Wegboy initial dose of $149 per month was one of the most interesting price decisions to be discussed in 2026.

** Logic of viewing:**
- Low prices allow rapid access to customers, first by establishing drug habits and then by upgrading the dosage (higher dosage prices) to raise the income of single customers
- Reach the pay end (insurance coverage + self-paying), significantly expanding the patient pool
- Foundayo, a market mind for oral GLP-1

** Logical of the viewer:**
- Injection version: Wegboy priced $1,349, oral, $149 per month -- if oral injection replaced injection, that's a significant reduction in income.
- The polite Foundayo is also launched with a similar low-cost strategy (business guarantees are low to $25/month), and the low price is no longer different.
- Proactive price reduction is an irreversible signal - once the price is given, it's hard to take back.

Nord and Nord also announced that, as of January 2027, the US bid for the injection versions Weghy and Ozempic would be reduced to $675/month - a 50 per cent drop** (source: Fox Business, February 2026).

This means that Nord and Nord are making a proactive choice: ** to exchange prices**. The success of this strategy will be seen in the 2027-2028 financial statements.
