# Four, like the valley of history

Investors like to look for historical analogies. No and Nord are currently in a situation that compares two cases:

# # Similarity I: Coca-Cola 1999 (companion of profit)

In 1999, Coca-Cola faced a slowdown, a change of management, a rise in Pepsi-Cola competition, and PE fell from a peak of 50 to more than 20 times. The market thought the growth engine was out.

Outcome: Coca-Cola does not decline. It adapts to the new competitive pattern and continues to make profits in the oligarchic structure. PE returns to 25-30 times the level.

**The similarities between Nord and Nord:** Centuries, from monopoly to oligarchy, management change, PE significantly compressed.

# # Similarity 2: 2023 Pfizer (Littlespace analogy)

In 2023, Pfizer was pushed below 10 times the threshold of the new crown vaccine/drug income cut off. The market considered it "the normal return after the one-time income disappeared."

And it turns out that Pfizer's PE is still depressed because it really doesn't find the growth engine after the new crown.

** The similarities between Nord and Nord:** PEs are down 10 times closer, and core products are dropping sharply.

** Key difference:** Pfizer's new crown income is "unsustainable after a single outbreak." Nord and Nord's GLP-1 income is "sustained income from a chronic chronic disease market" - penetration is only 6%, far from saturated.** These are two completely different quality of income.**

No and Nord are more Coca-Cola than Pfizer in terms of income continuity and predictability. But cashing depends on the actual direction of the pipeline and competition.
