# A two-wheel drive of consulting and outsourcing

> "Understanding Accenture" Series · Part 02 · Business Model
> Reading time is about 10 minutes

---

## The net profit margin is only 11%, why can the ROIC reach 27%?

The net profit margin is 11%, which seems mediocre among technology companies. But that same year, Accenture's ROIC was 27%, free cash flow was $10.9 billion, and capital expenditures were less than 1% of revenue. These three figures appear in one company at the same time, indicating that the nature of this business is seriously underestimated by the net profit margin.

**Accenture’s money-making logic, in one sentence:**

> Global large companies don’t know how to use new technologies → Pay Accenture to come up with solutions (consulting) → Spend more money to ask Accenture to help with implementation and operation and maintenance (hosting) → The more in-depth the system becomes, the more inseparable it becomes → The next round of new technologies is here, please ask again.

This is a flywheel of "consulting to solicit customers and outsourcing to lock customers". The consulting business is like the key that opens the door, and the hosting business is like the long-term lease behind the door.

## Two-wheel drive: How to cooperate with two income lines

| Line of business | Share of revenue | Contract characteristics | Profit characteristics | Core role |
|--------|--------|---------|---------|---------|
| Consulting services | **About 55%** | By project, 6-18 months | Gross profit is high but volatile | Customer acquisition + demonstration capabilities |
| Custody service | **About 45%** | Long-term contract, 3-7 years | Stable and predictable gross profit | Locked customers + cornerstone of cash flow |

(Data source: Accenture FY2025 Annual Report)

**Consulting is like a forward, hosting is like a defence. ** Goals from the forwards attract attention, but what really makes the team win the league is the stability of the defense.

The data for Q2 FY2026 speaks for itself: consulting revenue increased **7%** year-on-year, and managed services revenue increased **10%** year-on-year. The faster growth rate of custody shows that customers are "renewing contracts + expanding cooperation" rather than just buying and selling.

(Data source: Accenture Q2 FY2026 financial report)

## Why are customers stuck?

The customer locking mechanism of IT consulting has four layers, one layer deeper than the other:

### First level: System integration locking

When Accenture helps a bank deploy a SAP system or migrate the entire IT architecture to AWS - this set of things is deep in the bones. Changing suppliers is not about "changing someone to do it", but "tearing down the already built house and rebuilding it."

**How high are the switching costs? ** A large ERP implementation project typically costs hundreds of millions of dollars and takes 2-3 years. Substitute someone midway? Almost impossible.

### Second level: Personnel embedded locking

Accenture consultants stay in client offices for a long time and understand the client's business processes, system architecture, and internal politics. Over time, these consultants become "people who know the system better than the client's own IT department." You can change Accenture's contract, but you can't change the experience in those people's heads.

### The third level: Contract cycle locking

Managed service contracts are typically **3-7 years**, with higher renewal rates at maturity. This means Accenture has 1-2 years of revenue visibility even if the market moves wildly. Q2 FY2026's new contracts reached a record of **22.1 billion US dollars**, of which **41 customers** signed more than 100 million US dollars in a single quarter - large customers are increasing their investment, not retreating.

(Data source: Accenture Q2 FY2026 financial report)

### The fourth layer: Technology ecological lock-in

Accenture is a top partner for all major platforms including Microsoft, AWS, Google, SAP, and Salesforce. It is essentially the "translation layer" between customers and the technology ecosystem.

CIOs of large enterprises are faced with five cloud platforms, three AI frameworks, and more than a dozen SaaS products - they cannot handle it themselves, and they dare not bet on the wrong technical route. The value of Accenture is: "You don't have to choose. I will help you choose and implement it. If there is any problem, I will take care of it."

**Contrary view: This lock-in is not absolute. ** Every time a new project is tendered, customers have the right to choose. Price wars have always existed, especially with Indian companies using low costs to hit the high-end market. The lock is "medium strength", not "iron wall".

## Financial Portrait: A company that "looks like it doesn't make money" but is actually very profitable.

At first glance at Accenture’s profit margins, you might think this business is average:

| Indicators | FY2025 | Judgment |
|------|--------|------|
| Revenue | **69.7 billion US dollars** | No. 1 in the global IT services industry |
| GAAP Operating Margin | **14.7%** | Not high |
| Net profit margin | **About 11%** | Looks mediocre |
| ROIC | **Approximately 27%** | Well above cost of capital |
| Free Cash Flow | **$10.9 billion** | FCF/Net Profit > 130% |
| Capital expenditures | **Approximately $600 million** | Less than 1% of revenue |

(Data source: Accenture FY2025 Annual Report)

**Core findings: The net profit margin of 11% does not seem high, but the ROIC of 27% is extremely excellent. How do these two numbers coexist? **

The answer lies in **asset-light**. Accenture does not need to build factories, buy equipment, or stockpile inventory. Capital expenditures were only $600 million (less than 1% of revenue), so while it only had 11 dollars in profit for every 100 dollars it made, it needed to invest almost no more capital to stay afloat.

The result: **$10.9 billion in free cash flow, FCF/net profit of over 130%** - and every dollar earned is "real money," not paper profit eaten up by depreciation and capex.

A more intuitive comparison:

| Company | Net Margin | ROIC | CapEx to Revenue |
|------|--------|------|--------------|
| Accenture | 11% | 27% | <1% |
| TSMC | 41% | 28% | ~35% |
| Amazon | 8% | 18% | ~12% |

TSMC's net profit margin is nearly 4 times higher than Accenture's, but its capital expenditures are also more than 35 times higher. In terms of ROIC, the two are about the same. **Net interest rate is the appearance, ROIC is the essence. **

## Capital allocation: How to spend the money you earn?

Total FY2025 shareholder returns were approximately $8.3 billion**, accounting for **72%** of operating cash flow:

| Project | Amount |
|------|------|
| Share buybacks | **$4.62 billion** |
| Dividends | **Approximately $3.7 billion** |
| Total rewards | **Approximately $8.3 billion** |

(Data source: Accenture FY2025 Annual Report)

In addition, **more than $3 billion** is invested annually in acquisitions to complement AI, cloud computing and industry vertical capabilities.

- Quarterly dividend **$1.63/share**, increased by 15% in FY2025 and another 10% in FY2026
- Increase dividends for **more than 20 consecutive years**
- The current dividend payout rate is approximately **54%**, which is safe and sustainable
- The current dividend yield is about **3.6%**, which is at the highest range in history

**This is a company that is very disciplined in capital allocation: three-pronged approach: buybacks, dividends, and strategic acquisitions. ** The money earned was not spent on cosmetic projects, and most of it was returned to shareholders or used for capacity building. 72% of operating cash flow is distributed to shareholders, among the highest among large technology services companies.

## AI transformation: not a slogan, but real income

This may be the most noteworthy change in Accenture currently.

| AI Indicators | FY2024 | FY2025 | Changes |
|---------|--------|--------|------|
| GenAI orders | Approximately $3 billion | **$5.9 billion** | **Doubled** |
| GenAI Revenue | Approximately $900 million | **$2.7 billion** | **3x growth** |
| Number of people trained in AI | — | **550,000 people** | The largest in the global consulting industry |

(Data source: Accenture FY2025 annual report, Q1 FY2026 earnings conference call)

By Q1 FY2026, the company said AI has penetrated **80% of large deals** and no longer discloses AI orders separately - as AI is no longer a stand-alone business line but integrated into almost all projects.

**What does it mean for 550,000 people to complete AI training? ** From 30 people when ChatGPT launches in November 2022, to 550,000 people by the end of FY2025. This speed of execution is itself a competitive barrier—no other company has completed a talent transformation of this scale in this time window.

The organizational restructuring led by CEO Julie Sweet is rare: overturning the company’s 50-year-old organizational structure, linking employee promotions to AI capabilities, and establishing in-depth cooperation with Anthropic (Claude) to deploy autonomous agents (Agentic AI).

**Contrary view: AI orders are growing rapidly, but still account for a small proportion of total revenue (about 4%). If AI tools eventually allow clients to eliminate the need for so many advisors, today’s AI order growth may just be a return to form. **

This is a legitimate concern and will be discussed in the third article.

**Contrary view: North America accounts for 48%. Once the U.S. economic recession or policy risks (DOGE) continue to ferment, the impact will be huge. The federal business, which accounts for about 16% of Americas revenue, is shrinking. **

## Core conclusion: What kind of money-making machine is this?

If you define Accenture’s business model in one sentence:

> **A global cash flow machine with light assets, high ROIC, and running on the flywheel of "consulting customers + managed customer locking" is currently turning AI from a threat into a new growth engine. **

**The advantages are clear**: high scale barriers, strong customer stickiness, excellent cash flow, disciplined capital allocation, and leading AI transformation.

**The disadvantages are also very clear**: It is essentially "selling people", the net profit rate ceiling is limited, and AI may erode this model in the long term rather than strengthen it.

This is not a "make money with your eyes closed" business like Coca-Cola, but it is the best business of its kind.
