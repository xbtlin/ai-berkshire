# Accenture Business Model Analysis

> Analysis framework: business essence, user value, competitive moat, and whether management is "doing the right thing"
> Data cutoff: May 2026 | Latest report: Q2 FY2026 (ended February 2026)

---

## 1. What Kind of Business Is This?

Accenture is the world's largest IT consulting and outsourcing company, with FY2025 revenue of USD 69.7 billion and about 786,000 employees, serving 120+ countries worldwide. The business has two major parts:

- **Consulting services (about 55%)**: helping enterprises with digital transformation, cloud migration, AI deployment, and similar work, charged by project
- **Managed services (about 45%)**: long-term outsourcing of operations, charged by contract term, with stronger stickiness

The essence of the business is **selling a mix of brainpower and labor**. Consulting sells plans and judgment; managed services sell execution and scalable delivery.

## 2. User Value: Why Do Customers Choose Accenture?

The core customers are Fortune 500 companies and large government agencies. Customers choose Accenture rather than building in-house teams or using smaller firms for a few clear reasons:

1. **Scaled delivery**: a team of 786,000 can mobilize global resources quickly, allowing large projects to proceed in multiple countries at the same time
2. **Full-stack capability**: from strategy consulting to systems integration to post-launch operations, one vendor can do it all, reducing multi-vendor coordination costs
3. **Partner network**: deep ties with Microsoft, AWS, Google, SAP, Salesforce, and all other major tech vendors make Accenture a "translation layer" between clients and the tech ecosystem
4. **Brand trust**: a CIO at a large company can choose Accenture without being challenged by their boss - it is the "safe choice"

In Q2 FY2026, new bookings were USD 22.1 billion, and 32 clients signed contracts above USD 100 million in the quarter, showing that large customers are still increasing spend.

## 3. Customer Lock-In

IT consulting has medium-to-high customer lock-in:

- **System integration projects**: once Accenture deploys ERP or cloud architecture, switching vendors means starting over, so switching costs are high
- **Managed services contracts**: usually 3-7 year contracts with relatively high renewal rates
- **Embedded personnel**: Accenture consultants often sit at customer sites for long periods, forming "quasi-employee" relationships
- **Knowledge accumulation**: years of joint work create industry knowledge and system understanding that cannot be copied easily

But this is not like Coca-Cola's consumer mindshare lock-in. Customers still choose at every new RFP, so pricing pressure is always present.

## 4. Is AI Incremental or Substitutive?

This is the most important question right now. AI affects Accenture in two ways:

**Positive / incremental side:**
- FY2025 full-year GenAI bookings were USD 5.9 billion, doubling year over year; Q1 FY2026 reached USD 2.2 billion (up 76% YoY)
- GenAI revenue in FY2025 reached USD 2.7 billion, up 3x
- Every large enterprise needs help with AI transformation, creating a huge wave of consulting demand
- CEO Julie Sweet has already trained 550,000 employees on GenAI

**Threat / substitutive side:**
- AI coding tools such as Cursor and GitHub Copilot may let customers finish development with fewer people, reducing outsourcing demand
- In the long run, agentic AI could replace parts of low-end consulting and operations work
- The consulting model is fundamentally a "sell-headcount" model; if AI makes headcount less valuable, pricing will compress

**Short term, AI is a net positive** - customers do not know how to use AI and need Accenture's help. But in 5-10 years, if AI tools become mature enough, some consulting demand may be automated. That is a "sweet first, bitter later" structure.

## 5. Is Accenture "Doing the Right Thing"?

Using Duan Yongping's framework - **do the right thing, then do the thing right**:

**What it has done right:**
- Heavy early AI investment, with large GenAI capability building already underway in FY2024
- More than USD 3 billion of annual acquisitions to keep adding capabilities
- 550,000 employees trained on AI, which is exceptional execution for a global consulting firm
- Deep partnership with Anthropic (Claude), positioning for agentic AI

**What to watch carefully:**
- Federal government business is being hit by DOGE-related cuts, and FY2026 may see a 1-1.5 percentage point drag on revenue
- The core contradiction of a "sell-headcount" business - the better AI gets, the fewer people each deal needs
- Organizational inertia in large consultancies and whether Accenture can truly become "AI-native"

## 6. Core Conclusion

Accenture is a **good but not top-tier business**. Its strengths are high-scale barriers, strong customer stickiness, and excellent cash flow. Its weaknesses are that it is still fundamentally labor-intensive, its profit margin ceiling is limited (net margin about 11%), and it faces long-term AI substitution risk.

In Duan Yongping's words: "This business can be done, but it is not the kind of business where you can make money without thinking." Management is doing the right things, but the business model itself is not as simple as a consumer brand or a platform company.
