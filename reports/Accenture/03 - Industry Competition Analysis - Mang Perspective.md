# Accenture (ACN) Industry Competitive Analysis

> Analysis framework: industry ecological niche, competitive advantages and disadvantages, moat type and width
> Data as of: May 2026

---

## 1. Industry panorama: What kind of battlefield is IT consulting?

The global IT consulting and services market is approximately US$70 billion (narrow consulting), and the broad IT services market exceeds US$1.2 trillion. The industry growth rate is about 6-7% (CAGR), and the demand for AI transformation is the largest increase currently.

Characteristics of this industry:

1. **Highly fragmented**: Even the largest, Accenture, only accounts for about 12% of global corporate consulting contracts
2. **Medium entry barriers**: Technical barriers are not high, but scale barriers, brand barriers and customer relationship barriers are high.
3. **Fierce price competition**: Especially in the field of offshore outsourcing, Indian companies are impacting the high-end market with low costs
4. **Medium Cyclical**: Enterprise IT spending will be delayed but not eliminated in an economic downturn

## 2. Comparison of main competitors

### Comparison with the Big Four consulting firms (Deloitte/PwC/EY/KPMG)

| Dimensions | Accenture | Deloitte |
|------|-----------|----------|
| IT Services Revenue | ~$70 billion | ~$30 billion (IT segment) |
| Core Strengths | Technology Implementation + Operation and Maintenance | Audit + Taxation + Consulting |
| Employee size | 786,000 | 450,000+ |
| Listing Status | NYSE Listed | Private Partnership |
| AI investment | GenAI orders $5.9 billion | Undisclosed detailed data |

Deloitte is stronger in strategy consulting and auditing, but not as strong as Accenture in large-scale technology implementation. Accenture's advantage is "end-to-end" - from strategy to implementation to operation and maintenance, one-stop service. Deloitte accounts for about 10% of global IT consulting.

### Comparison with Indian IT giants (TCS/Infosys/Cognizant)

| Dimensions | Accenture | TCS | Infosys | Cognizant |
|------|-----------|-----|---------|-----------|
| Revenue | $69.7 billion | ~$30 billion | ~$19 billion | ~$20 billion |
| Net profit margin | 11% | ~20% | ~17% | ~12% |
| Per capita income | ~$89,000 | ~$50,000 | ~$55,000 | ~$65,000 |
| Positioning | High-end full stack | Large-scale delivery | Digital transformation | US market |
| GenAI orders | $5.9 billion | Undisclosed | Hundreds of millions | Undisclosed |

Key findings:
- **TCS has surpassed Accenture** in market capitalization to become the world’s largest IT services company (by market capitalization), something unimaginable just two years ago
- Indian companies have much higher net profit margins than Accenture as labor costs are structurally 30-50% lower
- But Accenture’s per capita income is higher, indicating that employees create greater value
- In the field of GenAI, Accenture has a clear first-mover advantage - $5.9 billion in orders is the largest number in the entire industry

### Comparison with management consulting firms (McKinsey/BCG/Bain)

These three companies are higher-end than Accenture at the strategic level, but are far inferior to Accenture in terms of technology implementation and large-scale delivery. In the AI ​​era, they are also increasing their technical capabilities, but the size gap is huge. Does not pose a core competitive threat.

## 3. Moat analysis

### Moat 1: Economies of Scale ★★★★☆

- A global delivery network of 786,000 people that no new entrant can replicate in the short term
- Procurement bargaining power, global delivery flexibility and multi-industry expertise brought by scale
- But scale also brings management complexity and organizational inertia

### Moat 2: Customer switching cost ★★★★☆

- System integration projects have natural customer lock-in: you won’t change suppliers in the middle of SAP implementation
- Hosting service contract for 3-7 years, forming a stable income base
-Knowledge lock-in and interpersonal relationship lock-in formed by consultant’s presence
- But each new project is a "re-bid" and the lock-in is not absolute

### Moat Three: Brand and Trust ★★★★☆

- Brand value of US$41.5 billion, No. 1 global IT service brand
- The psychological safety of CIOs who "choose Accenture and will not be fired"
- Deep partnerships with all major technology platforms

### Moat 4: Intangible assets (methodology and tools) ★★★☆☆

- Own platforms myWizard, SynOps, etc. to improve delivery efficiency
- Accumulation of industry-specific solutions and methodologies
- But these do not have the same legal protection as patents, and competitors can imitate them

### Overall rating of the moat: medium to wide (3.5/5)

Accenture has real competitive advantages, but not the "almost impossible to disrupt" moat like Coca-Cola. Advances in AI may simultaneously widen and erode moats—widening because demand for AI transformation surges; eroding because AI tools may allow smaller competitors to approach the delivery capabilities of larger companies.

## 4. Key changes in the competitive landscape

### "Reshuffling" in the AI Era

The traditional competitive landscape is being reshaped by AI:

1. **Accenture temporarily leads in AI consulting field**: $5.9 billion in GenAI orders far exceeds the number publicly disclosed by competitors
2. **Indian IT companies are catching up**: TCS and Infosys are also investing heavily in AI, but the scale of orders lags behind
3. **New types of competitors emerge**: AI-native companies (such as Palantir) compete for food in specific vertical areas
4. **Direct competition among technology giants**: Microsoft and Google reduce customers’ reliance on third-party consulting through their own AI services

### DOGE Blast Wave

Cuts to U.S. federal business are hitting not only Accenture but all government IT vendors including Deloitte and IBM. But Accenture's federal business (~8% of global revenue) makes it more vulnerable. Defense Department terminates $5.1 billion in IT contracts and GSA asks agencies to review consulting contracts.

## 5. Core Conclusions

Let’s look at Munger’s multiple thinking model:

**Ecological niche**: Accenture occupies the most favorable ecological niche in the IT service industry - a high-end full-stack service provider. But "most advantageous" does not mean "irreplaceable".

**Competitive Updates**: No competitor will take Accenture's position anytime soon. But AI is changing the rules of competition - in the past, we relied on "more people" to win, but in the future, we may rely on "good use of AI" to win. Accenture has a temporary lead in this transformation, but the game is far from over.

**The biggest risk**: Not any specific competitor, but the technology itself - if AI tools significantly lower the threshold for companies to build their own capabilities, the value proposition of the entire consulting industry will be redefined.

Munger would say: "This is a good business, but not a great business. A good business can still make money in headwinds, and a great business can widen its moat in headwinds. Whether Accenture can widen its moat in the AI wave is still unclear."
