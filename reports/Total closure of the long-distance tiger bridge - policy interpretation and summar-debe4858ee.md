# The stock accounts of 3.37 million people in Futu can only be sold but not bought

*May 22, 2026*

---

**If you have an account in Futu, Tiger or Changqiao, you must understand today’s news: from now on, your account can only sell, not buy, and the App will be completely shut down in 2 years. **

The following uses the shortest possible length to clearly explain the policy and the impact.

---

## 1. What is banned?

On May 22, the China Securities Regulatory Commission officially announced: ** Futu Securities, Tiger Brokers, and Changqiao Securities have illegally expanded their businesses across borders and plan to confiscate all illegal gains and impose severe penalties. **

On the same day, with the approval of the State Council, the China Securities Regulatory Commission, together with eight departments including the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology, the Ministry of Public Security, and the Central Bank, jointly issued the "Implementation Plan for Comprehensive Rectification of Illegal Cross-Border Securities and Futures Fund Business Activities."

The core content is translated into vernacular:

| Stages | Time | What You Can Do |
|------|------|-----------|
| Centralized rectification period | 2 years from now | **Only selling + transferring funds**, buying and transferring in are prohibited |
| After the expiration of the rectification period | 2 years later | All domestic apps, websites, and servers will be shut down** |

The targets of the rectification are not only these three securities firms, but also the domestic affiliates, illegal intermediaries, and even self-media that help them attract customers. **It's not a point, it's a line. **

One important exception: the scheme** does not restrict foreign institutions from providing services** to investors located abroad. In other words, if you are overseas, it will not be affected.

---

## 2. Is your money safe?

The original text of the plan clearly stated that "the property safety of investors will not be affected by the renovation." There are three layers of logic behind this sentence:

**First, the asset custody structure. ** The Hong Kong stocks you buy on Futu are actually hosted in the Central Settlement and Settlement System (CCASS) of the Hong Kong Stock Exchange; the US stocks you buy are hosted in the Depository Trust Company (DTCC) in the United States. **The stocks are not on Futu’s server, and the shutdown of the App will not affect the existence of the stocks. **

**Second, investor protection mechanism. ** Futu is a member of SIPC in the United States, and each customer's securities and cash are protected up to US$500,000. Customer funds and company funds are stored separately. Even if something goes wrong with Futu, your assets will not be included in bankruptcy liquidation.

**Third, the plan designs an exit channel. ** The two-year transition period, requiring overseas institutions to communicate with investors and make arrangements for account disposal - these all show that decision-makers have considered the impact and made buffers.

**So "shutdown" closes the trading channel, not your account and assets. **

> It should be noted: Futu’s own operating conditions are currently good (net profit in 2025 increased by 101.9% year-on-year, customer assets were HK$1.23 trillion, according to Futu’s 2025 annual report), and there is no risk of company bankruptcy in the short term.

---

## 3. Why is it true this time?

Some people will say, "Didn't you shout before?" This time is indeed different:

- **2021**: The China Securities Regulatory Commission separately expressed "concern" and the wording was mild → Result: Only new account openings were stopped
- **End of 2022**: China Securities Regulatory Commission promotes rectification, "containing increases and resolving existing problems" → Result: Existing users continue to use their usual methods
- **May 2026 (today)**: **The State Council agreed + Eight ministries and commissions jointly issued a document + Confiscation of illegal income + 2-year comprehensive banning timetable**

The specifications were upgraded from "speech" to "combat plan". Eight departments cover network blocking (Network Information Office), fund interception (Central Bank, Foreign Exchange Bureau), law enforcement crackdown (Ministry of Public Security) - the entire chain is arranged.

**When supervision starts to design an exit channel for you, it means that the door is indeed about to close. **

---

## 4. Impact on Chinese Concept Stocks

Let’s look at the data first: Futu has 3.37 million asset customers and customer assets of HK$1.23 trillion (according to the 2025 annual report). Together with Tiger and Changqiao, the total number of users affected by the three companies is in the order of millions, and the capital scale exceeds one trillion Hong Kong dollars. The most concentrated holdings of these users are Chinese concept stocks.

When these accounts are restricted to "sell only", a **one-way, continuous selling pressure** is formed. But the impact is not uniform:

### Dual-listed large-capitalization Chinese concept stocks (Alibaba, Tencent, JD.com, etc.)

It is listed on both the US and Hong Kong stocks. Investors who are forced to sell U.S. stocks can re-buy Hong Kong stocks through Southbound Trading. **The US stock market is under pressure, but the Hong Kong stock market may receive capital inflows**, and there is a mechanism to take over.

### Chinese concept stocks that are only listed on the US stock market (Pinduoduo, etc.) - have the biggest impact

**Pinduoduo is only listed on US stocks. Without Hong Kong stocks, Hong Kong Stock Connect cannot connect. ** There is no legal channel of equal size to accept the chips that are forced to be sold.

After the news came out, Pinduoduo fell by more than 6%. In contrast, the decline in dual-listed Chinese concept stocks during the same period was relatively mild, confirming the logic that "companies with Hong Kong stocks are more resistant to decline."

> The above stocks are only used as case studies and do not constitute any buying or selling recommendations.

### Small market capitalization mid-level concept stocks - the most vulnerable

The market is small, the liquidity is poor, and the proportion of institutional positions is low. The same selling amount will cause the decline to be doubled. This category requires extra vigilance against stampede risks.

### How to understand this impact?

**The company's fundamentals have not changed a penny because of this policy. ** Pinduoduo’s revenue and profit are still the same. What forced selling creates is **price dislocation**, not **value destruction**.

But we must also admit honestly: If this part of the funds no longer returns to the U.S. stock market due to channel restrictions, there will indeed be a structural decrease in the demand side of Chinese concept stocks, which may affect the valuation center to a certain extent. The degree of impact depends on the proportion of these retail funds in the total trading volume of Chinese concept stocks, and precise data is currently lacking.

## 5. Several currently known paths

Let’s sort out the options that currently exist in front of existing users. Each path has its own pros and cons. The specific choice depends on each person’s position status, risk preference and personal judgment.

**Rollover (stock transfer)**: Transfer stocks directly from Futu to other overseas securities firms (such as Interactive Brokers). There is no need to sell and no transaction taxes will be triggered. For the specific process, you can check "stock transfer out" on Futu App or contact customer service. However, it should be noted that unnamed overseas securities firms such as Interactive Brokers may also face compliance risks in the medium and long term - the rectification plan targets all illegal cross-border business activities, not just these three.

**Rebuilding positions through Hong Kong Stock Connect**: Applicable to stocks dual-listed in Hong Kong and the United States such as Alibaba, Tencent, and JD.com. Open Southbound Connect with a mainland brokerage (with an asset threshold of 500,000), sell U.S. stocks and then buy Hong Kong stocks of the same company through Southbound Connect. But for companies such as Pinduoduo that are only listed on the U.S. stock market, this path will not work.

**QDII Fund**: For example, China Internet ETF, Nasdaq 100 ETF, etc. can indirectly invest in overseas markets. However, individual stock positions cannot be accurately controlled, and QDII quotas are often tight.

**Cross-border Wealth Management Link**: Currently only available to residents of the Guangdong-Hong Kong-Macao Greater Bay Area.

**If you do nothing**: The stocks will remain in the account within 2 years and can be held but cannot be added to the position. The App was shut down after 2 years, but the stocks were still hosted in the exchange’s central clearing system and would not disappear. Futu should then come up with a specific account disposal plan (the plan requires overseas institutions to make relevant arrangements).

---

## 6. What do you think about this matter?

The essence of this matter: **A gray window for ordinary Chinese investors to directly participate in the global capital market is being closed. **

Futu and Tiger have done one thing in the past few years - allowing Chinese retail investors to buy U.S. and Hong Kong stocks as conveniently as A-shares. The experience is good and the threshold is low, but from a legal perspective, it has always been in a gray area. This renovation has cleared up the gray areas.

The windows are closed, but the door is still open: **Hong Kong Stock Connect, QDII, Cross-border Financial Management Connect**, these legal channels are still available. Frankly speaking, they are inferior to Futu in terms of target range, operational flexibility, and rates. This is a fact. But they are compliant and sustainable.

Eight ministries and commissions jointly issued a document, the State Council agreed, and there was a clear timetable—this level of policy has no precedent in history. Everyone needs to make their own judgment based on their own circumstances.
