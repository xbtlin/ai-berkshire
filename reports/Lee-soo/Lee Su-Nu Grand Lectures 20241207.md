# Second part: Thinking about confusion

# History

These confusions are not unique to China. Looking back at the world’s history over the past 500 years, all countries, after industrial take-off and middle-income, will enter the middle-end period – the 2.5 stage.** China is in the 2.5 stage today.**

All countries that have experienced industrial take-off (e.g. Germany, Japan, South America, and South-East Asia) have faced the challenges of China today. Some have crossed, and some have been struggling with the middle-income trap to date.

# The nature of the problem

** Definition of modernization**: The essence of modernization is automatic, compound and sustained economic growth brought about by the combination of market economies and modern technology.

The economy’s compound growth is a “horrifying mathematical concept.” China’s economy grew in real terms dozens or even hundreds of times more than it did in the early years of reform and opening. However, despite the growth of the economy,** social governance, human psychology, and political systems did not undergo the same degree of compoundity**

The human nature has not changed fundamentally for 200,000 years since the wise came into existence, and our economy has gone through three great leaps: from hunting to agricultural, to modern technological civilization.

** The emergence of a gap**: When the early industrial take-off phase is over, there will inevitably be a huge gap between the reality of economic growth in compound and the slow changes in social, psychological and political governance. This gap raises problems that all countries will experience at this stage.

# History case analysis

** Time pattern of industrialization**: Thirty-40 years of compound growth is sufficient for a economy to undergo significant economic changes, particularly during the economic take-off phase:

- Japan: 1868 to 1905, the victory over Russia, 30 years old.

# First example: the concept of land

In agricultural civilization, land and population are the most important indicators of economic size. Agricultural economies exist in the "Scalm" (Marsas trap) where population growth exceeds the capacity of land to carry. Land expansion has been the most important demand of communities, races and countries.

** However, in the age of science and technology civilization, as the economy enters a sustainable and compound growth phase, the reasons and drivers for economic growth are no longer land and population, but the full extent of the market size and the flow of factors of production**

This difference in old and new concepts is the most important cause of World War I. When World War I broke out, many thought that war would end in a few weeks, but the deep-rooted psychological nature of the land struggles led to the death of 50 million people and the collapse of all the participating empires.

** The results of the war are contrary to claims: Germany and Japan — the main initiators of both wars — achieved their quest for the land and the space of survival after the defeat of the Second World War. The claims they failed to achieve through war were made in the post-war peace.

The most important event of the post-World War II era: the US became the first country in human history to return all the territory acquired after the war to its countries of origin without compensation. In exchange, the US established a system of international trade, commodity and capital trading based on American ideas.

** Land was no longer the most important factor in economic growth in the era of science and technology civilization, and was replaced by the full flow of market size and economic elements, including technical, human and capital elements.**

** Conclusion**: The virtual and real economies are already inextricably linked, and this distinction is no longer necessary. Many old concepts are still deeply influencing our judgment of today's economy.

# Issue one: Consumption and savings

China’s share of individual consumption in the economy as a whole is only 40%, which has been declining steadily in recent years. Meanwhile, the savings rate has increased from 40% to about 50%.

To allow savings funds to enter a system that is truly conducive to economic growth, a modern capital market and an efficient financial system are needed to facilitate the efficient allocation and flow of funds.

# The history of financial innovation #

Venice (the middle of the century to Napoleon, for over a thousand years) is the longest-running republic in human history. In the 1,000-1500 years, despite a mere 100,000 people, it has monopolized the most important trade linking Asia to Europe.

** The most significant achievement in Venice**: for the first time, a significant part of the modern financial system — double-entry, as well as a stock system, insurance system and a modern banking system — was invented.

After independence in 1581, the Netherlands went through 70 years of independent war, and in the 17th century it emerged as the most important long-distance trading empire. The Netherlands invented a public participation regime for limited shares companies – the first public company in the world to be the Dutch East India.

** UK breakthrough**: The glorious revolution of 1688 achieved two achievements:

1. The republican, constitutional monarchy of governance is applied, and the royal power is subject to various forces
2. Completion of a consolidation of the system: "Medification" for the Dutch financial system

William III and his wife Mary II became the common kings of Britain, and William III transferred the entire financial system of the Netherlands to the United Kingdom.

The merger brought the UK a complete modern financial system.** Capital markets provide not only finance, but also, and more importantly, credit systems.**

The function of credit systems: Entrepreneurs need the credit of entrepreneurs, investors need the credit of investors, and intermediaries need the credit of intermediaries. Ultimately, they can pool the small money of ordinary savers who do not know business, creating a huge investment force.

The process has established a complete credit system, which is also relevant to legal systems, dispute resolution mechanisms, customary practices and trust.
