Many companies are managed by career managers — decisions made by them are governed by boards of directors and the direction of the company is not entirely one person.

The Internet is not like that.

This is both the greatest advantage and the greatest risk to the web.

# "Big Child" -- an assessment by a prominent investor

A prominent investor once said that Ding is a "big boy". The phrase is not derogatory — it accurately describes a central feature of Ding Bing:** there is a child-like enthusiasm and commitment to products, but sometimes less mature or systematic in capital operations and business management**.

The evaluation is worth pursuing on both sides:

** Positive: The enthusiasm of the product brings with it a powerful product.** The Internet-friendly game can be "a product for 20 years" because the founder is the biggest product manager in itself. Ding’s demands for quality -- he will try, advise, or even overthrow the team’s program. This “founder is the chief product officer” model, which has been validated in Apple and Nintendo.

** Negative: "Big Child" also means that individual preferences may be superior to business rationality.** Nets are easy to feed pigs, make hard choices, make music -- and these businesses are often under the shadow of personal interest. Not that these business are bad, but they divert management's attention and corporate resources. But the "know what can change" side of Ting is also shown -- a strategic contraction that shows what interests he can understand are not suitable for business.

# The fear of managing style

45.5 per cent of holdings bring not only control but also characteristics of a highly centralized organizational structure.

The web-based decision-making chain is short -- but it's in the hands of the Ding. The advantage is that decisions are fast, that they are strong in implementation, that they do not show the "internal game drain" that big companies often use. The disadvantage is that there is no checks and balances -- no one can stop them effectively if Ding is wrong (e.g. over-inputing a project).

** The counter-view**: High centralization is a strength rather than a disadvantage at a particular stage of company development. Web-based e-commerce has proven to be able to maintain 33% net interest rates and a very high return on capital under the control of Tin Ding – much better than many "good governance structures" but inefficient companies.

# Three, the lesson of breaking up

# What's going on #

In January 2023, the Internet was officially terminated in collaboration with the agent of snowstorm entertainment.

The Internet has been operating iconic games like the World of the Beast under the Snowstorm, the Pioneer of Watching Stones, The Darkest Disruption. Breaking up means that these games disappear overnight from the Internet.

# What's the lesson?

** First, agency business is naturally fragile.** No matter how many years of cooperation, how well it works, brands and intellectual property rights are not in their own hands -- break up when you say break up. 14 years of user accumulation, business experience, community building, and nothing.

** Second, the financial impact is manageable.** After the break-up of the snowstorm, the income from the game did not fall back — 92.1 billion (+ 11%). This suggests that the snow agent accounts for a small share of the income structure of the net, and that the growth in self-research products completely offsets this gap.

** Third, the strategic direction.** After the snow broke up, Ding-jung stressed on several occasions the strategy of self-researching - no longer relying on any external brand agent. But this sentence needs to be read together with the next question.

# V. Summary risk

In addition to the branding authorization risks discussed, the network is also exposed to the following risks:

Risk one: 82% income dependency in games

The game accounts for 82 per cent of the total — a very high concentration of single operations.

The game industry is subject to systemic shocks (policy, technology substitution, user preferences), and other business buffers, which are easily accessible.

# Risk two: Release number policy

Although the 2025 edition number was issued with 1,771 of its seven-year high, the essence of the number policy is** regulatory discretion** — government can tighten at any time.

The 2021-2022 edition of the winter has now brought new industrial memory. If similar policy tightening is repeated, it will have a direct impact on the online rhythm of the new game.

# Risk three: Central exit (very low probability)

The net is easily listed at NASDAQ and Ports. The net is as easily off-market as all central stock - short-term stock shocks, long-term port buffers.

But the speciality of Internet access is that **45.5% of the equity is concentrated in the hands of individuals in the Ding Ding.** If the deterioration of US-China relations leads to extreme scenarios such as the assets freeze (with a very low probability), the impact on companies is more concentrated than on companies with decentralized governance.

Risk four: AI's subversion of the game industry

AI is changing the way in which games are developed -- from art generation to code development to testing optimization. In the short term, AI reduces the cost of playing development, which is good for the web. But in the long run, if AI flattened the development threshold -- so that small companies can also make fine games -- the logic that allows the web to be eroded "high R & D input = high quality protected rivers."

# Six, summing up

I'm going to take this analysis and put it in a few words:

** Net access is buying the stock ** - 45.5% ownership means that the company's fate and the depth of the individual is tied to the individual
**Ding is a wonderful producer** ** ** but the label "Big Child" means that it may not be as strong as the product in capital operations and organizational management.
** Capital feedback is good** — buy back over $2 billion, split $2.ADS, and a distribution rate of 60% — but 163.5 billion cash is still in pile, indicating a lack of high return investment.
** Snowbreaker is a prior case** ** ** The Mandarin branding authority faces similar structural risks
** Risk list is not short** - Game single dependency, code policy, overseas blockage, absence of minor program, mid-term exit - none of them could be "killed" in the short term

** In sum, the Internet is not susceptible to a single fatal risk, but rather to a multiplicity of intermediate risks.** Each single one is not sufficient to deny investment value, but the sum of the — plus the centralized risk of "one is the boss" — requires a higher margin of security in valuation.
