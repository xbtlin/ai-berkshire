# Liquor fell by 65%. Is it now a gold pit or a value trap?

It has been falling for five consecutive years, from 18,000 points to 6,335 points, a drop of 65%. The net value of China Merchants CSI Liquor Index C (012414) fell from 1 yuan to 50 cents. People who bought this fund lost half of their money.

What's even more exciting is the valuation: PE is 18.45 times, which is at the lowest 2% position in 5 years - meaning it is cheaper than 97.94% of the time in the past 5 years. PB is 3.59 times, and the 5-year score is 0%, directly hitting the lowest level in history.

This position only appeared briefly during the 2014 plasticizer crisis and the 2018 bear market. The last time liquor was this cheap was ten years ago.

The question arises: Is this a bargain-hunting opportunity or a carefully disguised value trap?

## What happened to Baijiu?

Let’s look at a piece of data first: Liquor production has declined for nine consecutive years, and will drop another 12.1% year-on-year in 2025.

This is not a cycle, this is a trend.

Profit margins of 86% of liquor companies are declining, and 65% of companies have seen both sales volume and sales decline. The entire industry is undergoing a brutal liquidation.

There are three reasons.

First, the macroeconomic slowdown. Liquor consumption is highly tied to business banquets, real estate, and local finance, and these three engines are decelerating at the same time.

Second, young people don’t drink liquor. Liquor consumption among people aged 20 to 35 accounts for only 12%. Milk tea, craft beer, whiskey, tipsy wine, young people have too many choices, and liquor is no longer the default option.

Third, stock the barrier lake. The inventory turnover days of sub-high-end liquor range from 900 to 1,661 days. Translated into vernacular: It takes two and a half to four and a half years to sell out the wine in the hands of dealers. With the channels so blocked, it’s no wonder that sales can be good.

## Are the leaders okay?

Looking at the 2025 annual report, the differentiation of the five leading companies is already very obvious.

Moutai: Revenue was 172 billion, down 1.21% year-on-year; net profit was 82.3 billion, down 4.53%. This is the first time in the 24 years since Moutai went public that both revenue and profits have declined. However, the gross profit margin is 91.2%, ROE is 32.5%, and the money printing machine is still turning, but at a slower pace.

Shanxi Fenjiu: Revenue was 38.7 billion, an increase of 7.52%. Among the 18 listed liquor companies, the only one is still growing. The dividends of the fragrance track have not been exhausted yet.

Luzhou Laojiao: Revenue was 25.7 billion, down 17.5%; net profit was 10.8 billion, down 19.6%. Income dropped by almost 20%, but the dividend rate was as high as 78.5%, almost all the money earned was distributed. The dividend yield is 6.7% to 7.5%, which is higher than many bank financial products.

Wuliangye: It is in an adjustment period, but its size and brand barriers are still there, with a dividend rate of 6.1% to 6.6%.

Yanghe: Revenue was 19.2 billion, a plunge of 33.47%; net profit was 2.2 billion, a plunge of 66.94%. This is Yanghe’s worst performance since its listing. Provincial wine faucets are the most seriously injured in the national ebb.

To sum up in one sentence: Maotai is still Maotai, Fenjiu is growing against the trend, Luzhou Laojiao compensates shareholders with high dividends, and Yanghe is experiencing a test of survival.

The other side of the industry is that concentration is increasing rapidly—CR5 (the top five market shares) has historically exceeded 50%. The logic that the strong will always be strong has not changed, but the whole cake is shrinking.

## How cheap is the valuation?

Three sets of data illustrate the current valuation level.

First, PE TTM is 18.45 times, and the 5-year quantile is 2.06%. The ROE of the liquor sector is as high as 19.46%, which is 2.5 times the market average. For an industry whose profitability is twice that of the market average, its valuation has fallen to a historical bottom. The gap in between is the market's pessimistic pricing for the future of liquor.

Second, PB is 3.59 times, with a 5-year score of 0%. The price-to-book ratio has reached a historical low, which means that the market has given the lowest valuation to the brand premium and channel value of liquor.

Third, the leading dividend rate has entered the "fixed income replacement" range. Luzhou Laojiao has 6.7% to 7.5%, Wuliangye has 6.1% to 6.6%, and even Maotai has 3.9% to 4.2%. Who would have thought ten years ago that the dividend yield from buying Moutai stocks could be as high as bank financial management?

Let’s make a horizontal comparison: the last time the valuation reached this position was the plasticizer crisis in 2014. After that low point, the liquor index nearly quadrupled in three years. But it should be noted that that time was a short-term event impact, and the industry fundamentals did not change; this time it was a structural change in consumption trends, which cannot be simply compared.

## What is the biggest risk?

Cheap doesn't mean safe. The biggest risk faced by liquor is the generational gap in consumption.

Young people account for only 12% of liquor consumption. Behind this figure is an irreversible trend: the core consumer group of liquor is aging, but the new generation of consumers has not caught up.

This is fundamentally different from past cycles. During the troughs of liquor in 2008, 2014, and 2018, the core consumer group was still there, but they stopped drinking temporarily due to economic cycles or policy impacts, and would come back once the environment improves. But this time, it will be difficult for young people who don’t drink liquor to become loyal consumers of liquor in the future.

This raises a key question: Is the current low valuation a "cycle bottom" or "the middle of a long-term downward channel"?

If it's the former, buying now will reap huge rewards. If it's the latter, then cheap may be cheaper, and low valuations simply reflect the true prospects of the industry.

To put it bluntly, this is the risk of a "value trap": it looks cheap, but in fact the industry is permanently deteriorating.

However, there is a piece of negative evidence worth noting: Moutai bucked the trend and raised prices by 8.55% in March 2026. Dare to raise prices when the industry is in dire straits, which shows that the real leaders still have pricing power. The volume can be reduced, but the price can not only sustain it, but also increase it.

## What is the future of liquor?

To understand the future of baijiu, the best reference is not the history of baijiu itself, but the American tobacco industry across the ocean.

The story of American tobacco is this: Since the 1960s, smoking rates have continued to decline and the industry has continued to shrink. According to common sense, stocks in this sunset industry should fall all the way. But in fact, U.S. tobacco stocks have been among the highest-returning sectors over the past few decades.

The reason is simple: volume shrinks, price rises, high dividends and compound interest.

Fewer people smoke, but tobacco companies continue to raise prices, maintaining or even increasing profits. At the same time, because there is no need for large capital expenditures, a large proportion of the money earned is distributed to shareholders. Shareholders receive dividends and then reinvest, and the long-term compound returns are astonishing.

Liquor is following a very similar path.

Output has declined for nine consecutive years (volume shrinking), Moutai bucked the trend and raised prices (prices rose), Luzhou Laojiao's dividend rate is 78.5% (high dividend), and industry concentration exceeds 50% (leaders take all).

This means that liquor will most likely not return to the golden era of "double-digit annual revenue and profit growth" from 2015 to 2020, but leading companies can still provide shareholders with good long-term returns through their ability to raise prices and high dividends.

The logic of investing in liquor is switching from "making money for growth" to "making money for dividends." From "growth star" to "cash cow".

Only by accepting this change can we correctly value liquor.

## Should I buy it? how to buy

We made three scenario calculations, taking the current net value of 0.5041 as a starting point and looking at the results three years later.

Optimistic scenario: The industry recovers, profits grow by 10% annually, and the valuation is restored to 25 times PE. After three years, the net worth is 0.91, and the return rate is about 80%. This scenario requires a significant improvement in the macro economy and the completion of industry destocking.

Neutral scenario: earnings remain unchanged and valuation remains at 18 times PE. Three years later, the net worth was 0.49, basically the same, with a slight loss of 2%. Including dividend income of 3% to 4% per year, the total return is about 7% to 10%. This scenario corresponds to the logic of "cash cows" - earn dividends but not valuation.

Pessimistic scenario: The industry continues to deteriorate, profits fall by 10% annually, and valuations fall to 15 times PE. Three years later, the net worth was 0.30, another loss of 41%. This scenario corresponds to the full outbreak of the generational fault in consumption.

Taken together, the probability distribution of the three scenarios is approximately 25% optimistic, 50% neutral, and 25% pessimistic.

Specific operation suggestions are divided into three situations.

If you want to buy a short position: Use 3 to 6 months to build a position in batches, and add a position each time it falls by 5% to 8%. The total position should not exceed 10% to 15% of the personal investment portfolio. Liquor is not a deterministic opportunity and is not worth a heavy position.

If you are already trapped: It is not recommended to cut the flesh in this position. Current valuations already include a lot of pessimism, and unless you judge that the industry will permanently collapse, it makes more sense to hold on and wait. You can consider small cover positions at low levels to reduce costs.

Index or individual stocks: This fund tracks the CSI Liquor Index, with the top five holdings accounting for 72%. The problem is that there are strong players like Maotai and Fenjiu, as well as poor households like Yanghe. If you have the ability to analyze individual stocks, give priority to leading stocks (Moutai, Luzhou Laojiao, Fenjiu). If you don’t have the energy to do research, index funds can save you the trouble, but you have to accept the price of being “averaged”.

Also note the rate: the comprehensive rate of this fund is 1.30%/year. If liquor companies rely on dividends to make money in the future, fund companies will take away 1.3% of the 3% to 5% annual dividend income, which is equivalent to one-third of the dividends paid as management fees. If held for the long term, this cost can significantly erode returns. This is also a reason to prioritize individual stocks.

## Write at the end

The best days of the liquor industry are most likely over, but the worst pricing may have already happened.

Looking back ten years from now, liquor will not be a growth stock that people regret not taking a heavy position in, but it will most likely be a cash cow that quietly produces milk - provided that the price you buy is cheap enough and you buy the strongest ones.
