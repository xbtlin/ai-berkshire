#3rd floor: associated transactions and governance structures

The long-light core was born in ** Shigeon Shigeon Institute**, founder Wang Xinyang, who joined the company in 2012. This source raises two remaining problems:

**1. Associated transactions: differences between the A and Ports Units**

During the board review, regulation focused on the trade between long-light ether cores and the parties involved. According to the new wave of wealth, there was a "large discrepancy" between the shares A offer and the associated transactions in the Hong Kong stock offer.

(Source: New Wave Finance, Science and Innovation Board Enquiry)

**2. Spouses holding shares + multiple responsibilities**

Wang Xingyang and his wife Zhang Yanxia are in total control **49.53%** of the shares.

The good is that decisions are quick and strong. The bad is the lack of effective internal checks and balances — the minority shareholders have weak mechanisms for protecting their interests when the founder's husband and wife control nearly half of the equity and embrace core management positions.

**3. Remuneration disputes**

Wang Xinyang’s salary in 2023 amounted to **36.5 million**, most of which was paid for shares. Although the share payment was a one-time payment (not equal to the amount of cash that is paid annually), this figure still attracted market attention in a company that had a net annual profit of $170 million.

(Source: Bedo Finance)

** Risk rating: medium probability + high impact. Not fatal risk, but continuous tracking.**

The couple-stock model is not unusual in private chip companies (e.g. Weir shares are in danger). The share payment in remuneration is a one-time payment that does not affect operating cash flows.
