## 1. The essence of business model: definition of core business and dismantling of revenue structure

### Core business definition

Xiaomi is essentially a "hardware + Internet" platform company that uses smartphones as traffic portals, expands user touch points through IoT hardware, and monetizes Internet services. It will superimpose the second growth curve of smart electric vehicles starting in 2024. Lei Jun defined it as "the whole ecology of people, cars and families".

But we must be honest: Although this definition has a beautiful narrative, its profit structure reveals a simpler truth** - Xiaomi is still essentially a hardware company, and the monetization ability of Internet services is far less than that of pure Internet companies.

## 2. Platform/product flywheel effect

### Theoretical flywheel

```
Mobile phones (traffic entrance) → IoT (expanding contact points/increasing stickiness) → Internet services (traffic monetization) → automobiles (high customer unit price/brand upgrade)
      ↑                                                                    |
      └─────────────Brand potential energy return/ecological lock-in ←────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Is the flywheel truly closed loop? ——Ring-by-ring verification

**The first link: mobile phone → IoT (partially established)**
The number of connected devices on the Xiaomi AIoT platform has reached 1.04 billion, and the number of users with 5 or more IoT devices has reached 22.7 million, a year-on-year increase of 23.6%. Mijia monthly active users exceeded 100 million. The data is very good on the surface, but we need to ask further: these 22.7 million deep users only account for 3% of the 742 million monthly active users, and the vast majority of users are not locked into the IoT ecosystem.

**Second Ring: IoT→Internet Services (weaker)**
Internet service revenue was 37.4 billion yuan, and ARPU value increased by only 3%. Advertising revenue is the main component, and growth in games and value-added services has stagnated. Compared with pure Internet companies such as Tencent and ByteDance, Xiaomi's user monetization efficiency is extremely low.

**Third Ring: Automobile → Ecological Synergy (Initial Verification)**
45% of car users simultaneously purchased Xiaomi mobile phones, and 32% purchased additional smart homes. The data may seem eye-catching, but please note: ** Early buyers of Xiaomi cars are themselves loyal fans of Xiaomi, and this conversion rate will inevitably decline after the base is expanded. **

**Conclusion: The flywheel exists but the speed is not high. It is more like a "loose alliance" than a "tight closed loop". **

**Rating: ★★★ (3/5)**

## 3. Moat analysis - verification one by one

### 1. Brand moat: ★★☆☆☆ (pseudo moat)

The core cognition of Xiaomi brand is "cost-effectiveness". A brand moat means consumers are willing to pay a premium for the brand itself. Xiaomi is just the opposite - its brand promise is "no premiums". The gross profit margin of mobile phones dropped from 12.6% to 10.9% (only 8.3% in Q4). Although the share of the 6,000-10,000 yuan price segment doubled, it was only 4.5%.

**Duan Yongping’s judgment**: Brand moat requires consumers to pay a premium because of the brand. Xiaomi's brand promise is "not charging more", which is a fatal flaw in Duan Yongping's framework.

### 2. Switching cost: ★★☆☆☆ (pseudo moat)

Brand switching within the Android ecosystem is almost zero cost. There are only 22.7 million deep IoT users (more than 5 items), accounting for less than 3% of the total users. **The switching cost for the vast majority of users is close to zero. **

### 3. Network effect: ★★★☆☆ (weak moat)

The network effect of the IoT ecosystem is unilateral (user-device) rather than bilateral (such as Taobao buyer-seller). Users will not want to use Xiaomi just because "others also use Xiaomi IoT".

### 4. Scale effect: ★★★★☆ (real moat, but limited)

Xiaomi’s strongest moat. The bargaining power of the supply chain brought by the annual shipment of 165 million mobile phones is real. However, competitors also have scale (Samsung 226 million units, Apple 234 million units) and do not have exclusive advantages.

### 5. Technical barriers: ★★☆☆☆ (does not constitute a moat)

Xiaomi will invest 33.1 billion yuan in R&D in 2025, but there is a huge gap compared with Huawei (160 billion+). Self-research on chips is progressing slowly, and smart driving lags behind Tesla and Huawei by 2-3 years.

**Moat overall rating: ★★★ (3/5)**
> Lack of real "wide moat". In Duan Yongping's words: **This is not a business that can "make money while sleeping". **

## 4. User/customer value

### Does "cost-effectiveness" constitute real differentiation? ——Does not constitute

1. **The price-performance ratio is replicable**: Any company with supply chain integration capabilities can do it
2. **Value for money depends on "follower" positioning**: Always benchmark against others, rather than define the category
3. **There is a natural conflict between cost performance and profit margin**: The mobile phone gross profit margin of 10.9% explains everything
4. **Duan Yongping’s core rebuttal**: The price/performance ratio is “an excuse for why our products are not good enough.” Really good products, users are willing to pay a premium

**The only exception is automobiles**: SU7's 24.3% gross profit margin shows that the automobile category has temporarily achieved a combination of "high cost performance + reasonable profits", but it mainly benefits from the immaturity of the category.

**Rating: ★★★ (3/5)**

---

## 5. Quantitative test of synergy effects

If the synergy is really strong, Internet service ARPU should increase significantly as the number of IoT devices increases. But in fact, ARPU only increased by 3% (much lower than the 22% increase in device count). **The increase in the number of device connections has not been effectively translated into an increase in monetization capabilities. **

**Rating: ★★★ (3/5)**

## 6. Duan Yongping’s “Good Business” Standard Evaluation

| Evaluation Dimensions | Standards | Xiaomi Performance | Rating |
|---------|------|---------|------|
| **Differentiation** | Does the product have unique and difficult-to-copy characteristics | The price-performance ratio can be copied, and the ecology has certain uniqueness but lacks depth | ★★☆☆☆ |
| **Pricing Power** | Can prices be raised without losing sales | The drop in mobile phone gross profit margin to 10.9% proves the lack of pricing power | ★★☆☆☆ |
| **Sustainable Competitive Advantage** | Whether the moat will be wider in 10 years | Depends on the deepening of automobile + IoT, currently uncertain | ★★★☆☆ |

### Why has Duan Yongping never invested in Xiaomi?

1. **"Value for money is just an excuse for yourself"** - long-term profit margins will be squeezed by competition
2. **Opposition to diversified expansion** - "Companies with too wide a range of product areas will find it difficult to make good products in the long run."
3. **Commitment of 5% hardware net profit margin** - Actively giving up the core feature of "good business"
4. **Lack of "what not to do" ability** - Duan Yongping emphasized that "knowing what not to do" is more important than "knowing what to do"
5. **Inspiration from Apple’s abandonment of cars** - Duan Yongping praised Apple’s right decision to give up building cars, but Xiaomi happened to do the opposite.

**Rating: ★★(2/5)**
> By Duan Yongping’s standards, Xiaomi is not a “good business.”

---

## Overall conclusion

**Overall Rating: 2.8★/5★**

**The core judgment from Duan Yongping's perspective: Xiaomi is a "mediocre business that works hard." ** Lei Jun is undoubtedly an excellent entrepreneur with amazing execution ability, but the business model itself determines the profit ceiling. Xiaomi's core contradiction is: ** Its brand promise (cost-effectiveness/kindness) is precisely the root cause of its inability to build a wide moat. **

Duan Yongping would say: Xiaomi may be a good company, but it is not a good business - and if a good company does bad business, it will ultimately be the bad business that wins. **

The automobile business is the only variable that may change this judgment. If Xiaomi can establish real brand premium and pricing power in the automotive category, the entire investment thesis may need to be rewritten. But there is currently insufficient evidence.
