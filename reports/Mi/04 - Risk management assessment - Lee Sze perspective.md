**Analytical framework: Li Lu’s value investment perspective | Assessment date: June 23, 2026**

## Part One: Management Assessment

### 1.1 Lei Jun’s comprehensive assessment

| Dimensions | Scores | Core Judgments |
|------|------|---------|
| Circle of Competence | ★★★★ | Deep experience in consumer electronics + Internet, cars are an extension rather than the core |
| Integrity | ★★★★ | Open communication and transparency, but the high placement of HK$42.5 billion in 2025 will dilute shareholders by 3.1% |
| Strategic vision | ★★★★★ | The most outstanding ability - from Kingsoft to Xiaomi to car manufacturing, seizing every opportunity of the times |
| Capital allocation | ★★★ | The weakest link - zero dividends since listing, cumulative financing exceeds HK$80-90 billion |
| Owner mentality | ★★★★ | Holding 23.4% of the shares, 55.7% of the voting rights, personal wealth is highly bound |

**Structural flaws in the circle of competence**: Lei Jun is good at the "efficiency-driven" business model (ultimate cost performance), but lacks a successful record in the "brand premium-driven" model. After years of high-end development, models priced above 4,000 yuan account for only 17.3% of the market.

**Capital Allocation Controversy**: In March 2025, 800 million shares were placed at a high price of HK$53.25, with net financing of approximately HK$42.5 billion. The placement caused the stock price to plummet by 6.32%, and the market value evaporated by more than HK$90 billion. It has never paid dividends since its listing.

**Management comprehensive rating: ★★★★—Excellent but not outstanding, with outstanding strategic capabilities but capital allocation discipline needs to be strengthened. **

### 1.2 Core Management Team

The co-founder camp is divided. Li Wanqiang and Hong Feng have withdrawn from domestic subsidiaries. Lin Bin was transferred from president to vice chairman. The core team has evolved from the "Eight King Kong" to Lei Jun's personal centralized model.

Positive: High decision-making efficiency and strong strategic consistency. Negative: Over-reliance on Lei Jun’s judgment and lack of effective internal checks and balances.

## Part 2: Regulatory Risks

### 2.1 Indian regulatory risks [Severity: Very high | Occurred]

- About 4.8 billion RMB of funds have been frozen for more than 4 years, and about 545 million US dollars are still restricted.
- India’s market share plummeted from peak 31% to 12%
- An additional US$72 million in tax disputes will arise in February 2026
- **Hidden Risk**: The Indian model may be copied by other emerging markets. Xiaomi’s overseas revenue accounts for about 50%. This is not a tail risk that can be ignored.

### 2.2 U.S. geopolitical risks [Severity: Medium | Probability: Medium]

Xiaomi's reliance on Qualcomm chips is a core vulnerability. Self-developed chips are still a long way from core SoC independence.

### 2.3 Automobile safety and recall risks [Severity: High | Occurred]

- Two serious fatal accidents in 2025 (3 deaths in Tongling and 3 deaths in Chengdu)
- Recall nearly 117,000 SU7 vehicles (intelligent driving function defects)
- The brand gene is consumer electronics, and consumers’ tolerance for car accidents is much lower than that of mobile phones

## Part 3: Competitive Risks

### 3.1 Huawei’s full return [Severity: Extremely High | Already Occurred]

**Mobile Phone Battlefield**: In 2026Q1, Huawei led the Chinese market with a 20% share, while Xiaomi plummeted 35% to fifth place. Huawei has blocked Xiaomi’s upward path to high-end products.

**Auto Battlefield**: Hongmeng Zhixing will deliver 589,000 vehicles in 2025 and targets 1-1.3 million vehicles in 2026.

**IoT battlefield**: Huawei’s Hongmeng Ecosystem directly threatens Xiaomi’s IoT connection advantage.

**Hidden risks**: Huawei represents "independent innovation" (self-developed chips), and Xiaomi represents "integrated innovation" (reliance on Qualcomm). In the Chinese market where national sentiment drives consumption, this narrative difference may lead to continued pressure on brand premiums.

### 3.2 Mobile phone gross profit margin ceiling [Severity: High | Probability: High]

It will be 10.9% for the whole year of 2025 and further drop to 10.1% in 2026Q1. The real impact of the 300% surge in memory chip DDR5 is expected to be fully apparent in Q2-Q3.

## Part 4: Business Risks

### Automobile business uncertainty [Severity: High]

- Another loss of 3.1 billion yuan in 2026Q1 (including the initial investment in YU7 mass production)
- There are only two models in the product line. Once a certain model stalls, it will fluctuate violently.
-Does the long-term rate of return in the automotive industry match invested capital? Historically few new entrants have achieved long-term excess returns

## Part 5: Governance Structure

### Different rights for the same shares [Risk rating: medium to high]

Lei Jun controls 55.7% of the voting rights with 23.4% of the economic equity. External shareholders have almost no veto power on major decisions. High-level placement in 2025 is a typical case.

### Shareholder Returns

Dividends have never been distributed. Although the repurchase intensity has been increased, the net effect has been diluted by placement financing. A company with an annual profit of 39.2 billion chooses to pay zero dividends. Whether this judgment is correct depends on the long-term returns of the automobile and AI businesses.

## Part 7: Long-term certainty from Li Lu’s perspective

### What will happen to Xiaomi in ten years?

| Scenario | Probability | Description |
|------|------|------|
| Optimistic | 30% | The ecology of people and cars is globalized, with annual sales of 2 million+ cars and a market value of over HK$2 trillion |
| Neutral | 45% | Top three mobile phones but gross profit margin is under pressure. Annual sales of 1-1.5 million cars have profit margin less than 5%, profit is 50-70 billion |
| Pessimistic | 25% | Huawei is squeezed by two lines, chip control is upgraded, and profits fall below 20 billion |

### "Long Slope with Heavy Snow" Assessment

**The slope is long enough**: consumer electronics + smart cars + IoT + AI, the track space is large enough.

**The snow is not thick enough**: Core competitiveness - ultimate cost performance, rapid iteration, ecological synergy - is more of an operational advantage than a structural barrier. Operational advantages can be replicated, and structural barriers can generate lasting excess returns.

### Core risk judgment

**One sentence conclusion: Xiaomi is an "efficiency" company led by outstanding entrepreneurs, but it is not a "monopoly" company with a deep moat. **

There are three core concerns:
1. **Insufficient moat depth** – Success is highly dependent on Lei Jun’s personal
2. **Radical capital allocation** - Five fronts invest heavily at the same time, which is in great tension with the "conservative capital allocation" concept advocated by Li Lu
3. **The valuation implies too many optimistic assumptions**——Car success + high-end breakthrough + smooth globalization must be established at the same time

**Final score: Management 4★, business model durability 3★, comprehensive risk rating "medium-high" - not a bad company that needs to be avoided, but not a "certainty" target that Li Lu will hold heavily for ten years. **
