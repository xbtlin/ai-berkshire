### 1.3 The impact of Huawei’s return

Huawei returned to the top spot in the Chinese mobile phone market in 2025 with 46.7 million units, capturing 69% of the folding screen market share and forming a monopoly in the ultra-high-end market priced above 6,000 yuan. Xiaomi's shipments in Q1 2026 plummeted 19% year-on-year, the largest decline among the top five manufacturers.

**Competitive Rating: ★★★ (3/5)**
> Ranked third in the world, but China’s market ranking has dropped to fourth. High-endization is still in its early stages, and the impact of Huawei’s return continues. The 19% plunge in 2026Q1 is a serious warning sign.

## 5. Munger "thinks the other way around": What will kill Xiaomi?

### Five fatal scenes

**Scenario 1: Smartphone shipments continue to decline, and the foundation of Internet services is shaken**
The 19% drop in Xiaomi mobile phone shipments in 2026Q1 is a wake-up call. Mobile phones are the "entrance" to the entire ecosystem, and narrowing the entrance means that the foundation of the business model is damaged.

**Scenario 2: Large-scale losses in the automobile business come back**
In 2025, automobiles will barely make a profit of 900 million yuan (profit margin is less than 1%). Once the gross profit margin drops from 24.3% to less than 15%, it will return to large losses. SU7 Ultra's steep decline from monthly sales of 3,000+ units to only 45 units exposed product life cycle risks.

**Scenario 3: Full squeeze from Huawei**
Huawei is Xiaomi's common opponent in the three battlefields of mobile phones (high-end 38% vs. 7%), IoT (Hongmeng Intelligent Connect), and automobiles (589,100 vehicles in the market). The rare dilemma of "encountering the same strongest opponent on all battlefields at the same time".

**Scenario 4: Dispersing energy across borders**
Xiaomi's R&D investment of 33.1 billion seems huge, but it is divided into four areas, and the investment in each area is less than that of its dedicated rivals: BYD's R&D of over 50 billion is devoted to cars; Huawei's R&D of 164.7 billion is mainly focused on ICT.

**Scenario 5: Smartphone industry in 10 years**
Shipments are slowly shrinking, and high-end products are concentrated in the "triple oligarch" pattern of Apple + Huawei + Samsung. If new forms of terminals such as AI glasses and AR devices emerge, the "entry" status of mobile phones may be partially replaced.

---

## 6. Final judgment from Munger’s perspective

**"This company is fighting in four red oceans at the same time, three of which have no pricing power."**

The only thing Munger might be interested in is the IoT ecosystem—the network effect of 1.079 billion devices creates real switching costs. But whether this moat is enough to support the entire company’s valuation narrative depends on: Can Xiaomi convert the ecological stickiness of IoT into sustainable profit growth, instead of letting huge capital expenditures on cars and AI eat up these profits?

**Core conclusion of thinking the other way around**: Xiaomi's biggest industry risk is not the failure of a certain business, but the dispersion of resources caused by "four-front operations." At present, the proof that the value of "ecological synergy" is greater than the cost of "dispersion" has not yet been completed.
