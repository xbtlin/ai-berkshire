# Adobe Business Model Analysis

> Analysis dimensions: business essence, user value, competitive barriers, long-term certainty
> Data cutoff: 2026-05 | Based on FY2025 annual report + Q1 FY2026 earnings report

---

## 1. What Kind of Business Is This?

Adobe's core business is a **subscription platform for creative productivity tools**. Products such as Photoshop, Illustrator, Premiere Pro, and After Effects form the global standard toolkit for creative professionals. The business has three defining characteristics:

1. **Extremely high switching costs**: Designers spend years mastering Photoshop and Illustrator workflows and accumulate large libraries of PSD and AI files. Switching tools means relearning the software and losing compatibility with historical assets. That is a real lock-in effect.
2. **Industry-standard status**: .psd, .ai, and .pdf are ubiquitous file formats. Files passed between clients and vendors are typically expected to be in Adobe formats, so the entire creative supply chain runs on Adobe's ecosystem.
3. **Subscription cash flow**: FY2025 revenue reached USD 23.77 billion, and annual recurring revenue exited the year at USD 25.2 billion, up 11.5% year over year. Subscription revenue accounted for more than 93% of total revenue, which is a textbook recurring-revenue model.

## 2. Why Do Users Rely on It?

**User stickiness is the most important feature of this business.**

- **Deeply embedded in professional workflows**: A video post-production team's Premiere + After Effects workflow, or a brand design team's Illustrator + InDesign workflow, is not just about the software itself. It is about the whole production line.
- **File format lock-in**: Billions of PSD, AI, and INDD files around the world create a huge stock of historical assets, and Adobe is still the only company that can open and edit them perfectly.
- **Collaborative network effects**: Creative Cloud's cloud collaboration features make team members dependent on one another. When colleagues, clients, and vendors all use Adobe, it is hard to switch unilaterally.

In 2025, Adobe took a bold pricing move by restructuring Creative Cloud All Apps into Standard (USD 54.99/month) and Pro (USD 69.99/month), with some plans rising as much as 16.7%. Revenue still grew 11% in FY2025, which shows that users accepted the price increase. **Being able to keep raising prices without losing customers is the best proof of pricing power.**

## 3. Is AI an Enhancement or a Disruption?

This is the question the market cares about most right now. My view is that **AI is an enhancement for Adobe, not a disruption - at least within the foreseeable future.**

**Strategic value of Firefly:**
- By the end of 2025, Firefly had generated more than 24 billion assets in total, and 75% of Fortune 500 companies had adopted it.
- AI-related ARR grew more than 3x year over year in Q1 FY2026.
- Generative credit consumption grew more than 45% quarter over quarter, and Firefly subscription plus credit-pack ARR grew 75% quarter over quarter.
- Direct revenue contribution was about USD 400 million in cumulative 2024-2025.

**The "commercial safety" advantage:**
Adobe Firefly is trained entirely on licensed content (such as Adobe Stock) and public-domain content, and does not use customer data. That allows Adobe to offer enterprise customers **intellectual-property indemnification** - something competitors such as Midjourney and Stable Diffusion cannot provide. For brand owners and large enterprises, this matters a great deal.

**A monetization innovation:**
Adobe introduced a "Generative Credits" consumption model, where USD 19.99 buys 4,000 credits. This adds a blade-style monetization layer on top of the subscription business and could become a new growth engine.

## 4. How Long Can This Business Last?

**Core view: as long as humans still need visual content creation, Adobe's business will continue.**

- Demand for visual content is growing exponentially with the rise of short video, social media, and e-commerce.
- AI lowers the barrier to creation, but that also means more people need tools; the market expands instead of shrinking.
- Adobe has already survived four major technological shifts: desktop publishing, the web, mobile, and now AI.

**Risk points:**
- Canva, with 180 million monthly active users, is rapidly penetrating the non-professional segment.
- Figma dominates the UI/UX market with an 80-90% share.
- Open-source AI tools are eroding the low-end market.

But these are mostly edge encroachments, not core replacements. Adobe's professional creative workflow remains the core business, and there is no near-term sign that it will be disrupted at the foundation.

## 5. Summary From a Duan Yongping Perspective

> "Buying stocks is buying companies, and buying companies is buying the present value of their future cash flows."

Adobe fits the criteria for a good business:
- **High switching costs + network effects = strong stickiness**
- **Subscription model = predictable cash flow**
- **Sustained pricing power = real pricing power**
- **AI is an enhancement, not a disruption = the moat is deepening**

The main concerns are uncertainty during the CEO transition and whether AI's long-term evolution could fundamentally alter creative workflows. But as a business, this is an **excellent company**.
