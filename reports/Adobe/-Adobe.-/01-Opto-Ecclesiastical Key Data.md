## A set of facts that will make you re-examine Adobe

Mr. Market sometimes makes very extreme mistakes. Over the past two years, Adobe has continued to post record financial numbers—record revenue, profits, and cash flow—but its stock price has fallen 60%. This kind of split of "fundamentals up, stock prices down" only occurs every few years in history. It's either a mispriced opportunity or the market is seeing something ahead of us that we haven't seen yet. Figuring out which situation is where the real value of studying Adobe lies.

**A company that makes $10.3 billion in cash annually has a market cap of only $100 billion. ** Over the past two years, Adobe has undergone a dramatic repricing:

- February 2024: **$620** - The climax of the "AI beneficiary" narrative, Firefly has high hopes
- September 2024: **$450** - The aftermath of the failed Figma acquisition + the fermentation of fear of AI disruption
- December 2025: **$400** - Record results for FY2025, but market remains hesitant
- March 2026: **$280** - CEO announces departure, stock price drops 7% in one day
- May 2026: **~$248** - down ~30% year-to-date, market cap ~$100 billion**

**In two years, the same company’s market value has evaporated by more than $150 billion. ** Revenues are growing, profits are growing, cash flow is growing - but the stock price is plummeting.

Open Adobe's latest financial data (FY2025 annual report + Q1 FY2026 financial report), you will see a set of highly contradictory signals:

| Indicators | FY2025 | Magnitude judgment |
|------|--------|---------|
| Total Revenue | **$23.77 billion** (+11%) | All-time record |
| GAAP Net Income | **$7.13 billion** | Net Margin **29.5%** |
| Free Cash Flow | **~$10.3 billion** | FCF Margin **42%** |
| ARR (Annualized Recurring Revenue) | **$25.2 billion** (+11.5%) | Subscription revenue accounted for over 93% |

| GAAP PE | **About 14-19 times** | Lowest range in the past decade |
| Price/Free Cash Flow | **~9.6x** | FCF Yield ~10.4%** |

**A software company with annual free cash flow of $10.3 billion, net profit margin of nearly 30%, and ARR of $25.2 billion has a market capitalization of only about $100 billion. **

If you think this is a "value trap in the AI era" - you may be underestimating the depth of Adobe's moat.
If you think this is a “missed opportunity” – you may be underestimating the potential of AI to disrupt creative workflows.

What this series wants to do is to first explain clearly what Adobe is, and then let you judge whether it is worth buying.

## What kind of company is Adobe?

Most people's understanding of Adobe remains as "Photoshop that company". This understanding cannot be wrong, but it is far from enough.

Adobe has three businesses, each of which is an independent cash flow engine:

| Business Segments | Core Products | FY2025 Revenue (Estimated) | Nature |
|---------|---------|-------------------|------|
| Creative Cloud | Photoshop, Illustrator, Premiere Pro, After Effects, Lightroom | ~**$14 Billion** | Creative Tool Subscriptions |
| Document Cloud | Acrobat, Adobe Sign, PDF Services | ~**$3.5 Billion** | Document/Electronic Signature Subscriptions |
| Experience Cloud | Analytics, Target, Campaign, Marketo | ~**$5.5 billion** | Enterprise Digital Marketing Platform |

**Adobe's real business is not "selling software" - it is an "infrastructure tax" on the global creative and digital content industry. **

Just like every digital payment goes through the Visa/Mastercard network, the creation, editing, and output of most professional visual content in the world go through Adobe's tool chain. Designers use Photoshop to retouch pictures, Illustrator to make vector graphics, Premiere to cut videos, InDesign to typeset, and Acrobat to generate PDFs—**the entire creative production line is completed in the Adobe ecosystem from beginning to end**.

This "infrastructure" attribute is the starting point for understanding Adobe's valuation logic.

## Three common misconceptions about Adobe

### Myth 1: Adobe is just Photoshop

Photoshop is Adobe's best-known product, but it's just one of more than two dozen tools in Creative Cloud. What really makes Adobe difficult to replace is not any single product, but the synergistic effect of the entire tool ecosystem:

- Designers use Photoshop + Illustrator + InDesign at the same time, seamlessly connecting the three
- The film and television post-production team uses Premiere Pro + After Effects + Audition, and the project files are nested in each other
- Brand teams share materials in Creative Cloud Libraries and call them across tools

**The difficulty in replacing Adobe is not to replace a single tool, but that no competitor can replace this entire workflow at the same time. ** Canva cannot replace Premiere, Figma cannot replace Illustrator, and Midjourney cannot replace After Effects.

### Misunderstanding 2: Adobe is a traditional software company and AI will eliminate it

This judgment ignores the fact that **Adobe completed the transformation from one-time licensing to a subscription system in 2012** - this was regarded as a huge risk at the time, and revenue fell for a time. But after the transformation was completed, Adobe's recurring revenue ratio increased from less than 30% to more than 93%, and its market value increased from 20 billion to a peak of more than 300 billion.

This shows that Adobe's management has a successful history of navigating technology paradigm shifts. From desktop publishing to the Internet to mobile to cloud to AI, Adobe has completed at least four transformations.

**Contrary view**: But being able to transform in the past does not mean that it will be possible in the future. The subversiveness of AI may far exceed previous technological changes - if "speaking a word can produce an advertisement" becomes a reality, the existence value of Premiere Pro will be fundamentally shaken. We discuss this in detail in Part 03.

### Misunderstanding 3: Adobe is too expensive and PE is very high

This is an outdated knowledge. **Adobe’s current valuation is its lowest in nearly a decade. **

| Valuation Metrics | Current Value (May 2026) | Past 5 Year Average |
|---------|-------------------|--------------|
| GAAP PE | **About 14-19x** | 30-40x |
| Price/Free Cash Flow | **About 9.6x** | 25-35x |
| FCF Yield | **~10.4%** | 3-4% |
| EV/EBITDA | **Approximately 9-10x** (estimate) | 20-25x |

The market's pricing for Adobe has been downgraded from a "high-growth technology stock" to a level close to a "value stock." **The implicit assumption behind this is that Adobe’s growth will slow significantly, or that AI will disrupt its business model. **

Is this assumption correct? This is the core question to be answered in the following articles.
