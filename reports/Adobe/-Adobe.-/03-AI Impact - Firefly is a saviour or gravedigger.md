# "Commerce security": Adobe's most underestimated AI moat

Before discussing AI threats, say one advantage that most people ignore.

# The question of "cleanness" in training data

The AI image generation tools Midjourney, Staable Diffusion, DALL-E, etc. have a common legal grey area:** their training data sources are not transparent and may well contain a large number of unauthorized copyright works.** A number of lawsuits are ongoing (Getty Images vs Stability AI, multiple artist class actions, etc.).

The Adobe Firefly training data are completely different:

- ** Only use Adobe Stock authorized content + public domain content**
- ** Data uploaded by clients not used**
- ** Content not crawling over the Internet**

# What does that mean?

For a personal user, this is not a big difference -- you use Midjourney to create a picture of a circle of friends, and no one will sue you.

However, for the business clients**, this difference is decisive:

♪ The scenes of the MiddleJourney/Stable Diffusion ♪

<unk> Brand advertising <unk> Copyright risk is uncertain <unk> Adobe provides** intellectual property compensation guarantee** <unk>

** Adobe's "Intellectual Property Compensation" means that if someone sues you for using the content generated by Firefly, Adobe will pay for you.** This is a promise that Midjourney, Stable Division cannot offer.

In the years 2025-2026, when AI copyright litigation was frequent, the value of this advantage was increasing. **75% of the wealth 500 strong have already adopted Firefly - Business selection Firefly is largely not because it generates the best quality, but because it is the most "safe" **.

** Counterpart **: If AI copyright law finally establishes the principle that "AI generates content without infringement" (currently not in a unanimous United States court case), Adobe's "commercial security" advantage will be significantly reduced. In addition, competitions like Midjourney are gradually filling this short panel by purchasing training data authorizations.

# AI three levels of threat

Now look at the other side of the coin: AI's threat to Adobe. The threat is not a single one, but three levels, each of which is of different gravity.

# First layer: democratization of low-end markets (has occurred)

** Status**: A good-quality picture can be generated by using Midjourney input. Canva+AI can make a social media poster in 5 minutes.

** Impact**: Work that would have been done by an entry-level designer (simple poster, social media map, product map modification, etc.) can now be done by non-designers.

** Impact on Adobe**: ** Limited.** These users are not the core clientele of Adobe -- either with Canva or with a low-cost designer. AI democratization is about "expanding markets" rather than "stealing Adobe's customers".

**Several: low**

# Second layer: IA penetration in professional workflows increases (ongoing)

** Status**: Professional designers increasingly integrate AI tools into work streams - using Midjourney for inspiration and sketching, and photoshop for refinement; saving time with AI extension/complementary.

** Key question**: When AI becomes part of the workflow, is AI function ** embedded in Adobe tool** or ** independent of Adobe tool**?

The current data is biased towards Adobe:

- The "Generative Fill" built-in Photoshop is one of the most frequently used AI features by professional designers
- Firefly accumulates 24 billion generating assets, mostly used through the Adobe product.
- Adobe's AI functionality is embedded directly in the user's existing workflow and no switch is required

** But there are also adverse signals**: some designers have developed a two-tool process for "Midjoourney biograph <unk> Photoshop". If Midjoourney continues to improve its quality to "no need for photoshop overhaul," Adobe's value will be downgraded from "essential necessities" to "optional".

**Several: medium, continuous monitoring**

#3rd floor: AI fundamentally redefined creative work (not yet done)

** Scenario **: 3-5 years later, AI can directly export a full commercial of commercial quality by simply typing "a 30-second poster for Nike on sneakers, style reference super bowl ad 2024".

If this scenario becomes a reality:

Premiere Pro Timeline Editor lost most of its value
- After Effects, the need for special effects has declined dramatically.
- The need for photoshop's overhaul was replaced by an AI key processing.
- Adobe's core value downgraded from "Creative Tool" to "Ali's Output Fine-Tech Tool"

** This is the greatest long-term risk that Adobe faces — not progressive competition erosion, but a change in the overall paradigm of creative work**

** However, several factors limit the probability of this scenario (at least for 3-5 years)**:

**Accurate control requirements**: Brands need to be accurate to pixel level controls - fonts, colours, graphics, placement of brand elements. AI is not currently able to provide this accuracy.
2. **Addressed change of demand**: Real business projects need to be modified repeatedly ("Move logo 3 pixels to the left" "Calm the color") and AI 's not sufficiently manageable.
3. **Quality consistency**: Brand requires visual consistency of all materials. AI produces random results that are not acceptable in brand management.

** Probability assessment: within 3-5 years, AI has a total probability of replacing professional creative tools by about 15-20%.** The more likely result is that the AI enhances the human creators' model is dominated, and Adobe is in a favourable position in this model.

** Contrast view**: AI may be moving faster than expected. Nobody in 2022 thought AI could make a better picture, and Midjourney V5 could do it in 2023. If AI video generation reached "commercial" level in 2027-2028, Premiere Pro's moat could be more vulnerable than we thought.
