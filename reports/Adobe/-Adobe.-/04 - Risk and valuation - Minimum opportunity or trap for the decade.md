# Risk one: CEO handover -- 18 years of steering is about to leave

# What's going on #

On March 12, 2026, Adobe CEO Shantanu Narayen announced that he would resign from the CEO but remain Chairman after his successor was identified. After the news, the stock price fell 7% per day**.

The 18-year term of Narayen is an extraordinary record:

** This is one of the most successful CEOs in the technology industry in the last 20 years.** He left not because he was bad, but because he was long enough -- 18 years.

# Why is this a risk

1. **AI Strategy is in a critical phase of implementation**: Financial monetization is just beginning, the consumption of points is running and the contract for Enterprise Custom has just begun.
2. ** There is no clear timetable and no successor**: the Board appointed Frank Calderoni, Chief Independent Director, to head the successor committee, taking into account internal and external candidates. As of May 2026, the results were still not available.
3. ** Markets hate uncertainty**: investors need to know who will be at the helm, especially in the midst of AI’s competition for a hot and white world.

# Why don't you panic too much

**Narayen retained as Chairman** - Strategic direction will not be broken off a cliff.
** Internal candidates have a thickness** - David Wadhwani (Director of Digital Media) and Anil Chakravarthhy (President of Digital Experience) have extensive management experience.
3. ** The company does not need "fire fight"** - Adobe is in excellent financial condition, and the new CEO is facing a healthy company, not a mess.
** Repurchase plan is locked** - $25 billion to 2030, not affected by CEO replacement.

** Overall assessment: This is a real but manageable risk.** If the successor is identified in 2026 and is a candidate with strategic AI implementation, the risk will be absorbed. If the succession search is delayed to 2027 or a disputed candidate is selected, the stock price may be further pressurized.

# Risk two: Increased competition — Canva and Figma are about to IPO

# The threat of Canva #

Canva expects IPO 2026. After IPO, it will have more funds:
- Continue the Affectity Freeness strategy to attract Adobe low-end users
- Increase AI functional input
- Canva Enterprise

** But the core user base of Canva has limited overlap with Adobe.** Canva services are the demand for "non-designer design" and Adobe services for "professional designer design." The convergence between the two is mainly among freelancers and small teams, and this segment of the market has an impact on Adobe's total income of about 10-15%.

# Figma's threat

Figma has submitted S-1 confidentially, with 41% income growth (2025).
- Expanding product lines with additional funding (possibly from UI/UX to a wider design area)
- Branding effects are magnified to attract more business customers

** Figma has won in UI/UX.** The key question is: Will it enter Adobe's other core areas (image editing, video, layout)? There is no indication that Figma has this plan, but Figma after IPO has more resources to do this.

# Together #

** The most alarming thing is not the combined strength of the individual competitor, but of the three "Canva + Figma + Open Source AI".** If:
- Unprofessional design. - Eated by Canva.
- UI/UX Designed by Figma
- AI Image/Video Generation <unk> split by Midjourney/ Sora

Adobe's "core area" is still there, but the outer edge is being eroded to the core. It's like a castle -- walls are still there, but the moat is getting narrower.

** Reverse view**: Adobe’s core market (professional image editor, video production, layout, PDF) is very large, and there has been no significant decline in Adobe’s share and stickyness in these areas. Even if the outer edges are eroded, the core plate is of sufficient quality and size to support the current valuation.

# Risk three: Price counter-depreciation + FTC settlement

Adobe price increase of 16.7 per cent, and FTC settlement of **$150 million** in March 2026 for non-transparent subscriptions.

The potential impact of these two events:
- ** User dissatisfaction accumulate** — There are long-standing complaints in social media about the pricing of Adobe subscriptions
- ** Regulation may require a simplified cancellation process** — if cancellation becomes easier, the rate of renewal may be slightly reduced
- ** Brand reputation damaged** -- "Adobe, you can't cancel to lock the user" narrative is spreading.

** But the data are another story**: Q1 SY2026 ** 13% increase in subscription income**, the highest in recent quarters. This suggests that, at least for the present, price reversals have not been reflected in actual data.

** Overall assessment: medium and low risk.** Pricing rights are derived from real conversion costs and competitive underperformance, not sustained by difficulty in eliminating them. FTC reconciliation is more of a one-time reputational damage and is unlikely to change Adobe’s fundamentals.

# Risk four: macroeconomics

If the global recession:
- Businesses may cut their budgets for creative tools to affect Exchange Cloud (market budgets are cut first)
- Possible downgrading of SME packages or suspension of subscriptions
- Consumer-grade consumption may decline

** Mitigating factor**: Adobe was resilient during the 2020 epidemic - home-based work stimulated digital content creation. The "inertity" of subscription mode means that users do not cancel subscriptions because of short-term economic fluctuations.

** Overall assessment: medium risk, but Adobe is more resistant to cycles than most technology companies.**

In April 2026, the Adobe Board of Directors approved a new **25 billion** equity buyback plan, which lasted until April 2030. This is the largest buyback authorization in Adobe ' s history, after the 25 billion buyback plan of March 2024 was almost completed.

**The multiplier effect of the $25 billion buyback on EPS**:

Assuming an average repurchase price of $280, 4 annual repurchases of about 89 million shares, or 22 per cent of the current liquidity unit (approximately 404 million shares)**. Even if the income growth rate is maintained at 10 per cent, the EPS will increase at a rate of **12-15 per cent**.

** Management expressed confidence in corporate value with real silver.** During the sensitive period of the forthcoming handover of the CEO, the board approved such large buybacks, indicating that they considered the current stock price to be significantly below the intrinsic value.

** Counterpart view** Repurchase does not in itself create value – if Adobe's business goes down the path of AI, the buyback is simply a high-level interface with shareholder money. Moreover, Adobe does not pay red, buyback is the only way to return capital, and investors' returns depend entirely on the price of the stock — which may cause embarrassment when market mood continues to decline.

What does market pricing imply?

Inverse: If Adobe calculates the FCF rate of return at current prices (~10.4 per cent), assuming zero growth, investors will return 10.4 per cent annually, which means that the market implicitly assumes that:

- **Adobe future growth is zero or close to zero**
- Or, **AI will significantly erode Adobe's cash flow in the coming years**
- Or, **CEO handover would lead to strategic failure and lower corporate value**

But the actual data for Adobe are:

- Q1 SY2026 Income growth **11%**, subscription income growth **13%**
- AI related ARR growth **3 times + **
- Free cash flow **103 billion **, Q1 record

** There is a marked disconnect between market pricing and real performance.** Either the market is right (knowing something we don't know) or the market is over-exaggerated.
