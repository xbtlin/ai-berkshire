# Jensen Huang Says AI Is a Five-Layer Cake. Who Is Making Money in Each Layer?

This year at Davos and GTC, Jensen Huang repeatedly explained one framework: AI is not a product; it is an entire new industrial system with five layers from bottom to top.

He calls those five layers the "AI cake." I spent a week taking apart each layer, and the investment logic inside is much clearer than I expected.

## First, Define the Five Layers

```
Layer 5  Application layer    AI Agents, autonomous driving, robotics
Layer 4  Model layer          Large language models, multimodal models
Layer 3  Infrastructure layer Data centers, networking, cooling
Layer 2  Chip layer           GPUs, memory, advanced packaging
Layer 1  Energy layer         Power, transformers, nuclear power
```

Jensen Huang's original line: "In the future, every company will have two factories - one that produces products, and one that is an AI factory. The AI factory does only one thing every day: produce tokens."

He offered one core metric for measuring AI factory efficiency: how many tokens are produced per watt. In other words, the AI business will ultimately look like manufacturing: the competition is about how much intelligence each unit of electricity can produce.

## Layer 1: Electricity - The Most Boring but Highest-Certainty Opportunity

Global data center electricity consumption reached 485TWh in 2025 and needs to double within five years. But nearly half of planned U.S. data center projects have already been delayed or canceled. The issue is not a lack of GPUs; it is a lack of power and transformers.

Lead times for large power transformers have already stretched to 4 years. That means 2026 capacity was decided back in 2022, and orders placed today will not be delivered until 2030 at the earliest.

This layer is the least sexy, but it has the highest certainty. Transformer shortages cannot ease before 2028. Power equipment companies such as Eaton and GE Vernova are among the most reasonably valued beneficiaries in the AI supply chain.

## Layer 2: Chips - The $5.0tn Monopoly King

Nvidia remains the "standard weights and measures" of AI compute. FY2027 Q1 revenue reached $81.6bn, up 85% year over year; data center revenue was $75.2bn, up 92%; gross margin was 75%. Its market cap is $5.2tn, the largest in the world.

But the most important shift is this: custom-chip growth (+45%) has surpassed general-purpose GPU growth (+16%) for the first time. Google, Meta, Amazon, and Microsoft are all developing in-house AI chips or working with Broadcom / Marvell on custom designs, with the goal of reducing dependence on Nvidia.

Broadcom's AI revenue this quarter was $10.8bn, up 143% year over year, growing even faster than Nvidia. CEO Hock Tan said he is "confident" AI chip revenue can exceed $100.0bn in 2027, and the company has $73.0bn of backlog.

The five key roles in the chip layer are Nvidia (standard-setter), Broadcom (king of custom chips), AMD (only general-purpose alternative), TSMC (manufacturing monopoly), and SK Hynix (62% HBM memory share).

## Layer 3: Infrastructure - A $725.0bn Arms Race

The four major cloud providers are set to spend a combined $725.0bn in capex in 2026, roughly doubling year over year. Not one of them is cutting spend.

| Company | 2026 Capex | YoY |
|------|--------------|------|
| Amazon | $200.0bn | +60% |
| Microsoft | $190.0bn | +138% |
| Google | $180.0bn | +97% |
| Meta | $135.0bn | +80% |

Where does the money go? Roughly 40-60% goes to GPUs / chips, 20-25% to data center construction, 15-20% to server components, and 10-15% to network equipment.

$725.0bn is real cash, not a PowerPoint slide. As this money flows down the supply chain, it supports companies in optical modules (Zhongji Innolight with RMB 10.8bn in 2025 net income), network equipment (Arista), liquid cooling systems (Vertiv), and servers (Dell).

Zhongji Innolight is especially worth mentioning. It is the strongest pricing-power point for Chinese companies across the entire AI value chain. It supplies roughly half of the world's 800G optical modules, and 35% of Nvidia's orders go to it.

## Layer 4: Models - The Fastest Revenue Growth in History

Anthropic's annualized revenue rose from $1.0bn to $47.0bn in only 14 months. This may be the fastest enterprise revenue growth in business history.

| Company | Valuation | Annualized Revenue |
|------|------|----------|
| Anthropic | $965.0bn | $47.0bn |
| OpenAI | $852.0bn | $24.0bn |
| xAI (SpaceX merger) | $1.75tn target | $3.2bn (xAI portion) |
| DeepSeek | $51.5bn | Undisclosed |

The second half of 2026 will be a major AI IPO window: Anthropic has confidentially filed, and OpenAI is targeting a September listing. That will be the moment when these sky-high valuations are tested by public markets.

DeepSeek is the most interesting variable in this layer. V4-Pro has already surpassed Claude in mathematical reasoning, and API pricing is only one-tenth to one-seventy-fifth of Western competitors. It proves one thing: the model-capability gap is narrowing, but the commercialization gap remains orders of magnitude wide.

## Layer 5: Applications - Few Companies Are Truly Making Money from AI

The harsh reality in this layer is that most "AI concept stocks" still get only a low share of revenue from AI. But valuation dispersion is extreme.

| Company | AI Revenue Growth | P/E |
|------|-----------|-----|
| Palantir | +85% | 105x |
| Tesla | FSD subscriptions +51% | 317x |
| ServiceNow | AI cross-sell 10x | 25x |
| Salesforce | Agentforce +200% | 14x |
| Adobe | Firefly credit consumption 3x | 10x |

Palantir is the purest AI application company, with 85% growth crushing peers, but 105x P/E already discounts a lot. Salesforce and Adobe offer the best risk-reward: 14x and 10x P/E, respectively. Their AI products are growing rapidly, but the market assigns low valuations because total revenue growth is only 10-12%.

If you believe AI application monetization is still early, Salesforce at 14x P/E may be the most undervalued name in the entire AI value chain.

## Who Is Strongest in Each Layer in China?

| Layer | China's Strongest | Gap vs. Global Leader |
|------|---------|-------------|
| Chips | Huawei Ascend | Hardware is 1 generation behind; CUDA ecosystem is 1 era behind |
| Infrastructure | Zhongji Innolight | Has global pricing power in optical modules; the exception |
| Models | DeepSeek | Technology lags by 3-6 months; commercialization lags by 100x |
| Cloud | Alibaba Cloud | Revenue gap is 6x; globalization gap is enormous |
| Applications | iFlytek | Vertical deployment is not weak, but general-purpose applications lag far behind |

China's biggest AI progress is in the model layer (DeepSeek), its biggest bottleneck is in the chip layer (the CUDA ecosystem), and its most underestimated gap is commercialization: the technology has caught up, but the ability to turn technology into money still lags by orders of magnitude.

## Two Closing Thoughts

Jensen Huang's five-layer cake framework gives us a very clear way to think: the lower, more physical, and harder-to-bypass a layer is, the higher the investment certainty.

The sexiest layers are models and applications: Anthropic's $47.0bn revenue, Palantir's 85% growth. But these are also the least certain layers. Valuations are already in the sky, and if growth slows, drawdowns will be sharp.

The most boring layers are energy and infrastructure: transformers, liquid cooling, optical modules. But these are the true bottlenecks of the AI industry. A 4-year transformer lead time is a bottleneck nobody can route around.

In the AI value chain, selling picks and shovels is always more certain than digging for gold. The only problem is that the best shovels are no longer cheap either.
