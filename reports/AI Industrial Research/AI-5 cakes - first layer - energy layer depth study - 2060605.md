# The First Layer of the AI Five-Layer Cake: Who Controls AI's Power Supply?

*In-Depth Research Report | June 2026*

---

## Core Logic: Why Energy Is AI's Ultimate Bottleneck

Every discussion about AI eventually runs into the same wall: there is not enough electricity.

It is not simply a shortage of chips, weak algorithms, or insufficient data. It is electricity. Physical electricity, from power plants to data-center racks. This is the most primitive, cumbersome, and certain bottleneck in the AI value chain.

The International Energy Agency's 2026 report presents striking numbers: global data-center electricity consumption is expected to double from 415 terawatt-hours (TWh) in 2024 to 945 TWh by 2030, implying a compound annual growth rate of roughly 15%, more than four times the electricity-demand growth rate of other industries globally. Electricity consumption at AI-dedicated data centers is growing even faster and is expected to triple between 2025 and 2030. In the United States alone, data centers consumed roughly 4.4% of national electricity in 2023; by 2028, that share is expected to surge to 6.7%-12%. By 2030, U.S. data centers will consume more electricity than all energy-intensive industries combined, including aluminum, steel, cement, and chemicals.

(Sources: IEA, "Energy and AI", 2026; Brookings Institution, "Global energy demands within the AI regulatory landscape", 2026)

Those figures are already remarkable. But what should really keep investors awake at night is not demand growth itself, but the rigid constraints on the supply side.

Transformers, the most critical and least visible equipment in the power grid, have seen lead times stretch from 24-30 months before 2019 to 3-5 years today. The average lead time for large power transformers has reached 128 weeks, or about 2.5 years. Generator step-up transformers (GSU) average 144 weeks, or about 2.8 years, and some high-capacity models require a 4-year wait. Wood Mackenzie data show that the U.S. power-transformer market faces a supply-demand gap of roughly 30% in 2025, while distribution transformers face a 10% gap. Even more critically, domestic U.S. manufacturers can meet only 20% of power-transformer demand; 80% depends on imports.

(Sources: Wood Mackenzie Q2 2025 Survey; PV Magazine, "U.S. transformer market faces severe supply constraints", 2026.5; Power Magazine, "Transformers in 2026: Shortage, Scramble, or Self-Inflicted Crisis?", 2026)

Switchgear, the key equipment connecting data-center campuses to the power grid, is already sold out beyond 2028. A data center may take only 12-18 months to move from land acquisition to completion, but grid interconnection can take 4-10 years. What does that mean? It means "you cannot buy electricity even if you have the money" is not a metaphor. It is a fact. Some 30%-50% of large data centers scheduled to come online in 2026 will be delayed or cancelled, and 11 gigawatts of announced capacity still shows no sign of construction.

(Sources: World Economic Forum, "Is power grid connectivity the strategic bottleneck for AI?", 2026.5; Fortune, "U.S. data center development has hit snags", 2026.3)

Faced with this constraint, hyperscale cloud providers are responding aggressively: they are building their own power plants and bypassing the public grid. Amazon signed an $18 billion nuclear-power direct-supply contract with Talen. Oracle signed a 2.8-gigawatt fuel-cell agreement with Bloom Energy. Meta signed a 2,609-megawatt nuclear power purchase agreement with Vistra. The essence of these deals is the same: the grid is too slow, so we will do it ourselves.

Infrastructure capex plans across 51 U.S. utilities have already jumped to $1.4 trillion, up 27% year over year. But even with that much money being deployed, the pace of grid expansion still cannot keep up with AI's appetite, constrained by equipment lead times, labor shortages, and permitting processes.

(Source: Tech Insider, "US Utilities Plan $1.4T for AI Data Centers: 27% Capex Surge", 2026)

This is why the energy layer is the most certain investment opportunity in the AI five-layer cake. Chips can be replaced; Nvidia's position is not eternal. Software can be disrupted; OpenAI's advantage today may disappear tomorrow. But the physical constraints of power infrastructure are rigid. A transformer that takes 4 years to deliver will not change because of anyone's PowerPoint deck. The need to upgrade the grid will not stop even if the AI bubble bursts, because even without AI, the U.S. grid is already old enough to require large-scale replacement.

The certainty of the investment logic comes from rigid demand on the demand side, as AI electricity consumption will only rise, and bottlenecks on the supply side, as equipment lead times are measured in years and capacity expansion is slow. In the five-layer cake, the closer one gets to the "dirty work" at the bottom, the less glamorous the business looks, but the more certain it becomes.

Below is a company-by-company analysis of 10 firms that sit at critical choke points in AI power.

## 1. AZZ Inc. (AZZ) — Hot-Dip Galvanizing: The "Anti-Corrosion Layer" for Grid Steel

**Bottleneck Positioning**

AZZ does something very simple: it galvanizes steel. That may not sound exciting, but every transmission tower, every substation support structure, and every bridge steel structure needs hot-dip galvanizing to resist corrosion and operate outdoors for 30-50 years. Grid expansion means more transmission towers and substations, and each one needs AZZ's services. This is one of the least visible but least avoidable links in the AI energy chain.

| FY2026 revenue | $1.65 billion (+4.6%) |

| Adjusted EPS | $6.19 (+19%) |

(Sources: AZZ FY2026 full-year earnings report, 2026.4.22; StockAnalysis.com)

**Irreplaceability Analysis**

AZZ is North America's largest independent post-fabrication hot-dip galvanizing company, operating 42 galvanizing plants. The moat in this industry is not advanced technology, but a "heavy geographic monopoly": hot-dip galvanizing plants are capital-intensive, with tens of millions of dollars required to build a single plant, and their service radius is limited because the cost of transporting steel components restricts how far they can economically serve customers. That naturally creates oligopolistic regional markets. New entrants face both high capital expenditure and local customer relationships that are difficult to dislodge. AZZ's 42-plant network across the United States is its moat: dense enough, broad enough, and difficult for competitors to attack one market at a time.

**Valuation View**

At 12.8x P/E, AZZ is the cheapest name across the AI energy chain. EBITDA margin is 22.3%, revenue is growing steadily, and free cash flow is ample. Compared with peers, this valuation is almost being given away. The market assigns AZZ a low multiple for a simple reason: galvanizing is not exciting, and no one is likely to hype a stock around a "hot-dip galvanizing theme." But that is exactly what value investors should like: market neglect equals margin of safety.

**Risk Factors**

Revenue growth is only 4.6%, so this is not a high-growth story. If steel prices fluctuate sharply or infrastructure investment falls short of expectations, performance will be affected. Over the long term, if grid expansion is slower than expected, for example if an AI bubble bursts and data-center demand declines, AZZ's growth logic would weaken. But it would not disappear: replacement demand from grid aging alone is enough to support the base business.

**One-Sentence Conclusion**: A regional-monopoly hidden champion at 13x P/E, AZZ is the most reasonably valued and most overlooked company in the AI power chain.

## 2. Archrock (AROC) — Natural-Gas Compression Services: The "Blood Pump" of AI Power

**Bottleneck Positioning**

Natural gas is the largest fuel source for U.S. electricity generation, accounting for roughly 40%. Throughout the process of moving gas from wellhead to power plant, continuous compression is required to keep gas flowing through pipelines. That is what Archrock does. Without compression services, natural gas cannot leave the well, move through pipelines, or reach power plants. As AI data-center electricity consumption surges, demand for natural-gas power generation surges with it, and so does demand for compression services. This is the "circulatory system" of the AI energy chain.

**Key Financial Data**

| Metric | Value |
|------|------|
| Market cap | $6.1 billion |
| Share price | $34.61 |
| P/E (TTM) | 18.7x |
| 2025 full-year revenue | $1.49 billion (+28.7%) |
| 2025 adjusted EBITDA | $900 million |
| 2026 EBITDA guidance | $865-915 million |
| Dividend yield | 2.56% |

(Sources: Archrock 2025 full-year and Q1 2026 earnings reports; StockAnalysis.com)

**Irreplaceability Analysis**

Archrock is the largest natural-gas compression services provider in the United States. Its moat lies in scale and customer stickiness: compression equipment is heavy-asset infrastructure, with individual units worth millions of dollars, and once installed at a customer's well site, the switching cost is extremely high. Archrock owns roughly 3.6 million active horsepower of compression units, equipment utilization has stayed above 95% for 11 consecutive quarters, and 85% of 2026 horsepower has already been contracted. This model of "heavy assets + long-term contracts + high switching costs" naturally creates a moat. The company's acquisition of Total Operations Processing Services (TOPS) in early 2026 further strengthened its market position.

**Valuation View**

At 18.7x P/E and 17.7x forward P/E, Archrock is reasonably to modestly valued among energy-services companies. The 2.56% dividend yield provides an additional cushion. 2026 EBITDA guidance is slightly below 2025 because 2025 included some one-time gains, but the core business is still growing. Compared with USA Compression (USAC, P/E 29x), Archrock's valuation is more attractive.

**Risk Factors**

Natural-gas price volatility can affect upstream production, which in turn affects demand for compression services. If natural-gas prices remain depressed, some marginal oil and gas wells may be shut in, reducing compression demand. Debt is also relatively high, with $2.4 billion of long-term debt and a 2.6x leverage ratio, so financial-cost pressure rises in a higher-rate environment.

**One-Sentence Conclusion**: The clear leader in natural-gas compression, with a sub-19x P/E and a 2.5% dividend yield, Archrock is a direct beneficiary of AI-driven electricity demand and offers a reasonable valuation with downside support.

**One-Sentence Conclusion**: A same-lane competitor to Archrock, USAC suits income-oriented investors with its 7%+ dividend yield, but the valuation is slightly higher and the MLP structure adds complexity.

Hubbell sits across the full chain from grid "upgrade" to grid "connection." Its products cover arresters, insulators, connectors, switches, and other transmission and distribution grid equipment, as well as data-center wiring systems, power-distribution equipment, and lighting. Put simply: between a substation and a server rack, Hubbell has something wherever electricity needs to flow. Grid expansion needs Hubbell, and data-center construction needs Hubbell. Both demand streams converge in the same company.

**Irreplaceability Analysis**

Hubbell's moat is "wide but shallow": it is not the sole supplier for any single product, but its product breadth, distribution-network depth, and customer-relationship density in electrical infrastructure create an overall advantage. In Q1 2026, data-center-related revenue surged 40% year over year, while grid infrastructure grew 12%. **One-Sentence Conclusion**: An "all-around player" in electrical infrastructure, Hubbell trades at 29x P/E, low relative to peers; 18% ROIC proves operating quality, while data centers and the grid provide dual growth drivers.

## 5. Vistra (VST) — Nuclear + Natural-Gas Generation: Selling Power Directly to AI

**Bottleneck Positioning**

Vistra does not sell equipment; it generates electricity directly. With roughly 50 gigawatts of generation capacity after completing the Cogentrix acquisition, including nuclear, natural gas, coal, solar, and storage, it is one of the largest competitive power producers in the United States. It controls the most fundamental link: where electricity comes from. Its nuclear assets in particular, including Comanche Peak and Perry, have become targets for technology giants. Meta signed a 2,609-megawatt nuclear power purchase agreement, and the Perry nuclear plant received NRC approval to operate through 2046.

**Key Financial Data**

| Share price | $152.79 |
| P/E (TTM) | 25.8x |
| Forward P/E | 16.6x |

| TTM revenue | $19.45 billion (+7.4%) |

(Sources: Vistra Q1 2026 earnings report; StockAnalysis.com)

**Irreplaceability Analysis**

Nuclear plants are Vistra's deepest moat. Building a new nuclear plant takes 10-15 years, NRC licenses are almost impossible to replicate, and existing nuclear plants have very low operating costs, zero carbon emissions, and extremely high generation stability. These traits make nuclear power the ideal baseload electricity source for AI data centers. Vistra also owns substantial natural-gas generation assets, adding 5,500 megawatts after the Cogentrix acquisition, which provide peaking capacity. The combination of "nuclear for baseload + natural gas for peaking" perfectly matches the 24/7 electricity demand of data centers.

**Valuation View**

The TTM P/E of 25.8x does not look cheap, but the forward P/E is only 16.6x because 2026 EBITDA guidance of $6.8-7.6 billion implies a major increase from 2025. On a market-cap-to-EBITDA basis, the current valuation is roughly 7x EV/EBITDA, reasonable for an independent power producer. The analyst consensus target price is $225, implying roughly 47% upside. The current share price of $152 is down about 30% from the 52-week high of $220, offering a decent entry window.

**Risk Factors**

Power-price volatility, especially in the ERCOT market, directly affects profits. If natural-gas prices decline and power prices fall, revenue may disappoint even if electricity volume increases. The Cogentrix acquisition, expected to close in the second half of 2026, carries integration risk. While nuclear plants have strong safety records, any accident would be devastating for the share price.

**One-Sentence Conclusion**: At 16.6x forward P/E, America's largest competitive power producer offers a nuclear + natural-gas mix that perfectly fits AI electricity demand; after a 30% pullback from the high, Vistra is one of the best large-cap risk-reward opportunities in the energy layer.

## 6. Powell Industries (POWL) — Customized Medium-Voltage Switchgear: The "Power Gatekeeper" for Data Centers

**Bottleneck Positioning**

Every large data center needs medium-voltage switchgear to manage and distribute electricity entering the campus. That is exactly what Powell makes: customized medium-voltage and low-voltage switchgear and power-distribution systems. The key word is "customized," because each data center has a different power architecture and standardized products cannot meet every requirement. As switchgear is sold out beyond 2028 and becomes a physical bottleneck for AI infrastructure, Powell, as a specialist in this niche, has seen orders explode.

**Key Financial Data**

| Metric | Value |
|------|------|
| Market cap | $11.0 billion |
| Share price | $301.50 |
| P/E (TTM) | 58.9x |
| Forward P/E | 51.9x |
| FY2025 revenue | $1.1 billion (+9.1%) |
| TTM revenue | $1.13 billion (+4.5%) |
| Backlog | $1.8 billion (+33%) |
| Cash | $501 million |

(Sources: Powell FY2025 and Q2 FY2026 earnings reports; StockAnalysis.com)

**Irreplaceability Analysis**

Powell's moat lies in "customization capability + engineering experience + customer trust." Medium-voltage switchgear is not a standardized commodity. It must be custom-designed and manufactured around each project's power architecture, with high engineering complexity and long delivery cycles. Powell has accumulated 70 years of experience in this field, and once customers validate the company, they rarely switch suppliers. The company recently won the largest single order in its history: an AI data-center project worth more than $400 million, using a behind-the-meter design, meaning self-owned generation directly distributes power. This is exactly the new trend among hyperscale cloud providers seeking to bypass the grid. Backlog of $1.8 billion (+33%) and a doubling of new orders, with Q2 FY2026 new orders at $490 million versus $249 million a year earlier, provide very high order visibility.

**Valuation View**

At 59x P/E, Powell is expensive by any conventional measure. But the issue is that $1.8 billion of backlog, roughly 1.6x annual revenue, is still accelerating, creating high certainty around revenue over the next 2-3 years. If one values the company on revenue and margins after backlog conversion, the P/E could fall to 30-35x. Still, the market has already priced in a meaningful portion of this growth: the stock has risen more than fivefold from its 2024 low of $56 to above $300. At the current price, the margin of safety is insufficient.

**Risk Factors**

The valuation is extremely high. If order growth slows or the pace of data-center construction falls short of expectations, the risk of a share-price correction is significant. The company is relatively small, with a $11.0 billion market cap and only $1.1 billion of annual revenue, so changes in orders from a single large customer could create meaningful earnings volatility. Manufacturing capacity expansion takes time; if demand arrives too quickly and capacity cannot keep up, orders may be handed to competitors.

**One-Sentence Conclusion**: Powell sits squarely in the bullseye of the AI data-center power bottleneck, with extremely high order-growth certainty, but a 59x P/E has already discounted too much optimism; it is better suited for investors willing to wait for a pullback.

## 7. NuScale Power (SMR) — The Only NRC-Certified SMR: A "Future Bet" on Nuclear Power

**Bottleneck Positioning**

If existing nuclear plants such as Vistra and Talen are the "stock solution" for AI electricity demand, NuScale is the "incremental solution." It is the only small modular reactor (SMR) company in the world to receive standard design approval from the U.S. Nuclear Regulatory Commission (NRC), with a single-module output of 77 megawatts of electricity. The appeal of SMRs is that they are smaller, more flexible, faster to build, and can be deployed near data centers. In theory, they are the ultimate solution for AI data centers seeking local power supply.

**Key Financial Data**

| Metric | Value |
|------|------|
| Market cap | $4.4 billion |
| Share price | $12.09 |
| P/E | N/A (continuing losses) |

| Net loss (TTM) | $386 million |
| EPS (TTM) | -$1.82 |
| Cash and capital resources | About $1.2 billion |

(Sources: NuScale Q1 2026 earnings report; StockAnalysis.com)

**Irreplaceability Analysis**

NuScale has only one moat: NRC certification. This certification took 15 years and billions of dollars to obtain. Any competitor trying to start from zero would need at least 10 years to repeat the process. But this moat is "conditional": if NuScale's technology path ultimately proves commercially infeasible because costs are too high or construction takes too long, the certification loses much of its value.

The key progress item is the 6-gigawatt deployment plan with TVA (Tennessee Valley Authority) and ENTRA1, the largest nuclear deployment project in U.S. history, with power purchase agreements expected to be finalized before the end of 2026. The Romania RoPower project, involving 6 modules, is also moving forward. But all of these remain in the "agreement/planning" stage and are still far from contributing meaningful revenue.

**Valuation View**

With a $4.4 billion market cap, annual revenue below $20 million, and a TTM loss of $386 million, this is entirely "option pricing." Buying NuScale is not buying a company with current revenue; it is buying a long-dated option on "SMRs becoming a standard power source for AI data centers." The $1.2 billion cash reserve means the company is unlikely to fail in the near term, but with a burn rate of roughly $400 million of losses per year, if the TVA contract and commercialization progress disappoint, refinancing may be needed in 2-3 years, making equity dilution unavoidable.

**Risk Factors**

The risk is extremely high. There is no stable revenue, and the valuation depends on one or two flagship projects. SMR construction costs and timelines may substantially exceed expectations in practice; the cancelled UAMPS project is the cautionary example. At the current burn rate, $1.2 billion of cash covers only about 3 years. Although the technology path has received NRC certification, there is still a large gulf between certification and scaled commercialization. Beta is 2.22, implying very high volatility.

**One-Sentence Conclusion**: NuScale is the purest listed name in the AI nuclear-power narrative, but it is essentially an expensive option: if TVA's 6GW project lands, the share price could multiply; if the project is delayed or cancelled, most of the $4.4 billion market cap could evaporate.

High valuation is the biggest risk. Prolec integration will take time and could weigh on margins in the near term. If AI infrastructure investment slows because of macroeconomic or policy factors, the very long backlog may face cancellation or delay risk. The company was spun out from GE only in 2024, so it has a short operating history as an independent entity.

**One-Sentence Conclusion**: GE Vernova is the most comprehensive "super arms dealer" in the AI energy chain, with a $163.0 billion backlog providing very strong revenue certainty, but a $258.0 billion market cap and 50+ forward P/E require a careful balance between growth validation and valuation risk.

## 10. Bloom Energy (BE) — Fuel Cells Supplying Data Centers Directly: The "Ultimate Workaround" for the Grid

**Bottleneck Positioning**

If grid interconnection takes 4-10 years, why not bypass the grid entirely? Bloom Energy's answer is solid oxide fuel cells (SOFC), installed directly next to data centers and powered by natural gas or hydrogen. The system generates power on site and can be deployed quickly. The fatal attraction of this approach is speed: fuel cells can be deployed within months, while traditional grid expansion is measured in years. Oracle (2.8 gigawatts), AEP ($2.65 billion), AWS, CoreWeave, and Equinix have all signed contracts.

**Irreplaceability Analysis**

Bloom Energy's moat is its lead in its chosen technology path. Solid oxide fuel cells (SOFC) are not a new concept, but globally, Bloom is the only company that has commercialized and deployed them at scale. Its products can use natural gas, biogas, and hydrogen, enabling a gradual future transition to zero-carbon fuels. Fast deployment, measured in months rather than years, small land footprint, and high operating efficiency, with electrical efficiency of roughly 60%, far above gas turbines at 30-40%, make it the preferred "emergency power" option for hyperscale cloud providers.

The roughly $20.0 billion total backlog, consisting of $6.0 billion of products and $14.0 billion of services, plus Brookfield's $5.0 billion financing framework, validates confidence among leading customers. If Oracle's 2.8-gigawatt order is fully executed, that one customer alone can support several years of revenue.

**Valuation View**

An $83.0 billion market cap, only $6 million of TTM net income, and a 126x forward P/E make this a "belief-based valuation" by any standard. What the market is pricing for Bloom Energy is not today's profits, but the future scenario in which "fuel cells become a standard power source for AI data centers."

It is worth noting that the average analyst target price is $263, below the current share price of $292. That means mainstream Wall Street analysts believe the current share price is already expensive. A beta of 3.75 means this stock is nearly 4 times as volatile as the market. It rises violently, having climbed from a 52-week low of $20 to $323, up 15x, but it can also fall brutally.

**Risk Factors**

Valuation is the biggest risk: the $83.0 billion market cap has almost no profit support. Long-term reliability and maintenance costs for fuel cells may reveal problems after large-scale deployment. If natural-gas prices rise sharply, the economics of fuel-cell generation deteriorate. Competition is a risk: if traditional generation solutions such as gas turbines or diesel generators catch up in deployment speed, or if grid expansion accelerates, Bloom's "bypass the grid" narrative weakens. Technology substitution is another risk: next-generation storage technologies, such as long-duration storage, could provide another way to bypass the grid.

**One-Sentence Conclusion**: Bloom Energy is the most aggressive "breakthrough" name in the AI energy layer, with a $20.0 billion backlog and 130% revenue growth that are genuinely impressive, but an $83.0 billion market cap with almost no profit support makes it a high-upside but extremely high-risk bet.
