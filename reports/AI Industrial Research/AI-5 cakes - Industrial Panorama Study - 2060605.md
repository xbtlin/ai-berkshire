## Stock-Selection Criteria (Investment Candidate Screening Framework)

Goal: identify companies that control bottlenecks, or companies the market is overlooking, as investment candidates.

| Dimension | Criterion | Description |
|------|------|------|
| **Bottleneck purity** | Bottleneck business contributes >30% of revenue | The purer the better; avoid paying for an entire mine just to own one diamond |
| **Irreplaceability** | Sole supplier or oligopoly supplier | The longer it takes for alternatives to emerge, the better; more than 2 years is preferable |
| **Market pricing** | Whether the share price fully reflects the bottleneck thesis | A P/E above 100x is likely already fully priced; overlooked angles can still exist even in mega-cap companies |
| **Catalyst window** | Whether near-term validation events exist | Earnings, capacity-expansion milestones, contract signings, industry supply-demand inflection points |

Each company is scored on four dimensions (1-5 stars), with a maximum total score of 20.

## Framework Definition

In Jensen Huang's 2025 Davos conversation with BlackRock CEO Larry Fink, he described the AI industry as a "five-layer cake":

```
Layer 5 | Application layer    | AI Agents, autonomous driving, robotics, vertical solutions
Layer 4 | Model layer          | Large language models, multimodal models
Layer 3 | Infrastructure layer | AI factories, data centers, networking, cooling
Layer 2 | Chip layer           | GPUs, accelerators, HBM memory, advanced packaging
Layer 1 | Energy layer         | Power supply, transformers, nuclear power
```

His core point: AI is not a product; it is an entirely new industrial system. Every layer contains major investment opportunities, and the lower layers are the hardest to replace.

He calls data centers "AI factories" - facilities that "do only one thing every day: produce tokens." In the future, every company will have two factories: one that makes products, and one that is an AI factory.

## Layer 1: Energy Layer - The Most Undervalued Bottleneck

**Core logic:** The endpoint of AI compute is electricity. Global data center power consumption reached 485TWh in 2025 and is expected to double to 950TWh by 2030. Nearly half of planned U.S. data center construction projects have already been delayed or canceled, not because GPUs are unavailable, but because power and transformers are unavailable.

**Bottleneck data:** Lead times for large power transformers have stretched to 2-4 years, and high-voltage substations have lead times of 3-5 years.

### Representative Companies (5)

| Company | Positioning | Key Data |
|------|------|----------|
| **Eaton** | Electrical equipment leader | Global leader in data center power distribution systems; benefits from transformer shortages |
| **GE Vernova** | Power equipment + gas-fired generation | Energy company spun out of GE; benefits from both transformers and gas turbines |
| **Constellation Energy** | Nuclear power operator | Largest U.S. nuclear power operator; signed nuclear power supply agreement with Microsoft |
| **NuScale Power** | Small modular reactor (SMR) | Only NRC-certified SMR design, targeting long-term data center power supply |
| **TBEA** | China's transformer leader | China's transformer exports to the U.S. surged from 1,500 units in 2022 to 8,000+ units in 2025 |

**Why these names:** A 4-year transformer lead time means this bottleneck cannot ease before 2028. Nuclear power is the ultimate long-term answer for AI data center power supply. This layer has the highest certainty, the lowest attention, and the most reasonable valuations.

## Layer 2: Chip Layer - The Most Value-Dense Battleground

**Core logic:** The AI accelerator market is expected to exceed $150.0bn in 2026. Nvidia still holds roughly 75-80% share, but custom ASIC growth (+45%) has surpassed general-purpose GPU growth (+16%) for the first time. Hyperscalers' push to reduce dependence on Nvidia is a structural trend.

### Representative Companies (5)

| Company | Positioning | Key Data |
|------|------|----------|
| **Nvidia** | AI GPU monopolist | FY2027 Q1 revenue of $81.6bn (+85%), data center revenue of $75.2bn (+92%), 75% gross margin, $5.2tn market cap |
| **Broadcom** | King of custom ASICs | Q2 FY2026 AI revenue of $10.8bn (+143%); designs custom chips for Google / Meta / OpenAI; $73.0bn backlog |
| **AMD** | Only general-purpose GPU challenger | Q1 2026 data center revenue of $5.8bn (+57%); OpenAI chose AMD to build 6GW of compute capacity |
| **TSMC** | AI chip manufacturing monopoly | More than 90% global share in advanced processes; CoWoS packaging fully booked through 2026; the physical bottleneck of the entire chain |
| **SK Hynix** | HBM memory leader | 62% HBM market share; Samsung plunged from 41% to 17%; HBM is the indispensable "ammunition" for AI chips |

**Why these names:** Nvidia is the standard-setter but faces erosion from custom chips; Broadcom is the biggest custom-chip winner and growing faster; AMD is the only viable alternative; TSMC and SK Hynix control the physical bottlenecks of the whole chain. Together, these five names define the AI chip layer.

## Layer 3: Infrastructure Layer - A $725.0bn Arms Race

**Core logic:** The four major hyperscale cloud providers (Microsoft + Amazon + Google + Meta) are expected to spend roughly $725.0bn in capex in 2026, up 97% year over year. Not one is cutting investment; all are raising it. The money goes into servers, network equipment, data center construction, and cooling systems.

### Representative Companies (5)

| Company | Positioning | Key Data |
|------|------|----------|
| **Arista Networks** | AI data center network switches | AI networking revenue growing rapidly; 800G / 1.6T Ethernet solutions; competing with Broadcom as an InfiniBand alternative |
| **Vertiv** | Data center power + liquid cooling | No. 1 in global liquid cooling with 11.3% share; NVL72 architecture forcing liquid cooling is the core catalyst |
| **Zhongji Innolight** | Global leader in AI optical modules | 2025 revenue of RMB 38.2bn (+60%), net income of RMB 10.8bn (+109%), roughly 50% share in 800G |
| **Dell Technologies** | Leader in AI server shipments | AI server backlog above $9.0bn; deeply tied to Nvidia |
| **CoreWeave** | Pure-play GPU cloud infrastructure | 2025 revenue of $5.13bn (+170%), $99.4bn backlog, but total debt of $21.4bn and GAAP losses |

**Why these names:** Networking (Arista), power and cooling (Vertiv), optical interconnects (Zhongji Innolight), servers (Dell), and GPU cloud (CoreWeave) cover the full infrastructure chain from power to compute delivery. Zhongji Innolight is the Chinese company with the strongest pricing power across the entire AI value chain.

OpenAI's monthly revenue is $2.0bn. The second half of 2026 will bring a dense wave of AI company IPOs, with Anthropic having confidentially filed and OpenAI targeting September.

## Layer 5: Application Layer - Who Is Actually Making Money from AI?

**Core logic:** Most so-called "AI concept stocks" still derive only a small share of revenue from AI. Few companies are truly making money from AI, but valuations are extremely dispersed: Palantir trades at 105x P/E, while Salesforce trades at only 14x.

### Representative Companies (5)

| Company | Positioning | Key Data |
|------|------|----------|
| **Palantir** | AI decision operating system | Q1 2026 revenue of $1.63bn (+85%), 44% net margin, 75% AIP Boot Camp conversion, but P/E of 105x |
| **Salesforce** | Enterprise AI Agent applications | Agentforce ARR above $1.0bn, 29,000 deals, FY2026 total revenue of $41.5bn, P/E only 14x - best risk-reward among AI application names |
| **ServiceNow** | Enterprise AI workflows | Now Assist ACV target above $1.0bn, subscription revenue +22%, ITSM monopoly position, P/E 25x |
| **Tesla** | Autonomous driving + robotics | 1.28mn FSD subscribers, Robotaxi operating in Dallas / Houston, Optimus mass production in summer 2026, but P/E 317x |
| **Adobe** | AI creative tools | Firefly generated 24.0bn+ assets, 75% Fortune 500 adoption, but direct AI revenue only $400.0mn, lowest forward P/E at 10x |

**Why these names:** Palantir has the fastest AI application revenue growth (+85%); Salesforce is the most reasonably valued large-cap AI application name (P/E 14x); ServiceNow is the clearest enterprise AI monetization case; Tesla represents AI's ultimate physical form (autonomous driving + robotics); Adobe defines the AI creative category.

## Investment Perspective Summary

### Investment Opportunities Ranked by Certainty

| Priority | Layer | Logic | Representative Names |
|--------|------|------|----------|
| 1 | Energy layer | 4-year transformer lead times cannot be resolved quickly; the highest-certainty bottleneck | Eaton, GE Vernova |
| 2 | Chip layer | The "standard weights and measures" of AI compute, with the deepest moats | Nvidia (valuation requires scrutiny), Broadcom (faster growth) |
| 3 | Infrastructure layer | $725.0bn in capex is real money | Zhongji Innolight, Vertiv, Arista |
| 4 | Model layer | Most explosive growth, but most leaders are still private | Wait for Anthropic / OpenAI IPOs |
| 5 | Application layer | Few companies are truly making money from AI | Salesforce (P/E 14x) and Adobe (P/E 10x) offer the best risk-reward |

### One Sentence

The lower, more physical, and harder-to-bypass a link is, the higher the investment certainty. The sexiest layers - models and applications - are actually the least certain.
