** Worst-case scenario:** If US-China relations deteriorate further, US government may restrict exports of more semiconductor products to China. ** Good news:** McVal is "natural slamming" -- as the data centre (mainly US clients) continues to rise from 74%, China will fall in relative terms. Management expects that the data centre ratio will increase further, which will dilute China’s exposure.

** The counter-view:** "Natural dilution" sounds good, but what if China's revenues were not diluted but cut off suddenly**? A single export control order could have caused 43% of revenues to fall sharply in a few quarters, while data centre revenues could not fill the gap in the same time frame.

Source: Marvell Calendar Report

**FY2024 Income fell by 7% -- just two years ago.** The narrative changed from "semiconductor supercycle" to "stock adjustment, weak demand".

The current AI narrative is indeed supported by real demand, but "this time different" is the most dangerous word in investment.** AI capital spending is also periodic.** Cloud producers base their decisions on capital spending on expectations of AI income -- once AI is commercialized less than expected (most AI applications have not yet found a stable profit model), capital spending may contract rapidly.

** Inverse:** AI capital expenditure is different from the previous semiconductor cycle - not a "building stock"-driven cycle, but a structural input driven by "infrastructure." Similar cloud computing 10-year capital expenditure cycle for 2015-2025, rather than a chip inventory cycle for 2021-2022. But even structural inputs, the rate of growth fluctuates - from +50% to +15%, and is a disaster for companies about 61 times as much as GAAP PE.

# Write it in the end

Four articles, starting with "Who's Maywell?", we've de-structed business models, AI opportunities, risks and valuations.

Finally, leave a few core judgements for you to weigh:

** On quality of business:** Maywell is a business with a "middle-top" -- real technical barriers and customer lock-in, but essentially "big-client outsourcing design," limited bargaining power and less autonomy than platform-type companies.

** On growth prospects:** The structural demand for AI custom chips is real, and Mayir is in the right place. But growing from second to first in parallel takes a lot of things to do at the same time. More likely, it ends up being the most popular of the AI era: important but un-dominated players.

**Assessment:** GAAP PE about 61 times, and has been priced with too much optimism. Good companies can also be a poor investment in the wrong price.

** On management:** Murphy’s strategic vision and implementation are the most scarce assets of Mawell. If there is any reason why this company deserves long-term follow-up, this person is the most important one.
