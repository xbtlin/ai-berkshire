## Three common misconceptions about Michael

### Misunderstanding 1: McVell is a competitor of NVIDIA

This is the most common misconception.

What NVIDIA does is a **universal GPU** - a chip sold to everyone, and it relies on the platform ecosystem and CUDA to lock in developers. What Maywell does is **customized ASIC** - designing special chips according to customer needs, one chip for each customer.

The relationship between the two is more like **customized suit shop vs. Uniqlo**:

| Dimension | NVIDIA (General Purpose GPU) | Marvell (Custom ASIC) |
|------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Products | Standardized GPU, sold to everyone | Customer-specific chips, one-to-one design |
| Customers | Thousands of enterprises + cloud vendors | 3-4 very large cloud vendors |
| Pricing power | Extremely strong (monopoly position) | Limited (the customer is Party A) |
| Gross profit margin | **75%+** | **~61%** |
| Scale | Revenue **~$130 billion** | Revenue **~$8.2 billion** |

McVell is not a replacement for NVIDIA - but rather an alternative for cloud vendors who don't want to rely entirely on NVIDIA. AWS uses Trainium not because it’s better than Nvidia GPUs, but because AWS doesn’t want to leave its fate to one vendor.

**Contrary view:** Precisely because custom ASIC is an "alternative solution" rather than an "optimal solution", once NVIDIA significantly cuts prices or launches more cost-effective products, cloud manufacturers' demand for customized chips may shrink rapidly.

### Misunderstanding 2: Marvell is just a "small company"

With a market capitalization of US$160 billion, Marvell is already among the top 10 companies in the global semiconductor industry. But compared with the real giants:

| Company | Market Cap | Multiples to Marvell |
|------|------|------------|
| NVIDIA | ~$3.2 trillion | **20x** |
| Broadcom | ~$1.1 trillion | **7x** |
| Marvel | ~$160 billion | Benchmark |

Marvell ranks second in the custom ASIC field, but there is a clear gap with the first Broadcom. Broadcom holds about **55%-70%** of the custom ASIC market (Source: TradingKey, SimplyWall St), and McVell has about **15%**.

**This is not an inspirational story of "small companies chasing big companies" - this is a survival issue of "can the second place live well?" **

### Misunderstanding 3: AI chip companies are all the same

In the semiconductor industry, "AI chips" include at least three completely different businesses:

| Type | Representative company | Business model | Source of moat |
|------|---------|---------|-----------|
| General GPU | NVIDIA | Platform + Ecosystem + Standard Products | CUDA Developer Lock |
| Customized ASIC | Broadcom, Marvell | Design special chips according to customer needs | Design cycle lock + IP accumulation |
| Optical interconnection | Marvell, Coherent | High-speed channels connecting chips | Technology generation difference + yield barrier |

Melville does the latter two at the same time - **Custom ASIC + Optical Interconnect**. This is the key to its differentiation from Broadcom: Broadcom's ASICs are larger, but Marvell has more than **60%** of the market share in optical interconnects (Source: Marvell IR).

**Contrary view:** Although optical interconnect has a high market share, its TAM is much smaller than ASIC. Marvel's "differentiation" is essentially to be a big fish in a smaller pond, rather than competing head-on with Broadcom on the main battlefield.

## Why watch Marvel now?

Three things happen at the same time:

**First, the custom ASIC market is exploding. **

Hyperscale cloud vendors (AWS, Google, Microsoft, Meta) are collectively "de-Nvidia" - not to abandon Nvidia, but to reduce dependence on a single supplier. Custom ASICs are one of their core strategies.

Data given by management on the FY2026 Q4 earnings call: The custom ASIC business grew from near zero to annualized **$1.5 billion** in just one fiscal year. Management forecasts overall revenue in FY2027 to exceed $11 billion** (+30%+) and to approach $15 billion in FY2028**.

**Second, Nvidia personally invested in the game. **

On March 31, 2026, Nvidia announced an investment of **2 billion** in Marvell, and the two parties will cooperate to develop the NVLink Fusion interconnect solution. This means that NVIDIA does not regard McVell as a competitor, but as an "ecosystem partner" (Source: NVIDIA official announcement).

**Contrary view:** NVIDIA's investment in Marvel may also be "turning potential opponents into vassals" - when you accept NVIDIA's money and ecosystem, how much independence do you still have?

**Third, valuations are already extremely optimistic. **

Currently **~61x GAAP PE**, **~47x Forward PE**, **~19x PS** - the market has priced in the next 3-4 years of growth. If everything goes well, the returns may be good; if anything goes wrong, the downside is huge.

**This is the core paradox of Maywell’s current situation: Growth is indeed happening, but prices are indeed reflecting it. **
