# Product Line I: Customise ASISC (Custom XPU)

What's this?

ASIC = Application-Specific Integrated Circuit, dedicated to integrated circuits. Translation into speech: ** AI chip designed to customize to client needs**.

The GPU of Yveta is the "general kitchen knife" -- anything cut, but not the most efficient; customized ASIC is the "special surgery knife" -- one thing, but very efficient and very low-capacity.

Why would a super-large cloud manufacturer customize a chip? Three reasons:

** Reduced reliance on British Weida** - not to be stuck by a supplier
** Efficiency of specific loads** — ASIC performance power consumption in the reasoning scenario can be 3-5 times higher than GPU
** Reduction of total cost of ownership (TCO)** — Efficiency differences per watt are magnified when deployed on a large scale

# What's Mavel doing in there?

Maywell did not design the whole chip from scratch -- it provided a key IP module** (SerDes high-speed interface, interconnection logic, containment program, etc.), working with the client's self-research design team, and eventually integrating it into a complete chip.

The analogy is: AWS is an architect and Mawell is a professional contractor for steel structures and plumbing systems. The house is AWS, but the core bone is from Mawell.

# Product Line II: Optical DSP

What's this?

AI training and reasoning requires thousands of chips to work in parallel. The speed of data transfer between these chips determines the efficiency ceiling of the system. The optical connection is ** the "highway" that connects these chips.

The core product of Mawell is PAM4 optical DSP chips -- converts the telecommunications into light signals, transmits them at high speed in fibre-optics, and then returns them. The current mainstream is **800G**, the next generation **1.6T** is building up the slope.

# Product Line Three: Network Exchange and DPU

What's this?

In addition to customizing ASIC and optical interconnections, Mawell has two products at the data centre:

1. **Net exchange chip** - Traffic command within the data centre, deciding where to take the data package
**DPU (data-processing module)** - Take over "bundling" from CPU, so that CPU can focus on computing

These two operations were acquired in 2017-2021 through the acquisition of Cavium (**$6 billion**) and Inovum (**$1.1 billion**).

# Key judgments

** Inconsequential position:** Network exchange, Botón is the absolute boss; DPU, Bluefield, and AMD are both robbed. Mawell is not a leader in either sub-area.

** Values are in combination:** It's not so great to look at alone, but the value of integration of "one-stop" becomes apparent when custom-made ASIIC+ optically linked packages are sold to the same client. This is also where Mawell differs from pure ASIC design companies (e.g. Alchip, GUC).
