## Core viewpoints of four dimensions

### 1. Business model (Duan Yongping’s perspective)

Mastercard's business essence is the "toll gate" of the global payment network - it does not bear credit risks, does not require large capital expenditures, and the marginal cost of each additional transaction approaches zero. This is one of the very few business models that has "high profit margin + high growth + high certainty" at the same time.

The flywheel effect is accelerating: value-added services (fraud detection, data analysis, identity verification) account for more than 40%, adding a new dimension to the flywheel. The Mastercard Move platform connects 17 billion endpoints and is expanding its business from "swiping your card" to "everything that moves money."

> **One sentence**: This is a "do the right thing, do things right" business and can be held for more than ten years.

### 2. Finance and Valuation (Buffett’s Perspective)

Mastercard is a predictable compound interest machine: net interest rate 45.7%, ROIC 43-50%, and free cash flow conversion rate 109%. Textbook level of capital allocation - FY2025 returned US$17.6 billion to shareholders while maintaining strategic investment in value-added services and new platforms.

Valuation is not cheap but acceptable: PE 29x corresponds to an earnings yield of 3.4%. If EPS growth of 15% is maintained, the forward-looking PE will drop to approximately 19 times after three years. Compared with Visa (forward PE 24.4x), Mastercard has a premium of about 13%, which can be partially rationalized by faster growth (EPS CAGR 15.8% vs Visa 12.5%).

> **One sentence**: Not a "bargain", but it is better to buy a good company at a reasonable price than a mediocre company at a cheap price.

### 3. Barriers to competition (Munger’s perspective)

Mastercard's moat is "deep and multi-layered": network effects (3 billion+ cards, 100 million+ merchants), switching costs (the cost for banks to switch networks is astronomical), regulatory barriers (200+ national licenses), data assets (trillions of dollars in transaction data-driven value-added services), and brand trust.

The biggest potential threats - stablecoins and central bank digital currencies - are being actively absorbed by Mastercard rather than passively tolerated. US$1.8 billion acquisition of BVNK, three-layer stablecoin payment stack, and cooperation with Circle for USDC settlement. Consumers don't care what the underlying technology is, what they care about is "it works by swiping their card."

> **One sentence**: For Mastercard to fail, major countries around the world would need to launch interconnected alternative systems at the same time - almost impossible.

### 4. Risk and Management (Li Lu’s Perspective)

CEO Michael Miebach has demonstrated a management style of "keeping integrity and surprising things": guarding the core of the payment network while actively embracing new paradigms such as stablecoins and agency commerce. The financial report communication is honest (proactively disclosing the impact of the conflict in the Middle East), and the guidance is conservative and pragmatic.

Main risks: (1) Continued downward pressure on interchange fees (already dealt with through diversification of value-added services); (2) Geopolitical impact on cross-border business (the impact of the Middle East conflict in Q1 2026 has emerged); (3) Macroeconomic recession reduces transaction volume (but no credit risk exposure).

> **One sentence**: Management sees the future and is taking action. Risks mainly come from the macro level rather than the company level.

## Investment advice

### Current judgment: You can buy, but you need to wait patiently for a better price

**Investor portraits suitable for buying**:
- Value long-term certainty and be willing to pay a reasonable premium for high quality
- Investment cycle is more than 5 years
- Able to accept short-term fluctuations (geopolitics, macro recession-induced corrections)

**Ideal buying price reference**:
- Current price (PE ~29x): You can open a small position
- PE falls back to around 25x (about 15-20% correction): you can increase your position
- PE falls back below 22x (about 25%+ correction): very attractive, should be boldly bought

**Not recommended to buy**:
- PE continues to be above 35x: growth expectations are fully priced
- The growth rate of value-added services has slowed down significantly (less than 15%): the transformation story is questioned
- Global cross-border transaction volume has experienced negative growth for two consecutive quarters: the core engine stalled

### Choices with Visa

Both are top assets. If you can only choose one:
- **Prefer growth and aggressiveness**: Choose Mastercard (faster growth, more aggressive new business layout)
- **Prefer robustness and safety**: Choose Visa (higher profit margins, lower leverage, larger scale)
- **Best strategy**: Both configured in 6:4 or 5:5
