## 2. Capital Allocation: Textbook-Quality Shareholder Returns

FY2025 returned **USD 17.6 billion** to shareholders, including:

- **Share repurchases**: large ongoing buybacks, with a new USD 14 billion authorization
- **Dividend growth**: quarterly dividend increased 14% from 76 cents to 87 cents
- Q1 2026 continued with USD 4 billion of buybacks

ROIC is an eye-popping **43%-50%** depending on methodology, far above the 7.6% WACC. That means every dollar reinvested creates substantial value.

Free-cash-flow conversion is extremely strong: FY2025 FCF of USD 16.4 billion / net income of USD 15.0 billion = **about 109%**. Almost all earnings turn into real cash.

Sources: [Mastercard ROIC data](https://www.gurufocus.com/term/roic/MA), [shareholder returns](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/mastercard-turns-cash-tap-buybacks-boom-dividends-bloom)

## 3. Revenue Growth Trend: Three Growth Curves

**First curve - payment network (about 60% of revenue):**
- Global payment volume of USD 10.6 trillion, up 9% in constant currency
- Growth drivers: cashless penetration + expansion in emerging markets
- Q1 2026 payment-network revenue grew 8% in constant currency

**Second curve - value-added services (about 40% of revenue):**
- FY2025 value-added-services revenue grew 23%, and Q4 grew 26% in the quarter
- Includes cybersecurity, analytics, fraud detection, and identity verification
- Higher margin than core payments, and it is changing the revenue mix

**Third curve - cross-border and new payments (Mastercard Move):**
- Cross-border volume grew 15% in FY2025 and 13% in Q1 2026
- Mastercard Move connects 17 billion endpoints and transaction growth is above 35%
- B2B cross-border payments and stablecoin settlement are opening new markets

3-year revenue CAGR reached 13.8%, and EPS CAGR is even higher thanks to buybacks. Adjusted EPS is expected to grow at a 15.8% CAGR from 2025 to 2028.

## 4. Valuation Analysis: Side-by-Side with Visa

### Key valuation comparisons

| Metric | Mastercard | Visa | Notes |
|------|-----------|------|------|
| Market cap | ~USD 450 billion | ~USD 590 billion | Visa is larger |
| Forward P/E | 27.7x | 24.4x | MA trades at about a 13% premium |
| Net margin | 45.7% | ~52% | Visa is slightly higher |
| Operating margin | 59.2% | ~67% | Visa is higher |
| FY2025 revenue growth | 16% | 11% | MA grows faster |
| EPS growth forecast (25-28 CAGR) | 15.8% | 12.5% | MA grows faster |
| ROIC | 43%-50% | ~35% | MA is higher |
| Debt ratio | 73.7% | 35.3% | MA is more leveraged |

Sources: [Visa vs Mastercard comparison](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/03/13/visa-vs-mastercard-which-stock-will-make-you-richer/), [valuation data](https://seekingalpha.com/article/4855892-visa-vs-mastercard-two-payment-giants-one-clear-favorite-for-me)

### Interpretation

Mastercard's valuation premium versus Visa (about 13%) can be partially justified by faster growth:

- **Factors supporting the premium**: faster revenue growth (16% vs. 11%), stronger EPS growth forecast (15.8% vs. 12.5%), and a larger, faster-growing mix of value-added services
- **Factors questioning the premium**: Visa has higher margins, lower leverage, and larger scale, which gives it stronger bargaining power

---

## 5. Buffett-Style Valuation Judgment

At a 29x P/E, the implied:

- **Earnings yield** is about 3.4% (1/29), slightly above long-term U.S. Treasury yields
- **If EPS keeps growing at 15%**, the forward P/E would fall to about 19x in 3 years, which is a very reasonable valuation
- **Free-cash-flow yield**: 16.4 / 450 = 3.6%; add 15%+ growth and total return expectations move above 18%

Buffett bought large amounts of both Visa and Mastercard (later managed by Todd Combs for compliance reasons). He has often said he regretted not buying earlier and in larger size.

**Core conclusion:** Mastercard is not "cheap," but it is a predictable compounding machine. A 29x P/E is not excessive for a company with 45% net margins, 43% ROIC, and 15%+ growth. The main risk is a growth slowdown - if growth drops below 10%, the current valuation will start to look rich.
