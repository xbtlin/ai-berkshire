# How?

The MasterCard has about **13% value premium ** (foresee PE 26 vs 24). Is this premium reasonable?

** Arguments in favour of premium**:
- Higher income growth (16% vs 11%)
- EPS Growth forecasts are higher (15.8% vs 12.5%)
- Value-added services are higher and faster than the rest of the population.
- ROIC is better, more efficient.

** Arguments challenging the premium**:
- Visa higher profitability (52% vs 45.7%), which indicates that the basic business model of Visa is more efficient
- Visa has lower liabilities (35% vs 74%) and more robust financial position
- Visa is bigger, more bargaining power in negotiations.
- The "better growth" of MasterCard is already reflected in the valuation.

** Overall**: Both are top assets. If one is the only option - ** Preference for Growth and MasterCard, Preferably for Visa**. But the gap is small, and there is no overriding reason to choose only one.

# What kind of investment is this?

Finally, a character judgement.

MasterCard** Not **:
- ** Deep value investment** - PE 31 is not "false."
- ** Growth investment** - It's already a huge market of 450 billion, and it won't grow 10 times more.
- ** High-point investment** - dividends return of 0.7%

MasterCard** is:
- **Intellectual ** - Continuous double-digit growth + Sustained stock buyback + Sustained valuation is reasonable, and three add up to long-term compound interest
- ** For certainty, premium ** -- you're for "this business, probably still making money after 10 years." 26-31 times PE.
- ** "buy the sleeping investment"** - No need to stare, no need to worry about the black swan.

It is closer to the classic framework of ** "buy first-class companies at reasonable prices" ** - rather than the value trap hunter framework of "buy second-class companies at very low prices".
