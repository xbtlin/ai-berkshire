# The three curves of growth and the ceiling

> "Understanding MasterCard" Series·Part 03
> Reading time is about 10 minutes

---

## Core logic of growth

Mastercard revenue grew 16% in FY2025 and its market capitalization has reached $450 billion. This volume can still grow at double digits because its growth logic is different from that of most companies - it does not rely on grabbing share, but on making the entire cake bigger**.

Three growth curves support this logic:
1. **Basic Payment Network**—The migration of global cash to digital payments
2. **Cross-border payment**—globalization + tourism + e-commerce cross-border transactions
3. **Value-added Services** - Upgraded from "collecting tolls" to "selling navigation, selling insurance, and selling data"

Take them apart and look at them separately.

---

## The First Curve: Cashless – a decades-long trend

The total annual global consumer spending is an astronomical figure. Of this total, a significant amount is still done in cash.

| Region | Proportion of cash payments (estimated) | Trends |
|------|-----------------|------|
| North America | **~15%** | Slowly declining |
| Western Europe | **~20%** | Steady decline |
| India | **>50%** | Rapid decline (UPI boost) |
| Southeast Asia | **>50%** | Rapid decline |
| Latin America | **>50%** | Accelerating decline |
| Africa | **>70%** | Early Stage |

Note: The proportion of cash in each region is an industry estimate, and the calibers vary from different sources.

Every percentage point of cash being replaced by electronic payments means an additional "toll" on the MasterCard network. There is more than one driver of this trend:

- **Government push**: India’s UPI, Brazil’s Pix, Nigeria’s eNaira – governments of various countries are promoting electronic payments for tax regulation and financial inclusion considerations
- **E-commerce Penetration**: Global e-commerce penetration continues to increase, and online shopping is naturally adapted to card payment.
- **Consumer Habits**: After the epidemic, even in countries with high traditional cash usage, consumers are becoming more and more accustomed to contactless payments.

FY2025 data verification: Global total payment volume (GDV) reached **10.6 trillion US dollars**, with an increase of **9%** in local currency. Payment volumes are expanding even against the backdrop of slowing global economic growth.

**Contrary view**: India's UPI and Brazil's Pix are real-time payment systems run by the government, with very low or even free fees, and the transaction volume is already very large. Will Mastercard be "bypassed" in these markets?

This concern is valid, but there are two key details that are often overlooked:
1. UPI/Pix is mainly for **domestic payments** – cross-border payments are still dominated by card networks
2. Instead of fighting these systems, Mastercard works with them – for example in India, Mastercard is integrating with the UPI ecosystem

For MasterCard, the cashless trend is not "all electronic payments go through my network", but "the greater the total amount of electronic payments, the greater the share I can get."

---

## The Second Curve: Cross-border Payments—The Gold Mine with the Highest Profit Margin

Cross-border payments are Mastercard's most profitable business - the rates are much higher than domestic transactions and the profit margins are also higher**.

FY2025 data:
- Cross-border transaction volume increased by **15%** (local currency basis)
- Q1 2026 Cross-border transaction volume growth **13%**

Why are cross-border payments a gold mine? Because it involves **currency conversion, compliance review, real-time settlement** - these are the strengths of the Mastercard network and are something that regional payment systems (UPI, Pix) cannot do.

Growth drivers of cross-border payments:
- **Global Tourism Recovery**: In the post-epidemic era, international tourism consumption continues to rebound
- **Cross-border e-commerce**: Consumers are increasingly accustomed to shopping from overseas platforms
- **B2B Cross-Border Payments**: Global cross-border B2B payments are expected to exceed **$250 trillion** by 2027 – a much larger market than consumer payments

To capture B2B opportunities, Mastercard has launched the **Mastercard Move** platform:

| Mastercard Move | Data |
|----------------|------|
| Connection Endpoints | **17 Billion** |
| Q4 2025 Transaction Volume Growth | **35%+** |
| Covered Scenarios | Remittance, B2B payment, salary payment, insurance compensation |

Data source: [Mastercard Move](https://www.mastercard.com/global/en/business/payments/mastercard-move.html)

The significance of Mastercard Move is that it expands Mastercard from a "card swiping network" to an "infrastructure for all fund flows"**. It's not just about buying coffee with your card - businesses pay suppliers, platforms pay drivers, and cross-border remittances are sent to family members - all can be done through Mastercard Move.

**Opposite view**: Although the B2B cross-border payment market is large, there are many competitors - SWIFT, inter-bank direct connection, and blockchain payment (Ripple) are all vying for this piece of cake. How big a share Mastercard will get is uncertain. This is a growth point with "huge potential but yet to be proven".

## The third curve: value-added services - from "toll station" to "comprehensive service platform"

This is Mastercard’s most important structural change.

FY2025 data:

| Indicators | Data |
|------|------|
| Proportion of value-added service revenue | **>40%** |
| Full-year growth rate of value-added services | **23%** |
| Q4 2025 single quarter growth rate | **26%** |
| Q1 2026 Growth (local currency) | **18%** |

Data source: [Mastercard Q1 2026 Financial Report](https://www.tikr.com/blog/mastercard-q1-2026-earnings-revenue-hits-8-4b-eps-up-23)

What do value-added services include?

| Service type | Specific content | Customer |
|---------|---------|------|
| Network security and fraud detection | Real-time transaction risk assessment, fraud interception | Banks, merchants |
| Data Analysis | SpendingPulse Consumption Insights, Market Trend Analysis | Government, Enterprises |
| Identity verification | Digital identity verification, biometric authentication | E-commerce platform |
| Precision marketing | Marketing optimization based on transaction data | Merchant |
| Open Banking | Access bank account data through Finicity/Aiia | Fintech companies |

Key features of value-added services: **Extremely low marginal costs and higher profit margins than basic payment services. **

They are essentially "liquidating data assets" - the data Mastercard has accumulated from processing 175.5 billion transactions each year - and selling them to banks and merchants through products such as fraud detection and consumer insights. The data is already there, and the cost of selling an additional analysis report is almost zero.

The proportion of value-added services has increased from less than 30% to more than 40%, and the growth rate far exceeds that of basic payment business - this means that Mastercard's revenue structure is becoming higher quality.

### Agency commerce: the next growth point?

Mastercard is actively developing Agentic Commerce - when AI agents begin to make purchase decisions instead of humans, who will verify the identity? Who will ensure payment security?

Mastercard’s answer: establish standards for AI agent commerce—including authentication, governance frameworks, and trust mechanisms.

This is a layout that is extremely forward-looking but has not yet generated revenue. If AI agent commerce really explodes (5-10 year perspective), Mastercard's value as a "trust layer" will further increase.

Data source: [Mastercard Agency Business Layout](https://www.pymnts.com/earnings/2026/mastercard-leans-into-agentic-commerce-stablecoins-while-card-volumes-rise/)

**Opposite view**: Although value-added services are growing rapidly, their competitive landscape is far less concentrated than basic payment networks - in the fields of fraud detection and data analysis, there are a large number of professional companies (such as FICO, Palantir, and various security vendors) competing. Mastercard’s data advantage is obvious, but that doesn’t mean it has a monopoly.

## Where is the ceiling?

### Optimistic scenario

| Curve | 5-Year Outlook |
|------|---------|
| Basic payment | Global cashlessness continues to advance, with GDV growing at an annual rate of 7-10% |
| Cross-border payment | Tourism + e-commerce + B2B driven, annual growth of 12-15% |
| Value-added services | Proportion increased from 40% to 50%+, with annual growth of 18-22% |
| **Comprehensive income growth** | **Annual average 12-15%** |

### Conservative scenario

| Curve | 5-Year Outlook |
|------|---------|
| Basic payments | Emerging markets UPI/Pix divert some of the incremental growth, GDV growth slows to 5-7% |
| Cross-border payments | Frequent geopolitical conflicts affect tourism and trade, with growth falling to 8-10% |
| Value-added services | Competition intensifies, growth slows to 12-15% |
| **Comprehensive income growth** | **Annual average 8-10%** |

### Key Observations

Even under a conservative scenario, Mastercard still has **8-10%** annual revenue growth -- still a very healthy growth rate for a company that already has a **$450 billion** market capitalization.

But the lower limit of the growth rate is important - because the current valuation of **PE of about 31 times** implies the market's expectation of **15%+ EPS growth**. If actual growth falls into a conservative range, valuations may face downward pressure.

## Use one picture to summarize three curves

```
                Revenue proportion Growth rate Certainty Profit rate
Basic payment ~60% 8-10% ★★★★★ High
Cross-border payments (included above) 12-15% ★★★★☆ Maximum
Value-added services ~40% 18-23% ★★★☆☆ Higher
```

Basic payment is the "ballast stone" - stable growth and high certainty; cross-border payment is the "profit engine" - the highest rate and rapid growth; value-added services are the "second growth pole" - the fastest growth rate and changing the income structure.

The three curves reinforce each other: the greater the basic payment volume and the more data, the more valuable the value-added services are; the better the value-added services are, the more willing banks and merchants are to access the network, the greater the basic payment volume.
