> 5 main articles that break down the "toll booth" of the global payments network layer by layer.
>
> Estimated reading time: about 50 minutes; recommended to read in order.

---

## Series Positioning

Mastercard is not a bank, not a credit-card company, and not even a traditional financial company.

It is a **global payments network that collects tolls** - every time you swipe a card, shop online, or send money across borders, a small amount of money flows into Mastercard's pocket. It does not lend money and does not bear bad-debt risk, while marginal cost approaches zero.

The result is **45.7% net margin, 43% ROIC, and 109% free-cash-flow conversion**. Those numbers are extremely rare among large listed companies worldwide.

But the market values it at roughly **31x trailing P/E and 26x forward P/E**, which is not cheap.

This series aims to **explain why the business makes money and lay out the basis for judging whether it is expensive**, so that you can decide for yourself.

## Writing Principles

1. **Start with the data, then the logic, and only then the conclusion** - do not pre-judge bullish or bearish
2. **Attach a counterargument to every major conclusion** - let readers weigh both sides
3. **Source every data point** - annual reports, SEC filings, and third-party institutions
4. **Do not invent data** - if something is uncertain, mark it as "estimated" or "non-official"
5. **Not investment advice** - every article ends with a disclaimer
