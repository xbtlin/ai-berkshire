# I, textbook answers vs, real answers

# 1.1 MM Theory (Modigliani-Miller, 1961)
Under perfect market assumptions (no taxes, no costs, no information difference), ** splits/backs/retentions have no effect on company value at all**:
- You give me a dollar.
I'm short of a share, but I'm worth one cent more.
- It's essentially "left for right."

# 1.2 The world's perfect market is not working, so it's having an impact
1. **Tax** — Red Cross tax (10% of Ports, 5/10% of As) and no tax on buybacks
2. ** Difference in capital allocation capacity** — Will management spend money?
** Agent issues** — Retentional profits may be squandered (luxury office buildings, high-paying, bad buying)
4. **Intra-information asymmetry** — Repurchase signals "undervalued by insiders"
5. ** Market sentiment** - Shareholder return policies influence market judgement on the "quality" of companies

# III. Retention vs Redeal vs Repurchase: Real value differences between three disposals

#3.1 Retention profits (without shareholder returns)
** Value created when and only when ** meets one of the following conditions:
Companies have RIC > 15% reinvestment opportunities (embracing/research and development/acquisition)
2. Companies have strategic reserve requirements (response cycle, M & As window)
3. Corporate debt-servicing needs

** Otherwise, it's the value of destruction** — typical:
- Bank deposits with low cash accumulation (RMB 500 billion cash on the toll account, 2 per cent interest rate, inflation over inflation)
- High premium buy-out (AOL-era Warner, Hewlett-Packard-EDS, Minki-Simen)
- Investment in corruption/rewards (high-level relatives)

# 3.2 Red
** Accomplishments**:
- Force to return, avoid the flowers.
- Shareholders are free to be configured (reinvested in other high ROIC companies)
- "Symphoid" is strong -- once the bonus increases, the market is not expected to decrease.

** Shortcoming**:
- ** Taxed** (10% Port Unit, 0% A Unit on a permanent basis, 10% Central Foreign Investment in the Mainland)
- Decline in share-sharing (deterioration in book value)
- Forced return may be contrary to the best interests of the company (cash requirements compared to the following cycle)

##3.3 Repurchase
** Advantages (almost than the score)**:
- ** No taxes** (directly converted into increased per share in the retention of shareholders)
- Upgrading of each indicator, e.g. EPS, ROE
- Repurchase when share price is below inner value = **Shareholder Value Amplifier**
- Flexibility - Rest when there's pressure to do business.

** Shortcoming**:
- Stock buybacks above inner value = ** Value destroyer** (typically counter-case: Apples in 2018)
- Flexibility is also a double-edged sword - management can stop at will.
- It's very signalistic -- "No better reinvestment opportunities," and the market may be interpreted as "the company's lack of growth."

# 3.4 The most crucial insight: the "price" of buybacks determines everything
> Buffett ' s Shareholder Letter 2016:
> ** "Management should buy back only in two cases: (1) surplus funds and (2) share prices below the intrinsic value of conservative estimates."**

♪ Why is the Tweeting Repurchase "value creation" ♪
- Repurchase of PE scope 17-20x 2024-2025, with reasonable undervaluation
- Negative link to PE submission**-0.65** (opportunism buyback)
- The more you fall, the more you buy.
- ** Not "buy back for the share price"** but "buy back for value."

# Six, a few perceptive insights

# 6.1 The company that doesn't buy back is not necessarily the bad company
**/PDD why not red?** – because it is still in high ROIC growth, and the market is willing to offer a premium on growth. If it is forced to split, it will destroy value (the negative sign that "PDD is not growing”).

** Why not Birkhshire?** – Barfitt has proven for 59 years that every dollar retained can be converted into a $3-5 market value, a split = a value destroyed.

# 6.2 The big red buyback companies don't always "many friendly"
**GE/IBM** – The root cause of long-term high-scoring red + high-purchases is not "friend to shareholders" but "no good reinvestment opportunities in the house." This is ** recession signal**, not advantage.

# 6.3 Reds are more committed than buybacks
- The market is not expected to decrease once the red has increased (motivated)
- Repurchases can be cancelled flexibly (e.g., evasively downsized in 2026)
- That's why many value investors value the credit for steady growth.

# 6.4 Repurchase + red "signal value"
- And we're going to keep buying back a lot.
- Repurchases above intrinsic value = "Management impulse" or "equity price increment" - negative signals
- Repurchase below intrinsic value = "Management rationality" - positive sign

# VIII. Decision-making framework: assessing shareholder return policies in any company

# Step 1: Assessing reinvestment opportunities
- **Roic ** 15%? ** Should have kept the profits.
- **RoIC 8-12%?** See marginal project quality
- ** Company ROIC < 8%?** Must return to shareholders

# Step 2: Assessing management ' s capital allocation discipline
- ** Any historical history of premium-disposal purchases?**
- ** Is the cash pile idle for long?**
- ** Does the buyback take place at a low valuation?**

# Step 3: Assessing shareholder returns
- ** Weak but tax**
- ** Repurchases without taxes but flexible**
- ** Special distribution (in kind)** <unk> Treatment of extreme surplus cash (e.g. 2022 Kyoto and 2024 United States Mission)

# Step 4: Evaluation of "$1 test"
- The increase in the share price over the past five years Total retained profit > 1: Retentioned creative value
- = 1: Barely passed
- < 1: Preservation of value of destruction

# Nine, one sentence to sum up

> ** Repurchase and redecorating do not "create" value, but they can "release" value that already exists and "restraint" management from destroying value**
>
> ** The ultimate return on shareholders is not about the share of the bonus or the buy back, but rather - whether the company retains profits per $1 and becomes the market value of at least $1.**

That's why Buffett values ROIC as far as the red rate, why Manger says, "Don't spend money with overweight red-coupled companies," and why Yong-Ping says, "Look if the boss pays more than he does."
