## 1. Valuation Method: SOTP (Sum of the Parts)

Tencent is not a single-business company, so valuing it with a single P/E ratio hides important differences across segments. A better approach is to break it apart:

**Core operating businesses (2025 annual-report data):**

| Business | Revenue | YoY | Note |
|------|------|------|------|
| Value-added services (games + social) | RMB 369.3 billion | +16% | Of which domestic games were RMB 164.2 billion and overseas games RMB 77.4 billion |
| Advertising | RMB 145.0 billion | +19% | Channels include Channels, Moments, mini programs, and search |
| Fintech and enterprise services | RMB 229.4 billion | +8% | CSIG reached scale profitability in Q4 2025 |
| **Total revenue** | **RMB 751.8 billion** | **+14%** | |
| **Non-IFRS net income** | **RMB 259.6 billion** | **+17%** | Gross margin 56% |
| **Free cash flow** | **RMB 182.6 billion** | | |

**Investment portfolio (non-consolidated, about RMB 1.04 trillion):**
- Listed holdings: PDD (largest single position, equity value about USD 20 billion), Sea Limited (18.7%), Kuaishou (15.8%), Spotify, Universal Music, and others
- Unlisted holdings: ByteDance, Epic Games, Supercell, WeBank, and others
- Note: Meituan was reduced to about 1.5% after the 2022 year-end distribution; Riot Games is fully consolidated and is not included in this tally
- Listed investments at fair value of RMB 672.7 billion + unlisted investments at book value of RMB 363.1 billion = about RMB 1.04 trillion in total

**Net cash:** about RMB 107.1 billion (cash and equivalents minus interest-bearing debt)

The point of SOTP is not to produce a perfectly precise number. It is to help you understand what you are actually buying when you buy Tencent: how much comes from the core business, how much from the portfolio, and what is certain versus uncertain.

## 2. Historical Valuation Percentiles - A Rearview Mirror, Not a Navigation System

| Metric | Current | 10-year median | Historical low (2022.10) |
|------|------|-----------|-------------------|
| P/E (Non-IFRS) | ~14.5x | ~27x | ~10x |
| P/B | ~3.4x | ~5x | ~2.7x |
| FCF yield | ~4.9% | ~2.5% | ~8% |

The current P/E sits in a relatively low percentile versus the last 10 years.

But we have to stress: **historical percentiles are a rearview mirror.** A 27x median over the past decade does not mean the stock has to return to 27x in the future. The valuation center of gravity in Hong Kong may have shifted structurally lower because of geopolitics, capital flows, and liquidity discounts. Using the historical median as an anchor could overstate fair value.

What historical percentiles do tell you is this: at the current profit level, the market is paying a relatively low price for Tencent. That is all.

## 3. What Really Determines Valuation Is Future Profit

Historical P/E is the rearview mirror. What determines your return after buying is **how much Tencent can earn in the future**.

Tencent's 2025 Non-IFRS net income was RMB 259.6 billion. The key future profit drivers are:

- **Channels / video feed**: current monetization rate is about 1.8% vs. Douyin's 6%-7%, implying a 3-4x gap on a pure math basis, but whether that gap closes depends on e-commerce ecosystem maturity
- **AI**: 2025 capex hit a record RMB 79.2 billion, and its contribution to cloud revenue and operating efficiency remains to be proven
- **Overseas games**: overseas game revenue surpassed USD 10 billion in 2025, up 33% YoY; sustainability depends on product cycles
- **Buybacks**: over the past three years Tencent has reduced shares outstanding by 1%-2% net per year, directly lifting EPS
- **Fintech and enterprise services**: CSIG reached scale profitability in Q4 2025, and profit contribution is starting to show

Based on those variables, **here is a forecast range for future profit** (note: these are forecasts, not facts, and actual results may differ):

| | Non-IFRS net income in 3 years (2028) | Implied CAGR |
|---|---|---|
| Bull | ~RMB 395 billion | ~15% |
| Base | ~RMB 320 billion | ~7% |
| Bear | ~RMB 280 billion | ~3% |

The current P/E is about 14.5x. Future returns will depend on two things: where actual profit lands inside that range, and how many times earnings the market is willing to pay at the time. Both are unknown.

## 4. Watchlist Red Lines

If any of the following happens, the thesis should be revisited:

1. WeChat MAU declines quarter over quarter
2. Native AI apps materially divert time spent from WeChat
3. Pony Ma or Martin Lau leaves abruptly with no clear successor
4. Gross margin stays below 50% for a sustained period

Triggering one of these does not mean automatic selling, but it does mean the holding thesis must be re-examined.

---

## 5. Summary

- **SOTP view**: what you are buying is the core business (games + ads + fintech) + a trillion-RMB investment portfolio + net cash
- **Historical percentile**: the current P/E is low versus the 10-year range, but historical percentiles are a rearview mirror and cannot be extrapolated mechanically
- **Key variable**: profit growth over the next 3-5 years determines everything. A 14.5x P/E means the market is not pricing in much growth - if profit keeps compounding, returns will come from earnings growth itself plus possible valuation repair
- **Risk**: Hong Kong valuation multiples may not return to historical medians, and geopolitics plus AI competition are real uncertainties

Valuation is not about producing a precise answer to "what is it worth." It is about understanding how much you may gain or lose under different future scenarios, and then deciding whether the odds are worth taking.
