# Why is "net cash" the core indicator?

Net cash = cash + short-term investments - interest-bearing liabilities.

This figure tells you:** the company has paid all interest-bearing debts and how much more money is left to be left to the bank**.

Net cash from the telecommunications network rose from $26 billion in 2024 to **10.7 billion in 2025 ** growth of 311 per cent a year** (calibre: cash + short-term investments — interest-bearing liabilities).

The increase is from three drivers: (1) operating cash flows of $303.1 billion (+17%) that are continuously infused; (2) part interest-bearing liabilities that are not re-repayable and reduce denominators; and (3) part short-term investments that are re-classified back to cash equivalents. AI investments, though large (CAPEX + 57%), are still FCF + 18%, without erosion of financial health.

** The financial implication of this figure is **: the "security cushion" on the news check is very thick -- even if the main business suddenly breaks out tomorrow, the company has sufficient cash to respond.

# Seven, ROE: From 30% to 17% -- good or bad?

ROE is the central measure of the company's "profit efficiency with shareholder money." The ROE is declining in recent years:

Year
|------|-----|
| 2018 | ~30% |
| 2021 | ~25% |
| 2024 | ~20% |
<unk> 2025 ~ 24% (after normalization) <unk>

ROE decline is driven mainly by ** net asset inflation** and the source of the larger denominators is split as follows:

- ** Continuous increase in fair value of the portfolio** (PDD, Spotify, Sea, UMG, etc., substantial upswings in recent years): accounted for about 40-50 per cent of net asset growth
- ** Undistributed retained profit accumulation** (free cash flow continues at 180 billion plus, buyback offset by a net annual retention of tens of billion): about 30-40 per cent of the increase
- ** Other consolidated gains** (exchange, foreign currency investment revaluation, etc.): **

ROE is bad? Look at the angle you're in:

- ** Absolute angle**: 24% still well above the ROE threshold of 15% of traditional value investments
- **Relativity**: from 30% to 24% is a real setback, a reflection of the industry's overall downturn.
- ** Structural angle**: "Big-mode" due to portfolio appreciation, which is essentially a build-up of assets rather than a deterioration of business -- but this inflated ROE contribution is no longer available (high base), and future ROE is likely to be stable between 20 and 25% of the area

The ROE downside is one of the things that deserves constant observation in the RT finance, but absolute levels are still excellent.
