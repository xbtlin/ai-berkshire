# Seven, why the market is playing 30% discount on the portfolio

Back to the core question: why is the market giving it a 30% discount on a trillion-dollar equity asset on the news check?

5 Reasons:

# 1. Valuation is not naturally transparent

The real valuation of the off-market portion (Riot/Epic/Supercell/bytes/subscribes) is difficult to obtain precision - the first-market valuation often contains priority clearance rights, a bet clause, and the difference between book value and realizable value may be 30-50%. The market tends to "reduce to avoid mistakes".

The tweaks are about 13.76% of the total amount of money that is held by passive financial investments,** cannot be sold on a market-by-sale basis ** - a large reduction in stock prices will hit. Riot 100% of subsidiaries** can be split up theoretically, but are actually part of the tweaking industry and not sold alone**.

#3. Uncertainty of the shifting of the pie

Two major reductions in the Quinter history: 2021 Kyoto and 2022 America. This in-kind bonus, while friendly, is accepted passively by shareholders in the reduction of time and prices.** The market does not like this uncertainty.**

## 4. Risks at the end of the relationship between China and the United States

Riot (United States), Epic (United States), Krafton (South Korea) and others may be forced to sell in extreme Central American decoupling.** This is a real but unlikely tail risk** — but the market will give a discount.

##5. Valuation logic mismatch

** The main business of the Stretch is valued by PE, the portfolio is valued by NAV - two sets of valuations taken together without a standard model**. The seller's analytical DCF model rarely takes the portfolio's NAVs into its entirety – so the hidden value of the portfolio is often ignored.

# Ten, a constant confused judgment sequence

Two opposing arguments often arise around the "Treete portfolio":

- One said, "The current price of the news is equivalent to sending a trillion dollars portfolio for nothing -- this is a huge security cushion."
- Another said, "The portfolio valuation is 30% discount is normal and should not be expected to be real."

Both parties make sense. But more correctly, the order of judgment** is as follows:

** First step: Determining whether the main business is worth buying first.** The main industry (micro-plus games + advertising + FBS) must independently pass your investment criteria – deep enough for the moat, profitable enough, and reasonable valuation. If the main business is not worth buying, the portfolio should not buy a tweak (you should go straight to the PDD, buy Spotify).

** Step 2: The portfolio is a sub-section, not a central case.** After the main business, the portfolio is "a little flower" -- it gives you a more security pad and a hidden upfront, but ** it is not the central reason for you to buy the news.**

If the order is reversed -- just focus on the portfolio valuation for the good news, you probably get it wrong:
- Fair value of the portfolio fluctuates with the market, with the price of the share of the telecommunications falling
- Large reduction in stock prices, which is uncontrollable at a point (reference: 2022, exchange rate reduction in Kyoto, US market response)
- Some overseas assets may be disposed of under the risk of the tail of the US-China relationship

** The conclusion is simple **: look at the main business and then the gift. You buy the edgy, not the edible ed.
