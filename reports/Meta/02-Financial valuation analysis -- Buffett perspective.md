# Meta Platforms Financial and Valuation Analysis

> Perspective: financial quality and intrinsic value, using a Buffett-style framework
> Data cutoff: May 2026 | Based on Q1 2026 results

---

## 1. Revenue and Profit Trends: A Powerful Money Machine

### Revenue

Meta's Q1 2026 revenue was USD 56.31 billion, up 33% year over year (29% in constant currency). Over the past three years, revenue CAGR reached 20%, which is rare for a company with a trillion-dollar market cap.

Breakdown of the growth drivers:
- **Ad impressions**: +19% YoY, driven by more user time and higher Reels ad load
- **Ad price**: +12% YoY, as AI-driven targeting improved advertiser ROI and supported pricing
- **Q2 guidance**: USD 58-61 billion, implying full-year revenue could exceed USD 230 billion

Meta's full-year ad revenue is on track to surpass Google's for the first time in 2026, reaching an estimated USD 243.46 billion and accounting for 26.8% of the global digital ad market. That would be a historic turning point.

### Profit

Q1 net income was USD 26.8 billion, up 61% YoY, though that includes an USD 8.03 billion one-time tax benefit. Adjusted EPS was USD 7.31 (reported EPS was USD 10.44).

Key profitability metrics:
| Metric | Q1 2026 | Notes |
|------|---------|------|
| Operating income | USD 22.9 billion | Operating margin 41% |
| Net margin | 33.1% (adjusted roughly 26%) | Still top-tier even after removing the tax benefit |
| ROIC | 35% | Far above the cost of capital |

## 2. Capital Expenditures: An Unprecedented AI Bet

Meta raised its 2026 capex guidance from USD 115-135 billion to **USD 125-145 billion**, which deserves serious scrutiny.

### Capex Evolution
| Year | Capex | Notes |
|------|----------|------|
| 2023 | ~USD 28 billion | Baseline after the "efficiency year" |
| 2024 | ~USD 39 billion | AI investment starts to accelerate |
| 2025 | ~USD 72.2 billion | Large-scale AI infrastructure buildout |
| 2026E | USD 125-145 billion | Historic peak, up about 80% |

The spending goes into power, land, and GPU compute, primarily to support large-scale AI model training and inference. Meta is also pushing its in-house MTIA chip to reduce long-term dependence on NVIDIA.

### The Key Question: Where Is the Return?

Buffett's core capital-allocation test is: **can every dollar of retained earnings create more than one dollar of market value?**

Arguments in favor:
- Q1 revenue growth of 33% is already well ahead of cost growth, so the AI investment is producing returns
- Advantage+ tools are handling more than USD 60 billion of annualized ad spend and directly driving revenue growth
- Management says 2026 operating income in absolute dollars will exceed 2025

Risk factors:
- 2026 expense growth will be "significantly faster" than in 2025
- Zuckerberg's answer on ROI was vague: "that's a very technical question"
- If the AI race escalates, capex could rise even further

## 3. Free Cash Flow and Shareholder Returns

Meta's free-cash-flow generation is one of its biggest financial advantages:
- Expected 2026 free cash flow of about USD 114.2 billion, even though capex is heavy
- EV / FCF of about 31.8x
- Continued share repurchases; shares outstanding have fallen about 1.45% over the past year

Meta is still producing strong cash returns while making huge AI investments. That is uncommon among large tech companies - usually, heavy capex causes a sharp cash-flow drop, but Meta's ad business growth is enough to fund the investment.

## 4. Valuation Analysis

### Current Valuation
| Metric | Value | Peer comparison |
|------|------|----------|
| Market cap | USD 1.593 trillion | — |
| Trailing P/E | 22.2x | Peer average 29.1x |
| Forward P/E | 18.4x | Well below peers |
| Change in P/E since Q4 2025 | 27.5x -> 20.5x | Valuation has compressed materially |
| GF Value estimate | USD 787 | Current share price USD 601, about 23.6% discount |

### Valuation Judgment

At a forward P/E of 18.4x, the market is implying roughly 20% earnings growth. Given Meta's 20% revenue CAGR over the past three years, 33% Q1 growth, and plenty of unmonetized inventory in Reels and WhatsApp, **the current valuation is not expensive and may even be cheap**.

The peer comparison makes the point even more clearly:
- Google (Alphabet): forward P/E around 20x, but revenue growth only 12%
- ByteDance (TikTok parent): unlisted, but implied growth is slowing
- Snap: still struggling to get to break-even

**Meta is offering higher growth at a lower valuation than peers.** That combination of "growth + value" is rare among mega-cap tech stocks.
