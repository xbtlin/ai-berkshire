## From $90 to $736 and back to $597 – same company, three stories

In November 2022, Meta dropped to **$90**. There is no new bad news, but the market suddenly unified a narrative: this is a machine that burns money to buy the Metaverse, and no one will use the Metaverse at all. At that time, Wall Street's rhetoric was almost unanimous - "Social media has lost its direction."

Two years later, with the stock hitting **$600**, the same people had a different phrase: AI King of Advertising. Hitting an all-time high of **$736** in February 2025, it fell back to about **$597** in May 2026, and the narrative flipped again to "AI capital expenditures are out of control." What happened in between? At the beginning of 2023, Zuckerberg announced the "Year of Efficiency" and laid off **21,000 people** - the company did not change tracks or invent new products. In three and a half years, the narrative flipped three times, and each flip was probably an overreaction in hindsight.

**This rule itself is worthy of vigilance: the speed of narrative switching is much faster than the change of the company's fundamentals. **

Opening the latest financial report, Meta has a set of data that makes people have to re-examine it:

| Indicators | Numerical values | Magnitude judgment |
|------|------|---------|
| Monthly active users (family apps) | **3.98 billion** (early 2025) | Covering about half of the world’s population |
| Daily active users | **3.56 billion** (Q1 2026) | +4% year-on-year |
| Full Year 2025 Revenue | **$201 billion** (+22%) | |
| 2025 full-year net profit | **60.46 billion US dollars** | A slight decrease of about 3% year-on-year (impact of tax rate) |
| 2024 full-year net profit | **$62.36 billion** (+60%) | 2024 is the year of profit explosion |
| Q1 2026 Revenue | **$56.31 billion** (+33%) | Growth reaccelerates |
| Q1 2026 Net Profit | **$26.77 billion** (+61%) | Including $8.03 billion one-time tax benefit |
| Q1 2026 Adjusted Net Profit | **$18.7 billion** | Excluding tax benefits, still beat expectations by 9.6% |
| 2026 CapEx Guidance | **$125-145 billion** | Approximately 54%-63% of projected revenue |
| Current Market Cap | **About $1.59 Trillion** | |
| Current PE (TTM) | **About 22x** | |
| Forward P/E ratio | **About 18.4 times** | Peers’ average is about 29 times |
| 2025 buybacks + dividends | **26.26 billion + US$5.32 billion** | Generous rewards to shareholders |
| Reality Labs cumulative losses | **Approximately $83.6 billion** | Cumulative 2020-2025 |

Put these numbers together - annual revenue of $200 billion, net profit of $60 billion, monthly active users covering half of the world's population, and a forward price-to-earnings ratio of only 18 times. At the same time, more than $125 billion is poured into AI every year, and the Metaverse has burned a total of $83.6 billion.

Do you think "white horse growth, cheap valuation"? You may be underestimating the uncertainty of $145 billion in capex.
Feel like "burning money into a bottomless pit"? You may be overlooking the 33% revenue growth and 35% return on capital.

What this series wants to do is to dismantle Meta layer by layer and let you decide for yourself how to look at it.

## What kind of company is Meta?

Define Meta in one sentence:

> **Meta = the world’s largest attention monetization machine (main advertising industry) + a $145 billion AI gamble (capital expenditure) + a Metaverse experiment (Reality Labs) that burned 83.6 billion and has yet to see a return. **

Some people have analyzed these three things separately, but few people have weighed them together. **The market's disagreement on Meta is essentially a disagreement about assigning different weights to these three things**.

The weighting of the bullish side: 90% of the main advertising industry + 10% of AI acceleration = underestimation
The weight of the bearish faction: 60% of the main advertising industry + 30% of the money-burning risk of AI + 10% of Zuckerberg’s gambling behavior = high risk

**The two groups looked at the same report and came to completely different conclusions. **

## Three common misconceptions about Meta

### Myth 1: Meta is a social media company

This label is technically correct, but it misleads your understanding of its business model.

Meta **98%+ of revenue comes from advertising**. It doesn’t sell social products to its users — users use Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp and Messenger for free. Meta’s real customers are advertisers, and the real product is **accurate user attention**.

In terms of revenue structure, Meta is more like **Google** (advertising revenue accounts for 77%) than Netflix (subscription revenue) or Apple (hardware + services).

It is important to understand this: **Meta's core competitiveness is not "to do social networking", but "to use social networking to grab users' attention, and then use AI to sell their attention at the highest price"**. This understanding determines how you evaluate the legitimacy of your investment in AI.

### Illusion 2: The Metaverse has failed, so Meta cannot

Reality Labs has accumulated losses of **83.6 billion US dollars** (Source: Outlook Respawn summarizes Meta’s financial report data over the years). This number looks shocking. But there are a few things to understand:

1. **Meta’s advertising business fully covered this loss**. The operating profit of the family application division will exceed **70 billion US dollars in 2024** (Source: Meta 2024 annual report). Reality Labs’ losses are only a drag, not a fatal threat
2. **Zuckerberg is already shrinking**. The Horizon Worlds VR version will be shut down in March 2026, laying off approximately 1,500 Reality Labs employees. Management no longer mentioned the "Metaverse" during the earnings call
3. **83.6 billion is a sunk cost**. Investment decisions should be based on future cash flows, not past losses

Counter-argument: Even with the contraction, Reality Labs will still lose **$19.19 billion** in 2025 (Source: Meta 2025 annual report), and management said losses in 2026 will be "at a similar level." If Zuckerberg takes a new direction and makes such a big gamble in the future, how many times can investors bear it?

### Illusion 3: Investment in AI will definitely pay off

The Q1 2026 numbers are really good - revenue growth is 33%, ad impressions are up 19%, unit prices are up 12%, and the AI-powered Advantage+ tool is processing over **$60 billion** in ad spend on an annualized basis (source: Meta Q1 2026 financial report).

But 2026 capital spending of $125-$145 billion is a staggering number:

| Comparison items | Data |
|--------|------|
| Meta 2023 CapEx | **Approximately $28.1 billion** |
| Meta 2024 CapEx | **Approximately $39.2 billion** |
| Meta 2025 CapEx | **Approximately $72.2 billion** |
| Meta 2026 CapEx Guidance | **$125-145 billion** (+73% to +101% year-over-year) |

It has nearly tripled in three years.

When a reporter asked Zuckerberg "What is the evidence of return on investment for the $145 billion investment in AI", his answer was: "This is a very technical question" (Source: Fortune, April 29, 2026).

This is not a reassuring answer.

**AI investment has already paid off initially – this is a fact. But whether $145 billion is a reasonable investment is an open question. ** The following series will dismantle it in detail.

## A small preview: Comparison of Meta with Pinduoduo and Tencent

If you have watched "Understanding Tencent" and "Understanding Pinduoduo" - Meta is the third category of targets and is worth comparing:

| Dimension | Tencent | Pinduoduo | Meta |
|------|------|--------|------|
| Asset quality | WeChat is irreplaceable + evergreen games | Algorithmic advertising machine + political options | Global social graph + AI advertising engine |
| Valuation | 14.5x P/E | 10.5x / 6.3x excluding cash | **18.4x forward P/E** |
| Management | High transparency + generous buybacks | Low transparency + zero buybacks and zero dividends | Absolute control by founders + generous buybacks |
| Risk Structure | Regulatory Tail | Temu Political Gambling + Trust Discount | AI Capital Expenditure + Founder’s Gambling Nature |
| The biggest uncertainty | Sino-US relations | 420 billion unavailable cash | **145 billion spent cash** |

Tencent’s uncertainty lies in the external world (geopolitics), Pinduoduo’s uncertainty lies in its management (whether it is willing to part with money), and **Meta’s uncertainty lies in capital allocation (whether it is worth the money spent)**.

Three companies, three completely different investment propositions.
