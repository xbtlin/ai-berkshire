# AI, have you paid back for the input?

Yes, and the return is quite clear:

1. **Advantage+ Automotive Advertising**: Annual processing over **60 billion United States dollars** advertising expenditures, directly contributing to the efficiency of advertising and to the increase in unit prices (source: Meta Q1 2026)
2. ** Proposed upgrade**: The AI model improvement has increased the length of stay of users and has led to an increase in advertising displays 19%
3. **Q1 Income growth 33% **: far more cost increases, indicating that AI inputs are accelerating outputs

If short-term data alone are taken, the return on AI input is positive — each dollar contribution brings value growth of more than one dollar.

# So what's the problem?

The problem is ** scale and sustainability**.

**Scalation **: Capital expenditure in 2026 was US$ 125-145 billion, and even if Meta had earned US$ 230 billion (based on the Q2 guidelines) year-round, capital expenditure still represented 54-63% of income. Free cash flows, while still projected to exceed US$ 100 billion, were significantly reduced.

** Sustainability **: The AI race is an arms race. Google, Microsoft, and Amazon are investing in AI infrastructure on a massive scale. If the competition continues to build up, could Meta stop? The history of Zuckerberg suggests that he is unlikely to slow down.

** Transparency **: When asked about the return on investment of $14.5 billion invested, Zuckerberg said, "This is a very technical question." Management promised that the absolute profit of business in 2026 would exceed 2025, but warned that the cost increase would be "manifestly faster than" the income increase.

Inverse: ** "Impartially seen" does not mean "no" **. Amazons did not prove the return on investment in the early years of AWS, but later it was the best investment in the company’s history. If AI is really a paradigm shift like the Internet, it is better to over-in-in-in-the-in-year than under-in-in-in-out.

Response: The Amazon analogy is reasonable, but there is one key difference – AWS is a new business with independent income, with input output being measured independently. Meta’s AI input is mainly to enhance existing advertising, making it difficult to accurately isolate AI’s independent contribution. This makes it more difficult to judge whether "input is excessive".

# Open-source business logic

Why should the model, which spent billions of dollars in training, be made freely available?

1. **Reducing its own R&D costs**: external developers help to identify bugs, optimize performance and expand application scenarios
2. ** Establish industry standards**: When Llama becomes the "general base" for the AI industry, all Llama-based applications and data may eventually return to Meta ecology
3. ** Reduce OpenAI ' s pricing rights**: If open-source models are good enough, OpenAI 's closed-source models are difficult to overprice
4. ** Attracting talent**: AI Researchers prefer to join a open-source friendly company

# New movement of the closed source

Notably, Meta started a two-track strategy in 2026**:
- **Open Source**: Continue the Llama Series
- **Closing**: Release of the first business closed-source model**Muse Spark** (source: CNBC, 28 April 2026)

This suggests that Meta's open source strategy is not "unconditional sharing" but selective open source base models, closed source-most profitable layer models.

Inverse: Open-source models give competitors (including TikTok/byte beats) access to advanced AI technology free of charge, and Meta is actually arming his opponents with its own money. And Llama’s downloads and community activity, while high, do not translate directly into Meta’s income.

Response: This concern is not unreasonable. But the biggest beneficiaries of Llama are currently SMEs and cloud computing platforms, not Meta’s direct competitors. Byte beats and Google has its own AI R&D capability, and is less dependent on Llama. SMEs use Llama to build applications, and may end up getting visitors through Meta’s advertising platform – an indirect but valuable positive cycle.

# Zuckerberg's capitalist philosophy

When you look at AI input and metacosity losses together, you can see the philosophy of the Zuckerberg capitalization:

** Feature I: Big bet**
- 2012: 1 billion for Instagram -- right.
- 2014 19 billion dollars for whatsapp -- right.
- 2021, all in the Woncos - Wrong bet
- 2024-2026 All in AI - Right now, size to be verified

** Feature II: Wrong will be acknowledged, but not fast enough**
- The universe has been shrinking substantially for almost five years and $83.6 billion between 2021 and 2026.
- After nearly two years of criticism, we started downsizing and restructuring.

** Feature III: Double-share = no one can stop**
Zuckerberg holds about **60% of voting rights. This means that he can still make his own decisions even if all external shareholders object.

** Meta’s investment risk is essentially a question of "trusting in Zuckerberg."** His record of decision-making over the past two decades is generally excellent (Instagram, WhatsApp’s acquisition is one of the best M&A cases in the history of technology), but the $83.6 billion deficit in the Won universe proves that he would also make a big mistake.
