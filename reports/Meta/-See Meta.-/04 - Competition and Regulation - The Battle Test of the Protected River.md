# First war: Meta vs TikTok

# What's the threat of TikTok?

In 2020-2022, TikTok was the biggest competitive threat to Meta. Its offensive was as follows:

- **User time**: TikTok uses more time per person in the younger user group than Instagram
- ** Content **: The immersion experience of short videos makes the traditional flow of information "out of date"
- **The budget of the ads is being seized**: some advertisers have started to shift the budget from Meta to TikTok

Byte beat (TikTok + tremor) is expected to account for the share of the global digital advertising market ** 7.9%** in 2026 (source: eMarketer).

# How did Meta fight back?

Meta's counter-attack strategy is very direct — ** copying, then crushing on scale**.

Instagram Reels was launched in 2020 to directly replicate the core product pattern of TikTok. The market first laughed at it as a "cucket," but the result was that everything was:

- **Reels annualized advertising revenue of about $50 billion** (source: Gotrade analysis), nearing the realization of the efficiency of information flow advertising
- Instagram Reels has been leveled and even exceeded by TikTok in multiple markets.
- Meta has a social relationship chain of **3.5 billion ** live users - TikTok cannot do this

# Social map vs Pure content algorithm

This is the key to understanding the Meta vs TikTok competition:

**TikTok is a pure content platform** -- it's highly recommended algorithms, but there is no deep social relationship between users. You spend time on TikTok, but you don't maintain relationships.

**Meta is a social + content platform** - Users maintain friends, contact businesses, share photos of their lives, and join groups. The social profile brings stickyness that is not recommended by pure algorithms.

A analogy: TikTok is like a television station that always has new programs -- interesting, but you can change. Instagram and Facebook are like your social circle -- even if the content is not so interesting, your friends are there, and you have to be there.

Inverse: Young users are increasingly socializing on TikTok and Discord, and Facebook social profiles are becoming less sticky to Z generation. If the social profiles of the next generation of users are not formed on Meta, the moat will shrink naturally over time.

Response: This is a legitimate long-term concern. Facebook brands are indeed ageing among young people. But Instagram remains one of the core social platforms for young users, and WhatsApp is a noun for communications in emerging markets around the world.

# TikTok policy risk in America

This is an additional structural benefit. US government policy pressure on TikTok persists, and while the blanket ban has not yet been set, uncertainty itself has stifled TikTok’s growth expectations and advertising owner’s confidence.** If TikTok is restricted or banned in the US, Meta will be the biggest direct beneficiary**.

But note that this double-edged sword could cut Meta -- if "anti-big science platforms" become political consensus, Meta is not safe.

# Second battle: Meta vs Apple

# ATT, how big is the impact?

In 2021, Apple launched the App Tracking Trade Policy (ATT) policy, which requires applications to have a clear user authorization to track their cross-application behaviour.

The impact on Meta is huge:
- Meta's ad precision placement relies heavily on user-wide behavioral data
- A lot of users choose "not allowed to trace" when ATT is online.
- ** 2022 Meta's advertising revenue fell for the first time in history**

Zuckerberg publicly accused Apple of causing a significant decline in the impact of SME advertising.

# Meta's backlash

Meta's response strategy is ** Replace lost data with AI**:

1. ** Modelling alternative tracking**: development of AI-based advertising attribution systems that do not rely on third-party Cookie or device identifiers, but rather use statistical models to extrapolate advertising effects
** Site closed ring**: Promote power provider functionality in stations such as Meta Shops, leaving user browsing, clicking, purchasing behaviour in Meta platform - no cross-application tracking is required
3. **Conversations API**: Allow advertisers to directly connect their server data to Meta, bypass Apple restrictions

Outcome: **2023: Meta advertising revenue rebounded strongly (US$ 134.9 billion, +16 per cent for the year), further accelerated in 2024 (US$ 164.5 billion, +22 per cent), and Q1 2026 increased at 33 per cent**.

The significance of this case is that ** Meta not only carried out the apple attack, but also became stronger in the counter-attack** - AI-driven advertising systems were more efficient than systems that relied on third-party data.

Inverse: If apples further tighten privacy policies (e.g. limit the way Meta collects data on iOS), or Google introduces similar privacy restrictions on Android, Meta may need to adjust again.

Response: This risk does exist. But Meta has demonstrated a capacity to adjust. More importantly, Meta’s AI system is increasingly relying on first-party data (the user’s behaviour within the Meta platform) rather than third-party data – which makes it less sensitive to changes in external privacy policy.

** The FTC filed an appeal**

(Source: NPR, FTC Official Website)

# The key logic of the verdict

The judge's core judgment is:** in the presence of TikTok and YouTube, Meta does not have a monopoly on the social media market**. This judgement is consistent with market realities – and if Meta is really a monopoly, it cannot face such intense competitive pressure from TikTok.

# What does the appeal of the FTC mean?

The FTC filed an appeal in January 2026, which means that the proceedings are not yet finally concluded. But several factors reduce the seriousness of the risk:

** First instance judgement favourable to Meta** — High threshold for cassation of first instance judgement by the Court of Appeal
2. ** Current political environment**: US political environment in 2026 has eased pressure on large technology companies to regulate
** Even if the appeal were successful, the likelihood of splitting Meta is low** — very few court-ordered splitting of mergers and acquisitions that have been in operation for more than a decade

Counterpart: The risk of antimonopoly litigation is not "will it be split" but rather ** long-term restrictions on behaviour ** ** ** e.g., prohibiting Meta from sharing user data between different applications or limiting future acquisitions. These "soft limits" may be more practical and likely to occur than splits.

Response: Indeed. The EU Digital Market Act (DMA) already imposes similar restrictions on behaviour. This is a risk that requires long-term surveillance but is unlikely to be triggered in the short term.

# Comprehensive assessment of the moat

After four field tests, Meta's moat river was assessed:

# Protecting River I: Network effects (exceeding)

**39.8 billion ** The social networking network of the live monthly users is the core moat. New competitors can make better product functions (the algorithms of TikTok are certainly better), but they cannot copy the social profiles of the users. Google+ failed, Clubhouse disappeared -- because the users' friends are not on it.

# # Protected River II: economies of scale (extremely strong)

Meta's advertising system serves millions of advertisers, with marginal costs close to zero. AI's fixed costs of inputs are high ($1,250-145 billion), but when these inputs are completed, they can serve all 3.5 billion users and all advertisers.** The larger the size, the lower the unit cost – and the larger the Meta is the world's number one**.

# Three of the moats: data advantage (extremely strong)

Meta has the largest user behaviour data set in the world – social interaction, content preferences, shopping behaviour, and newsletters. These are the core fuel for AI model training, creating a "more data -- better model -- better experience -- more users — more data" loop.

# Accompaniment of River IV: Conversion costs (strong)

- **end of user**: social links and historical content are difficult to migrate
- **Adverter **: Learning curve and data accumulation in advertising delivery systems - The system of acquisition of a large number of SMEs is built around Meta
- **Developerend**: binding effects of Llama ecosystems

# # Protect the river V: brand (medium)

Instagram has a strong brand among young users, and whatsApp is a communications noun in emerging markets. But **Facebook brands are ageing among young people** — a continuing challenge, and the only moating river.

# A combination of judgment #

** The moat of Meta has survived the Triple Test of TikTok Competition, Apple Privacy Bump, and FTC Antimonopoly Proceedings, which is assessed as "deep and battle-tested".** The only structural erosion is from the ageing of Facebook brands and the migration of young users to social habits, but Instagram and WhatsApp have largely made up for this.

Inverse: The moat is a retrospective assessment -- past holding does not mean future holding. AI is lowering access barriers to content platforms, and a future "AI original social platform" may be created to destabilize existing patterns.

Response: This possibility cannot be ruled out. But historically, social platforms with network effects have rarely been directly subverted by technological innovation — MSN has not been replaced by better chat software, but by a new form of mobile end, such as micro-mail/whatsapp. The next subversion, if it comes, is more likely to come from changes in hardware patterns (e.g. AR glasses instead of mobile phones), rather than purely software innovations. Interestingly, Meta has a layout on AR glasses (Ray-Ban cooperation), although it is very small.
