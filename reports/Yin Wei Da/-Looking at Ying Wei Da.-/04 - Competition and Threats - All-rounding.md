# AMD real bottleneck

The biggest problem for AMD is not hardware, but software**.

The gap between ROCm and CUDA has narrowed from 2-3 in 2023 to 10-30% in 2026 -- but the difference between 10-30% is "unacceptable" in the training scene for extreme performance.

More crucial are the long tail ecology: debugging tools (AMD does not have Nsight level products), optimising closed rings (cudNN manually adjusts to 90% + utilization per generation of GPU), and frame integration (ROCm remains a second-class citizen in PyTorch, bug repair 6-12 months behind).

The strategy of the **AMD is "No compared to British Weights, Winning in the Field of Sex Prices"**-MI355X in a large number of reasoning scenarios where TCO is already inversely British. This is valid in the reasoning market, but it is almost ineffective in the training market.

# History references: lessons from Intel

Intel's share of the x86 server market fell from **98 per cent** in 2017 to **73 per cent** in 2025 (x86). Adding to the erosion of ARM servers, Intel's "extended share" may have fallen to 55-60 per cent.

** From 98% to 73%, it took about eight years.** Intel's decline is due to its own failure (decree, missed port end).

** But the biggest lesson is **: Intel's Māori rate is enough to attract a replacement from around 60%.
