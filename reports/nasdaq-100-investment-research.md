# Simulate the evaluation

> ** **Quality dimension of business:** NASDAQ 100 is a good business, but investors are prone to make a mistake -- to equate "good business" with "good investment." Good business is good investment if it is bought at good prices. Prices are not cheap, but they are not too expensive. If you want to hold them for more than five years, they are probably not going to cost you. But if you expect 21% annualized returns in the next five years, it is almost impossible -- the valuation starting point is too high.

> **Campine river cover:** The Nusdak 100 moat does not lie in the indispensability of a company, but in the overall leadership of the US science and technology ecosystem. This moat is short-term, but faces two long-term threats: China AI ' s catch-up is faster than expected, and regulation may divide these giants.

> **Risk dimension:** My biggest concern is not valuation -- 32 x PEs are high but profitable. The real risk is "AI Capital Overinvestment." The $690 billion in capital spending is the largest investment in human history, and if returns fall short of expectations, the free cash flow of these companies may continue to deteriorate, buy backs may be suspended for a long time, and the valuation hub may be permanently moved down. This is not a 2000 bubble (when companies don't make money), but it could be a structural disappointment "inputs too much, returns too slow".

> **Cultural Trends Dimension:** AI is a real technological change at the civilized level, which is beyond question. But investors need to distinguish between "AI will change the world" and "Nasdak 100 is now in possession of a higher return." Historically, railways, electricity, and the Internet have changed the world, but investors in companies involved in infrastructure reconstruction often experience painful feedback. The key question is not whether AI is important (the answer is clearly yes), but whether current prices already adequately reflect AI's importance.
