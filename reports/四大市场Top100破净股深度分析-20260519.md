## III. In-Depth Analysis by Group

### A. Bank Stocks (6 names)

#### Industrial Bank (601166.SH) - PB 0.41

**Business essence**: Core income comes from net interest income (67%) and non-interest income. Two distinguishing features: first, it is the "king of interbank business," with 1.23 trillion yuan in interbank assets and 2.48 trillion yuan in interbank liabilities (29% of total liabilities); second, it is steadily transforming into a "green bank, wealth bank, and investment bank." The client base is mainly corporate.

**Moat**: Banking license plus multiple licenses such as Industrial Fund; the "bank-to-bank platform" connects thousands of small and medium financial institutions to form a network effect; total assets have surpassed 11 trillion yuan. But compared with the physical branch networks and retail stickiness of state-owned banks, the moat is weaker.

**Fundamental trend**:

| Revenue growth | -5.2% | +0.66% | +0.24% | -1.07% |

| ROE | ~10.7% | 9.98% | 9.2% | - |
| Net interest margin | 1.93% | 1.82% | 1.71% | down 18 bps YoY |
| NPL ratio | 1.07% | 1.07% | 1.08% | - |
| Provision coverage ratio | ~237% | ~234% | 228% | - |

**Why PB is low**:

| Factor | Classification |
|------|------|
| Net interest margin fell faster than peers (down 18 bps vs industry 3 bps) | **Fundamental deterioration** |
| Interbank-business structure is a structural drag (high-cost liabilities funding low-yield assets) | **Fundamental deterioration** |
| ROE fell from 12% to 9.2% | **Fundamental deterioration** |
| Net profit stagnated for two consecutive years (+0.1% / +0.3%) | **Fundamental deterioration** |
| Uncertainty during the transformation phase | Sentiment / market bias |
| Overall undervaluation of the A-share banking sector | Liquidity / structural |

**Overall view**: PB 0.41 does reflect real difficulties - faster NIM compression, heavy interbank dependence, and ROE decline. But 0.41 means the market is effectively assuming 60% of net assets will "evaporate," which is too pessimistic. The NPL ratio of 1.08% and provision coverage of 228% are healthy, and the dividend yield above 5.8% provides a cushion. **Reasonably low, but there is still no clear sign of fundamental improvement.**

### B. Property / REIT (4 names)

#### China Resources Land (1109.HK) - PB 0.55

**Business essence**: The flagship SOE developer. It has three engines: development sales, income-producing properties, and light-asset management. 2025 revenue was 281.4 billion yuan. Recurring business (such as mall rent) accounted for more than half of core net profit for the first time (51.8%). It operates more than 100 MixC-branded malls, with rental income of about 21.1 billion yuan (+9%) and occupancy above 97%.

**Moat**: SOE financing-cost advantage (overall financing cost about 3.1%, among the lowest in the industry); MixC / MixC World is a top-tier domestic commercial-property brand; land bank is concentrated in five core cities, accounting for nearly 80%.

**Fundamental trend**:

| Metric | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|------|--------|--------|--------|
| Revenue (RMB 100m) | 2,511 | 2,788 | 2,814 |
| Core net profit (RMB 100m) | 277.7 | 254.2 | 224.8 |
| Core net profit from recurring business (RMB 100m) | 96 | 103 | 116.5 |
| Mall rental income (RMB 100m) | ~178 | 193 | ~211 |

**Why PB is low**: Development gross margin fell from 27% to 16.8% (fundamental deterioration); the market discounts China Resources Land together with private developers that blew up (sentiment bias); recurring business value is not fully priced in (sentiment bias); and there is a Hong Kong stock liquidity discount (structural).

**Overall view**: PB 0.55 is low. Recurring business profit exceeds 10 billion yuan and keeps growing. If the malls were valued separately like REITs, the implied value would be far above book. **This is a high-quality name that got caught in an industry-wide selloff.**

#### Sun Hung Kai Properties (0016.HK) - PB 0.63-0.67

**Business essence**: One of Hong Kong's largest developers, driven by property development + property investment (rental income). Revenue in FY2024/25 was HK$90.1 billion. It owns Hong Kong's top commercial landmarks (such as ICC), and NAV per share is about HK$213.

**Moat**: Hong Kong's largest private farmland reserve (31 million square feet, impossible to replicate); core commercial properties sit in prime locations; net gearing is only 15.1%, so the balance sheet is very strong. Annual rental income is HK$24.5 billion.

**Fundamental trend**: Underlying profit has been roughly flat for three years (about HK$21.8 billion), and rents remain stable at HK$24.5-25.0 billion. ROE is low (about 3.2%) but stable.

**Why PB is low**: Hong Kong office vacancy exceeds 15% and rents are down 20-30% (fundamental deterioration); residential development margins are under pressure (fundamental deterioration); farmland conversion is uncertain (structural); and low ROE makes it less attractive to capital focused on returns efficiency (fundamental).

**Overall view**: PB 0.63-0.67 is defensible but still somewhat low. Asset quality is excellent and leverage is very low. If Hong Kong real estate continues to recover, there is 30-40% rerating potential.

#### CK Asset Holdings (1113.HK) - PB 0.43

**Business essence**: A property and diversified investment platform under the Li Ka-shing family. It has already transformed into a diversified investment company spanning property, infrastructure, and utilities. Net profit attributable to shareholders in 2025 was HK$10.85 billion. After selling its UK power-grid business for about HK$22.2 billion, the group will move to a net-cash position. Net gearing is only 2.4%.

**Moat**: Diversified asset allocation (global diversification); extremely conservative finances; Li Ka-shing family's capital-allocation capabilities.

**Fundamental trend**: Net profit attributable to shareholders has declined for three straight years (17.3 -> 13.7 -> 10.8 billion), but operating profit excluding property revaluation has been basically stable (about HK$12 billion).

**Why PB is low**: Conglomerate discount (structural); three straight years of lower reported net profit (fundamental deterioration - on an accounting basis); the asset sale is interpreted as "divestment" (sentiment bias); canceling the earnings press conference caused dissatisfaction (sentiment bias); and asset NAV is hard to verify (structural).

**Overall view**: PB 0.43 is low, but there is value-trap risk. The core issue is not poor assets but low market trust in transparency. **A catalyst such as a large buyback or special dividend is needed to unlock value.**

#### Link REIT (0823.HK) - PB 0.64-0.67

**Business essence**: Asia's largest REIT. Its assets include Hong Kong retail properties (former public-housing malls), parking lots, and properties in mainland China and overseas. FY2024/25 total income was HK$14.22 billion, and DPU was 272.34 Hong Kong cents (+3.7%).

**Moat**: Monopoly-like position in community retail properties (public-housing malls have no direct substitutes); occupancy has stayed above 97% for a long time; investment-grade credit ratings (A/A2).

**Fundamental trend**: Revenue and DPU continue to grow moderately, but NAV per unit has declined for three straight years (74 -> 71 -> 63-67 HK$), mainly because of technical downward revaluations of properties. Core operating metrics remain healthy.

**Why PB is low**: Rising rates have weighed on REITs (liquidity / structural); cap rates for Hong Kong retail property have expanded (fundamental deterioration on the balance-sheet side); exclusion from Stock Connect limits demand (liquidity / structural); and DPU growth has slowed (fundamental).

**Overall view**: PB 0.64-0.67 is low. Core operations are excellent, and community-retail demand is highly sticky. A rate-cut cycle plus inclusion in Stock Connect would be directly positive. Dividend yield of 6.6-7% provides a good cushion. **This is the most certain name among the four property / REIT names.**

#### Sinopec (0386.HK) - PB 0.58-0.60

**Business essence**: One of the world's largest refining companies, with more than 30,000 gas stations (No. 1 in China) and 28,200 Easy Joy convenience stores. 2025 revenue was 2.78 trillion yuan.

**Moat**: Refined-oil retail and wholesale licenses are impossible to replicate; the 30,000-station retail network cannot be replaced; it also benefits from SOE resource endowment.

**Fundamental trend**: Net profit has more than halved from 60.5 billion to 31.8 billion (2023 -> 2025), and ROE fell to 3.86%. But the company kept an 81% payout ratio, and non-fuel businesses grew 21% against the trend.

**Why PB is low**: Lower global oil-price center (fundamental deterioration); peak refined-oil demand (fundamental deterioration - long-term structural); overcapacity in chemicals (fundamental deterioration); and Hong Kong offshore discount (liquidity / structural).

**Overall view**: **Low, but somewhat justified.** Fundamentals are indeed deteriorating. But the transformation value of the 30,000 gas stations is underestimated, and an 81% payout ratio implies a 6-7% dividend yield that offers a cushion.

#### China Unicom (0762.HK) - PB 0.53-0.58

**Overall view**: **Low.** ROE is indeed not high enough, but the speed of free-cash-flow improvement is being underestimated. The 61.3% payout ratio corresponds to a 5-6% dividend yield. The key catalyst is whether ROE can continue moving toward 7%+.

## IV. Core Findings and Conclusions

### 1. Classification of reasons for low PB

The reasons for low PB across all 23 below-book companies can be grouped as follows:

| Reason type | Typical names | Share |
|---------|---------|------|
| **Mostly fundamental deterioration** | Zhongsheng, Xinyi Solar, Industrial Bank, Sinopec | ~4 names (17%) |
| **Mostly sentiment bias + structural factors** | CITIC, CK Hutchison, ICBC, CCB, CMB, Sinopharm, Ping An | ~7 names (30%) |
| **Mixed fundamental + sentiment** | Bank of China, China Resources Land, CK Asset, Sun Hung Kai, ZhongAn Online | ~5 names (22%) |
| **Reasonable pricing due to low ROE** | China Unicom, China Telecom, CRRC, MTR, China Resources Power | ~5 names (22%) |
| **Borderline below book (PB close to 1)** | Ningbo Bank, Ping An | ~2 names (9%) |

### 2. Hong Kong's structural discount is the biggest common denominator

Of the roughly 15-17 below-book names, the vast majority are Hong Kong stocks. The net-asset-discount phenomenon in Hong Kong is structural:
- **AH premium is widespread**: for the same company, H-share PB is usually 15-25% lower than A-share PB
- **Foreign investors remain underweight China**: geopolitical and macro uncertainty suppress valuations
- **Liquidity is insufficient**: average daily trading volume in Hong Kong is far below A-shares
- **Southbound capital is repairing the gap**: since 2026, Southbound flows have remained net positive, but the process is slow

### 3. Bank-sector net-asset discounts are an industry-wide phenomenon

Among the 42 listed A-share banks, almost all trade below book. The key reasons are:
- Net interest margins continue to compress (from above 2% to around 1.3%)
- ROE is trending down
- The market worries about exposure to real estate and local government debt

But one important point: NPL ratios are stable or declining, and provision coverage is above 200% across the board. The banks' underlying asset base is much stronger than the market pricing implies.
