# Core features of business models

When you put five lines together, the business model of ant numbers has three distinct features:

Characteristic I: High unit cost, low client count

The ant count section is more like Palantir (high unit price, government/large enterprise customer) or permanent electronic (financial IT monopoly) than Internet subscription software.

The average unit price is calculated at approximately **42 million**/clients (5.5 billion/13 million customers), but the distribution is highly uneven - the head bank integrated programme may be tens of thousands of grades, and long-tail clients may be only tens of thousands.

# Characteristic II: Income structure transition from "project" to "subscribe plus pay-for-effect"

The CEO's "pay-for-effect" model, promoted by Zhao, is an interesting innovation. The Nimbaan Bank case (a proportional 30 million annual impairment) shows that the ant count section is willing to link income to the client's business performance.

This means ** higher customer sticker** (paying for good results on a continuous basis), but also ** greater income volatility** (stopping payments on a negative basis).

# Character III: Cross-sales between five lines

The ant count section is designed to cut into the client using a line (e.g. SOFAStack intermediate or Moss privacy calculations) and then gradually extend to other product lines such as AI smart bodies, block chains and others.

The premise of this "land expansion" strategy is ** customer depth** -- and in the financial sector, this is exactly the strength of ant count. 100% state-owned, 60% local coverage provides huge cross-market space.
