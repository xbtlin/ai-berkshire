# First risk: profit may be high in the cycle

This is the core issue of understanding the Progress valuation**, none.

Progress 2025 combined ratio **87.4%**, the company's own goal **96%**. Nearly 9 percentage points are missing. What does this 9 percentage point mean?

We do a simple pressure test:

|------|---------|-----------|-----------|---------|
<unk> Current (2025) <unk> 87.4% <unk> 12.6% ~ $11.3 billion <unk> <unk> 10.5 times**

<unk> Normalization 93-94% 6-7% ~ 70-8 billion United States dollars (estimate) <unk> 15-17 times**
<unk> Pressure scenario 96% (target line) <unk> 4% ~ 50-55 billion (estimate) <unk> 22-24 times** <unk>

Note: Net profits are rough estimates, assuming that premium income is constant, investment returns are flat, and only the insured profit rates are adjusted. This is more complex.

** See? The same share price is $203, and the PE is 10.5 to 24 times, depending on what you think the "normal" ratio is.**

If you think that Progress can sustain 90 percent of the combined ratio (which is really leading by its data and technology advantages), 10.5 times PE is attractive.

If you think that the combined ratio will eventually return to 93-96% (historical averages and industry cycle patterns), the current profit is overestimated, and the actual PE may be 15-24 times.

** Anti-conversion**: The combined rate of 87.4% is not sustainable. Over the past 20 years, the combined average of Progress has been between 92 and 95% over the long term. The current level benefits from the delayed effects of 2022-2023 price increases and a favourable compensation environment, which will inevitably rise as tariff competition increases.

** Positive response** Yes, 87.4% are not sustainable. But the Progress rate is structural in its combined ratio of 3-5 percentage points to the long run of the industry (Snapshot Data Barrier + Direct Cost Advantage + Insurance Discipline), and even if it returns to 91-93%, it is still an efficient profit machine. Propressive purchases are not a sustainable 87.4%, but a wager that keeps it better than the industry.

** My judgement**: The reasonable normalization combined ratio is about **91-93%**, corresponding to the net profits of normalization **75-90 billion**, normalization PE approximately **13-16 times**. This level is not cheap in large American insurance companies, but it is not expensive given the quality of growth of Progress (market rate driven by not purely price increases).** One word in general: reasonable low**

# Third risk: Autopilot -- far-flung or near-fate?

This is a long-term risk that hangs on the entire industry. The logic is simple: ** If the car is not in accident, does it need insurance?**

But how far is it?

(b) Impact on vehicle insurance
|--------|-------------------|-----------|
<unk> 2026-2030 <unk> Level 2 predominantly AV taxis operating locally <unk> very small impact**
<unk> 2030-2035 <unk> Level 3/4 Progressive production <unk> Impact started but controlled <unk>
<unk> 2035-2045 <unk> Level 4/5 may account for 10% of American vehicles <unk> Substantive effects are beginning to appear <unk>
<unk> 2045+ <unk> Mass (if it happens) <unk>

Source: Fitch Ratings Sensitization Frame

Fitch Ratings concluded that:** the substantive impact of autopilots on insurance companies is at least 10 years old.**

Core causes:

1. The United States has **280 million registered vehicles, with an average useful life exceeding **12 years. Even if all new vehicles are to be driven automatically tomorrow, stock replacement will take a generation.
2. Level 2 Auxiliary Drivers (currently mainstream) still require the driver to be responsible throughout the process and the insurance liability remains unchanged.
Even if accidents are reduced, the cost of maintenance may increase — sensors, cameras, laser radars are much more expensive than traditional parts.
4. The subject of responsibility may shift from "personal driver" to "manufacturer/software vendor", which would create a new insurance demand** (product liability insurance) rather than eliminate it.

** Anti-conversation**: Technological change often occurs at a faster than expected rate. Waymo has run a fully automated taxi in six cities, and Tesla FSD continues to overlap. If the breakthrough comes faster than expected, the entire industry's valuation framework will have to be rewritten.

** Positive response**: Even if autopilot is faster than expected, the data capability of Progress is the best preparation for a shift – both for human drivers and for hybrid driving models, with companies with the most data and the most actuarial capabilities. And, if the industry is hit, Progress is probably the one with the "last down."

**5-year dimension judgement**: The impact of autopilot on Progress is negligible. 10-year dimensions require sustained attention. 20-year dimensions are real structural risks.

# Fourth risk: climate change and catastrophic loss

Progress is expanding the housing insurance business through the Robinson strategy. The risk profile of housing insurance and car insurance is very different -- ** it is exposed to the great risks of hurricanes, hails, wildfires, etc.**

The frequency and intensity of climate disasters in the United States have increased significantly in recent years:

- 2023 USI **93.5 billion** (Standing Storms Record)
- 2024-2025 Billions of dollars lost in Southern California wildfires.
- Hails, hurricanes are increasing in frequency.

Progress continues to have a low share of housing insurance and to use reinsurance to hedge the risk of disasters. But as Robinson moves forward, the share of housing insurance will increase and the exposure to climate risk will expand.

** Probability judgement**: Impacts are manageable in the short term (3-5 years), but this is a risk factor that requires continuous monitoring.

Tricia Griffith has been in the CEO position since 2016, almost **10 **. Her enforcement is the first in the industry -- 9 years of revenue from $20 billion to $83.2 billion, with the city rate from 11% to 18.6%.

Key career:

- 28 years of progressive promotion, almost all core positions in the company
- CEO, the first woman in the company ' s history.
- In 2025, Cleveland Business Hall.

But 10 years of tenure means that the succession issue begins to surface. In 2026, Q1 appointed a new CFO, which may be one of the signals of management handover.

**Risk assessment**: Progress competitiveness is more from systems and cultures** (insure discipline, data wheels, two-channel models) than from the wise decision-making of a single leader. Griffith, as long as his successor remains equally disciplined, has limited impact. If the new CEO changes its strategic direction (e.g., to sacrifice discipline for growth), the consequences can be severe – the lessons of GEO 2022 are the lessons of the road ahead.
