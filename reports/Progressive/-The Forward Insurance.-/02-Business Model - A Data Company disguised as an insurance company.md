# Answer the question first: How does an insurance company earn money?

Most people buy their lifetime car insurance, never think where the insurance company gets the money. The intuition answer is "investing" -- they collect premiums for stock and buy bonds. That's half the answer.

To be precise, the insurance company has two profit engines, and Progress is special because it has done the most of the engines that most of its peers ignore:

<unk> Insured profits - premium revenue - payment of expenses - operating costs <unk> ~ $10.2 billion** (Composite rate 87.4%) <unk>
The return on investment, the return on the investment from the floating cash, the contribution (75.9 billion dollars in portfolio)

Most insurance companies have combined ratios of 97-102 per cent — that is, insurance is not in itself profitable or even poor and is fully financed by investment earnings.

** The combined rate of 87.4% means a net gain on the insurance ** $12.6** per $100 premium. Investment returns are additional bonuses.

This raises a key question:** why is Progress able to cover substantial profits, and most of the peers unable to?**

The answer is in three places: direct marketing patterns, data pricing, and insurance discipline.

# Second floor: Snapshot -- 16 years of grinding data barriers

In 2008, Progress launched a project called **Snapshot**.

The initial form was simple: a small device (OBD interface) for the bodyguard's car, recording driving behavior - the frequency of brakes, the length of the journey, the mileage, etc.. According to these data, the premium for "good driver" is lower and the premium for "bad driver" is higher.

This is the grandkid of Usage-Based Insurance, UBI.

Sixteen years later, the amount and technology of Snapshot data is no longer a level of magnitude:

|------|------|
♪ The size of the user ♪ ♪ The whole country ♪ 21 million ♪ The insurance has shared the data on driving behaviour ♪

A new generation of remote information processing applications driven by AI was launched in November 2025
** 5-8 years ** Start building up **

Source: Progress Officer Network, Investor Relations Page

** The data constitute a barrier that is almost non-repelicable.** The logical chain is as follows:

```
The more data are, the more accurate the actuarial model, the more accurate the pricing.
* Good driver gets a lower price (willing to insure)
* Bad driver faces higher prices (resent to come)
• Sustained improvement in client quality * Continuous decline in the rate of payment
♪ More profitable, more resources can be invested to optimize models
• Data volume continues to increase (cycle)
```

This is a classic ** data wheel**. And the many "low-cost" and "send efficiency wheels" of the US Corps are essentially the same structure -- ** slowly starting, but it's hard to catch up with them when they turn.**

** Reverse view**: Tesla has introduced its own insurance product, using vehicle real-time data to price it directly. If the garage has more data on behaviour than Snapshot (after all, the data for the plant is 24 hours real-time), the data advantage of Progress could be reversed.

This risk is real, but it is small. Tesla insurance covers only some states, and faces the US **280 million registered vehicles ** stock market. No single car factory will have enough holdings to pose a systematic threat to Progress.

# Third floor: Insure discipline - no-loss policy

Data and technology are "capacity," and discipline is "option." Progress is third level of competitiveness is ** cultural **.

Progress sets a clear business objective:** a combined ratio for calendar years does not exceed 96 per cent.**

This means that management commits to making at least $4 a year (per $100) in the insurance chain. It doesn't sound too high, but the reality in the insurance industry is that many companies can't even hold out 100%.

This discipline was severely tested in 2022. The US inflation surged in that year, the price of spare parts for repairs, the cost of medical treatment surged, and the cost of paying for the entire industry deteriorated. Progress

** Fast-tracked increases. I'd rather lose my client for a short period than a loss policy.**

See the comparison? 2022 Progress composite rate 95.8% — although close to the target line, still profitable. The industry averaged over 100% — means that most companies are losing money in the insurance chain.

** The same period, the GEOCO story is the opposite: ** The price increase was hesitant, with insurance losses in 2022 ** $1.9 billion**. Buffett subsequently acknowledged in the shareholder letter that the GEOCO "was behind in technology and pricing" and led the reform itself. GEICO took two years to turn the loss into a gain - the cost was a fall in the market share from ~14% to ~12.3%.

The example of GEOCO is precisely proof of the value of insurance discipline:** in the insurance industry, the price of hesitation is a double blow to loss + loss.** Progress achieved a decisive increase in prices that resulted in a two-fold harvest of profits and shares in 2023-2025.

# A analogy: Progress like who?

If you use a company familiar with Chinese investors, you can use an analogy:

- **Progressive ** Unlike China's Peace** - Peace is a consolidated financial group with much more complex operations
- **Progressive is more like "Rocker of Car Risks" ** - in a seemingly ungrowth industry, the share is being consistently stolen by algorithms and efficiency differences.

But there is one key difference: the multi-collegeed moat is being eroded by competitors like tremors (for details, see More More), and the Progress data wheel is accelerating for the foreseeable future **21 million users' data on driving behaviour is a barrier to self-enforcement over time, not a barrier to weakening competition**.

** Anti-conversation** Any technical barriers have the potential to be subverted. If a tech giant (apple, Google) or car factory (Texra) cuts into car risk pricing in a completely different way, the data advantage of Progress could be weakened in 10-15 years. This is a real long-term risk, but not a present threat.
