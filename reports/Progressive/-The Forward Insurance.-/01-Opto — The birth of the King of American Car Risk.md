## Something that just happened

After the end of World War II, the leader of the U.S. auto insurance market changed once—in the first quarter of 2026.

On May 19, 2026, S&P Global Market Intelligence released an inconspicuous news: ** Progressive (Progressive Insurance) surpassed State Farm's $17.1 billion to become the largest auto insurance company in the United States with quarterly direct premiums of $18.1 billion. ** State Farm sat in this position for nearly eighty years, and this time, it went down.

Progressive didn’t get there by relying on industry dividends. The U.S. auto insurance market only grows by 3-5% per year and is a typical mature stock market. It took 10 years for it to increase its market share from 11% to 18.6% in this pool with almost no incremental growth, while its profit margins were still increasing** - this was purely based on efficiency differences to squeeze out its opponents.

Let’s look at a set of key figures first:

| Indicator | Value | Source |
|------|------|------|
| Net Premium Income 2025 | **$83.2 billion** | Progressive 2025 Annual Report |
| 2025 Net Profit | **$11.3 Billion** | Progressive 2025 Annual Report |
| 2025 Combined Ratio | **87.4%** | Progressive 2025 Annual Report |
| Personal auto insurance market share (2025) | **18.6%** | NAIC / S&P Global |
| Q1 2026 Net Income | **$2.8 billion** (EPS $4.80) | Progressive Q1 2026 10-Q |

Putting these numbers together - a company with an annual net profit of **11.3 billion US dollars**, ROIC of **29%**, and a rapidly increasing market share has a PE of only **10.5 times**.

Looks ridiculously cheap. But there is a big problem here, which will be expanded upon later.

## The essence of the auto insurance business: collect money first, pay compensation later

Before dismantling Progressive, first understand a premise: **Auto insurance is one of the best business models in the world. **

The logic is simple:

1. **Policyholders pay first**——fixed monthly/quarterly premium payment
2. **The insurance company takes this money to invest** - this money is called "float"
3. **Compensation will only be paid after an accident** - and the auto insurance claim settlement cycle is short (unlike asbestos compensation cases that drag on for decades)

If an insurance company's "combined ratio" is less than 100% (combined ratio = loss ratio + expense ratio), it means that the underwriting itself is making money -- not counting investment income.

Progressive's 2025 combined ratio is **87.4%**. Translated into human language: **For every $100 in premiums collected, only $87.4 was spent (losses + operations), netting $12.6. Investment income is calculated separately. **

How good is this number? Compare this:

| Company | 2025 Combined Ratio | Meaning |
|------|----------------|------|
| Progressive | **87.4%** | Netted $12.60 per $100 in premiums |
| GEICO (owned by Berkshire) | **~81.5%** (2024 data, not separately disclosed in 2025) | Rebound after significant cost cutting |
| Industry average | **~97%** | Barely breaking the bank |
| State Farm | Undisclosed (mutual aid system) | Not disclosed to the public |

**Considers**: The combined ratio of 87.4% is well below Progressive's own target of 96% and well below historical averages. This may be a cycle high - when the industry is profitable, competitors will cut prices to grab share, and the combined ratio will eventually recover. If it returns to 93-94%, net profit may drop from US$11.3 billion to US$7-8 billion, and the PE will change from 10.5 times to 15-17 times.

This is the core question for understanding Progressive's valuation: **How much of current excess earnings is cyclical and how much is structural? ** This will be discussed in detail in Part 04.

## Understand the rise of Progressive in one picture

Progressive’s growth in the past 10 years has not been based on an explosion of one or two quarters, but a stable upward curve:

| Year | Net premium income | Net profit | Combined ratio | Personal auto insurance market share |
|------|-----------|--------|---------|-------------|
| 2016 | ~$20 billion | — | ~94% | ~11% |
| 2020 | ~$39.7 billion | ~$5.3 billion | ~87.7% | ~14% |
| 2022 | ~$49.7 billion | ~$1.3 billion | 95.8% | ~14% |
| 2023 | ~$62.3 billion | ~$3.9 billion | 94.9% | ~15% |
| 2024 | ~$75.3 billion | ~$8.5 billion | 88.8% | ~17% |
| 2025 | **~$83.2 billion** | **~$11.3 billion** | **87.4%** | **~18.6%** |

Data source: Progressive annual reports, NAIC

Several key nodes:

1. **2022 is a stress test**: U.S. inflation is soaring, car repair costs and medical bills are skyrocketing, and industry-wide claims are worsening. Progressive's response was to raise prices quickly and would rather lose customers in the short term than make a loss-making policy. As a result, profits fell sharply that year to $1.3 billion—but it survived and began taking share from its competitors.

2. **GEICO made a fatal mistake** during the same period: Hesitant to raise prices, resulting in an underwriting loss of **$1.9 billion** in 2022. After Buffett personally led the reform, GEICO vigorously laid off employees and cut costs, and profits rebounded to US$7.8 billion in 2024 - but at the cost of a market share drop from **~14% to ~12.3%**. A large part of these lost shares were eaten up by Progressive.

3. **2024-2025 is the harvest period**: Price increases are fully transmitted, the premium base is enlarged, and "both volume and price rise." The market share jumped from 17% to 18.6%, approaching and eventually surpassing State Farm in Q1 of 2026.

**In 10 years (2016-2025), revenue has increased from 20 billion to 83.2 billion, and market share has increased from 11% to 18.6%. ** This is almost unprecedented in the mature U.S. insurance industry.

## Why Chinese investors should pay attention to Progressive

Three reasons:

**First, it is a "perfect sample" of Buffett's most admired business model. ** Buffett’s favorite business in his life is insurance—Berkshire’s core engines are GEICO and reinsurance. Progressive is using more advanced technology to take this model to the extreme. To understand Progressive is to understand the contemporary version of the classic value investment framework of "float + underwriting discipline".

**Second, it proves that it is possible to "continue to grab share by relying on efficiency differences in mature industries." ** Haidilao, Pinduoduo, and Meituan, which are familiar to Chinese investors, are essentially doing similar things - not high growth in the industry, but "crowding out" their peers by relying on differences in operating efficiency. Progressive’s story is similar to those of these companies.

**Third, its valuation issues are instructive. ** 10.5x PE looks cheap, but if earnings are at cycle highs, "cheapness" may be an illusion. This judgment framework is applicable to investments in all cyclical industries.
