## 2. Combined Ratio Trend - The "Thermometer" of Underwriting Profit

Combined ratio = loss ratio + expense ratio. Below 100% means underwriting itself is profitable.

| Year | Combined ratio | Trend |
|------|---------|------|
| 2020 | ~87.7% | Less driving during the pandemic lowered claims |
| 2021 | ~92.4% | Back to normal |
| 2022 | 95.8% | Inflation pushed claims higher, and the industry came under pressure |
| 2023 | 94.9% | Price increases started to work |
| 2024 | 90.0% | Strong improvement |
| 2025 | 87.4% | Best level in years; ROE reached 40% |

**Key takeaways:**

1. **2022 was a stress test**: inflation pushed repair and medical costs sharply higher, hurting industry combined ratios. Progressive responded by pricing quickly upward, which is a clear sign of pricing power.
2. **2024-2025 were the harvest years**: once price increases flowed through, claims costs fell, but the premium base had already grown, creating a "volume + price" tailwind.
3. **What does an 87.4% combined ratio mean?** It means USD 12.6 of underwriting profit per USD 100 of premiums, before investment income.

## 3. Investment Portfolio - Conservative but Efficient Float Management

Progressive manages a roughly USD 75.9 billion investment portfolio, of which:

| Asset class | Share | Notes |
|---------|------|------|
| Fixed income securities | 94.6% | Treasuries, corporates, MBS, etc. |
| Equities | ~5.4% | A small amount of stocks and preferreds |

**Investment income performance:**

- Q1 2026 investment income grew 13% YoY, helped by a larger portfolio and reinvesting maturities into higher-yielding securities
- The strategy is extremely conservative: mostly U.S. Treasuries and high-grade corporates, prioritizing principal safety over maximum yield
- Management says there are no material credit losses in the fixed-income book

**Compared with Berkshire / GEICO:** Progressive's investment style is closer to a traditional insurer - mostly fixed income, not Buffett-style equity investing with float. That lowers volatility, but it also caps upside. Given that Progressive's underwriting profits are already very healthy (87.4% combined ratio), the conservative approach is actually sensible.

## 4. Growth Engine and Sustainability

**Revenue growth:** 3-year revenue CAGR of 21%, driven by:

1. **Continued market-share gains**: personal auto share rose from about 14% in 2022 to 18.6% in Q1 2026, ranking first in 21 states
2. **Policy growth**: policies in force grew 9% YoY
3. **Price increases**: the lagged effect of aggressive 2022-2023 pricing actions
4. **The "Robinson" strategy**: targeting the bundled auto + home market (USD 230 billion addressable market), where bundled customers retain 15%-20% better and spend 30%-40% more per account

**Sustainability of profit growth:** the current 87.4% combined ratio may be slightly below the company's long-term target (96%), and as competition intensifies and the rate cycle changes, the combined ratio will probably move up again. But even if it goes back to 92%-94%, Progressive will still be highly profitable.

## 5. Valuation Analysis

| Valuation method | Value | Judgment |
|---------|------|------|
| Current P/E | ~11x | Looks cheap on TTM earnings |
| Normalized P/E | ~15-18x | If the combined ratio returns to 93%-94%, net income could fall 30%-40% |
| P/B | — | A high P/B is reasonable for an insurer earning 40% ROE |
| Dividend yield | About 1%+ (annual variable dividend of USD 13.50 per share) | Generous capital return |

**Key valuation judgment:**

1. **11x P/E looks cheap, but current earnings may be near a cyclical peak.** An 87.4% combined ratio is much better than the company's own 96% target and also better than the historical average. If the metric normalizes (say to 93%), net income could fall from USD 11.3 billion to USD 7-8 billion, making the P/E more like 15-17x.
2. **But the growth is real**: the move from 14% to 18.6% market share is structural, not just cyclical.
3. **Overall**: the stock is fairly cheap, but not an obvious deep bargain. If you believe Progressive can sustain a combined ratio below 90% for a long time (thanks to its data and technology advantages), then 11x earnings is attractive.

## 6. Capital Allocation

- **Annual variable dividend**: USD 13.50 per share paid in January 2026
- **Regular quarterly dividend**: USD 0.10 per share
- **Buybacks**: repurchased 2.3 million shares in Q1 2026 for USD 478 million
- **Debt issuance**: sold USD 1.5 billion of senior notes

Capital allocation shows management's confidence and discipline: return capital generously in good years while keeping plenty of solvency.

## 7. Summary

| Dimension | Rating | Notes |
|------|------|------|
| Earnings quality | Excellent | Underwriting profit plus investment income as a two-engine model |
| Growth quality | Strong | Driven by share gains, not just price increases |
| Balance sheet | Solid | 94.6% fixed income, very conservative |
| Capital return | Generous | Variable dividend + buybacks, shareholder-friendly |
| Valuation | Fairly cheap | 11x earnings, but the cycle matters |

**Using Buffett's framework: Progressive is an efficient insurance machine - underwriting makes money, float earns money, and scale keeps expanding. If 11x earnings can hold (meaning the combined ratio does not deteriorate too much), that is an attractive price. The key question is how much of today's excess profit is cyclical versus structural.**
