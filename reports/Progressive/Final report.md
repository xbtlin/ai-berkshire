## Summary of investment thesis

### Why Progressive is a good business

1. **Collect first and pay later**: Auto insurance is a natural float model. Progressive manages an investment portfolio of approximately US$75.9 billion in 2025.
2. **Underwriting itself makes money**: combined ratio 87.4%, net profit of US$12.6 per US$100 in premiums - not counting investment income
3. **Data barriers continue to deepen**: The Snapshot project has accumulated 16 years of driving behavior data (21 million+ users), ranking first in the industry in pricing accuracy
4. **Market share continues to increase**: from 11% in 2016 to 18.6% in Q1 of 2026, approaching State Farm’s first position
5. **Dual channels complement each other**: direct sales 58% + agents 42%, taking into account efficiency and coverage

## Core findings of four dimensions

### Business model analysis (see document 01 for details)

Progressive's core competitiveness is a combination of pricing discipline + data drive. The Snapshot project has created a data flywheel that "becomes more accurate the more it is used": the larger the amount of data, the more accurate the model, good drivers enjoy lower prices and come to insure, bad drivers leave because of high prices, customer quality continues to improve, and the loss rate continues to decrease. This is a positive cycle that reinforces itself over time.

### Financial valuation analysis (see document 02 for details)

11 times PE may seem cheap, but something to note: the current combined ratio of 87.4% is much better than the 96% target and historical average. If it returns to normalization (93-94%), net profit may drop from 11.3 billion to 7-8 billion, corresponding to a PE increase of 15-17 times. But market share growth is structural and will not reverse with the cycle. **Comprehensive judgment: The current valuation is reasonably low, but not extremely undervalued. **

### Industry competition analysis (see document 03 for details)

Progressive has four moats: data algorithm barriers (the widest), economies of scale and brand, dual-channel distribution network, and underwriting discipline culture. In an industry that continues to be concentrated, technology leaders are systematically eating up the share of laggards - GEICO's mistakes in 2022-2023 are the best negative teaching materials.

### Risk and management assessment (see document 04 for details)

Tricia Griffith is one of the best CEOs in the U.S. insurance industry in the past decade (revenue growth 4 times in 9 years). Autonomous driving is the biggest long-term risk, but Fitch Ratings believes that the substantial impact will take at least 10 more years. The biggest short-term risk is a return to the rate cycle - current excess profits cannot last forever.

## Investment advice

### Conclusion: It is worth holding for a long time, but you need to have a clear understanding of cyclicality

**Reason for buying**:
- The business model is excellent and the moat is wide and deepening
- Top management and first-class execution
- 11 times PE corresponds to 29% ROIC, attractive valuation
- There is still room for improvement in market share (goal: surpassing State Farm to become number one)

**Reasons to be cautious**:
- Current earnings are at a cycle high, and the combined ratio of 87.4% is unsustainable
- If the combined ratio returns to 93%+, PE will be re-priced to 15-17 times
- Although autonomous driving is far away, it will eventually arrive. It is a long-term structural risk hanging over the auto insurance industry.
- The CEO has been in office for nearly 10 years, and the succession plan needs attention

### Operation suggestions

- **Long-term investors**: You can open or increase a position at the current price. Progressive is the "best operator" in the U.S. auto insurance industry, and short-term cyclical fluctuations do not change long-term value.
- **Valuation-sensitive investors**: You can wait for the valuation adjustment when the comprehensive ratio rebounds and profits fall to add to your position. If the combined ratio returns to 93-94%, the stock price may see a 15-25% correction, which will be a better time to buy.
- **Position Suggestion**: As the core position of the insurance sector in the portfolio, the appropriate allocation ratio is 5-8%.

## Four-dimensional review

### Business model dimensions

Progressive is the "data company" for the auto insurance industry. It spent sixteen years of patience accumulating an almost unreplicable driving behavior database, and then used this database to build a screening mechanism for "good customers come and bad customers leave". This is not a short-term marketing advantage, but a long-term structural barrier. In the insurance industry where "accurate pricing is everything", this is the most essential competitiveness.

### Financial valuation dimensions

Progressive reminds me of Berkshire in the early years - making money from underwriting, making money from float investment, and still expanding rapidly. If you buy a company with an ROIC of 29% and a growing share at 11 times PE, the margin of safety is sufficient even if cyclical factors are taken into account. The key is to understand: What you're buying is not the 87.4% combined ratio (which will come back), but Progressive's ability to consistently outperform the industry (which won't go away).

### Industry competition dimension

The nature of competition in the auto insurance industry is an "actuarial arms race" - whoever sets prices more accurately will be able to offer lower prices to good customers (to attract them), and higher prices to bad customers (to let them go). Progressive is at least one position ahead in this arms race. GEICO’s 2022 debacle proved: In this industry, the penalty for falling behind in technology is twofold—losing both money and share.

### Risk Management Dimension

Tricia Griffith is a typical "internally trained" excellent manager - from claims adjuster to CEO, she has personal experience in every aspect of the business. Her "paranoid" mentality and persistence in underwriting discipline are the fundamental reasons why Progressive can recover quickly from the inflationary shock in 2022. Autonomous driving is a distant concern rather than a near-term problem, and Progressive’s data capabilities are the best preparation for this transition.
