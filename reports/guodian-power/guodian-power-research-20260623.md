# Four masters' simulations

> ** Buffett**: "I like utility shares -- predictable and red. But I prefer to have a price-fixed utility. The price of electricity is set by others, the price of coal is set by the market, and management does not choose it. 73% of the debt ratio makes me uncomfortable -- this company earns most of the money to the bank. 5% of the dividends is good, but if China's 10-year national debt is 2.3%, I'd rather buy the debt -- 3 percentage points less, zero risk. I'd only consider it when the stock price falls below $4 and the dividends are close to 7%."

> ** Manger**: "Counterly, what do you think -- you're losing the money for electricity? The price of electricity dropped + coal surged, and it happened once in 2021. In this case, the company loses directly, and the dividends are cut off. So you think the "high dividends security cushion" is not safe. And 73% of the debt ratio means that the company is a "leveraging bet" -- magnifying the gains in the wind and losses in the face of the wind. I don't like leverage."

> **Pixming**: "This is not my food. ROE 12%, zero pricing, management's not holding shares -- these three are enough to let me pass. The good business is 'for fools can run', the state electricity is satisfied, but it's not satisfied with 'for a sustainable excess return'. If I don't choose one on the power plate, I'd rather look at a company with a real monopoly on hydropower resources."

> **Lisu**: "In the perspective of civilization, electricity is the cornerstone of civilization. But state electricity is more like the "guard of old civilization" -- 65% of the lighter is in carbon and the age need to be replaced. It is transforming, but not fast enough. If I have to bet on China's energy future, I will see a new energy operator or storage company, not a traditional power company with a fire power that has more than 60% of the power. But I admit that fire power is irreplaceable in the short term -- this gives the state electricity a 's turn-in' window of time."
