## One sentence summary

Dalio defines the present moment as a turning point in the resonance of the five forces of the "big cycle" - debt/currency, internal political divisions, external geopolitical competition, nature/climate, and technological revolution all acting at the same time. The next two years (2026 mid-term election to 2028 general election) are a "particularly dangerous window." In terms of investment, he advocates leaving 60/40 and adding gold and crypto assets as a hedge against the depreciation of the US dollar.

---

## Five Forces Framework (the underlying model used throughout all interviews)

From his long-term "Big Cycle" research, interviews in 2026 repeatedly used this as the analysis skeleton:

1. **Money and Debt Cycle** - When a country's debt exceeds its solvency, it enters the redistribution stage
2. **Internal Order Cycle** - Divide between rich and poor + Split of values → Collapse of institutional trust
3. **External order cycle** - the transfer of power between the dominant country and the challenging country
4. **Natural Forces** - Force majeure such as climate, epidemics, earthquakes, etc.
5. **Technological Revolution** - This round is AI, which is both a productivity and a source of instability

> "One man's debts are another man's assets. So when the world is holding a lot of those bonds and when there's a large supply of those bonds that has to be sold..."
>
> One person's debt is another person's asset. When the world holds a lot of these bonds, and a lot of them have to be sold - the problem arises.
>
>——HBR Podcast, January 2026

## Interview 1: Exclusive interview with NYT Ross Douthat (2026-05-08) - "The Great Turmoil"

### Core argument

The United States will become "almost unrecognizable" in the next five years, and he described this dramatic, rapid, and disorienting transformation as "traveling through a time warp."

### Key Quotes

> "Almost unrecognizable" in five years.
>
> Within five years, almost unrecognizable.

> "Going through a time warp."
>
> Like traveling through a time warp.

### The "Heart Disease" Metaphor of Debt

- The United States has annual expenditures of approximately US$7 trillion and annual income of approximately US$5 trillion, leaving a shortfall of approximately US$2 trillion.
- Federal debt is about 6 times revenue, with weekly interest payments alone reaching about $19-20 billion (PGPF data)
- Dalio compared this state to the accumulation of plaque in the coronary arteries: "There's no heart attack yet, but the MRI is already showing it's coming."

> "My grandchildren and great-grandchildren not yet born are going to be paying off this debt in devalued dollars."
>
>My unborn grandchildren and great-grandchildren will repay this debt with devalued dollars.

### Policy recommendations

- The deficit must be reduced to 3% of GDP (in line with Finance Minister Bessent’s target)
- Otherwise, the final path is: the Fed is forced to print money → 1970s-style stagflation repeats itself

### Investment advice

- Leave the traditional 60/40 combination
- The total allocation limit for gold + cryptocurrencies is approximately 15%, as a hedge against the depreciation of the US dollar

## Interview Two: Prof G Markets Podcast – “How America went bankrupt” (April-May 2026)

### Core argument

Four forces are tightening at the same time, and the key window is within 2 years.

### Key Quotes

> "We're on the brink of some of these problems."
>
> We are already standing on the edge of some of these issues.

> "Particularly in the vicinity of, you know, two years away from what obviously is a confluence of more, more risks."
>
> In about two years or so, there will be a clear convergence of multiple risks.

### Four forces to tighten

1. Global demand for U.S. debt is declining
2. U.S. Treasury bond issuance is on the rise
3. The pace of technological disruption (AI) is accelerating
4. International conflicts are accumulating

Time window: Between 2026 midterm elections → 2028 presidential election.

## Interview 3: Fortune’s speech on "Stagflation has arrived" (2026-04-28)

### Core argument

The United States is already in a period of stagflation, and the independence and credibility of the Federal Reserve are key variables in this round.

### Key Quotes

> "We're certainly in a stagflationary period."
>
> We are indeed in a period of stagflation.

> "The Federal Reserve would lose its credibility."
>
> The Fed will lose its credibility (if it cuts interest rates now).

> "Everyone's going to look at how he behaves."
>
> Everyone will watch what he does.
>
> —— On Kevin Warsh, the incoming Federal Reserve Chairman

### Policy Paradox

- Interest rate cut → Inflation expectations lose anchor
- No reduction → Growth is suppressed
- The real key is not interest rates themselves, but the market's belief in "whether the Fed still cares about inflation"
