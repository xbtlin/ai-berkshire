## A company you use every day but barely understand

A contradiction: **749 million** people in China have used Didi at least once a year, with average daily orders of **40 million** and a peak of **65 million** orders. In terms of business volume, it is the world's largest single-market online ride-hailing platform, with an annual transaction volume of nearly half a trillion yuan. But you can’t find its shares on the New York Stock Exchange, Nasdaq, or Hong Kong Stock Exchange—any mainstream market.

Didi’s shares can only be purchased on the U.S. Over-the-Counter Pink Sheets (OTC) market under the code DIDIY, and its average daily trading volume is far lower than that of any ordinary Chinese concept stock. 700 million users and 40 million daily orders, in exchange for a "no one cares" label with no analyst coverage and no institutional participation. This dislocation is the real starting point for understanding Didi.

Let’s take a quick look at its share price trajectory:

- **June 30, 2021**: Listed on the New York Stock Exchange with an issue price of **US$14** and a market value of approximately **67 billion US dollars**
- **July 2, 2021**: On the third day after listing, the Cyberspace Administration of China announced a review and the App was removed from the shelves
- **June 2022**: Delisted from NYSE
- **July 2022**: Cyberspace Administration of China fined **8.026 billion**
- **January 2023**: App is back on the shelves, rectification passed
- **2024-2025**: OTC Pink Sheets trading, stock price fluctuating in the **3-7 USD** range
- **May 2026**: OTC stock price ~$3.55**, market cap ~$16.5 billion**

(Data sources: Yahoo Finance, Cyberspace Administration report, Didi 20-F annual report)

**In 5 years, the same company's market capitalization shrunk from $67 billion to $16.5 billion** -- but its business is bigger and more profitable than when it went public.

This is Didi’s biggest contradiction: **Business is getting better, but prices are getting worse**.

## Three common misconceptions about Didi

### Illusion 1: Didi is already "cold"

This is the most common perception.

The delisting crisis in 2021 has left a deep impression on the public - "Didi was fined", "Didi is delisted", "Didi is finished". But actual business data tells a different story:

- After the app was restored to shelves in January 2023, order volume** has seen double-digit growth for 12 consecutive quarters**
- Core orders in 2025 **18.24 billion**, more than when launched in 2021
- China Travel EBITA **~12.3 billion yuan**, main business cash flow is stable
- International business (14 countries including Brazil and Mexico) has achieved EBITA profit for two consecutive years

**Didi’s business is not only not declining, but is recovering quietly**. But because it trades in the OTC Pink Sheets market, there is no sell-side analyst coverage, no institutional investor attention, and no financial media tracking—most people’s awareness is still stuck in 2021.

**Opposite view**: The business recovery is true, but GAAP net profit is only **990 million** (vs adjusted 7.86 billion), and the gap between the two is huge. Adjusted net profit excludes items such as equity incentives and changes in fair value, and the true quality of earnings is still controversial.

### Illusion 2: Didi is a "taxi-hailing company"

To be more precise, Didi is a two-sided online platform that connects drivers and passengers and makes money through commissions and advertising.

The essence of Didi’s business model:
- Passenger pays **100 yuan**
- The driver took away about **79 yuan**
- Government taxes and fees + payment channel about **4 yuan**
- Passenger/driver subsidy is about **12-14 yuan**
- **Didi’s net income is about 4-4.3 yuan** (net take rate ~4%)

(Data source: Didi 20-F annual report, industry analysis and estimates)

This means that Didi's "real commission" is not the 20%+ as in the public impression, but **4%** after deducting all subsidies and incentives. This number determines two things:

1. **Didi is not a profit-making business** - the net profit margin is very thin
2. **Didi has almost no room to raise prices** - regulatory scrutiny, drivers complaining, and Amap keeping prices down

### Illusion 3: Delisting = No investment

After Didi delisted from the New York Stock Exchange in 2022, it traded on the U.S. OTC Pink Sheets under the code **DIDIY**.

Features of OTC trading:
- **Poor liquidity** - average daily trading volume is much lower than the main board
- **NO SELL-SIDE RESEARCH COVERAGE** – Not tracked by Wall Street analysts
- **Institutional Investors Restricted** - Many fund charters do not allow holding of OTC stocks
- **Serious information asymmetry** - But Didi still submitted a 20-F annual report as required by the SEC

This means: **Didi’s price discovery mechanism is incomplete**. The market value of US$16.5 billion may not reflect either the true value or the true risk - it reflects the superposition of "liquidity discount + information discount + regulatory discount".

**Contrary view**: The price of OTC may also be "correct" - the market is pricing a scenario in which "it may never be relisted and shareholders will never have access to normal liquidity". Cheap is not necessarily wrong.

## Comparison with "Understanding Pinduoduo"

If you have watched the "Understanding Pinduoduo" series - Didi and Pinduoduo have one thing in common: they are both Chinese concept stocks that have been seriously misunderstood by the market. But the reason for the misreading is completely different:

| Dimension | Pinduoduo | Didi |
|------|--------|------|
| Reasons for misunderstanding | Management deliberately does not let you see clearly | Delisting leads to information vacuum |
| Financial quality | Extremely high (gross profit 60%+, ROE 31%) | Average (net take rate 4%, net profit margin thin) |
| Valuation | PE 10.5 times (cheap but with trust discount) | PS 0.5 times (cheap but with liquidity discount) |
| Core Risks | Temu Political Risk + No Dividend | Regulation + Drivers’ Social Security + IPO Uncertainty |
| Suitable investors | Value arbitrage type who can accept high uncertainty | Event-driven type who can accept "special circumstances" |

Pinduoduo is like "a luxury product with a 40% discount, but I'm not sure if there are any holes."
Didi is like "a brand-name watch thrown into a pawn shop—it's not broken, but you don't know when you can redeem it."
