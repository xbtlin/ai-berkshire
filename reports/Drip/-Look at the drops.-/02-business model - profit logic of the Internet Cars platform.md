> "Understanding Didi" Series · Part 02 · Business Model

## How does Didi make money?

"Didi takes a 20% commission" is one of the most widespread misunderstandings in the Chinese Internet world. The real figure is: for every 100 yuan of fare, Didi gets about **4 yuan**. The remaining money was divided between drivers, subsidies, taxes and fees.

This 4% figure is the key to understanding Didi's entire business model - it determines where Didi's profit ceiling is, how narrow the pricing space is, and why a company with annual revenue of 226.7 billion has a GAAP net profit of only 990 million yuan. Let’s take a look at this machine taken apart.

Didi’s **226.7 billion** revenue in 2025 comes from three segments:

| Sector | Revenue (100 million yuan) | Proportion | Core indicators |
|------|------------|------|---------|
| **China Travel** | **2,019** | **89%** | Daily average ~40 million orders, GTV 333.8 billion |
| **International Business** | **149.5** | **6.6%** | Operations in 14 countries, orders +24.7% |
| **Other/Innovation** | **98.4** | 4.4% | Freight + Autonomous Driving + Refueling and Charging |

(Data source: Didi’s 2025 20-F Annual Report)

**89% of revenue comes from China travel**. To understand Didi, you must first understand this cash cow.

## How is the money for a typical order divided?

Suppose you hail an express train and pay **100 yuan**. The flow of this 100 yuan:

| Project | Amount (yuan) | Description |
|------|-----------|------|
| Driver in hand | **~79** | Official caliber: average commission **14-15%** |
| Government taxes + payment channels | ~4 | Value-added tax + WeChat/Alipay fees |
| Passenger subsidies/coupons | ~5-7 | Attract new customers and promote activity |
| Driver incentives/order bonuses | ~7 | Peak hours and bad weather subsidies |
| **Didi net income** | **~4-4.3** | **Net take rate about 4%** |
| **Didi EBITA** | **~1.8-2.0** | Excluding R&D, operations, and compliance costs |

(Data source: Didi 20-F, industry estimates. Note: There are large differences among cities and models. This is an approximate national average)

**Key Findings**:

1. **"20% commission" is a gross figure, and the net take rate is only 4%** - the middle 16 percentage points are all subsidies and incentives
2. **For every 100 yuan of GTV, Didi actually earns only 4 yuan** - this is an extremely "low-profit" business
3. **Almost no room for price increase** - Regulatory requirements will reduce the industry commission limit to **27%** from August 2024 (previously, some orders reached 29%), and public opinion continues to pay attention to driver income

**Opposite view**: Although the net take rate of 4% seems thin, the base is **450.8 billion yuan GTV** - the absolute value of 4% is ~18 billion yuan. Moreover, under the platform model, the marginal cost is extremely low, and there is almost no additional cost for each additional order.

## Bilateral network effect: Didi’s core moat

Online ride-hailing is a typical **bilateral network effect** business:

```
More passengers → more orders → more drivers willing to drive → shorter waiting times → more passengers
```

The strength of this flywheel directly determines the depth of the moat. Several metrics:

| Indicators | Didi (2025) | Description |
|------|-------------|------|
| Average daily orders | **~40-45 million** | The world’s largest single market online ride-hailing platform |
| Annual active users | **749 million** | Close to the total number of China Mobile Internet users |
| Driver waiting time | **< 2 minutes** (first-tier cities) | Direct result of network density |
| Passenger waiting time | **~3-5 minutes** (first-tier cities) | Experience barriers |
| ETA forecast accuracy | 12 years of data accumulation | Algorithm barriers |

(Data source: Didi 20-F, industry report)

**Core logic of network effects**: In a city, when the driver density of a platform reaches a critical point, it is almost impossible for latecomers to use subsidies to come back - because users can call a car by opening the app, and there is no reason to switch.

This explains a counter-intuitive phenomenon: **Didi App was removed from the shelves for two years from 2021 to 2023, and the market share dropped from 90%+ to ~70% - but after the App was restored, the share stabilized and did not continue to decline**.

**Consideration**: Network effects are real, but their intensity is decreasing. The AutoNavi aggregation model does not require the establishment of a self-built driver pool. It only needs access to small and medium-sized transport capacity to provide a "one-click entire network taxi" experience. Aggregation platform orders will grow from **7%** of the industry in 2019 to **31%** in 2024 (data source: industry report). If this trend continues, Didi's network effect will be "diluted".

## International business: Second growth curve or money-burning black hole?

Didi’s international business covers **14 countries**, 2025 data:

| Indicators | 2025 | Year-on-Year |
|------|------|------|
| Revenue | **14.95 billion yuan** | +24.7% |
| Order volume | **4.505 billion orders** | +24.7% |
| GTV | **117 billion** | +28.2% |
| EBITA | Profitable in the first three quarters, **Q4 single-quarter loss of 3.44 billion** (Brazilian food delivery expansion + Mexican subsidies) | — |

(Data source: Didi’s 2025 20-F Annual Report)

Core markets:

| Market | Brand | Status |
|------|------|------|
| **Brazil** | **99** | 55 million+ users, profitable in 2024, takeaway business online in 30+ cities |
| **Mexico** | DiDi | "Travel + Takeaway + Finance" three-piece set, subsidy period |
| Colombia, Chile, Peru | DiDi | Latin America expansion |
| Japan, Australia, New Zealand | DiDi | Asia Pacific Layout |
| Egypt | DiDi | African Bridgehead |

**Bull logic**: Brazil 99 is the most successful case of Didi’s internationalization - it is already profitable and is expanding from travel to food delivery and finance. If Mexico also gets through, international business can become Didi's "second leg."

**Bearish logic**: 2025 Q4 international EBITA loss **3.44 billion yuan** - full-year profits are concentrated in the first three quarters, with significant losses in the fourth quarter due to the expansion of Brazilian food delivery and the subsidy war in Mexico. The core issue of internationalization is whether subsidies are sustainable - Uber's experience in Latin America and Southeast Asia shows that local competitors (such as 99's competitor iFood) will not easily give up the market.

## Autonomous Driving: $5 Billion Option

Didi Autonomous Driving is an independent story:

| Key nodes | Content |
|---------|------|
| August 2023 | Sold the smart car + DaVinci team to Xpeng for **7.44 billion**, retaining L4 Robotaxi |
| March 2025 | Completed a new round of financing, valued at approximately **5 billion USD** |
| January 2026 | R2 Robotaxi, in cooperation with GAC Aion, begins road testing in Guangzhou |
| March 2026 | R2 obtained Beijing Intelligent Connected Vehicle Road Test License |
| 2026 (Planned) | UAE’s first overseas Robotaxi test |

(Data sources: Didi announcement, Gasgoo, electrive.com, CNBC)

**Core logic**: Didi’s unique advantage in autonomous driving is that it has the largest travel network in China. Robotaxi does not need to rebuild the demand side, and can directly connect to Didi App to place orders. This is something Baidu Apollo, Pony.ai, and WeRide do not have.

**Contrary view**: The commercialization timetable for L4 autonomous driving has been repeatedly delayed. The $5 billion valuation is priced at primary market financing, with a huge liquidity discount. Moreover, even if Robotaxi technology matures, it may **subvert Didi's own business model** - if drivers are no longer needed, the economics of the commission model will completely change.

## Electrification: an overlooked structural dividend

Didi’s 2025 annual report disclosed a data that is easily overlooked:

> **5.1 million electric vehicles are registered, accounting for 68% of monthly mileage**

(Data source: Didi’s 2025 20-F Annual Report)

What does this mean?

- The **energy costs** of electric vehicles are **one-third to one-quarter** of those of gasoline vehicles
- For drivers, the cost per kilometer is reduced → Net income increases → More willing to stay on the platform
- For Didi, driver costs are reduced → subsidy pressure is reduced → there is room for improvement in net take rate

**This is a structural change that is happening quietly**: China’s online ride-hailing electrification rate far exceeds that of other markets in the world, and Didi is the biggest beneficiary.

**Contrary view**: The dividends of electrification are shared by the entire industry and are not exclusive to Didi. Small and medium-sized transport companies on the AutoNavi aggregation platform are also electrifying.

## General judgment of business model

To summarize the above disassembly:

### Advantages (moat)

| Dimensions | Strength | Description |
|------|------|------|
| Bilateral network effect | **Strong** | Density barrier of 40 million daily orders |
| Brand is a verb | **strong** | "Didi" = taxi |
| 12 years of data + algorithm | **Zhongqiang** | ETA, order dispatch, pricing model |
| Electrified first | **Medium** | 68% of mileage electrified |
<unk> Shareholder rule **Tencent+Alibaba+SoftBank+Uber Jobendorment<unk>

### Disadvantages (vulnerabilities)

| Dimension | Severity | Description |
|------|-------|------|
| Net take rate is only 4% | **High** | Small profit, almost no room for price increase |
| Aggregation model erosion | **Medium High** | Amap rose from 7% to 31% of orders |
| Regulatory sensitivity | **High** | Data security is a "heart disease" |
| International business profits are unstable | **Medium** | Q4 single-quarter loss of 3.4 billion |

**Summary in one sentence**: Didi's business model is a combination of "strong network effect + weak pricing power" - the moat is on the demand side (user habits + driver density), and the vulnerability is on the supply side (commissions are suppressed + subsidies cannot be stopped). It is not an "algorithmic advertising machine" like Pinduoduo (gross profit 60%+), but closer to Uber - a matching machine with huge scale but thin profit margins.
