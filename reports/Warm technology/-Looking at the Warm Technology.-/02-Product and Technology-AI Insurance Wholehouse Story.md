# Core technical barriers: How deep?

This is the most serious question to discuss. Is it a barrier that Yanwa claims to be the biggest independent AI technology company in China's insurance industry?

Barrier I: Accumulation of data

By the end of 2025, the system in Henhua had cumulatively processed over **221 million** insurance cases, services over **44 million** insurance consumers, cumulative contribution to the first year ** $13 billion**.

This level of data is indeed a leader in the industry. The AI model is highly dependent on training data -- models that have handled 200 million cases, more accurate than models that have dealt with 20 million cases in theory.

** But there's a fatal question: to whom?**

The original ownership of the insurance case data belongs to ** insurance companies** and is not warm. What is warm and warm is the accumulation of ** model training experience** and ** statistical characteristics of de-esensitization** in the processing process, but the raw data cannot be taken with them.

** Counterpart **: Even if the original data were from insurance companies, the wind control rules, anti-fraud models, medical knowledge profiles that were extracted from the 221 million cases - these "experienced assets" are valuable and difficult to replicate quickly by competitors. Similar: Essenzel does not have data on his clients, but his industry experience and methodology are barriers.

# Barrier II: System switching costs

Once the insurance company has access to a warm insurance or compensation system, it is going to switch to other suppliers at a high cost - system interface, data migration, training of personnel, business interruption risk. This is typical **B2B SaaS sticky**.

The warm and warm data support this:
- 2025 policy renewal rate **98.4%**
- Cross-sales **65.0 ** (industry average 15-25%, source: offer quote from Frost Sullivan)

** But the paradox is: ** If the system is so sticky, why is the client retention rate only **69.6%** and is declining?

One possible explanation is the high stickyness of large clients (system depth embedded, not easily replaced), but the high rate of loss of ** small and trial clients (as long as it does not feel worth paying for). Of the 245 clients in warm-up, the real contribution to core income is probably only the dozens of heads.

# Barrier III: Industry understanding

Insurance is not a universal technology industry that can easily be introduced. Nuclear insurance rules, medical diagnostic logic, and criteria for the review of claims -- these require a depth of industrial knowledge.

CEO Luang's curriculum vitae: the IES network - - Aliun - - General Manager of the IT Department of Shanghai Conservancy - - Founder of the Warmwa Technology. Twenty years of experience in insurance technology.

** But the other way around **: Large insurance companies (peace, eunuch, human security) have their own technology subsidiaries, and the pool of talent and money is much warmer. They may only feel "outsourcing is more profitable than self-building" for the time being – and once AI technology matures further and the threshold is lower, the value of self-build reverses.
