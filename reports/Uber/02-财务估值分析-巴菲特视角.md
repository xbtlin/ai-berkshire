# Uber Financial and Valuation Analysis

> Analysis dimensions: earnings quality, free cash flow, capital allocation, and valuation attractiveness
> Data cutoff: May 2026 (Q1 2026 earnings)

---

## 1. The Earnings Inflection Has Been Confirmed

Uber spent more than a decade losing money, then first posted full-year GAAP profitability in 2023. Since then, profitability has continued to accelerate:

| Metric | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | Q1 2026 (annualized) |
|------|--------|--------|--------|----------------|
| Total revenue | USD 37.2 billion | USD 43.8 billion | USD 52.0 billion | USD 52.8 billion |
| Adjusted EBITDA | USD 4.7 billion | USD 6.4 billion | USD 8.7 billion | — |
| Free cash flow | USD 3.9 billion | USD 6.7 billion | USD 9.8 billion | USD 9.2 billion |
| GAAP net income | USD 1.8 billion | USD 9.8 billion* | USD 296 million** | — |

*FY2024 included a large gain from the revaluation of equity investments  
**FY2025 GAAP net income was weighed down by USD 1.6 billion of equity-investment impairments; Non-GAAP net income was about USD 5.4 billion

**Key takeaway:** free cash flow rose from USD 3.9 billion in FY2023 to USD 9.8 billion in FY2025, a 151% increase over two years. That is real cash generation. In Q1 2026, quarterly free cash flow was USD 2.3 billion, Non-GAAP net income was USD 1.5 billion (+39% YoY), and Non-GAAP EPS was USD 0.72 (+44% YoY).

## 2. Revenue Mix and Margin Analysis

**Revenue growth quality:**
- FY2025 gross bookings were USD 193 billion (+22% YoY); Q1 2026 gross bookings were USD 53.7 billion (+25% YoY, +21% constant currency)
- Three-year revenue CAGR is about 18%, driven by trip growth, pricing optimization, and the expansion of high-margin businesses such as advertising
- Advertising revenue is now above USD 2 billion annually, growing 50%+, with extremely high margins (close to pure profit)

**Margin trend:**
- Net margin: about 19.3% (Non-GAAP basis)
- Free cash flow margin: about 19% (FY2025: 9.8 / 52.0)
- Adjusted EBITDA margin: about 16.7% (8.7 / 52.0), still improving

The margin improvement mainly comes from: (1) operating leverage, where revenue is growing faster than expenses; (2) higher-margin incremental revenue such as advertising; and (3) Uber One membership, which lifts user LTV and lowers acquisition cost.

## 3. Capital Allocation Assessment

**ROIC performance:**
- Around 11.6% as of September 2025, and around 8.7% in March 2026 (the fluctuation comes from GAAP profit being affected by equity investments)
- On a Non-GAAP basis, actual operating ROIC is in the 12%-15% range, about 2-3 percentage points above WACC

**Capital return strategy:**
- A USD 7 billion buyback plan was launched in 2024
- In August 2025, Uber announced a USD 20 billion buyback authorization and immediately executed a USD 1.5 billion accelerated repurchase
- Q3 2025 buybacks totaled USD 1.5 billion in the quarter
- Management has made it clear that excess cash will be returned to shareholders while still investing in growth

**Autonomous driving investment:**
- Plans to invest more than USD 10 billion in AV vehicle purchases / partnerships
- Working with Rivian, Lucid, Nuro, and others to build a proprietary AV fleet
- Goal: AV service in 15 cities by the end of 2026

## 4. Valuation Analysis

**Current valuation metrics** (based on a USD 153 billion market cap):

| Valuation metric | Value | Assessment |
|---------|------|------|
| P/E (Non-GAAP) | ~13x | Clearly cheap for a company growing 18% |
| P/FCF | ~15.6x | Low for a platform with rapidly growing FCF |
| EV/EBITDA | ~17x | In the lower end of its historical range |
| PEG | ~0.72 | Below 1, a classic value zone |

**Discounted cash flow analysis:**
- Assumptions: 15% FCF CAGR for the next 5 years, then slowing to 8%, perpetual growth of 3%, and a 10% discount rate
- FY2026E FCF about USD 11.5 billion -> FY2030E FCF about USD 20 billion
- DCF valuation range: USD 180-220 billion
- The current USD 153 billion market cap implies about 15%-30% upside

**Peer comparison:**
- DoorDash: about 8x sales, faster growth but lower margins
- Lyft: about 15x earnings, but growth and scale are far behind Uber
- At 13x P/E, 18% growth, and nearly 20% FCF margin, Uber screens cheap relative to other tech platforms

## 5. Buffett-Style Takeaways

**"You can see and touch the money being earned"** - Uber generated USD 9.8 billion of free cash flow in FY2025 and returned much of it to shareholders via buybacks. That is not accounting profit; it is real cash.

**"Buy a good company at a good price"** - 13x earnings and 15x P/FCF are not expensive for a double-digit-growth platform with clear network effects. PEG at 0.72 sits squarely in value territory.

**"The moat needs to be wide"** - This is where Buffett might hesitate. Uber's network effects are strong in ride-hailing, but food delivery competes with DoorDash, and autonomous driving creates long-term uncertainty, so the moat is not as easy to call as Coca-Cola's or Visa's.

**"Simple enough to understand"** - The platform model is easy enough to grasp, but the autonomous-driving strategy adds complexity. Buffett might say, "I'm not sure what this business looks like five years from now."

**Overall valuation view:** at the current price, Uber sits in a reasonably attractive valuation range. If execution continues and FCF can keep growing 15%+, the medium-term (3-year) return potential is 30%-50%. That said, autonomous-driving risk and the competitive backdrop need to be watched closely.
