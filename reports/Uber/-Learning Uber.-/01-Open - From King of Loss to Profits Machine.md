## A set of facts that will make you rethink Uber

The day Uber went public in 2019, no one applauded. It fell 7.6% on the first day, setting a record for the largest first-day loss for an IPO in the US stock market at the time. Wall Street media collectively ridiculed it - annual losses of **$3-4 billion**, relying on subsidies to buy traffic, and never making a profit. That year, "Uber" almost became synonymous with "tech companies burning money".

Seven years later, this "cash-burning machine" has an annual free cash flow of **9.8 billion**, holds 76% of the U.S. travel market share in 70+ countries around the world, and has a buyback plan of up to 20 billion US dollars. The market narrative has flipped four times, and Uber’s business itself is simpler than any of the narratives.

Share price record over 7 years:

| Time node | Stock price | What happened |
|---------|------|-----------|
| May 2019 IPO | **$45** | Dropped 7.6% on the first day, "the least promising large IPO" |
| March 2020 | **~$14** | Epidemic crushes travel demand, lowest in history |
| February 2021 | **~$64** | Post-epidemic rebound + takeout outbreak |
| October 2024 | **~$82** | First consecutive profit, market repricing |
| October 2025 | **$102** (52-week high) | FCF nearly 10 billion, buybacks initiated |
| May 2026 | **~$75** | Autonomous Driving Panic + Pullback |

**In the past 7 years, the market’s narrative of Uber has flipped at least 4 times**: Money-burning machine → Victim of the epidemic → Food delivery savior → Profit machine → Disrupted by autonomous driving?

## What kind of business is Uber?

Opening Uber's FY2025 annual report, **$52 billion** revenue comes from three lines:

| Business Segment | FY2025 Revenue | Proportion | Year-on-Year Growth | Core Logic |
|---------|------------|------|---------|---------|
| Mobility | **29.7 billion USD** | 57% | +18% | Global ride-hailing, US share 76% |
| Delivery | **$17.3 billion** | 33% | +25% | Uber Eats, US share ~23% |
| Freight | **$5.1 billion** | 10% | -1% | Freight matching, just breaking even |

(Data source: Uber FY2025 Annual Report/SEC 10-K)

**The first key understanding: Uber is not a taxi company, nor a food delivery company - it is a two-sided network platform that connects supply and demand. **

Uber doesn’t own a car, hire a driver, or cook a meal. There is only one thing it does: **Use algorithms to match "people who want to go out" with "drivers who want to make money" and extract about 28% commission from each transaction**.

What does this mean?

- **Asset-light**: no vehicle depreciation, no inventory, extremely low fixed costs
- **High Operating Leverage**: Revenue grew 18%, but free cash flow grew 42% - profits grew much faster than revenue
- **Platform Economy**: The larger the scale, the lower the unit cost and the stronger the network effect

People who understand Uber as a "taxi company" can never figure out why it makes money. Understand it as an "algorithmic matching platform" and everything will be clear.

## From Loss to Profit: What Happened?

Uber’s cumulative losses exceeded **$30 billion** from 2019 to 2022 (including pre-IPO). Then in 2023, suddenly it was profitable.

It's not magic, it's three things happening at the same time:

### 1. Changed a CEO

In 2017, founder Travis Kalanick was ousted from the company amid a series of scandals. Taking over is former Expedia CEO Dara Khosrowshahi.

Dara does three key things:
- **Cut off loss-making businesses**: Exit China (sold to Didi), exit Southeast Asia (sold to Grab), spin off autonomous driving R&D department ATG (sold to Aurora)
- **Stop "growth at any cost"**: No longer spend money on subsidies to seize the market, and instead pursue unit economic benefits
- **Reshape corporate culture**: From the aggressive culture of "winner takes all" to mature corporate management

### 2. Release of operating leverage

When Uber stopped burning money to expand, the scale effect of the platform economy began to appear:

| Indicators | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | 3-Year Change |
|------|--------|--------|--------|---------|
| Total revenue | **37.2 billion** | **44 billion** | **52 billion** | +40% |
| Adjusted EBITDA | **4.7 billion** | **6.4 billion** | **8.7 billion** | +85% |
| Free Cash Flow | **3.9 billion** | **6.9 billion** | **9.8 billion** | **+151%** |

(Data source: Uber FY2023-2025 Annual Report)

Revenue grew 40% and free cash flow grew **151%**. This is the operating leverage of the platform economy - when the scale is large enough and no more money is burned, most of every additional dollar of revenue earned will directly become profits.

### 3. Advertising business takes off

Uber’s annual advertising revenue has exceeded **2 billion**, with a year-on-year growth rate of more than **50%**. It’s a nearly zero-margin-cost business—merchants advertise on Uber Eats and Uber takes a cut, with a profit margin of nearly 100%.

Referring to the precedents of Meituan and Amazon, advertising business may become one of Uber's biggest engines of future profit growth.

**The answer is two words: autonomous driving. **

## What is the market worried about?

Since the second half of 2025, Uber stock has fallen from a high of $102 to around $75, a correction of about 27%.

The core reason is not performance - Q1 2026 performance exceeded expectations - but the market's fear of **autonomous driving**:

- **Waymo** (owned by Alphabet) has completed more than **450,000** paid self-driving rides per week
- **Tesla** is moving ahead with Robotaxi plans
- If autonomous driving matures, Waymo and Tesla can bypass Uber and go directly to consumers - Uber's "middleman" role may be eliminated

This is the core reason why Uber is valued at only 13 times PE: **The market gives a "profit machine" now, but gives a "possible disruption" discount. **

Is this discount reasonable? This is what Part 03 of this series will discuss in depth.
