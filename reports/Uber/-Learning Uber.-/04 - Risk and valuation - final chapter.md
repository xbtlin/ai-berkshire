# Risk I: Supervision — driver classification issues

# What's the risk #

The Uber business model is based on a key assumption:** drivers are "independent contractors" rather than "staff".

If the driver is recognized as an employee, the Uber will have to undertake:
- Social security, health insurance, unemployment insurance
- Guarantee of minimum wage
- Paid leave
- Compensation for work-related injuries

This would increase Uber's operating costs** by 20-30 per cent** and directly erode the profit margin.

# Look on both sides

** Pessimistic perspective**: New Jersey may be the first domino. If more states follow, Uber’s profit rate may be reduced by 5-10 percentage points. California casual workers are subject to retroactive compensation proceedings involving billions of dollars.

** An optimistic perspective**: Uber has demonstrated its political lobbying capacity in California Prop 22 (which spent $200 million on the referendum). The flexibility of the casual economy is supported by a large number of drivers and consumers, with a large political resistance to full "staffing." And even if some parts of the country are employeeized, Uber can transfer some of the costs by raising prices.

# # Quantified estimates

If the main state of the United States (which accounts for 50% or more of Uber's income) classifies the driver as an employee:
- Operating costs increase by approximately **20-30%**
- Assuming that Uber can transfer half of the cost through price increases, the net effect is a decline in profit margins of around **10-15%**
- Calculated in EBITDA, SY2025, with an impact of approximately $900-1.3 billion**

This is a real but manageable risk -- not fatal, but significantly reducing Uber ' s profitability.

# Risk two: Competition — DoorDash and others

# Takeout market

The pattern of the United States take-out market is clear: DoorDash **67 per cent** vs Uber Eats **23 per cent**.

DoorDash is expanding - from takeout to grocery, liquor, convenience stores. FY2025 income **13.7 billion **, growth from year to year **28%, up faster than Uber's takeout business.

** The question is: **Uber Eats holds 23% of the share in the United States?

**optimal **: The cross-platform effect of Uber One provides a line of defense that DoorDash cannot replicate. As long as Uber’s travel continues to attract users, the takeout business will not be completely eroded.

** Pessimistic perspective**: The switching costs of the takeout market are extremely low. If DoorDash continues to expand its advantages, Uber Eats may be squeezed to 15-20% shares, with the profit margin further deteriorating after losing the scale effect.

# Out in the market

Lyft does not pose a strategic threat in the short term (the market value is only 1/20 for Uber). But in the medium and long term, attention needs to be paid to the diversion of autopilot players from the market - as discussed in detail in section 03.

# Risk three: GAAP profit 'noise'

Uber SY2025 net GAAP profit is **10.1 billion United States dollars** (including significant revaluation of return on equity investments), but if the fluctuations in investment returns are excluded, the net Noon-GAAP profit is approximately $5.4 billion**.

The previous year, SY2024, GAAP net profit ** $9.8 billion**, mostly also revalued equity investments. **$1.6 billion** was also impaired by SY2025 Q4 for the single season.

** This does not affect the real ability of Uber to operate, but may be misread by the market.** When GAAP net profits fluctuate significantly due to impairments in investments, they may trigger panic selling. The core indicator that investors need to focus on is ** free cash flow and adjusted EBITTA**, rather than GAAP net profits.

# Capital allocation: what is management doing

# # $20 billion buy back

In August 2025, Uber announced **$20 billion** of the repurchase plan (previously 7 billion) which, in current market value terms, is equivalent to repurchase **13%** of the liquidity unit.

This is a positive sign:
- Means management believes that the stock price is undervalued
- Management incentives aligned with shareholders (CEO Dara holds a large share)
- In contrast to "42 billion dollars in cash" in over-screw.

# 10 billion + autopilot input

The drive-in-a-caravan input was **10 billion or more** in parallel with the buy-back.

** There's a tension here**: "My stock is cheap, buy back" while spending 10 billion dollars on a highly uncertain field.

** Supporters say **: Autopilot is a strategic investment in Uber's survival, waiting to die. 20 billion buy back + 10 billion AV inputs, Uber produces 9.8 billion FCFs in a year that is completely affordable.

** The opponents say **: 10 billion dollars in autopilots is tantamount to acknowledging that the core moat (human driver network) of ours may fail. If the $10 billion is also bought back, the returns will be better for short-term shareholders.

** Objective evaluation**: This strategy of "one side of the border pays" is reasonable, but implementation risks are not low. The key is whether 10 billion dollars of investment will yield tangible returns in 3-5 years.
