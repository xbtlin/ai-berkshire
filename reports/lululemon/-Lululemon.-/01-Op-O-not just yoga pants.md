## A company that is hard to understand

In two and a half years, $46 billion in market value has evaporated by $31 billion – is this a normal story of brand collapse, or an opportunity misjudged by the market?

The stock price went this way:

- December 2023: **$511** All-time High - The "Next Nike" Narrative reaches its peak
- Mid-2024: Around **$340** – growth starts to slow, market still hesitant
- Early 2025: **$250** - North America continues to experience negative growth, and confidence is shaken
- January 2026: CEO Calvin McDonald departs – the final straw
- May 2026: **~$125** – **75%** from peak

Now it only has a market cap of $15 billion. But when you open the financial report, you will find a set of numbers that are completely inconsistent with this stock price curve:

| Indicators | FY2025 (as of 2026.2) | Magnitude judgment |
|------|---------------------|---------|
| Total Revenue | **$11.1 billion** (+5%) | Slowing but still growing |
| Net profit | **$1.58 billion** | Down from peak, but still huge profit |
| Gross profit margin | **58.4%** | Top level of global consumer goods |
| ROIC | **30.6%** | Earn $0.3 back for every $1 invested |
| Growth rate in China | **+29%** | The only high-speed growth engine in the world |
| PE (TTM) | **~9x** | 10-year average of 41x, currently only 22% of the mean |
| Buybacks | 5-year cumulative **$4.8 billion+** | Shares outstanding decreased by 9.4% |

Putting this all together - **A global brand with 58% gross margin, 30% ROIC, and $1.58 billion in annual revenue trades at only 9x PE, which is 78% lower than the 10-year average**.

If you think this is just "too much" - you may not see the real crisis behind it.
If you thought this was "the end of the brand" - you probably didn't see another story told by the financial data.

What this series wants to do is to show both sides and let you judge for yourself.

## What kind of business is lululemon?

Most people’s impression of lululemon is that it sells yoga pants. This is the most common misunderstanding and the one that most affects judgment.

Open the income structure and see:

| Channel | Proportion of revenue | Characteristics |
|------|---------|------|
| Directly operated stores | **~55%** | Approximately **811** stores worldwide, high square footage efficiency |
| E-commerce (self-owned) | **~39%** | Breaking through $1 billion in a single quarter |
| Wholesale/Others | **~6%** | A very small proportion, the company intends to control |

**DTC (direct-to-consumer) accounts for more than 90%. ** This means: complete self-control of brand narrative, own customer data, and gross profit margin much higher than the wholesale model.

Let’s look at the regional structure:

| Region | Revenue share | FY2025 growth rate |
|------|---------|------------|
| Americas | **72%** | **-1%** (Same-store sales -3%) |
| Mainland China | **~16%** | **+29%** |
| Other International | **~12%** | **+17%** |

Data source: lululemon FY2025 annual report; China Daily

**lululemon's real business is not "selling yoga pants to earn the price difference" - it is a high-end sports lifestyle brand company with technical fabrics as the carrier, community culture as the barrier, and the DTC model as the skeleton. **

Its business model is closer to Hermès (brand premium + full price sales + direct sales control) than to Gap (wholesale drive + discounting to clear inventory).

This difference determines: lululemon's **58% gross profit margin** has nothing to do with the words "selling pants" - it is the ** in the consumer goods industry whose financial quality is second only to luxury goods.

## Three common misconceptions about lululemon

### Illusion 1: "Just another sportswear brand"

There are hundreds of activewear brands on the market. What is the essential difference between lululemon and them?

One number will suffice: **95% full price sell-out rate**.

In the clothing industry, being able to sell out 70% of the full price is considered excellent. Nike is 60%-70% year-round. And lululemon sells 95% of its products at full price - which means almost no discounts, no tail goods, and no brand dilution.

This is not a financial characteristic of an activewear brand. This is the financial characteristic of **Luxury**.

A pair of Align yoga pants costs **$98-$128**, which is more than **50%** more expensive than similar products from Nike. It’s not that consumers don’t know there are cheaper options—they know, and they still choose lululemon. This pricing power comes from two things:

1. **Product Power**: The touch of Nulu fabric is indeed different, and most people who have worn it admit this.
2. **Identity**: Wearing lululemon means "I pay attention to physical and mental health" - this is the same logic as wearing Moutai and wearing Apple

**Contrary view**: Pricing power is not eternal. Under Armor once had a similar brand premium, which has since dropped from $150 to $7. Once the "coolness" of a brand disappears, the premium evaporates quickly. And lululemon’s appeal among the 18-25 year old group is already being eroded by Alo Yoga.

### Illusion 2: "Growth has peaked"

Looking at the total, FY2025 revenue only grew 5%, which is really not sexy. But take it apart:

- **Americas (72% Revenue)**: **-1%**, 7 consecutive quarters of weakness
- **China (16% revenue)**: **+29%**, Q4 same-store growth +30%
- **Other International (12% revenue)**: **+17%**

**lululemon is not "peak growth" - it is "the American engine stalled and the Chinese engine just started." **

There are currently only **170 stores** in China, and brand awareness is only **more than a dozen percentage points**. For reference, Nike has more than **6,000** stores in China, and its brand awareness exceeds **90%**. If lululemon's popularity in China increases from 15% to 30%, China's revenue will at least double.

In 2026, lululemon will also enter six new markets** Greece, Austria, Poland, Hungary, Romania and India**.

Data source: lululemon official press release; Chain Store Age

**Opposite view**: There are geopolitical risks in the Chinese market. When consumer nationalism rises, foreign brands are the first to be affected. Local brands (Crazy Lolita, MAIA ACTIVE) are on the rise, with pricing 30%-50% lower than lululemon, and the quality gap is narrowing. Putting all growth in China is a risky strategy.

### Illusion 3: "9 times PE = cheap, it’s time to buy"

The math is correct - **9x PE is the lowest ever since lululemon went public** and is 78% lower than the 10-year average of 41x.

But low PE does not mean cheap. There may be three reasons for low PE:

| Possibility | Explanation | If this is the case |
|--------|------|---------------|
| The market is overly pessimistic | There is no problem with the brand, it is just a short-term crisis | Excellent buying opportunity |
| Permanent growth slowdown | North America will not recover, China is the only engine | Reasonable valuation, no bargains to be found |
| Irreversible decline of the brand | Falling from the altar like Under Armor | It will be cheaper |

**What kind of situation does the current 9 times PE reflect? ** This is the core issue of this series, which will be discussed in detail in Part 04.
