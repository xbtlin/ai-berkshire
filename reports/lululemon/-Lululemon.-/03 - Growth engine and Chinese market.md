# Two engines, one is out, one is on

In 2025, Lululemon sold less clothes in North America than in the previous year**. In China, the rate of increase was **+29%**.

This is not a story of "slower speed," but of "engine replacement" -- whether the old engine can be taken over by the new engine before the engine is completely out -- is the most central question. Put the numbers out:

From +12% to -1%, it took only two years. North America has at least four layers of fire out:

# 1. Market saturation

Lululemon has about 405 stores in North America** covering almost all major cities and mid-to-high-end business circles. The simple opening of shops has already ended.

#2. Competition encroaches

Alo Yoga and Vuori are eating marginal customers of lululemon. Not core loyal clients (their retention rate is still 89-92%), but new clients who "first time considering sports and leisure clothes" -- they have more options now.

The three-platform convergence of users has grown significantly over the years, implying that consumers are comparing shopping across brands** rather than loyal to a brand.

3. Macro-consumption environment

The US consumer confidence index continued to weaken in 2025-2026. High-end consumer goods, although more resistant than popular brands, would also delay purchasing $128 of yoga pants in times of economic uncertainty.

#4. Product innovation is not fast

Breezethrough, the Get Low visual event - The Cores Reverse not only lost revenue, but also shaken the "product excellence" brand narrative. In the consumer goods industry,** the product flipped = the brand trust loss**, and restoration took time.

** The counter-view**: Negative growth in North America may be cyclical rather than structural. Nike also experienced negative growth in North American inter-store sales in 2015-2017, and has recovered growth under the new product cycle (Air Max, Vaporfly). If the new CEO of Lululemon can launch a successful product line, North America is likely to return to positive growth.

Lululemon has only one sixth of Nike’s brand name and thirty-five percent of Nike’s number of doors.** If we raise the profile from 15% to 30%, there is room for doubling the income of the Chinese region**.

More importantly, Lululemon has benefited from two structural trends in China:

**1. Upgrading of health-care consumption among the middle class in China**

The country’s participation in sports has been increasing steadily over the past five years. Yoga, running, and outdoor sports have grown rapidly, driving demand for high-end sports.

**2. Consumption preferences for high-end foreign brands**

In sports and leisure, Chinese consumers still have a clear preference for high-end foreign brands. Lululemon is even more positioned in China than in North America – it is not just "motion costumes" in China, but it is a symbol of the middle class lifestyle**.

# Three risks to the Chinese market

But it is risky to keep all growth in China.

** Risk I: Geopolitics**

If extreme events (like the impact on H&M of the 2021 Xinjiang incident) occur, the growth of lullulemon in China may be instantaneous. China’s income ratio is currently 16% – and if it rises to 25-30%, the impact of this tail risk will be disproportionately greater.

** Risk II: Local brand rise**

The quality gap between indigenous brands is narrowing, and price advantages are evident. Among young, price-sensitive consumers, the attractiveness of indigenous brands is increasing.

** Counterpart **: For the past 10 years, every foreign-owned high-end brand that has entered China has faced the fear of "home-grown substitution," but the fact is that – the growth of real high-end brands (Apple, Starbuck, Lululemon) in China is rarely interrupted by native substitution. Local brands are being robbed of low- and medium-end markets, not high-end markets.

** Risk three: Inevitability of slowdown in growth**

+29% is unlikely to last forever. As the base becomes larger and the door penetration increases, the rate will slow. The company's own guidelines have been reduced from +29% to +20%.

The key question is: ** What level is it slowing? ** If it slows from +29% to +15%, China is still a good growth engine. If it slows to +5%, then the lullulemon really doesn't have an engine.

# This is a small conclusion

1. **Lululemon growth pattern is highly fragmented**: North America (72% income) is negative and China (16% income) is on a year-round basis + 29% (Q4 + 24%)
2. ** China is the most important engine of growth**: 170 doors, 15% brand name, low-digit city size, large space
3. ** China, however, cannot offset the drag in North America in the short term**: the income share is too small to reach more than 25% in 3-5 years
4. ** Six new markets for international expansion**: India has room for imagination, Eastern Europe is branded with limited short-term growth
** High level of certainty in the track** (8% CAGR) but good track does not mean that the company must win.
** Two of the most critical variables**: whether North America can stop the fall + how high can China keep up with the growth rate?
