**Why couldn't Nike?** Nike tried to push the DTC vigorously (2020-2023) and found that total abandonment of the wholesale channel could lead to a fall in income from the cliff and forced to embrace distributors like Root Locker. Lululemon has been on the DTC route since its inception, and there is no "transitional pain".

Consumers are willing to pay the premium not because they don't know there's a cheaper option, but because lululemon represents an **identity signal** -- "I'm healthy, I have taste, I belong in this circle."

This is the same logic as the Maotai: the Maotai sells not alcohol but social identity; the Lululemon sells not flour but lifestyle identity.

** The counter-view**: brand premium is the most vulnerable of all moats**. It relies on "coolness" -- and the coolness changes.

Piper Sandler's youth survey shows that in the 18-25 age group, **Alo Yoga's "cool" rating has surpassed the lululemon**. This is not today's problem -- 18-year-olds can't afford $128 in yoga pants today. But five years later, when these people become the core consumers of 25-30 years of age, they will choose what they think is cool, not what they're wearing.

**under Armour is the lead in the front car**: from $150 to $7, the loss of brand premium took only five years.

#Alo Yoga: The most dangerous challenger

The threat of Alo Yoga is not in size -- it's only a tenth of the share of lululemon. The threat is that it's redefinition of "cool"**.

At the high-end DTC sports and leisure market (which excludes wholesale channels), Alo already accounts for **14%**. It creates a lululemon image in young consumers through radical red marketing and fashion orientations like "street to yoga."

** The deadliest dimension of brand competition, not product, not price, not channel -- is "who represents the aesthetics of the time." ** Alo is trying to replace lululemon in this dimension.

#Vuori: Challenger of the Men's Clothes Market

Vuori has already valued **$5.5 billion**, and is preparing IPOs. With California Coast Wind, it has established a differentiated position in the **men's sports and leisure market**. Lululemon has been trying to make a man's dress (currently about 25% of the income), but Vuori is proving that consumers can choose a brand that is not called lululemon on a male dress.

** The real answer may be in the middle**: The signal of brand ageing does exist (the young population prefers to transfer, the product turns over, the forced discounts) but it is not yet irreversible (core core guest retention rate is still over 89% and Chinese market brand power is still rising).

** The key variable is time**: brand ageing is cyclical if the new CEO Heidi O'Neill is able to secure brand narratives and win young consumers again by 2027. If the situation continues to deteriorate by 2028, the probability is structural.
