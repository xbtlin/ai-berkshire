| Dimension | Score | Key rationale |

| **Overall** | **★★★★☆** | **Good business + reasonable management + excellent price; the key variable is the management transition and brand rejuvenation with younger consumers** |

## Final Investment Recommendation

### Conclusion: Buy gradually, in tranches

| Scenario | Probability | Target price | Implied return |
|------|------|--------|----------|
| Bull: new CEO successfully turns the business around, international growth stays strong | 25% | USD 320-340 | +170% |
| Base: low growth, stable margins | 50% | USD 195-210 | +65%-75% |
| Bear: competition worsens, brand weakens | 25% | USD 100-110 | -8% to -16% |
| **Expected value** | - | **~USD 195** | **+64%** |

### Suggested action

1. **At the current price (USD 119), start building a starter position**: 1%-2% of the portfolio, with ample margin of safety
2. **Add more at USD 100-110**: if the stock falls into that range, the risk/reward becomes more attractive
3. **Wait for O'Neill's first full quarter after taking over in September 2026 (FY2026 Q3, expected to be reported in December) before deciding whether to build to a full position**
4. **Street consensus target is USD 190, with a "Hold" rating**

### Key positive catalysts
- Release of Heidi O'Neill's first strategic plan after taking over
- China continuing to grow 20%+
- North American same-store sales returning to growth
- FY2026E gross margin stabilizing and recovering

### Key negative watch items
- North American same-store sales turning negative
- Gross margin continuing to decline
- China growth falling below 10%
- Forced large-scale discounting

## Four-Master Mock Commentary

### Business Essence View

> "Lululemon does not sell yoga pants; it sells the identity tag of 'I care about my health.' A DTC mix above 90% means the brand story is fully controlled in-house, which is a key sign of a good business. A 58% gross margin and the flywheel effect of 28 million members show that the underlying logic of the business has not been broken. But if slower North American growth and forced discounting keep getting worse, they will start to undermine the business itself."

### Valuation View

> "Buying a consumer company with 30% ROIC at 9x earnings is something I have not seen often in fifty years of investing. The real question is: will Lululemon be worth more in 10 years than it is today? I think the answer is probably yes - the long-term athleisure trend is intact and the brand still has deep roots. At this price, you do not need any heroic assumptions to get a satisfactory return. The risk is that you may have to endure one or two years of volatility."

### Inversion / Second-Order Thinking View

> "Think in reverse: in what situation would this investment lose a lot of money? The answer is if the brand completely loses appeal among younger consumers and falls from grace, like Under Armour. I do not think that probability is zero, but Lululemon's community model and product strength are much deeper than Under Armour's. What worries me more is the management transition - a CEO change is the biggest single risk event for a brand company. I would wait until the new CEO proves herself before going heavy."

### Civilization-Trend View

> "The rise in health-lifestyle penetration from 25% to 45% is a civilization-level trend, and that matters more than any company's short-term volatility. Lululemon sits at the center of that trend - especially in China, where low-single-digit share means enormous room to grow. If you can buy a leading brand in a rising civilizational cycle at 9x earnings when others are panicking, that is usually a good long-term decision."
