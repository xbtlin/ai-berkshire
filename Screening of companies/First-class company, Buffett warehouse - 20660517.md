### Summary of financial characteristics of Buffett’s stock selection

1. **High ROE is the core screening condition**: The 10-year average ROE of the companies that passed is generally above 15%, and the top targets (Apple, Moody's, AXP) exceed 25%. The only exceptions are OXY and PetroChina - both of which are "bargain speculation" rather than typical Buffett-style long-term holdings.

2. **Free cash flow is almost all positive**: Buffett rarely buys companies with negative cash flow. The only exception is buying at the bottom of the cycle (such as OXY in 2020).

3. **Obvious preference for high gross profit margin**: Apple (46%), Coca-Cola (62%), Moody's (74%), VeriSign (88%), American Express (63%). Most of Buffett's favorite companies have gross profit margins above 40%, which shows that they have strong pricing power.

4. **Net interest rate >10% is normal**: The net interest rate of most core positions is between 10-30%. Kroger (1.8%) and Nucor (5.4%) are rare exceptions with low net profit margins, and their positions are also relatively small.

5. **Repurchase and stock reduction rather than dilution**: Almost all positions are being repurchased. In the past five years, the share capital has not expanded but has shrunk. This is Buffett's clear preference for capital allocation.

6. **Adequate interest coverage**: Except for financial companies, the interest coverage of all positions is >5 times, and most of them are >10 times. Buffett is extremely averse to debt risk.

7. **The pattern of failure/edge cases**:
   - Kraft Heinz: High-priced acquisition + goodwill impairment = Buffett admits he "made a mistake"
   - OXY: a special opportunity to "buy when it's cheap", an atypical position
   - Cyclical industries (energy, steel) are naturally difficult to meet the stability requirements of ROE and net interest rate

**Core conclusion**: Buffett's positions are highly concentrated in companies with "high ROE + high gross profit margin + strong cash flow + low leverage + continuous repurchase". Such companies usually have strong moats (brand, network effects, switching costs) and can sustain excess returns over the long term. The 7-bar filter can effectively identify his core position logic.
